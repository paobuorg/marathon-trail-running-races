## 60-second summary: what this high-altitude downhill race is

The Tenzing Hillary Everest Marathon is an annual high-altitude trail event in the Khumbu region of Nepal, held each 29 May from the Everest Base Camp area to commemorate the 29 May 1953 first summit of Everest by Tenzing Norgay Sherpa and Sir Edmund Hillary. The 42.195 km full marathon runs from Everest Base Camp (Khumbu Icefall) via Gorak Shep, Lobuche, Thukla, Dingboche, the Bibre loop, Pangboche and Tengboche down to Namche Bazaar; the course follows trekking trails between Sherpa villages and is a long, large-net-descent mountain route.

This race cannot be prepared for like an ordinary city marathon. The start is already above 5,300 m, most of the race stays above 3,400 m, and acute altitude illness, cold and strong UV, trekking-acclimatisation pacing, and the cost and insurance boundary for helicopter evacuation matter more than raw pace. Entry requires a doctor's certificate of health and altitude comfort, and international runners must carry travel insurance that covers medical care and air evacuation; the packages exclude international medical insurance and emergency evacuation costs.

- **Distance and direction.** Full marathon 42.195 km, a point-to-point downhill line from Base Camp to Namche Bazaar; half marathon 21 km from Dingboche; UTME ultra 70 km from Base Camp; all three finish in Namche Bazaar.
- **Elevation.** Start 5,356 m (event-page text) / 5,360 m (profile chart) / 5,364 m (itinerary camp); finish Namche Bazaar 3,440 m; the point-to-point net descent is about 1,916–1,920 m by subtraction. Cumulative ascent and descent totals are not published by the organiser.
- **Next edition.** The homepage countdown points to 2027-05-29; the official Nepali registration page states 2027 is the 22nd edition and the 74th anniversary of the first summit. The exact start time was not located on official pages this pass and awaits the edition bulletin.

## History and origins

The official homepage records the event as an annual international high-altitude adventure sports event held on 29 May from Everest Base Camp, celebrating the historic 29 May 1953 ascent by Tenzing Norgay Sherpa and Sir Edmund Hillary. A third-party route compilation records a first test run on 19 May 2003; the official Nepali page counts 2027 as the 22nd edition. Read together: the event took annual form from 2003, and edition counting runs in parallel with the summit anniversary. This page does not mix the summit anniversary with the marathon edition count.

The event serves runners and non-runners alike: non-runners trek the historical route pioneered by Hillary and Tenzing to the Kala Patthar viewpoint (5,545 m) for views of Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse and the surrounding peaks, then descend to the Namche finish to watch. The finish area hosts traditional dance, yak dance, entertainment and food organised by the Namche Women's Group. Keep the event edition separate from the course version: edition and commemoration narrative come from the organiser's history, while mileage, elevation and cutoffs come from that edition's event page. Past package prices, trek schedules and cutoffs must not be carried over to the next edition.

## Course direction, segments and geometry

**Course shape.** The full marathon is a point-to-point downhill trekking trail: start at Khumbu Icefall / Everest Base Camp, via Gorak Shep, Lobuche, Thukla to Dingboche, around the Bibre loop, then Pangboche, Tengboche, Laublsasa and Kyanjuma to the Namche finish. The half starts at Dingboche and joins the same descending trail at the full marathon's midpoint. The 70 km ultra detours via Phortse, Machhermo, Phortse Tenga and Mong La after Pangboche before returning to Namche (third-party compilation basis).

## Common misreadings

- **"42.195 km means a standard flat-city marathon distance."** The mileage inherits the marathon standard, but the course starts above 5,300 m on mountain trekking trails; altitude, downhill eccentric load and altitude pacing cannot be estimated from a road marathon.
- **"The 5,356, 5,360 and 5,364 start figures are contradictions."** They come respectively from the event-page text, the profile chart and the itinerary, with different rounding; this page keeps all three and does not pick one as the precise altitude.
- **"A few extra days in Khumbu means you are acclimatised."** Acclimatisation lowers risk but does not remove altitude illness; headache, nausea or unsteady gait still require on-site medical assessment, not pushing through or self-medicating.
- **"The entry fee is the whole package price."** The USD 500 international payment is a non-refundable deposit to confirm the spot; package prices (USD 999–6,995) are listed separately, and international flights, visa, Kathmandu meals and personal expenses are excluded.
- **"Race insurance covers medical care and evacuation during the race."** The package accident insurance covers race day only; international travel/medical insurance and emergency evacuation or medical costs are exclusions, and runners must carry their own cover that includes air evacuation.
- **"The AllTrails line is the official GPX."** The page embeds a third-party AllTrails map only; no downloadable track was published, and the third-party route must not be treated as certified geometry.

## Timing and cutoffs

**Timing basis.** The official cutoff for full and half marathon is Tengboche (32.6 km) at 4:00 PM; runners who cannot reach it by the cutoff are disqualified. The official pages reviewed this pass did not publish chip-timing method, gun-vs-chip time distinction, the number of split timing points, or an overall full-marathon finish limit. Third-party-compiled ultra 70K cutoffs are Pangboche 11:30 AM, Machhermo 5:00 PM and Namche 11:59 PM; this set awaits verification against the official edition manual.

**Segment pacing reference (from the official profile, not an official pace table).** The early full-marathon section descends from 5,360 m to Dingboche (17.3 km, 4,359 m), about 1,000 m of drop, but aerobic capacity is reduced at altitude, so flat-downhill pace expectations do not apply. The middle section from Dingboche to Pangboche (27.5 km, 4,012 m) is a steady gentle descent; Pangboche to Tengboche (32.6 km, 3,868 m) is the cutoff-check section; the closing Laublsasa–Kyanjuma stretch rolls before the Namche finish. Cutoffs are the latest time to leave a checkpoint, not a pace guide. ## May Khumbu weather window

**Official weather description.** The Essentials page gives the May Khumbu temperature band: daytime 10–20°C, morning and night -5 to -10°C depending on altitude and wind chill; UV is strong above 3,400 m with SPF 30–50 sunscreen advised; clear nights -2 to -8°C; snow of 1–2 ft is possible. May is late dry season in Khumbu, but afternoon convection and snow can still appear; wind-chill at high altitude feels colder than the thermometer reading.

These are modelled grid-point reanalysis values, not course measurements; the 5,157 m grid sits below the Base Camp start and above the Namche finish and cannot represent either end exactly. Gear and pacing should be planned for three layers — sunny daytime, cold mornings, possible afternoon snow — not for a single daily high.

**Environmental risks.** Exposure at altitude combines wind, precipitation and hypothermia risk; moraine, scree and village trails alternate, and the downhill sections demand ankle strength and route judgment. May flights are weather-sensitive at Lukla and may be delayed, so the itinerary should carry a buffer.

## Medical and insurance boundary

**This page states boundaries only; it does not diagnose or prescribe.** Acute altitude-illness red flags include persistent headache, nausea or vomiting, markedly disturbed sleep, abnormal drop in performance, unsteady gait, altered awareness or slurred speech. With any of these, report to the nearest station or the accompanying doctor/guide; on-site medical staff decide whether to continue, descend or evacuate. Do not push through at altitude, add medication on your own, or self-manage oxygen. The Essentials page advises against sleeping pills at higher altitude; carry personal regular medication and check it with the accompanying doctor or guide first.

**Withdrawal and transfer path.** Doctors accompany the group to EBC and back to Lukla; for urgent medical need, helicopters are arranged at extra cost to transfer to the nearest facility or a Kathmandu hospital. The page's guideline figure is about USD 600 per person one way when the helicopter is full (five people); this is a general reference, not the race-day evacuation tariff. Actual cost, sorties and weather limits follow the organiser and rescue arrangements that edition. Runners who miss the cutoff or are unfit to continue are taken down by the team to lower villages and Lukla, then connected to flights.

**Insurance must cover helicopter evacuation.** The Essentials page states that all members require travel or medical insurance from their own countries; packages exclude international travel, trip cancellation and medical insurance, and emergency evacuation or medical costs. The local Nepali package accident insurance covers race day only. Before entering, confirm the policy covers: medical care at altitude, air ambulance / helicopter evacuation, and onward transfer to Kathmandu or overseas care if needed, plus trip cancellation and interruption. Check exclusions, altitude limits and rescue-first-payment clauses line by line. This page does not recommend a specific insurance product and does not replace a doctor's assessment of individual health.

## Travel, flights and evacuation path

**Arrival and domestic leg.** International flights arrive at Kathmandu (KTM); the event arranges the Kathmandu–Lukla fixed-wing return, or Ramechhap–Lukla return with shuttle bus connection. Lukla Airport (2,860 m) is a short high-altitude airstrip; flights depend on cloud, wind and visibility, and delays or rescheduling are not uncommon in the Khumbu season. The package does not cover a weather-driven helicopter change.

**Ascent and descent flow.** Team flow: Kathmandu → Lukla → Phakding → Namche → Khumjung/Deboche → Dingboche → Lobuche → Gorak Shep → Base Camp; race day descends from the Base Camp direction to Namche; post-race Namche → Monjo → Lukla → Kathmandu. Mobile coverage between Base Camp and Namche is limited; agree meeting points and communication with the team, leader and emergency contacts in advance.

**Visa and entry.** Packages exclude the Nepal visa; page figures are USD 50 (30 days) or USD 30 (15 days). Entry formalities, document validity and on-arrival arrangements follow the prevailing Nepali entry rules.

## Training transfer

1. **Build altitude adaptation before mileage.** The start is above 5,300 m and most of the race stays above 3,400 m; flat long runs do not translate directly into altitude endurance. Schedule staged high-altitude blocks, monitor sleep, resting heart rate and session feel, and know acute altitude signals; the framework is in [Altitude and running](/en/knowledge/altitude-and-running/).
2. **Downhill eccentric strength and mountain terrain are the core.** The net descent is around 1,900 m over the race, alternating scree, moraine and village trails; the downhill loads quadriceps eccentrically and demands ankle stability and route judgment. Training should include downhill repeats, down-stairs and trail-surface adaptation; see [Uphill and downhill running basics](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/).
3. **Back-count the arrival date against the acclimatisation itinerary.** The official schedule places adaptation halts at Namche and Dingboche and takes about ten days up to Gorak Shep/Base Camp. Flying straight to altitude and racing within days carries high risk; reserve the full adaptation window rather than compressing it to two or three days. Travel fatigue and jet lag are covered in [Jet lag and race travel](/en/knowledge/jet-lag-and-race-travel/).
4. **Practise cold, layering and trail gear in training.** Mornings at altitude can be below freezing while daytime sun is strong; layer changes and carried warmth need to be rehearsed. Gear trade-offs are in [Trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/) and [Cold-weather running](/en/knowledge/cold-weather-running/); walk through the [race checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/) before departure.
5. **Hold pace down deliberately at altitude.** Altitude limits aerobic supply, and the early downhill can pull pace too high, draining you later. Back-count segment pace from cutoffs and personal feel; pacing principles are in [Race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/). Do not project a low-altitude marathon finish time onto this race.

## Race-day execution card

**The day before.** At Base Camp confirm bib, ID and personal medication; check next-morning start arrangements and the team meeting point; inspect warmth layers, gloves, hat, sunscreen and supplies; leave insurance and emergency contact details with the leader; rest early with no extra intensity.

**Early section (Base Camp → Dingboche).** Start at altitude: hold effort down, do not chase the downhill inertia; complete the first water and food intake; watch headache, nausea and breathing rhythm; pass Gorak Shep, Lobuche and Thukla on the team rhythm.

**Middle section (Dingboche → Pangboche → Tengboche).** Refuel and self-check after Dingboche; Pangboche to Tengboche is the cutoff check (32.6 km, 4:00 PM). If condition drops, report to the station and leader early rather than deciding only at the cutoff.

**Closing section (Tengboche → Namche finish).** The end rolls; keep downhill cadence controlled; on reaching the Yak Parking Lot finish, warm up, rehydrate and refuel, and report to the team.

**Stop and withdrawal rules.** Persistent vomiting without food intake, unsteady gait, altered awareness, chest pain or obvious palpitations, or shaking that cannot be relieved: take the nearest station for medical assessment and do not continue through. Missing a cutoff means exiting per the rules and being arranged for withdrawal by the team; do not hitch a ride forward to keep scoring.
