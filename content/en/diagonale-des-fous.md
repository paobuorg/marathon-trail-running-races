## What this course is

The Grand Raid de La Réunion stages five races each year on the island, and La Diagonale des Fous is the longest of them, and its name is often rendered in English as "the madmen's diagonal". It starts in Saint-Pierre in the south and cuts diagonally across the island to Saint-Denis in the north. Start and finish are in different cities, which makes it a point-to-point volcanic-island trail ultra rather than a loop.

The organiser's own definition is blunt: about 180 km with 10,200 m of positive gain, crossing the Réunion national park (Pitons, Cirques and Remparts, a UNESCO World Heritage site) through volcanoes, cirques and cliff walls. The official description talks about narrow paths, ravines, rivers and summits, and states plainly that changing weather can make an already difficult route harder still.

The official cut-off table lists 33 points in total: one departure (DEP), 16 timed checkpoints (CP), 13 reference points with no cut-off (PR), two life bases (BV) and one finish (ARR). In table order the full sequence is: Saint-Pierre Ravine Blanche 0.0 km (DEP), Domaine Vidot 14.0 (CP1), Notre-Dame de la Paix–École 33.4 (CP2), Parking aire Nez de Bœuf 45.4 (CP3), Mare à Boue 55.6 (CP4), Coteau Kerveguen 65.1 (CP5), Croisée Coteau Kerveguen 67.6 (CP6), Intersection Cap Anglais 70.5 (PR1), Gîte de Bélouve 76.4 (CP7), Hell-Bourg 80.6 (BV1), Parking Bras Marrons 84.3 (PR2), Ilet Mare d'Affouches 85.9 (PR3), Plaine des Merles 87.2 (PR4), Intersection Col de Fourche/Plaine des Merles 93.1 (PR5), Plaine des Merles 93.8 (CP8), Départ Sentier Scout 96.0 (CP9), La Plaque 102.4 (PR6), Ilet à Bourse 104.4 (PR7), Grand Place les Bas–École 107.8 (CP10), Roche Ancrée 111.3 (PR8), Roche Plate–Plateau Cerf 116.4 (CP11), La Brèche 118.9 (PR9), Ti Col Maïdo 122.4 (PR10), Maïdo–Tête Dure 123.9 (CP12), Ilet Savannah 140.5 (BV2), Chemin Ratinaud–Kalla 147.9 (CP13), Lot. La Résidence Grande Montagne 153.9 (PR11), La Possession–Place Festival 155.1 (CP14), Grande Chaloupe 164.5 (CP15), Saint-Bernard–Les Bambous 169.0 (PR12), Colorado 173.8 (CP16), Belvédère de la Vigie 177.8 (PR13) and La Redoute 180.8 (ARR).

There is no Cilaos in that list. The 2026 edition puts its first major base at Hell-Bourg at 80.6 km, not at Cilaos as older material describes. The archived official 2013 rules still say the race crosses the three cirques of Cilaos, Mafate and Salazie, but that describes the old course. Old elevation profiles, GPX files and race reports that use Cilaos as a node do not match the 2026 route, so preparing for this edition means using the current official material. The official pages also state that the route may be modified for administrative, practical or path-condition reasons, and that some passages cross private land open only during the race.

The official 2026 results page reports 2,766 starters, 2,126 finishers and 640 DNFs. The men's winner, Hugo Deck, is listed at 22:54:19; the women's winner, Ruth Croft, is listed at 27:13:23. The official limit is 66 hours, with cut-offs running from Thursday 22:00 to Sunday 16:00.

Seven segment notes, all based on the 2026 official cut-off table's points, altitudes and cumulative values:

- **Segment 1 (0 → 55.6 km)** is the longest segment of the whole course, lifting from 2 m near sea level to 2,023 m at Nez de Bœuf and then dropping to Mare à Boue at 1,604 m. It crosses the volcano side, and the official description warns that changing weather can make the route harder. Domaine Vidot (14.0 km, 649 m) is the first timed checkpoint (CP1); Notre-Dame de la Paix (33.4 km, 1,686 m) already puts the race above 1,600 m.
- **Segment 2 (55.6 → 80.6 km)** first passes Coteau Kerveguen (65.1 km, 2,187 m, CP5) and climbs to Croisée Coteau Kerveguen (67.6 km, 2,475 m, CP6), the highest timed point in the official cut-off table. It then passes Cap Anglais (70.5 km, 2,154 m) and Gîte de Bélouve (76.4 km, 1,502 m) before descending to Hell-Bourg (80.6 km, 995 m). The segment loses 1,761 m, more than it gains.
- **Segment 3 (80.6 → 96.0 km)** climbs back from Hell-Bourg to around 1,800 m: Bras Marrons at 84.3 km, Ilet Mare d'Affouches at 85.9 km, Plaine des Merles at 93.8 km (1,808 m) and the Sentier Scout entrance at 96.0 km (1,635 m). It covers 15.4 km, and the official service table shows hot food and a drop bag at Hell-Bourg.
- **Segment 4 (96.0 → 116.4 km)** enters the inner cirque. After Sentier Scout the route drops to La Plaque (102.4 km, 896 m) and Ilet à Bourse (104.4 km, 889 m), then passes Grand Place les Bas (107.8 km, 665 m) and Roche Ancrée (111.3 km, 544 m) before climbing to Roche Plate (116.4 km, 1,256 m). The segment gains 1,549 m and loses 1,927 m.
- **Segment 5 (116.4 → 140.5 km)** has the largest descent of the race, −2,305 m. It passes La Brèche (118.9 km, 1,294 m), Ti Col Maïdo (122.4 km, 2,029 m) and Maïdo–Tête Dure (123.9 km, 1,950 m), then uses 16.6 km to fall from the 2,000 m level to Ilet Savannah at 133 m; subtracting the official cumulative figures gives about 1,904 m of descent over that stretch.
- **Segment 6 (140.5 → 155.1 km)** leaves Ilet Savannah through Chemin Ratinaud–Kalla (147.9 km, 440 m) and La Résidence Grande Montagne (153.9 km, 52 m) to La Possession (155.1 km, 20 m). It gains 834 m and loses 944 m, ending at 20 m, back at the coast.
- **Segment 7 (155.1 → 180.8 km)** closes along the western coastal and mountain edge: Grande Chaloupe (164.5 km, 10 m), Saint-Bernard (169.0 km, 456 m), Colorado (173.8 km, 682 m) and Belvédère de la Vigie (177.8 km, 347 m), then the final 3 km into La Redoute. It still gains 1,262 m, and the official cut-offs place it from Saturday night into Sunday.

The segment distance, gain and loss are not separately published profiles; they are differences between the official cumulative columns. Each difference can be checked against the official cumulative values: Mare à Boue (CP4) 2,894 / 1,295; Hell-Bourg (BV1) 4,043 / 3,056; Départ Sentier Scout (CP9) 5,364 / 3,736; Roche Plate (CP11) 6,913 / 5,663; Ilet Savannah (BV2) 8,095 / 7,968; La Possession (CP14) 8,929 / 8,912; La Redoute (ARR) 10,191 / 10,154 (cumulative gain first, cumulative loss second). The organiser publishes no separate segment profile, and this page uses no third-party profile or estimate.

A number of things need explaining beyond the table:

- **Only three drop-bag points**: Hell-Bourg (80.6 km), Ilet Savannah (140.5 km) and the finish at La Redoute. The official drop-bag page says Diagonale runners get three bags, all handed in at Ravine Blanche at the start; each bag is capped at 5 kg and must not contain cash, a camera or a phone, and uncollected bags are destroyed 24 hours after the race closes.
- **Only four hot-meal points, per the official Repas chaud column**: the 2026 table marks a hot meal only at Mare à Boue, Hell-Bourg, Ilet Savannah and the finish at La Redoute; "soup" elsewhere is not a meal.
- **Personal assistance is legal at only 15 points**: the official assistance page states that personal assistance is allowed only within 300 m before and after an official aid station, and only at designated points. Of the 33 points in the 2026 race, 15 carry an O in the Assistance personnelle column: the start, Domaine Vidot, Notre-Dame de la Paix, Nez de Bœuf, Mare à Boue, Hell-Bourg, Départ Sentier Scout, Grand Place les Bas, Roche Plate, Ti Col Maïdo, Ilet Savannah, La Possession, Grande Chaloupe, Colorado and the finish; Croisée Coteau Kerveguen, Gîte de Bélouve, Plaine des Merles, Maïdo–Tête Dure and Chemin Ratinaud–Kalla carry an X. Accepting a pacer, a bottle hand-off or private support outside those zones is penalised under the rules.
- **Medical cover has specific points, but is not an escape route**: the official Médecin column marks 10 points — the start, Mare à Boue, Hell-Bourg, Plaine des Merles, Grand Place les Bas, Roche Plate, Maïdo–Tête Dure, Ilet Savannah, La Possession and the finish; the Infirmier column marks 15 points, adding CP1 Domaine Vidot, CP2 Notre-Dame de la Paix, CP3 Nez de Bœuf, CP6 Croisée Coteau Kerveguen and CP16 Colorado. Physiotherapy and podiatry are only at the two bases and the finish, and osteopathy only at the finish. Grand Place and Roche Plate sit inside the cirque and are not served by the official shuttles, so "has medical" must not be read as "has a road out".
- **Trekking poles are banned for the whole race**: the official route page states that any type of pole is forbidden under penalty. It is an explicit race rule, so it has to be checked before the start.
- The cut-offs above come from the 2026 official table. Whether the next edition keeps them must be re-checked. The official table also has an "opening" column; this table uses only closing times so that opening times are not mistaken for deadlines.

## Surface and terrain

This page found no redistributable surface breakdown from the organiser, and it invents no percentages. All that can be confirmed is the organiser's own description: narrow paths, ravines, rivers and summits, crossing a national park of volcanoes, cirques and cliff walls. The route page also warns that some sections cross private land and are open only during the race, and that reconnaissance between Bassin Plat and Piton Sec is expressly forbidden.

The official point table shows the broad shape of the terrain, though this is not a surface ratio: the first 45 km climb above 2,000 m on the volcano side; the middle repeats up and down between Hell-Bourg and Grand Place/Roche Plate; the final section closes along the cliffs and ravines of the west coast. Ilet à Bourse, Grand Place and Roche Plate inside the cirque are not on the official shuttle list; the Maïdo side is a long descent.

The 2026 mandatory list includes a head torch with spare batteries, at least 1.5 litres of water, a whistle, a charged phone with the Sara Event app, two elastic adhesive bandages 2.5 m long and 6–8 cm wide, enough food to reach the next aid station, a waterproof hooded jacket with taped seams (a windproof shell is explicitly rejected as insufficient), a long-sleeved technical warm layer, a reusable cup, identity documents and a survival blanket of at least 1.4 × 2 m.

## Risks and escape points

- **Night**: the 22:00 start and the Sunday 16:00 finish cut-off give a 66-hour limit, and the official schedule runs across Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights. A head torch, spare batteries and a warm layer should follow the mandatory-equipment list.
- **Altitude**: the highest timed point in the official table is 2,475 m at Coteau Kerveguen, and the lowest is 2 m at the start, a span of 2,473 m; the mandatory-equipment list is written for carrying the kit over the whole course.
- **Weather**: the official description says changing weather makes the route harder, and mentions ravines, streams and summits; this page does not state specific wind speeds or rainfall intensities as course facts. The mandatory-equipment list covers night, wind, cold and rain with a head torch and spare batteries, a waterproof jacket and a long-sleeved warm layer.
- **Private land and reconnaissance**: the organiser forbids pre-race reconnaissance between Bassin Plat and Piton Sec because the land is private, and some sections open only on race day. Any plan to walk the course in advance must start with land permission; route material is not a right of access.
- **Semi-autonomy and the limits of help**: mandatory equipment must be carried for the whole race, and marshals may check packs anywhere. Personal assistance is allowed only within 300 m of 15 designated points. Together these mean that "a friend handing me a bottle by the roadside" is by default a rule breach on this course.
- **Escape points**: the road nodes covered by the official shuttles are the Maïdo summit (RF08), Ilet Savannah (140.5 km, base 2, from the Expobat car park) and the finish at La Redoute (SODIPARC/Citalis); the Sentier Scout/Salazie side is served by CIREST. Hell-Bourg is base 1 and an official drop-bag point. The organiser does not present these spectator shuttles as an evacuation service. Grand Place and Roche Plate inside the cirque are not served by the official shuttles, and the official pages do not list an internal evacuation shuttle; the current evacuation procedure should be confirmed with the organiser before the race.
- **Medical network**: the race page lists an ambulance, the Croix Blanche, doctors, nurses, physiotherapists, podiatrists, osteopaths and rescuers. A medical post is not an escape point, especially inside the cirques.
- **Attrition**: the 2026 edition had 2,766 starters, 2,126 finishers and 640 DNFs; those numbers come from the official results page's stats field.

## Getting there

The organiser publishes only the access information it can stand behind, and this page sticks to it rather than guessing at outside arrangements.

- **Airport**: the official material names Roland Garros airport as the arrival airport for international runners, and says volunteers met arrivals from 3 to 13 October 2026 with coffee, Creole snacks and updates on the route, the weather and the state of the trails. The organiser calls Roland Garros "the first aid station of every Grand Raid race". Regular scheduled transport from the airport to Saint-Pierre or central Saint-Denis is not published on the official pages, so this page marks it "not published".
- **Runner buses to the start**: on 15 October 2026 there were two runner-bus routes to Saint-Pierre. The western route left the car park opposite Cinepalmes in Saint-Denis at 17:10 and called at La Possession 17:25, Le Port 17:45, Saint-Paul 18:10, Boucan Canot 18:25, Saint-Gilles-les-Bains 18:35, Hermitage 18:45, La Saline 18:55, Saint-Leu 19:10, Étang Salé 19:30 and Saint-Louis 19:45 before reaching Ravine Blanche at 20:00. The eastern route left Sainte-Marie town hall at 16:15 and called at Sainte-Suzanne, Saint-André, Bras Panon, Saint-Benoît, Sainte-Anne, Sainte-Rose, Saint-Philippe, Saint-Joseph and Petite-Île before also reaching Ravine Blanche at 20:00. The organiser asks runners to register for the bus option in advance.
- **Spectator shuttles**: on the start side, the organiser runs a free 17:00–23:30 loop from the ZAC Canabady car park in Saint-Pierre to Ravine Blanche, with no reservation needed. On the volcano side, a shuttle runs between Bourg-Murat and the Nez de Bœuf area every 30 minutes from Thursday 18:00 to Friday 12:00. On the Salazie/Sentier Scout side, CIREST operates on Friday and Saturday. The Maïdo is served by RF08 from Friday 12:00 to Saturday 20:30. Ilet Savannah is served from the Expobat car park from Friday 15:30 to Sunday 00:00. The finish at La Redoute is served by SODIPARC/Citalis from Thursday to Sunday.
- **Driving**: the organiser treats shuttles as the main solution and arranges car parks around the sectors; the movement pattern it describes is to park at an official car park and switch to the shuttle for the start or the finish.
- **Rail**: the race website offers no rail option at all, so this page does not invent a rail connection.
- **Accommodation and car rental**: the official pages give shuttles and parking but no unified accommodation or rental package, so that is marked "not published" rather than filled in with personal booking experience.
