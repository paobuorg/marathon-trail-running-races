## What this race is

Mt.FUJI 100 is a long trail race at the foot of Mount Fuji. The main event, FUJI100mi, goes around the mountain's foot from Mount Fuji Children's World in Fuji City, Shizuoka Prefecture, to Fuji Hokuroku Park Fujisan GX Stadium in Fujiyoshida City, Yamanashi Prefecture. The 2026 racer information lists the race area as Fuji City and Fujinomiya City in Shizuoka and Narusawa Village, Fujikawaguchiko Town, Fujiyoshida City, Oshino Village and Yamanakako Village in Yamanashi. The 2027 race information extends the co-organisers to ten municipalities.

The race has not always carried this name. The official history table records the 100-mile event as UTMF from 2012 to 2022, as FUJI in 2023 and as FUJI100mi from 2024 to 2026. A press release dated 1 October 2023 explains the 2024 changes: the name becomes Mt.FUJI100 from the 12th edition, the start moves to midnight, the finish moves to Fuji Hokuroku Park, and KAI70k has a 21-hour limit.

The history page also preserves the race's breaks. The 2016 UTMF is listed as 44.8 km with 1,926 m of gain, marked as shortened with the finish moved to Asagiri Roadside Station; 2020 and 2021 are listed as cancelled; there is no 2017 row and the page does not explain why. The 2026 FUJI100mi is recorded as 165.3 km with 6,461 m of gain, won by Chris Myers in 17:50:13 and Ying Li in 22:18:12.

The 2027 outline is already published: 23 to 25 April 2027, FUJI100mi 165.3 km with 6,463 m of gain, three starts on 23 April at 17:00, 17:15 and 17:30 with about 700 runners each, limits of 45 hours 30 minutes, 45 hours 15 minutes and 45 hours, and a final cut-off on 25 April at 14:30. The 2027 aid-station and cut-off table is not published; the official download page says it will appear around March 2027.

One more official record fixes the 2026 identity. The World Trail Majors race list includes Mt.FUJI 100 with the dates 2026/04/24-26 and describes it as connecting the roughly 100 miles of trails at the foot of Mount Fuji with the people and culture around them. On 2026-11-08 that page listed 2026 dates only and did not say whether the race is in the series for 2027, so this page does not infer 2027 membership from it. The race's own 2027 information describes FUJI100mi as a World Trail Majors eligible race, a Western States Endurance Run 2028 qualifying race and an ITRA National League race. That is the organiser's wording for a different edition; the two are shown side by side and not merged.

## Entry: ITRA points and the self-responsibility clause

The FUJI100mi entry requirements are on the official entry page. Entrants must be at least 18 on race day and able to finish within the limit without getting lost; they must also have finished, between two years before the general entry opening date and the day before it, either one ITRA-certified race worth 4 points or more, or two races worth 3 points or more, and must be able to prove it on the ITRA results page. Three virtual events are explicitly excluded: Virtual FUJI100mi 2026, Virtual KAI70k 2026 and Virtual ASUMI40k 2026.

The 2027 entry has three routes. Priority entry is open from 19 October 2026 at 12:00 to 15 November 2026 at 23:59 to the top 100 overall in Mt.FUJI100 2026 and to elite athletes, and the secretariat sends the application link to those eligible in advance. General and donation entry run from 2 November 2026 at 12:00 to 15 November 2026 at 23:59, with a lottery if the quota is exceeded, results on 1 December 2026 and payment from 1 to 15 December 2026. Donation entries are first come, first served and are not part of the lottery. Elite invitations use ITRA Performance Index thresholds: free invitations at 850 for men and 715 for women, paid invitations at 800 for men and 650 for women, all based on 100 km or 100-mile races.

The fees are on the same page. The FUJI100mi general entry is 50,000 yen including tax; the donation entry is 105,000 yen, of which 55,000 yen is a donation and 50,000 yen is the entry fee, and the donation is not tax deductible; the private supporter entry is 5,500 yen, limited to 500 people with a lottery if oversubscribed. After payment no change of event or cancellation is accepted for any reason and fees are not refunded; the right to participate cannot be transferred; a photo ID such as a driving licence or passport is required when exchanging the number card.

The clearest documented difference from many Western races is not the lottery but the self-responsibility clause. The entry page requires entrants to understand the distance and the special nature of running through mountainous terrain for more than a day and night, to have trained for it, and to be able to handle trouble, bad weather such as low temperatures, strong winds, rain and snow, extreme fatigue, digestive and internal-organ problems, muscle pain and minor injuries on their own. It states that entrants must overcome these issues themselves and that the organisers are not responsible for them, and that a violation of the participation conditions in the 2027 race may lead to refusal of entry from 2028 onwards.

This page does not adjudicate between the Japanese and Western systems. What can be confirmed is that this official text makes self-responsibility, weather competence and ITRA proof part of the entry conditions; whether other races also write this is a question for their own official texts, and this page does not fill the gap with second-hand impressions.

Both the 2026 racer information and the 2027 race information put the FUJI100mi field at 2,000 including priority, general and donation entries, and the 2027 information lists 500 support staff. ## The volunteer system

The 2027 race information says FUJI100mi needs 500 support staff. The volunteer page still carries 2026 information and opens by saying that it is for the 2026 tournament and that readers should wait for an update. The 2026 recruitment ran in two rounds: first-round results on 19 January, confirmation on 28 January, LINE invitations on 29 January; second-round recruitment from 2 to 13 February, results on 27 February, confirmation on 7 March, LINE invitations on 8 March; manuals distributed through activity-specific LINE groups on 20 April.

One requirement is hard. From the 2026 tournament, both ordinary and emergency communication before and during the race uses LINE groups by activity, and people without their own LINE account cannot apply. The same page states that shifts are 8 to 10 hours depending on area and activity and may run against the body clock, and that the weather on the day may change activity times and volunteers may be asked to extend.

Transport and lodging are part of the volunteer page as well. Volunteers travelling to Fuji Hokuroku Park by public transport can use the event loop bus, which runs from the second parking lot via Fujisan Station, Fuji Resort Hotel and the Fujikyu Highland express bus stop and back, and does not pass Kawaguchiko Station during public-transport hours; drivers can use the parking-lot shuttle. Interpretation volunteers work mainly in English and Chinese, and the page says that if there are not enough interpreters, runners generally contact the interpreter at headquarters or ordinary volunteers handle the situation with an interpreter app.

Not every aid station is reachable by public transport. The volunteer page lists the stations that must be reached by car or motorbike: F1 Fujinomiya, F2 Fumoto, F3 Shojiko, F5 Oshino, F7 Nijumagari and F8 Fujiyoshida. That directly shapes volunteer and supporter movement: at those stations the race provides no ordinary public transport, and family members cannot arrive by train or bus at short notice.

## Cut-off culture: five documented Japanese practices

Several rules on this course are more specific than the cut-off times themselves.

The first is the double gate. Each aid station has an entrance and an exit time; a runner cannot enter at the entrance cut-off and cannot leave after the exit cut-off. A runner who has left may return before the entrance cut-off, but must use the designated passage and inform staff. The rules page explains this as a safety measure for runners arriving close to the cut-off, not simply as a time limit.

The second is the ban on sleeping. The rules page prohibits sleeping on the course for two reasons: the impact on the natural environment, and the risk that a sleeping runner may be mistaken for someone needing emergency help. This differs from many ultras that allow short sleeps at aid stations or rest areas; this page states the official rule and does not infer the enforcement details, because the organiser does not say which indoor areas may be used for rest.

The third is the pole ban until F5. The rules page prohibits poles on the whole FUJI100mi course except from F5 onwards; poles must be packed in a drop bag beforehand, tree branches may not be used as substitutes, and once a runner collects poles at F5 they must carry them to the finish. The organiser calls this an experimental measure to be reviewed from monitoring results and notes that pole-free zones remain after F5, mainly inside the national park.

The fourth is staying on the trail and no-overtaking zones. The rules page prohibits leaving the trail, including cutting corners, and sets no-overtaking sections where the natural environment needs protection. The course page adds that many course sections and venues are used with permission only during the race, and that runners who want to train on the route should use the four published test-run courses.

The fifth is that F7 Nijumagari is not a retirement point. The rules page states that, as a general rule, runners may not retire at the F7/K3 Nijumagari Pass, where they may have to wait a long time and where supporters, friends and family may not pick them up by car. The manual adds that a runner may not go back through a cut-off point or rejoin the course from it. For runners who assume every aid station is an exit, this changes the withdrawal plan directly.

Two timing rules are easy to miss. The manual states that there is a railway crossing next to Fujikyu Line's Fuji-Q Highland Station and that waiting for a train to pass is added to race time. The rules page states that the organisers aim to reach a runner within 3 hours of a call but that in FUJI100mi and KAI70k it can take 6 hours or more, and that runners should choose equipment on the assumption of waiting six hours in cold wind or freezing rain.

## Mandatory equipment: 19 items and 2 kg

The rules page lists mandatory items by event, with 19 shared by FUJI100mi and KAI70k. The table names two lights of at least 80 lumens and at least 4 hours of burn time with spare batteries for each; a cup of at least 150 cc; at least 1 litre of water at the start and when leaving each aid station; food; a survival blanket of at least 130 by 200 cm; a whistle; taping tape; a portable toilet; a hooded rain jacket and rain trousers; a fleece or similar long-sleeved top; leg cover to the ankle; gloves and a hat covering the ears; a first-aid kit; the number card and IC chip; a pack; and a power bank able to charge the phone.

Weight is a second hard line. The rules page states that total equipment must exceed 2 kg at the start and when leaving each aid station and must never fall below 1 kg anywhere on the course; checks take place during the race, and missing mandatory items can lead to immediate disqualification. Each runner wears an IC chip on each shoe, and runners who withdraw or are disqualified must return the chip at the retirement desk of each aid station.

The same page adds safety clauses: doctors and rescue teams may stop a runner who cannot continue; a runner still near a checkpoint after the checkpoint time, including near the finish, may be stopped and asked to return to their vehicle; and even without a safety problem the organisers may create walking sections, change the course or cancel the race if the trail cannot support the number of participants. Carrying extra equipment is not a private insurance policy but part of this rule system.

## Getting there

The start and finish are in different cities. The start is Mount Fuji Children's World in Fuji City, Shizuoka; the finish is Fuji Hokuroku Park Fujisan GX Stadium in Fujiyoshida City, Yamanashi. They are tens of kilometres apart, so "go back to the start to collect the car" is not a workable plan.

There is no runner parking at the finish. The entry page states that there is no parking available for athletes at the finish venue, Kitafuji Park, and that parking is reservation-only with tickets sold in advance. The manual is more specific: the main parking lot of Fuji Hokuroku Park is closed, only vehicles with a parking permit may enter during race hours, there is no parking for runners and supporters at the venue, and drop bags cannot be left at Fuji Hokuroku Park.

The main public transport is the event loop bus. The 2027 fares are 3,800 yen for a three-day ticket, 2,700 yen for two days and 2,200 yen for one day, all tax included, with sales from 29 January 2027. The route runs from Fujisan Station via Fuji Resort Hotel and the Fujikyu Highland express bus stop to the second parking lot of Fuji Hokuroku Park; only the 00:00-07:00 loop on 25 April passes Kawaguchiko Station. Buses run on 23 April from 7:30 to 19:45, on 24 April from 02:00 to 24:00 and on 25 April from 00:00 to 16:00, roughly hourly with about one every 30 minutes at peak times.

Lodging appears on official pages mainly as paid travel products. The 2026 official access tour is sold by Fujikyu Travel as a package tour and every plan requires advance reservation. It includes a three-night stay at PICA Fujiyoshida, about seven minutes on foot from the finish, with check-in from 10:00 to 19:00 and check-out by 11:00; and a plan to stay in Fuji City the night before, with check-in and gear check at a special venue in Fuji City from 18:00 to 22:30 and a bus to the start the next day. There are also buses from Shinjuku and Yokohama that stop at the check-in venue and continue to the start: Shinjuku at 9:00 and Yokohama at 8:30, reaching Fuji Hokuroku Park around 11:00 and the start venue around 15:30 after a 14:00 departure.

The organisers do not publish free lodging, a camp or an athletes' village. The manual states that the gymnasium at Fuji Hokuroku Park has a temporary rest area at the finish but no designated napping area, and strongly advises booking accommodation for the entire race period in case of early withdrawal through illness or injury; it also states that camping and tents outside designated campsites are prohibited and that sleeping in cars in the parking lot is not permitted. Within what can be checked here, the lodging limits around Mount Fuji are: no official campsite, no official free lodging, no runner parking at the finish, and no published lodging arrangement beyond the paid tour products.

For runners who want to see the course beforehand, the official page provides four Strava test-run routes and a warning that high mountain sections may still hold snow. The same page states that many course sections and venues are permitted only during the race and cannot be used as the race route at other times.

These are documented risk statements, not weather statistics.

## Difficulty and risk

The difficulty assessment here is an editorial judgement, not an official rating. The organisers publish no difficulty grade for FUJI100mi, only distance, gain, limit and cut-offs.

By the official figures this is a 165.3 km point-to-point course with 6,461 m of gain and a 45-hour-30-minute limit, starting and finishing in different cities, with cut-offs running from F1 at 22:30 on 24 April to the finish at 14:30 on 26 April. Its difficulty is not one steep climb but the combination of continuous night running, temperatures between 500 m and 1,600 m, and a rule set that removes sleep, easy retirement and public-transport escape.

The risks fall into four groups that the official text supports. The first is weather and cold: the entry conditions and gear rules repeatedly name low temperature, strong wind, rain and snow, and the rules page asks runners to plan for a six-hour wait. The second is fatigue and digestion: the entry conditions list extreme fatigue, internal-organ and digestive problems, muscle pain and minor injuries as issues runners must handle themselves. The third is limited escape: F7 Nijumagari is not a normal retirement point, supporters cannot pick runners up by car, and several aid stations can only be reached by car or motorbike. The fourth is the crossing and transport: waiting at the railway crossing is added to race time, there is no runner parking at the finish, and there is no all-night bus on the night of 24 April.

The official table gives only the start, eight aid stations and the finish as withdrawal points, with the F7 restriction. The organisers do not publish how long it takes to evacuate from each aid station to a hospital, nor ambulance response times; the rules page gives only the 3-hour target and the warning that it can take more than 6 hours. This page does not fill in those numbers.

## Training transfer

This is a 100-mile-class point-to-point loop: an official edition record of 165.3 km with 6,461 m of climb, about 39 m per kilometre; elevation 500–1,600 m, two nights on course, limit 45 h 30 for the first wave. The training focus is sustained night running, repeated foothill up-and-down and late-race cut-off margin.

The table below converts four finish targets to average speed over 165.3 km, excluding climb, rest, queueing and level-crossing waits; the 2026 manual's nine cut-offs remain the hard limits.

| Target finish | Total time (h) | Average speed (km/h) | Margin to the 45.5 h limit |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 30 h | 30 | 5.51 | 15.5 h margin |
| 36 h | 36 | 4.59 | 9.5 h margin |
| 40 h | 40 | 4.13 | 5.5 h margin |
| 45.5 h | 45.5 | 3.63 | at the barrier |

- Night: the 2026 four waves started 17:00–17:45 with cut-offs through 14:30 the next day; long training runs should include night sections and drills for cold and wind.
- Repeated up and down: elevation varies between the 527–1,144 m timing points over foothill trails and passes; quad strength for repeated descents needs specific work.
- Self-sufficiency and withdrawal: F1, F2, F3, F5, F7 and F8 are reachable only by car or motorbike, so family cannot arrive at short notice; plan aid and support around these station transport conditions.
- This page is not a training plan and does not replace a coach or medical assessment.

## Race-day execution card

- Dual cut-off: each aid station has an entry and an exit time; late at entry you cannot enter, and leaving early before the exit time is not allowed. After exiting, a runner may return before the entry cut-off, using the designated channel and telling staff.
- No sleeping: sleeping on the course is prohibited; which indoor areas allow rest is not stated.
- Poles: banned for the whole course before F5; poles must be dropped in bags beforehand, and twigs may not substitute. Once poles are collected at F5 they must be carried to the finish; pole-free zones remain inside the national park after F5.
- Withdrawal: F7 Nijūkurumazaka is in principle not a withdrawal point, and supporters cannot drive in; after passing a cut-off point, no return or backtracking is allowed; time waiting at the railway crossing counts toward race time.
- Kit: total kit weight must exceed 2 kg at the start and leaving each aid station, and never drop below 1 kg on any segment; two lamps of at least 80 lumens and 4 hours' battery life.
