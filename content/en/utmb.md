## What this course is

UTMB leaves Chamonix, circles the Mont Blanc massif and returns to Chamonix, crossing France, Italy and Switzerland. Starting and finishing in the same town does not make it a road race with a big entry field: the border crossings, valleys and passes in between make it a high-mountain ultra.

The organiser itself uses more than one distance figure. The page introduction says "approximately 170 km with about 10,000 m of positive gain"; the stat block says 174 km and 9,700 m; the official timing table totals 176.865 km with +10,000 m and −9,999 m. All three come from the organiser, so this is not a conversion error made by this site. Before quoting any UTMB figure, check which basis it uses.

Segment notes, all taken from the official timing table:

- **Segment 1** (0 → 22.89 km) reaches Les Houches (9.14 km, 1,009 m) and then Saint-Gervais (22.89 km, 809 m). It has the largest descent of the whole course, so going out too fast here costs more than it looks.
- **Segment 2** (22.89 → 52.29 km) climbs Col du Bonhomme (44.97 km, 2,329 m) and the Refuge de la Croix du Bonhomme (47.03 km, 2,448 m), then drops to Les Chapieux. Its 1,878 m of gain is the most concentrated climbing on the course.
- **Segment 3** (52.29 → 83.29 km) enters Italy: Col de la Seigne (63.08 km, 2,511 m), the course high point at Col des Pyramides Calcaires (65.77 km, 2,565 m), Lac Combal (69.50 km, 1,973 m) and the Arête du Mont Favre (73.90 km, 2,412 m) before the descent to Courmayeur. Its 2,172 m of descent is the hardest section for the legs.
- **Segments 4–5** (83.29 → 130.51 km) pass Refuge Bertone and Refuge Bonatti to Arnouvaz, cross the Grand Col Ferret (106.29 km, 2,527 m) from Italy into Switzerland, then continue via La Fouly to Champex-Lac. Both are net climbs and stay between roughly 1,700 and 2,500 m for long stretches.
- **Segment 6** (130.51 → 158.47 km) runs via La Giète, Trient, Les Tseppes and Vallorcine, with 1,635 m of gain and 1,852 m of loss — the last big rolling section.
- **Segments 7–8** (158.47 → 176.87 km) cross Col des Montets and Tête de Béchar to La Flégère, then drop almost continuously for the final 6.98 km (−849 m) into Chamonix.

What matters beyond the table:

- **Aid stations are for eating on site.** The official rules state that drinks and food at aid stations are for consumption there, and that the organisation provides only still water and/or sports drink for refilling bottles and hydration packs. Leave each station with enough fluid and food to reach the next one.
- **Medical does not mean a bus.** Lac Combal, Checrouit, Refuge Bertone and La Flégère have medical cover or drinks but no bus flag in the official table. Real escape points are the rows marked `bus`.
- **Only three drop-bag points**: Courmayeur, Champex-Lac and the Chamonix finish.
- **Only four hot-food points**: Les Chapieux, Courmayeur, Champex-Lac and the finish.
- These cut-offs are from the 2026 official timing table. A future edition must be re-checked against the organiser's current page.

## Risks and escape points

- **Night**: the 19:45 start and the official 45 h 30 min maximum mean two nights on the course for most runners. The timing table puts the Chamonix finish cut-off at Sunday 16:30, which is 45 minutes earlier than "start plus 45:30". Both figures are official; check the current rules and technical material for the edition you enter. Either way, a head torch and spare batteries belong on the mandatory-equipment check.
- **Altitude**: the timing table's high point is 2,565 m (Col des Pyramides Calcaires) and the GPX reaches 2,571 m; the low point is 805 m. That is a span of roughly 1,770 m, and the high sections feel nothing like the valley.
- **Weather**: the official rules require runners to handle night, wind, cold, fog, rain and snow at altitude without outside assistance, and state that the organisation's role is not to solve those conditions for the runner.
- **Semi-autonomy**: all mandatory equipment must be carried for the whole race; marshals may check packs anywhere, and non-compliance can lead to disqualification.
- **Escapes**: ten service points carry a bus flag: Les Houches, Saint-Gervais, Les Contamines, Les Chapieux, Courmayeur, Arnouvaz, La Fouly, Champex-Lac, Trient and Vallorcine. La Flégère has medical cover but no bus flag; if you plan to leave the race there, confirm lift and road access with the organiser beforehand rather than assuming it.
- **Language and rules**: the course crosses three countries and the French version of the rules prevails.

## Getting there

Transport facts published by the organiser:

- **Geneva airport (GVA)**: about 1 h 30 from Chamonix, with direct coach transfers or a train from Geneva Cornavin.
- **Lyon Saint-Exupéry (LYS)**: about 2 h 30, via TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde and then train or coach.
- **Milan Malpensa (MXP)**: direct coaches with BlaBlaCar or FlixBus.
- **Zurich airport (ZRH)**: by train through Switzerland.
- **Paris CDG/ORY**: direct TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde, then train or coach.
- **Rail**: Saint-Gervais-les-Bains-Le Fayet connects to the Mont-Blanc Express; Annecy connects to Saint-Gervais or a direct shuttle; Lyon Part-Dieu has FlixBus/BlaBlaCar Bus links.
- **In the valley**: the Chamonix Mobilité network runs from Servoz to Vallorcine.
- **Driving**: the organiser explicitly discourages arriving by car because town-centre car parks are saturated during the event. Leave the car at your accommodation and use valley public transport.

## Late-August season and weather

The 2026 edition started on 28 August at 19:45. To prepare for the same window, this page queried Open-Meteo's historical archive for 28–30 August in each year from 2015 to 2025 at Chamonix valley coordinates (45.9237 N / 6.8694 E) — 33 days in total:

- Daily maximum: 11.8 °C to 27.4 °C, median 23 °C.
- Daily minimum: 5.2 °C to 16.7 °C, median 13 °C.
- 15 of 33 days had at least 1 mm of precipitation; 10 of 33 had at least 5 mm.

These are **modelled values for a valley grid point, not measurements at the passes**. Above 2,500 m it is markedly colder, and wind, fog and precipitation change the felt temperature further; valley temperatures must not drive high-altitude gear decisions. The combination of night and altitude is the real weather risk on this course.

## Difficulty

The organiser publishes no single "difficulty grade", and this page does not invent an official one. On verifiable dimensions this is a high-mountain ultra:

- 174–176.9 km, depending on the official basis.
- 9,700–10,253 m of cumulative gain.
- High point 2,565–2,571 m, low point 805 m.
- Official maximum time 45 h 30 min.
- Semi-autonomy, two nights, three countries.

From those conditions it follows directly that fuelling, mandatory equipment and night movement demand more than a typical 100 km race. That conclusion comes from the official figures and rules, not from personal experience.
