## What this course is

UTMB leaves Chamonix, circles the Mont Blanc massif and returns to Chamonix, crossing France, Italy and Switzerland. The start and finish are both in Chamonix; the organiser's introduction describes the course as circling Mont Blanc and crossing Italy, Switzerland and France.

**Series boundary**: this page covers the UTMB race only, the 100M final. In the same week, CCC is the 100K final and OCC is the 50K final, each ranked independently; TDS is a World Series race (4 stones), not a final; PTL is a team adventure and does not count toward the series. Their distance, climb, services and cut-offs cannot be transferred to one another, and this page does not fold CCC or OCC figures into UTMB. The CCC course is documented on the [CCC page](/en/routes/ccc/).

The organiser itself uses more than one distance figure. The page introduction says "approximately 170 km with about 10,000 m of positive gain"; the stat block says 174 km and 9,700 m; the timing table now embedded in the page totals 173.6 km with +9,700 / −9,699 m. A 32-point official timing table this site captured earlier totalled 176.865 km and contained 14 mid-course aid stations, but it was no longer displayed when rechecked on 2026-11-08 and is kept below only as a historical basis. These figures come from the race page's stat block and introduction, the official timing table and the official GPX; the 173.83 km and +10,253 m are this site's parse of the official GPX. Before quoting any UTMB figure, check which basis it uses.

What follows directly from the table:

- **The longest segment is the start to Saint-Gervais**: 22.9 km with +1,040 / −1,267 m, also the biggest single descent on the course. Going out too fast here costs more than it looks.
- **The largest single climb is Les Chapieux → Lac Combal**: +1,053 m over 15.6 km; the highest climb density is Courmayeur → Refuge Bertone, +813 m over 5.3 km. Checrouit → Courmayeur is +20 / −303 m over 4.9 km (cumulative descent rises from 482 m to 785 m, and the difference is 303 m).
- **Two mid-course points have no cut-off**: Checrouit–Maison Vieille and Refuge Bertone, where the official cell is empty. No cut-off does not mean you can linger; work backwards from the next cut-off.
- **The official `SLOWEST` column is later than the cut-off at Arnouvaz and La Fouly** (19:36 vs 19:15 and 23:54 vs 23:45). The two columns are not the same basis; this page quotes only the cut-off column.
- The current 17-row table lists timing points only and gives no point-by-point kilometres for Col du Bonhomme, Grand Col Ferret and similar.

## Risks and escape points

- **Night**: the 19:45 start and the official 45 h 30 min maximum mean the race spans two nights. The current timing table puts the Chamonix finish cut-off at Sunday 17:30, 15 minutes later than "start plus 45:30". Both figures are official; check the current rules and technical material for the edition you enter. A head torch and spare batteries belong on the mandatory-equipment check.
- **Altitude**: the official GPX ranges from 805 m to 2,571 m; the current 17-row table only reaches 1,979 m at Refuge Bertone, and it does not list the mid-course passes. That is a span of roughly 1,770 m.
- **Weather**: the official rules require runners to handle night, wind, cold, fog, rain and snow at altitude without outside assistance.
- **Semi-autonomy**: all mandatory equipment must be carried for the whole race; marshals may check packs at any time, and non-compliance can lead to disqualification.
- **Escapes**: the 10 mid-course points with a bus icon are Saint-Gervais, Les Contamines, Les Chapieux, Lac Combal, Courmayeur, Arnouvaz, La Fouly, Champex-Lac, Trient and Vallorcine. Checrouit, Refuge Bertone and La Flégère carry no bus icon; La Flégère has medical cover and drinks, but if you plan to leave the race there, confirm lift and road access with the organiser beforehand rather than assuming it.
- **Language and rules**: the course crosses three countries and the French version of the rules prevails.

## Getting there

Transport facts published by the organiser:

- **Geneva airport (GVA)**: about 1 h 30 from Chamonix, with direct coach transfers or a train from Geneva Cornavin.
- **Lyon Saint-Exupéry (LYS)**: about 2 h 30, via TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde and then train or coach.
- **Milan Malpensa (MXP)**: direct coaches with BlaBlaCar or FlixBus.
- **Zurich airport (ZRH)**: by train through Switzerland.
- **Paris CDG/ORY**: direct TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde, then train or coach.
- **Rail**: Saint-Gervais-les-Bains-Le Fayet connects to the Mont-Blanc Express; Annecy connects to Saint-Gervais or a direct shuttle; Lyon Part-Dieu has FlixBus/BlaBlaCar Bus links.
- **In the valley**: the Chamonix Mobilité network runs from Servoz to Vallorcine.
- **Driving**: the organiser explicitly discourages arriving by car because town-centre car parks are saturated during the event.

## Late-August season and weather

The 2026 edition started on 28 August at 19:45. To prepare for the same window, this page queried Open-Meteo's historical archive for 28–30 August in each year from 2015 to 2025 at Chamonix valley coordinates (45.9237 N / 6.8694 E) — 33 days in total:

- Daily maximum: 11.8 °C to 27.4 °C, median 23 °C.
- Daily minimum: 5.2 °C to 16.7 °C, median 13 °C.
- 15 of 33 days had at least 1 mm of precipitation; 10 of 33 had at least 5 mm.

These are **modelled values for a valley grid point, not measurements at the passes**. These values must not be treated as pass temperatures or used to drive high-altitude gear decisions.

## Difficulty

The official documents publish no official difficulty grade. **This site's rating**: a high-mountain ultra, on these verifiable dimensions:

- 173.6–174 km on the current official bases (the GPX parses to 173.83 km; the introduction says about 170 km).
- 9,700–10,253 m of cumulative gain.
- High point 2,571 m (official GPX), low point 805 m; the timing-point table only reaches 1,979 m.
- Official maximum time 45 h 30 min.
- Semi-autonomy, two nights, three countries.

The distance, gain, cut-off and semi-autonomy rules above all come from the organiser.
