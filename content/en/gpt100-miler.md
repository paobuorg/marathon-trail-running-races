## What this race is

The GPT100 Miler is the full-length race on the Grampians Peaks Trail, a long-distance walking trail built through Gariwerd/Grampians National Park in Victoria, Australia. It starts at the northern trailhead, Mount Zero Picnic Area, follows the entire trail south, and finishes at the southern terminus in Dunkeld. It is not a loop or an out-and-back but a complete point-to-point traverse, listed by the organiser at 163 km.

The organiser's 2026 core figures are below. Gain and loss are labelled approximately (7700m+-), not measured.

The event also offers four alternative formats: a four-person Miler Team Relay, a four-day Stage Race, a three-person Stage Race Team Relay, and shorter 50K, 33K, 14K, 6K and Dunkeld 2K/5K races. The Stage Race splits follow the Miler's four major sections: Mount Zero to Halls Gap, Halls Gap to Mount William, Mount William to Griffin Fireline, and Griffin Fireline to Dunkeld. The shorter races are not covered here.

The GPT100 Miler joined the World Trail Majors in 2025 and remains one of the ten races in the 2026 series. It carries four qualification labels: Hardrock 100 qualifier, Western States 100 qualifier, AUTRA Australian 100 Mile Championships 2026, and UTMB World Series Qualifier Event. The last means only that a finish earns UTMB Index points; it does not make the race a UTMB World Series event or a Major.

The qualification years are stated precisely: finishers of the 2026 Miler can enter the Hardrock lottery for 2027 and 2028; 2026 finishers can enter the 2027 Western States lottery, and 2027 finishers the 2028 lottery. Pair finishers receive the same Hardrock and Western States qualification as solo finishers.

The official results page lists two course records under GPT100 Miler: Caleb Olson (USA) 21:19:39 and Man Yee Cheung (Hong Kong) 27:58:45. The page has no gender column, so this page does not assign one. ## The course

The organiser's course description gives these points in order:

- From Mount Zero Picnic Area, the race climbs the Flat Rock slab below Taipan Wall, then alternates between thin, rocky, uneven ground and smooth dirt to GAR Trailhead (aid station 1, 15.5 km).

![The orange-red sandstone wall of Taipan Wall, with horizontal ledges, dark water streaks, trees at its base and a blue sky; this is the rock wall passed on the first leg, not the start arch or a race scene](/media/routes/gpt100-miler/gpt100-taipan-wall-2016.jpg)

*Figure 1 | Taipan Wall (22 April 2016, Ed Dunens, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/)). It only describes the rock-wall setting of the first leg; the photograph has no start arch, course marking, aid station, timing mat, runner or race-day crowd.*
- A run of waterfalls leads into a long, ever-changing climb towards GAR/Mt Difficult. Much of it is exposed, with 360-degree views; runners do not summit GAR but turn onto a very technical, choppy descent.
- More climbing follows, switching between runnable smooth trail and very rocky ground, to Mt Difficult Rd (station 2, 35.5 km).
- The section to Halls Gap (station 3, 49.5 km) is relatively faster, but begins with a grind to Wartook Lookout, then a long descent. After Chatauqua Peak the route passes the arboretum and caravan park into central Halls Gap.
- Out of Halls Gap, the Wonderland Trail leads to the Grand Canyon Trail and the Pinnacles, then along technical rock to Rosea Carpark (station 4, 59.5 km). The climb over Mount Rosea, a cliff-edge traverse and a scrambling descent give way to an 8 km flowing descent to Borough Huts (station 5, 72 km).
- After crossing Grampians Road, a long slab climb reaches the Seven Dials, followed by a rocky traverse, a descent into the valley below Redman's Bluff and another climb over it. This can be very hot, with sun-heated rock and long gaps between stations. The route then nears the road and climbs steeply to Mount William Road / Mt William Carpark (station 6, 86.6 km).
- The only paved section follows: the road to the Mount William summit. From the top, the trail descends unevenly to Boundary Gap, the saddle between Mount William and Durd Durd.
- The steepest climb of the whole trail, stepped all the way, leads to Durd Durd on the Major Mitchell Plateau and the race's high point at 1,167 m. A very long descent follows over Banksia Hill, down newly cut rock steps, onto Stockyard Track and 8 km of gradual downhill to Jimmy Creek Camp (station 7, 106.7 km).
- A 1 km backtrack rejoins the GPT; after Jimmy Creek Rd a valley climb leads to rocky ground, a slow boulder field and a goat-track traverse to Yarram Campground, then a smooth descent to Yarram Gap Road (station 8, 118.5 km).
- To Griffin Fireline (station 9, 130.7 km) and Cassidys Gap (station 10, 142 km) the route rolls over newly made trail, past the open fields around Wannon Camp and boardwalks across flood plains.
- An extended climb leaves Griffin, then a rolling trail reaches Cassidys Gap. The final test is Signal Peak and Mt Abrupt (Mud-Dadjug), mostly stepped climbs that are hard on tired legs.
- The descent from Mud-Dadjug is hard and choppy, often on slabs, to Bainggug Carpark (station 11, 152.8 km). Two smaller climbs remain: the Bainggug rise and the final ascent of Mount Sturgeon/Wurgarri. From there, 6 km of descent through open grass and along the river lead into central Dunkeld and the finish in the gardens at the southern terminus.

Halls Gap, midway through the course, is both the event village and aid station 3 at 49.5 km; after leaving town the route climbs the Wonderland Trail to the Pinnacles and continues on technical rock to Rosea Carpark. ## Start, finish, travel and logistics

- Start: Mount Zero Picnic Area at the northern end of the Grampians. The pages read give the place name only, with no coordinates or parking rules.
- Finish: Dunkeld, at the southern terminus of the GPT in the town gardens; the organiser gives about 46 minutes' drive from Halls Gap.
- Event village and headquarters: Halls Gap, mid-course, about three hours and 260 km north-west of Melbourne. Stage Race runners return to Halls Gap each night; Miler runners do not.
- Crew access: stations 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11 and the finish. Mt Difficult Rd (2) and Yarram Gap Rd (8) are closed to crew. Stations 9, 10 and 11 are reached only by the Sterling Place, Dunkeld crew shuttle.
- Drop bags: Halls Gap (3), Mt William (6), Jimmy Creek (7), Griffin Fireline (9) and the Dunkeld finish.
- Nap beds: Borough Huts (5), Mt William (6), Jimmy Creek (7), Griffin Fireline (9) and Cassidys Gap (10), in limited numbers.
- Getting there: Halls Gap is the event village and shuttle hub. Parks Victoria gives about three hours (255 km) from Melbourne and 5.5 hours (500 km) from Adelaide by road; rail runs to Ararat, Horsham and Stawell are stops on The Overland, and the nearest airports are Melbourne Tullamarine and Avalon. The official shuttle point is the bus stop outside Centenary Hall, 115 Grampians Rd. Mt Zero seats must be booked on Race Roster: free for Miler, Stage Race and Team Relay runners, A$35 for GPT50k runners and spectators. Return shuttles from Mt William, Griffin Fireline and Dunkeld to Halls Gap do not need booking; Stage 2 starts in Halls Gap and needs no shuttle.
- The organiser also publishes separate Shuttles, Accommodation, Crew, Pacers and Mandatory Gear pages. Departure times are governed by the event schedule; this page does not treat 2026 times as 2027 times.

## Season and weather

Open-Meteo's Halls Gap village record for 6–8 November, 2015–2025 (33 days): daily maximum 12.1–28.9 °C, median 19.7 °C; daily minimum 5.0–14.0 °C, median 9.2 °C; 10 of 33 days had at least 1 mm precipitation, 3 had at least 5 mm.

This is the Halls Gap valley grid, not Mt William (Durd Durd at 1,167 m is nearby), Jimmy Creek or Dunkeld. November in the Grampians can be hot by day, drop to about 5 °C at night, or turn continuously wet and slippery; 99% of the course is single track, so wet rock and roots raise the technical load. The organiser publishes no race-period mountain measurements, and this page does not treat valley data as summit data.

## Difficulty, withdrawal and risk

- **Difficulty:** 163 km, about +7,700 / −7,700 m, 50 hours, 99% single hiking trail (WTM says 98%). The difficulty is not one climb but the boulders of Mt Difficult, the exposed traverse on Rosea, the Durd Durd steps, the boulder field after Jimmy Creek, and the final Signal Peak / Mt Abrupt climbs on tired legs. No separate official technical grade is published; this page describes the course as long, broken, exposed and repeatedly undulating rather than inventing a grade.
- **Crew/bail-out points:** GAR Trailhead (#1), Halls Gap (#3), Rosea Carpark (#4), Borough Huts (#5), Mt William Carpark (#6), Jimmy Creek (#7), Griffin Fireline (#9), Cassidys Gap (#10), Bainggug (#11) and Dunkeld finish; Mt Difficult Rd (#2) and Yarram Gap Rd (#8) do not allow crew.
- **Risk:** rock slabs around Redman's Bluff can be sun-hot, Yarram Gap is an emergency station, Jimmy Creek–Yarram Gap gives 4 hours for 11.8 km, and the Durd Durd plateau and boulder field are crossed at night. No per-station medical cover or sweep times are published, so they remain unknown.
- **Withdrawal:** report to the aid-station captain and hand in chip/bib. #2 and #8 have no crew access, so self-evacuation is awkward; ask about the official shuttle first. The 2026 sweep details are not published, and this page does not treat the 2025 guide as 2026 rules.

## Training conversion: 100-mile mountain preparation

The challenge of GPT100 is 163 km, about 7,700 m of repeated up-and-down, 99% single-track and night technical sections. Preparation centres on long mountain endurance, loaded self-sufficiency and stair capacity on tired legs, not road mileage. General methods are in [ultra-trail training plan](/en/knowledge/trail-ultra-training-plan/), [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), [recovery day](/en/knowledge/recovery-day/) and [load and overtraining](/en/knowledge/load-and-overtraining/).

| Phase | Focus | Practical content | Race-day constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-20 to T-14 weeks | Mountain aerobic base | One mountain long run plus easy runs a week; build weekly gain toward 2,000 m | Repeated up-and-down throughout needs a steady aerobic base |
| T-14 to T-8 weeks | Long run and gain accumulation | Long run to 70–100 km; practise stairs and loose-rock sections | Rosea edge traverse, Durd Durd stairs and the late boulder field |
| T-8 to T-3 weeks | Loaded self-sufficiency and night | Test carrying food and water under semi-self-sufficiency; one night technical session | Plateau and boulder field at night; carry supplies between stations |
| T-3 to race | Taper | Shorten distance and gain, keep short rhythm; no new shoes or kit | Recover fatigue before race day; see [taper and strength](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/) |

Boundary: this is a generic preparation framework, not medical advice. Runners with pre-existing cardiopulmonary conditions or recent injuries should consult a doctor first. Whether a runner is ready for a 100-mile event also depends on previous longest finishes, mountain experience and recovery.
