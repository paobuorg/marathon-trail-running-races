## 60-second summary

The Leadville Trail 100 Run (LT100) is an out-and-back 100-mile trail ultra that starts and finishes in Leadville, Colorado, at about 10,200 feet (3,109 m), and crosses Hope Pass twice (official profile high point 12,526 ft / 3,818 m). It suits runners who already have long mountain-race experience, can hold pace at altitude, and accept night-time cold and long self-supported intervals. It does not suit runners without high-altitude experience, without crew and pacer arrangements, or those treating a 100-mile race as a pure speed contest.

Three decisions matter most. First, build training around altitude adaptation and climbing endurance rather than flat mileage. Second, plan segment targets backwards from the cutoffs; the Winfield turnaround and the Twin Lakes round trip are the pacing pivots of the course. Third, sort out crew vehicles, shuttles and drop bags in advance: official support is allowed only at designated stations, and outside support can disqualify the runner.

- The core pressure is high altitude plus a long cutoff plus crew dependence: the start is already above 3,100 m, Hope Pass is near 3,820 m, the overall cutoff is 30 hours, and the big buckle is sub-25 hours.
- The official current-page 2026 reroute figure is 99.7 miles and 13,357 feet of gain; the archived traditional-course profile is 99.7 miles and 15,492 feet of gain. The two belong to different course versions and are not averaged.
- The 2027 course was marked pending by the event. Lottery entry runs 1–15 December 2026, results are announced 6 January 2027, the entry fee is $495, and entries are non-transferable and non-refundable.

## History and origins

The official history section records that the race was first held in 1983, created by local miner Ken Chlouber after the closure of the Climax Mine, as a way to draw attention back to the town of Leadville. Forty-five runners started the first edition. The 2023 race celebrated the 40th anniversary. The official history section does not publish a year-by-year results table, and this pass did not verify an official statement on the cancelled 2020 edition; the claim that 2020 was cancelled due to COVID-19 comes from a third-party compilation and is not written into this page's conclusions.

The slogan "Race Across the Sky" matches its high-altitude identity: the start is already above 3,000 m and the pass near 3,820 m, with terrain mixing above-timberline exposure and forested dirt trails. Keep the event edition separate from the course version: edition history comes from the event's historical narrative, while mileage, ascent and stations come from the course page of the relevant year. The 2026 race used a Willow Fire reroute, and the 2027 course was marked pending at verification time; earlier traditional-course mileage, cutoffs and stations must not be carried over to the next edition without checking the new bulletin.

## Entry and qualification

**Age and lottery.** Athletes must be 18 or older on race day. The 2027 lottery window is 1–15 December 2026, with results announced 6 January 2027. Entering the lottery is free, but card details are collected; if selected, the registration fee, processing fee and add-ons are charged automatically, and a donation does not improve selection odds. Only one application per person is allowed; athletes who already hold a 2027 entry may not enter the 2027 lottery. The team registration option was removed in 2025; all participants enter as individuals. If a selected athlete's card is declined, payment must be updated within one week or the spot is forfeited. The entry fee is $495; all registration fees are non-refundable and non-transferable, subject to the Leadville Race Series deferral policies.

**Qualifier route.** The official run qualifiers are the Leadville Trail Marathon, Silver Rush 50 Run and Austin Rattler Run. Each offers a minimum of 35 LT100 qualifier coins; coins go only to athletes who finish the longest distance, and athletes must be 18 on LT100 race day. Coin winners must be present at the awards ceremony and claim their entry immediately, then complete and pay for LT100 registration during the designated window. Coins are first distributed to the top overall men's and women's 1st–3rd (overall winners are removed from age groups) and to the top podium of each remaining category; unaccepted coins roll down within the category, and remaining coins are drawn by lottery among official finishers who completed the qualifier within its cutoff.

**Other entry routes.** The official page also lists Run Camp + Entry packages, Boundless Coaching + Entry packages, charity slots, volunteer lottery preference, and Life Time Club-Access member priority entry (member guaranteed spots open 10:00 a.m. MST on 24 November 2026; members not selected are entered into the general lottery twice). The 2027 camp runs 17–19 June 2027; Camp + Entry is $2,200 and Camp Only is $1,000. Camp participation age is 17+, but runners must still be 18 on race day.

**Deferral, refund and transfer.** An entry can be deferred only once and only to the following year's event; the original fee is neither refunded nor transferred, and re-registration requires paying 100% of that year's published entry fee. The LT100 Run 2026 deferral deadline was 11:00 a.m. MST on 21 August 2026 (start of expo); the 2027 deferral registration window is 1–15 November 2026. Deployed or recalled active-duty military, and pregnant or postpartum participants, may qualify for a refund or complimentary deferral with orders or an authorized medical note; an approved deferral transfers the original paid fee in full to the following year. No refunds or changes are permitted for transactions older than 365 days; add-ons, merchandise and donations are nonrefundable and final. If Life Time cancels an event, registered athletes may defer to the following year at no extra entry fee, or take a partial refund: 70% at 90 or more days out, 50% at 60–89 days, 40% at 30–59 days; service fees, merchandise and donations are excluded.

## Course and geometry

**Course shape.** LT100 is an out-and-back line: out of Leadville around the Turquoise Lake area, through Outward Bound, Half Pipe and Twin Lakes Village, up Hope Pass, down to the Winfield turnaround, then back over Hope Pass, through Twin Lakes, Half Pipe, Outward Bound and Turquoise Lake Dam, and back to the town start/finish. The official course description groups the route into early dirt roads out of town, high-altitude trails around the Sugarloaf area, and technical terrain on the Colorado Trail.

**Two official number sets must stay separate.** The current-page course overview states "over 15,000 feet" of elevation gain; the 2026 Willow Fire reroute section gives 99.7 miles and 13,357 feet; the archived traditional-course profile gives 99.7 miles, 15,492 feet of gain, a 9,206-foot low point and a 12,526-foot high point. The 13,357 and 15,492 figures belong to the reroute and the traditional course respectively; they are not rounding of the same line, and this page does not merge them into one "exact" ascent.

**2026 Willow Fire reroute.** The official explanation: working with local agencies, a reroute was created to avoid Willow Fire impact areas while keeping the race experience as close to the traditional route as possible. Runners leave Harrison Ave, loop Turquoise Lake as usual, return along the east side of the lake, and rejoin the traditional course into the Outward Bound aid station. Areas not featured on the 2026 route are Hagerman Pass Road, Sugarloaf Mountain and Powerline. The 2027 course was marked "Stay tuned for the 2027 course" on the event page at verification time.

**Geometry source.** The event page links Ride with GPS event/route entries and a Garmin Connect course for navigation; the page read this pass did not publish a directly downloadable raw GPX file URL. Geometry access is recorded as `official-link`: this site links only the official third-party track entries, and does not host, mirror or redraw the line, nor create its own KML or GeoJSON. No track redistribution licence was located on the page. Treating a third-party route centre point as a precise start or finish position is invalid; the map anchor is an editorial overview coordinate only.

## Common misreadings

- **"100 miles means exactly 160.93 km."** Official course info gives 99.7 miles; 100 miles is the historical name and brand figure. The card distance converts 99.7 miles to 160,452 m, not 160,934 m.
- **"13,357 ft and 15,492 ft are rounding of the same line."** They belong to the 2026 reroute and the archived traditional profile respectively; this page keeps both and averages neither.
- **"A few extra days at altitude fixes acclimatisation."** Acclimatisation reduces risk but does not remove altitude illness. The start is 3,109 m and the pass 3,818 m; symptoms still require on-site medical assessment, and IV use is prohibited by the rules.
- **"Crew can hand water to the runner anywhere along the trail."** Crew is allowed only at designated stations; outside support can disqualify the runner. Twin Lakes is shuttle-only, and Winfield admits no crew, spectators or pacers.
- **"One light is enough."** The official packing list includes a spare light on the start-line list, and the Winfield drop bag should carry lights packed for the worst-case finish time; the night pass and return cannot rely on someone else's lamp.
- **"The cutoff table never changes."** The current page marks cutoffs subject to change; after the 2026 reroute the stations and mileage already differ from the traditional line, and the 2027 course is pending. Check that edition's bulletin.

## Aid, logistics and crew/pacer support

**Aid stations and offerings.** The current page lists 12 aid stations (Carter Summit is a limited hydration/nutrition mini aid). Stations provide water, Neversecond gels and high-carb sports drink, Skratch Labs hydration mix, bars and chews, The Pickle Juice Company products, plus mixed foods such as bananas, figs, pretzels, chips and cola-type drinks. The archived traditional course had 11 stations. The event does not publish a per-station hot-food list or queue times; this page does not estimate them.

**Crew access (current page basis).** There are three official crew locations, each hit twice: Outward Bound (26.0 outbound / 78.3 inbound), Pipeline Alternate (29.6 outbound / 74.9 inbound), and Twin Lakes Village (40.3 outbound / 64.0 inbound). Accepting support outside these official locations is a violation and may disqualify the runner. Twin Lakes Village has no vehicle access; all crew must ride the shuttle from Outward Bound, and sites issue coloured wristbands (children under 10 are exempt). Pipeline is a crew point only, not an aid station; the next aid is Half Pipe two miles ahead. Crew rules: follow race rules and Lake County law; obey officials, staff, volunteers and law enforcement; stay within the marked crew area; do not litter. Crew may support any registered athlete, not only the one they follow.

**Archived traditional crew layout (comparison basis).** The archived page lists four crew points: Outward Bound, Pipeline, Twin Lakes Village and Mayqueen. Mayqueen is inbound-only, opens at 5:00 p.m., allows 4 crew plus 1 pacer, and traffic moves clockwise around the lake from the south/Turquoise Lake dam side. Twin Lakes is shuttle-only as well.

**Winfield support limits.** Official 2021 updates and later guides state that no crew, spectators or pacers are allowed at Winfield; the station is staffed by LRS staff, medical personnel and volunteers, and supplies are ample. Runners who drop out or miss the cutoff at Winfield are shuttled back to Twin Lakes Village; there is no direct shuttle to Leadville. Cell service returns once past Granite on the ride back, after which crew can be contacted at Twin Lakes.

**Pacer rules.** Pacers may join from Twin Lakes Village inbound; the pacer rules section writes mile 61.8 and the transport note writes mile 64, and both mileage formulations are kept side by side. A runner may use as many pacers as wished, but only one pacer at a time until mile 99. Each runner receives 2 pacer bibs at packet pickup; to add pacers, hand off the bib to the next pacer, or pick up extra bibs at Twin Lakes, Half Pipe, Outward Bound or Turquoise Lake Dam (the archived page also lists Mayqueen); Pipeline does not issue bibs. Every pacer must sign a waiver before entering the course and wear the pacer bib at all times. Pacers may carry food and equipment for the runner like a burro; they may not push, pull, carry or tow the runner; they may not pace by vehicle or bicycle; they may not take aid outside official locations; special cases such as visually impaired runners require prior approval by race officials. Half Pipe has no vehicle access and requires a hike or bike in.

**Drop bag rules.** Five bag locations: the current page lists Turquoise Dam, Outward Bound, Half Pipe, Twin Lakes and Winfield; the archived traditional list lists Mayqueen, Outward Bound, Half Pipe, Twin Lakes and Winfield. Except for Winfield each station is passed twice, and the event suggests one bag outbound and one inbound. Bags must be clear plastic, labelled in large letters with bib number, name and station (the archived checklist also asks inbound/outbound), and liquids must be leak-proof in non-breakable containers. Drop-off is Friday 11:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. at 316 Harrison Ave (the LRS store); return is Sunday about 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. at the same location; bags unclaimed after 5:00 p.m. Sunday are donated to local charities and are not mailed. There is no drop bag at Hope Pass.

**Packet pickup, expo and traffic.** The mandatory athlete meeting is Friday 10:00 a.m. at the Lake County High School Football Field. Packet pickup is Friday 11:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. at 135 E 6th Street, with a government-issued photo ID, in person only; no proxy pickup and no mailing. The start/finish is the corner of 6th St and Harrison Ave; the start opens 3:00–3:45 a.m. and the race starts at 4:00 a.m. Runner vehicles must park at the Rodeo Grounds/Community Fields, Lake County Intermediate School or Lake County High School, then shuttle or walk to the start; race-morning shuttles run 3:00–4:30 a.m. and post-race shuttles Saturday 10:00 p.m.–Sunday 10:30 a.m. The expo is downtown Leadville and open to the public free of charge. Lodging and local information link through Visit Leadville; parking and congestion in town are a repeatedly stated community constraint, so the shuttle system should be the first choice.

## Gear and rules

**Mandatory and inspection basis.** The official rules require a photo ID at sign-in; athletes must provide a working emergency contact number that is not their own, and international participants a working WhatsApp or equivalent international number. The course remains open to the public; runners must watch for conditions and follow directions from officials, medical staff, law enforcement or land managers. The official rules prohibit IV fluid use during the race; IV use results in immediate disqualification. The official course must be followed at all times; shortcuts or alternate routes are not permitted. Runners are responsible for safe navigation; the course is marked but a GPS device is recommended as backup, and GPS files are available on each event page. Headphone policy: no earbuds or over-the-ear headphones; bone-conduction headphones are allowed.

**Official packing list (recommended gear, not the mandatory inspection list).** Start-line list: race kit (shorts, top, sports bra/underwear, socks, shoes), beanie or hat, gloves, jacket, race number, sunscreen and lube (applied before start), watch or other device, bottles or race vest, gels or easily digestible carbs for the first 13 miles, electrolyte capsules, pace/split times, LED headlamp and/or flashlight, spare light, buff/bandana, toilet paper or baby wipes, essential medication/inhaler, and ID and emergency contact info. Pacer list: LED headlamp/flashlight, handheld bottles or race vest, snacks, split times, jacket, hat, gloves, basic medical kit, phone, space blanket.

**Drop bag additions.** The Twin Lakes outbound bag should add dry socks and running kit, a hooded windbreaker, polypro or wool socks, hat, gloves and a special treat to support the Hope Pass climb. The Winfield turnaround bag should add a headlamp/flashlight packed for the worst-case finish time, a spare light and dry shoes. Basic drop-bag contents: upset-stomach kit (wipes, anti-diarrhoea, antacid), electrolyte capsules, backup food, special treats, dry socks and running kit, safety pins, anti-chafing cream, sunscreen, insect repellent, blister kit, bagged toilet paper, essential medication and a bag for wet items. The list stresses: pack lights for your worst-case finish time, not your best-case one.

**Penalties and protests.** The race director and officials may disqualify participants, crew or spectators for rule violations or misconduct. Protests must be submitted to the event director no later than 30 minutes before the awards ceremony; final decisions are made by the event director and officials panel. Carelessly lost litter, clothing or race gear on the course violates Leave No Trace; deliberate littering is a disqualification reason. Use only official porta-potties on the course; if stopping in the field, dig a 6-inch pit 200 feet from water and carry WAG bags for toilet paper and hygiene waste.

## Weather and environment

**Official weather description.** The official weather section states that August Leadville ranges from daytime highs in the upper 70s F to night-time lows in the upper 20s F; mornings are sunny and afternoons bring showers. Rain, hail and even snow are possible during the race. Lightning is hazardous above timberline; falling temperature, wind and precipitation combined can trigger hypothermia quickly, and hypothermia can cause confusion and be deadly. The event states plainly: whatever the weather, do not attempt the Hope Pass round trip without adequate warm and protective clothing, because the summit is over 12,000 feet.

**Altitude and medical boundary.** The start is already 3,109 m and Hope Pass near 3,820 m; most of the race stays above 2,800 m. This page does not diagnose or give medication plans. The boundary for altitude symptoms (headache, nausea, poor sleep, degraded performance, unsteady gait, altered awareness) is: take the nearest aid station for medical assessment, and let on-site medical staff decide whether to continue; do not push through on the pass or self-manage fluids and oxygen. The rules make IV use during the race a disqualification; medical transport and search-and-rescue costs are the runner's responsibility (historical guide basis; follow that edition's manual). **Other environmental risks.** Above-timberline exposure means lightning shelter decisions follow official guidance; August afternoon convection builds fast, so the pass crossing in the afternoon needs weather margin. The course crosses public lands and the San Isabel National Forest; follow fire restrictions (the current page notes Stage 2 restrictions: open flames banned county-wide and on the course; fully closed propane/liquid/gas stoves excepted), do not disturb wildlife or vegetation, and store food and waste properly in bear country.

## Training transfer

1. **Altitude adaptation ahead of mileage.** The start is 3,109 m and the pass near 3,820 m; flat long runs do not translate directly into altitude endurance. Schedule staged high-altitude blocks, monitor sleep, resting heart rate and session feel, and know acute altitude signals; the framework is in [Altitude and running](/en/knowledge/altitude-and-running/).
2. **Climbing endurance and uphill/downhill technique are the core.** Two Hope Pass crossings make climbing capacity and downhilling protection matter more than flat pace. Training should include repeated steep climbs, downhill technique and leg-resilience sessions; see [Uphill and downhill running basics](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/). Compare the course elevation structure with the [route elevation tool](/en/tools/route-elevation/).
3. **Practise night and cold sections separately.** More than half the race may run after dark, when pass and lake temperatures can drop to single-digit Celsius. Train night navigation, backup lights and hypothermia protection; reference [Night running and traffic](/en/knowledge/night-running-and-traffic/) and [Cold-weather running](/en/knowledge/cold-weather-running/), and walk through the [race checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/) before departure.
4. **Reduce reliance on on-site crew.** Support opens only at designated stations, so long-interval fueling, self-care and gear organisation must be trained in practice. The full fueling plan is in [Long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/); carried-gear trade-offs in [Trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/).
5. **Plan pace backwards from cutoffs, not from champion results.** Derive allowed time per station from the cutoff table, and split the race into outbound, turnaround and return. Pacing principles are in [Race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/); segment arithmetic in the [splits tool](/en/tools/splits/).

## Race-day execution card

**Before the race (Friday).** Attend the mandatory athlete meeting; pick up in person and check bib and timing chip; drop the five station bags (clear bags, large labels, leak-proof non-breakable liquids); confirm crew vehicle passes, wristbands and shuttle plan; check headlamp and spare light, jacket, gloves, hat, sunscreen and electrolytes; pack lights in the bags for the worst-case finish time.

**Outbound targets (29.5 h plan reference).** Hold effort early out of town at altitude; do not chase pace before Outward Bound; complete first water and gel there; hold rhythm at Half Pipe; at Twin Lakes rehydrate, change dry socks and add warmth, and top up food for Hope Pass. Hope Pass outbound cutoff 4:45 p.m., target inside 12.75 h; Winfield turnaround cutoff 6:50 p.m., target 13.50 h, about 1.33 h margin.

**Return targets.** After Winfield switch into night rhythm immediately and confirm lights work; second Hope Pass crossing, then Twin Lakes inbound (cutoff 11:00 p.m., target 18.00 h); Half Pipe inbound (cutoff 2:00 a.m., target 21.00 h); Outward Bound inbound (cutoff 3:45 a.m., target 22.80 h); Turquoise Lake Dam inbound (cutoff 5:30 a.m., target 24.60 h); finish planned 29.50 h with 0.5 h buffer.

**Exit points and stop rules.** Persistent vomiting without food intake, unsteady gait, altered awareness, chest pain or obvious palpitations, or shaking that cannot be relieved: take the nearest aid station for medical assessment and do not continue through. Missing a station cutoff means exiting per the rules and being arranged for withdrawal by the station; do not hitch a ride forward to keep scoring. Winfield withdrawal goes only to Twin Lakes; cell service returns past Granite, so the crew pickup plan must be explained in advance.

**Gear change points.** Twin Lakes outbound: change dry socks, add windbreaker/gloves/hat; Winfield: confirm night lights and dry shoes; Twin Lakes inbound: add spare light, gloves, beanie; final stretch: top up sunscreen and lube as needed.
