## What this is: 56 km, 56th edition

The official 2027 Ultra Marathon page states that the race is scheduled for Sunday 4 April 2027 and is the 56th edition of the 56 km route.

The same page calls 2027 the milestone 56th edition and describes the 56 km route as globally iconic.

The race is organised by Two Oceans Marathon NPC and held under World Athletics, Athletics South Africa and Western Province Athletics.

The 2027 start is on Newlands Main Road in Cape Town, with runners leaving in batches from **05:15**. The official page said 06:00 when captured on 8 November 2026 and 05:15 when re-read on 20 November 2026; this page follows the re-read.

The finish is at the UCT Rugby Fields, Upper Campus, Rondebosch, on the University of Cape Town campus.

The 2027 entry limit is 21,000.

This page covers only the 56 km Ultra Marathon and does not apply Half Marathon rules to it.

## From 26 runners in 1970 to today's Newlands–UCT

The official history page describes the first race on 2 May 1970 as a 35-mile training run for Comrades: 26 runners started from Impala Park, 15 finished, the entry fee was 50 cents and Dirkie Steyn won barefoot in 3:55:50.

In 1998 the race moved its start to Main Road, Newlands and its finish to the University of Cape Town rugby fields, the same start-finish structure the Ultra uses today.

In 2000 the cut-off was extended from 6:30 to 7 hours. Chapman's Peak Drive closed after fires and rockfalls that year, and the race used Ou Kaapse Weg for four years while keeping the 56 km distance.

The men's course record is still Thompson Magawana's 3:03:44 from 2 April 1988. The official history page says he also set world bests for 30 miles and 50 km that day, and the record has not been beaten.

This is history, not the 2027 course map. Whether 2027 uses Chapman's Peak or a diversion still depends on the official route map published before the race.

## How it differs from Comrades: no up/down

Two Oceans and Comrades are both South African road ultras, but their direction structure is completely different.

Comrades switches between two cities, and in 2027 it is a Down Run from Pietermaritzburg to Durban whose distance changes with direction.

Two Oceans has one start and one finish: Newlands out, UCT in, with no up-run and down-run variants.

Both ends sit inside Cape Town, and although the straight-line distance is short, the route loops around the Cape Peninsula for 56 km.

This page therefore does not write "X km down, Y km up", and does not carry over Comrades' 12-hour limit, Big Five or personal seconding tables.

## The course: Chapman's Peak and Constantia Nek

The official page summarises the route as 56 km around the Cape Peninsula and names Chapman's Peak.

It lists Muizenberg, St James, Kalk Bay, Hout Bay, Noordhoek, Constantia and Newlands among the areas the route passes.

The official description calls the Noordhoek Main Road section about 4 km of gentle undulation before the first big climb of the day.

Chapman's Peak starts at Noordhoek Village and winds along the mountain edge to Hout Bay, which the organiser calls a bucket-list landmark.

The organiser also notes that this stretch is not accessible by car, so spectators have to walk up.

Hout Bay Main Road becomes a party zone from the foot of Chapman's Peak to the top of Constantia Nek, where runners also pass the marathon mark.

The organiser calls Constantia Nek the second and most taxing climb, with a steep incline.

These are official words, not elevation data.

The official 2027 route map will only be released before the race; the page gives no cumulative ascent, no 2027 elevation profile and no split figures for Chapman's Peak or Constantia Nek. The same official page still links the 2026 56 km route-map PDF, whose foot carries a 3–242 m elevation profile; that is a 2026-edition sheet and cannot be used as 2027 geometry or elevation.

This page therefore leaves cumulative ascent, segment gradients and high-point elevation unknown rather than filling them with third-party numbers. iRunFar's 2026 recap estimates about 700 m of gain over the second-half Chapman's Peak and Constantia Nek climbs; that is a third-party description, kept as reference only and not an official figure.

The 2026 page noted that if Chapman's Peak Drive were unavailable, the Ultra would be diverted over Ou Kaapse Weg; that is a contingency note, not a 2027 course change already in force.

Whether the same diversion applies in 2027 has not been published, so it is not written here as the established route.

## Private support (seconding) rules

This is where Two Oceans differs most from Comrades, and where rules are easiest to break.

Rule 6.13 of the official 2026 rules states that the no mobile seconding rule will be strictly enforced.

The same rule says no seconds may drive, cycle or run alongside any athlete on the route.

It allows stand-and-hand: an athlete's seconds may stand at any refreshment station on the route and hand refreshments to their athlete while they remain stationary.

It adds that no seconds may travel on the race route.

Seconding may not be conducted directly from a vehicle, whether moving or stationary.

The official FAQ says the refreshment stations should be adequate, but supporter points will be allowed as long as supporters do not leave a mess.

The same FAQ states that non-entrants are not permitted to run on the route.

So standing at a fixed point and handing up supplies is allowed, while driving alongside, cycling alongside or running a section with an athlete is not.

The 2027 rules are not published; if the 2027 text changes, the 2027 official rules govern, and this page does not present rule 6.13 as already confirmed for 2027.

## Official refreshments and medical support

The official page says refreshment stations are set at regular intervals and provide water, sports drinks and supplies.

The official FAQ describes the stations as well stocked and suggests runners should not need to rely on private support.

The official page lists medical stations at 12, 19, 28, 33, 39, 42, 46, 49 and 51 km.

Medical stations provide on-site first aid and transfer support, not personal feeding.

The 2027 Ultra page does not publish a full per-station product list, so this page does not invent one.

Exact station positions and products will be added when the 2027 route map and race manual are published.

## Cut-offs: 7 hours and intermediate points

The official FAQ states that the 56 km Ultra finish cut-off is 7 hours after the start gun time of the last batch.

The same page explains that the time-of-day cut-off is determined by the starting time of the last batch plus the 56 km cut-off to the finish.

That means "7 hours" is not one clock time for everyone; it runs from the last batch in each start group.

The 2027 batches start from 05:15, but the exact last-batch start time is not published on the Ultra page, so the absolute finish cut-off clock time is unknown.

For intermediate cut-offs, the official FAQ refers readers to the route map; the 2027 map is not published, so all intermediate cut-offs are unknown.

## Qualification

The official 2027 page states that all ultra entrants must submit a qualifying marathon or longer distance.

The qualifying window runs from 1 May 2026 to 7 March 2027.

A qualifying race must be a standard World Athletics-affiliated marathon or longer, with an officially measured course and official timing.

The official standards are banded by distance.

| Distance | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| 42 km | sub 5:00 |
| 48 km | sub 6:00 |
| 50 km | sub 6:30 |

| 90 km | sub 12:00 |

These are entry standards, not course cut-offs.

The organiser also seeds ultra runners into batches from the submitted qualifying times, accepting gun-to-mat or mat-to-mat official results.

The qualifying time must appear in official results; this page does not count training runs or unofficial timing.

## Getting there and weather

The official Ultra page lists a free race-day shuttle between the UCT finish and designated parking areas.

The start and end collection point is the UCT Upper Campus North Stop, opposite P6.

The official stops include UCT Middle Campus, Mowbray Bus Terminus, Sandown and Campground Road, Groote Schuur Primary School, Groote Schuur High School, Sans Souci Girls School, and Main Road and Dean Street.

Tog bags are handed in at the start just after Dean Street at Great Westerford, two matching bands are issued, and bags are collected at UCT Upper Campus; no bag is released without the matching wristband.

The official FAQ says parking details will be posted on the website and reserved parking tickets will be sold through the entry system and at Race Pack Collection; the 2027 parking areas and capacity are not published, so they are unknown here.

The Ultra page does not publish a 2027 race-day road-closure timetable, and this page does not treat the 2026 closure PDF as the 2027 plan.

The weather section gives regional climate only, not a race-day forecast.

Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis for Newlands (33.9785 S, 18.4481 E, 70 m) over the 300 April days from 2016 to 2025: mean daily maximum 21.6 °C, mean daily minimum 13.7 °C, mean precipitation 1.3 mm per day, with 16.7% of April days recording at least 1 mm.

These are reanalysis statistics for the Newlands grid cell, not organiser data and not measurements or a forecast for 4 April 2027.

The course crosses Chapman's Peak and Constantia Nek, so town-centre data cannot represent wind, fog or perceived temperature on the passes.

## Difficulty, withdrawal and risk

- **Difficulty:** 56 km with a 7-hour finish cut-off, around the Cape Peninsula, with Chapman's Peak and Constantia Nek as the two main climbs. The official page says runners pass the marathon-distance mark around Hout Bay Main Road, so the second half still has climbing and exposure. It is neither a flat road marathon nor a technical trail race; the load is road pace plus two long climbs plus coastal wind plus a 7-hour limit. The 2027 wave starts and intermediate cut-offs are not published, so 2026 clock times cannot be copied onto 2027.
- **Bail-out points:** Muizenberg, St James, Kalk Bay, Hout Bay, Noordhoek, Constantia and Newlands all have town/road access; the Chapman's Peak section is closed to cars and spectators must walk up, so evacuation there is harder than in town.
- **Risk:** coastal crosswind, exposure on Chapman's Peak, descending fatigue after Constantia Nek, and the wave-dependent 7-hour limit measured from the last start gun. No unified sweep vehicle time is published, so it remains unknown; report to the nearest checkpoint and do not walk the closed Chapman's Peak carriageway.
