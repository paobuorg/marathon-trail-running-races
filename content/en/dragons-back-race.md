## 60-second summary

The Dragon's Back Race is a multiday mountain ultra along the spine of Wales. It starts at Conwy Castle in North Wales and crosses Snowdonia, the Moelwynion, the Rhinogydd, Cadair Idris, the Cambrian Mountains, the Elan Valley and the Bannau Brycheiniog (Black Mountains) before finishing at Cardiff Castle in South Wales. The current Full Dragon is officially about 380 km with 16,400 m of ascent across six consecutive race days. This is not a single-day ultra scaled up: it is six connected mountain stages, each requiring independent navigation and decision-making in remote terrain.

The pressure has two layers. The first is multiday recovery: each night is spent in basic camp tents before starting again the next morning, while climbing, sleep debt and friction damage accumulate day by day. The second is mountain navigation: the route runs through remote ground with unreliable signal. Historically the route was unmarked; from 2026 the organisers set a single mandatory route, but navigation remains required — runners carry a baseplate compass, download the day's line offline and read the map themselves. In the 2026 official results table, 151 full-course starters produced 53 finishers and 98 DNF (ITRA basis, about a 35.1% finish rate); across all formats there were 210 runners from 20 nations (press basis).

- The format is six point-to-point stages, not one 380 km push; each day starts 06:00 and closes 22:00, with in-day aid-station cutoffs.
- The course runs self-supported: no crew may run with you, carry for you or hand over food; you rely on the daily water point, the resupply dry bag at the support point, camp meals and public shops and cafés on the route.
- Entries for 2027 are open on the official site; a third-party compilation lists 6-11 September 2027 at £2,095 plus a £400 deposit, pending the official 2027 bulletin.

## History and origins

The race was first held in September 1992 along the Welsh spine, originally over five days. After a twenty-year absence, Ourea Events revived the modern series in September 2012. The legacy Ourea page records the route crossing the Brecon Beacons, Cambrian Mountains, Rhinogs, Moelwyns and Snowdonia, with runners making independent mountain judgements within an unmarked, self-supported safety framework. The current official site describes the event as a legendary 380 km, six-day Welsh mountain journey.

2026 was the first edition under a new organiser. Ourea Events ceased trading in March 2026 and entered administration; Ultra X acquired the Dragon's Back Race and Northern Traverse from the administrators in April 2026. The 2026 edition proceeded on schedule, existing entries were honoured, and the brand continues as a separate event. The 2026 running was the 11th edition overall (press wire basis). The change of organiser does not alter how this page records route, rules and results; fees, camps and support follow the Ultra X bulletin for the edition.

This history matters when judging the benchmark: this is not a newly created commercial trail race but a mountain tradition descending from the 1992 inaugural Welsh spine crossing, revived in 2012 and adjusted over multiple editions. The Snowdonia rock ridges of the north, the middle highland open moor and the broad Black Mountains terrain of the south have long been part of the calendar. Distance, ascent and camp locations shift slightly between years, so when comparing older results or guides, confirm which route version the record belongs to; do not apply five-day old editions, six-day new editions or a short-format figure directly to a preparation plan.

## Entry and qualification

**This is not a race you can muscle through on road-running base fitness.** The entry page lists no qualifying-race requirement, and entrants accept the participant declaration; the compiled minimum age is 20. The absence of a formal qualifier does not make the race easy: six consecutive days, around 60 km per day on average, 16,400 m of total ascent, remote ridge navigation, Welsh weather and finishes before nightfall. Road speed and a road half or marathon result do not convert directly into finishing ability here; the finishing field historically consists of runners with mountain ultra, multiday and navigation experience.

**Fees and deposit (2026 official; 2027 pending bulletin).** 2026 pricing was tiered by booking stage: Super Early £1,899 (£99 deposit, before 31 August), Early Bird £1,999 (£199 deposit, through September), Standard £2,099 (£299 deposit, from October); the deposit is non-refundable and the balance is paid in £150 monthly instalments. The compiled 2027 basis puts Full Dragon and Hatchling at £2,095 with a £400 deposit; Fire £599, Tail £399. The official 2027 price page had not replaced the 2026 tariffs this round; amounts follow the official entry channel for the edition.

**Transfer, refund and deferral.** The Ultra X sibling official FAQ: entries cannot be transferred to another participant; there are no cash refunds; a deferral to the next edition once is issued as event credit minus the non-refundable deposit. The 2026 deferral deadline was 1 December 2026, 17:00 BST; after that date entries are non-deferrable and non-refundable. 2027 deadlines and credit terms follow the Dragon's Back Terms and Conditions for that edition.

**Lottery and waitlist.** This round the official site published no lottery mechanism or waitlist order; the compiled basis is first-come entry with stage-based pricing. If an edition sells out, waitlist rules follow the official entry channel.

**What the entry includes.** Five nights of camp accommodation (eight-person basic tents, portable toilets, no showers), a participant marquee, volunteers and an info point, Dragon Mail messaging and shared camp charging; a waterproof annotated Harvey map plus the digital version, GPS tracking and digital timing; the resupply dry bag at the daily support point and a manned daily water point; overnight dry bag transport to each camp and resupply dry bag transport to the daily support point; camp breakfast, finishing snacks and dinner, unlimited tea/coffee and hot/cold water, and the final-night celebratory meal; medical professionals and a mountain rescue response team.

## Course and geometry

**Overall alignment.** The route runs north to south from Conwy Castle to Cardiff Castle as a six-day point-to-point ridge line, not a repeating loop. The northern stages cross the Carneddau, Tryfan, the Glyderau, the Crib Goch ridge and the Snowdon Horseshoe; the middle passes Cnicht, the Moelwynion, the Rhinogydd, Dolgellau, Cadair Idris and the Cambrian Mountains; the southern stages run through the Elan Valley and the Bannau Brycheiniog before dropping to Cardiff. The terrain switches between slate trails, rocky ridges, dry mountain tops, grassy valleys, boggy sections and flatter running ground (post-race press observation).

**Navigation requirement.** Traditionally the route was unmarked, requiring constant autonomous decision-making and mountain judgement. For 2026 the organisers set a single mandatory route, so runners no longer choose their own line between checkpoints, but the navigation requirement did not disappear: a baseplate compass is mandatory carry, phone signal is unreliable on remote ground, and the day's line must be downloaded offline before leaving camp. Route reading, ridge and gully identification and fog navigation are part of the finishing skill set; methods are in [Trail navigation](/en/knowledge/trail-navigation/).

**Geometry basis.** The official entry page states that registered entrants receive the route GPX file and checkpoint locations in advance. This round no public GPX/KML download URL was located on the official site, so the entity geometry is recorded as `unavailable`: no line is drawn, no KML/GeoJSON is produced, and no third-party track is hosted or mirrored. The map anchor is an editorial overview coordinate for Conwy. Once an edition track is available, the [Route elevation tool](/en/tools/route-elevation/) can be used to check the day-by-day climb structure.

**Terrain risk points.** Day 1 includes rock ridges and scrambling at the Tryfan and Crib Goch level; trekking poles must be stowed on those sections per compiled guidance. Other stages mix grass slopes, scree, wet ground and tracks; elevation starts near the coast, climbs to the Snowdon massif and Cambrian high ground, then drops to the southern valleys.

Because the route crosses multiple upland blocks over six days, runners should expect conditions to change between stages as well as within a single day: an opening coastal morning can turn to wind and rain on high ridges, and the southern moorland may be drier under sun but still exposed. Route choice on open moor relies on map and compass rather than a waymarked trail, and the 2026 mandatory-line change means the expected line is fixed but not painted on the ground. Anyone unfamiliar with British hill conditions should treat grouse moor, tussock, peat bank and stream-crossing terrain as a distinct training gap rather than assuming generic trail experience transfers.

Even the shorter formats still run on the same self-supported mountain rules, with the same mandatory kit and camp logistics; selecting a shorter format reduces total days, not the per-stage terrain or navigation load.

## Aid, logistics and self-supported rules

**No crew on course.** The compiled basis is explicit: supporters cannot run with runners, carry items for them or hand over food, and cannot enter camps, aid stations or water points; family and friends can see runners off at Conwy Castle and meet them at the finish in Cardiff. Followers track the race through live GPS and Dragon Mail messages. Buying food from public shops and cafés on the route is allowed.

**Organiser support on course.** There is one manned water point per day; each day's support point holds the runner's resupply dry bag (10 L, 2.5 kg limit); the organiser transports the overnight dry bag (Ortlieb 59 L, 15 kg limit) to each camp. There are no personal attendants on course, so fueling must be planned around these two dry bags and public resources.

**Camp support.** Camps are eight-person basic tents with portable toilets and no showers; runners bring their own sleeping bag and mat. Camp provides breakfast, daily finishing snacks, dinner, unlimited tea/coffee and hot/cold water, plus the final-night celebratory meal; shared charging and the Dragon Mail info point are available at camp.

**Mandatory and suggested carry.** Each day carry enough water for the stage (1.5 L bottles or bladder) and food; beyond camp meals, carry carbs and electrolytes matched to your own digestion for long stages and high ground. The fueling framework is in [Long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/).

**Retirement and transport.** Announce withdrawal at an aid station or water point; the organiser arranges onward transport from the support point. DNF results are recorded by completed stage; the 2026 official table marks each runner Finish or DNF.

**Key multiday rhythm understanding.** Unlike a single-day ultra, each of the six days ends with the same repeatable logistics: collect the overnight dry bag, deal with wet clothing and footwear under limited washing facilities, repack the next day's resupply bag, compress sleep in a shared tent, then start again under the same morning routine. Practise these camp actions as part of the race rather than training running alone; no matter how strong the climbing is on a given day, losing control of camp recovery, gear drying or eating will break the following stages. The general load-management and recovery framework is in [Post-race recovery](/en/knowledge/post-race-recovery/), adapted to camp conditions.

## Gear and rules

**Mandatory carry (compiled, follow the edition bulletin).** Waterproof jacket or smock, waterproof trousers, survival bag, synthetic warm layer of at least 300 g, baseplate compass, head torch, red rear light, 1.5 L bottles or bladder, and a phone; the race provides the GPS tracker and map. In exceptional weather, additional kit checks may be added at registration or camp.

|---|---|---|
| Waterproof layers | Waterproof jacket, waterproof trousers | Carried every day |
| Warmth | Synthetic layer ≥300 g, survival bag | Highland cooling and wet-cold conditions |
| Navigation | Baseplate compass; map and tracker provided by race | Signal unreliable; track downloaded offline |
| Light | Head torch, red rear light | Stages may run toward nightfall |
| Water and food | 1.5 L water system; day's food | No personal aid outside water points |
| Other | Phone; trekking poles stowed on Day 1 scrambles | Poles must be stowed on Tryfan/Crib Goch |

**Camp gear.** Bring sleeping bag and mat; overnight clothing and items go in the 15 kg overnight dry bag; the 10 L resupply bag serves the daily support point. Tents are shared basic accommodation with no showers; runners expecting high-quality sleep or drying conditions should adjust expectations in advance.

**Rules and penalties.** Entry requires accepting the participant declaration and terms. Violations of navigation, support, kit or cutoff rules are ruled on by race officials under the regulations. This round the official site did not publish a separate protest and appeal procedure; it is listed under unverified items.

**Visibility and terrain risk.** Fog on ridges, wet rock, boggy valleys and scree descends can appear on the same stage; navigation and judgement carry more weight when weather turns. Uphill and downhill technique notes are in [Uphill and downhill](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/).

**Environment and health.** The main multiday risks are accumulated fatigue, foot friction, stomach issues, wet-cold hypothermia and sleep debt stacking up. This page does not diagnose or recommend medication. Persistent vomiting without food intake, unstable gait, confusion, chest pain or noticeable palpitations, or shivering that will not ease should be assessed at the nearest checkpoint or camp by on-site medical staff. Cold-weather layering principles are in [Cold-weather running](/en/knowledge/cold-weather-running/).

## Training transfer

1. **Multiday output and recovery.** Six stages require finishing a 60 km-class stage each day, sleeping under camp conditions and starting again the next morning. Training should include consecutive days of short-to-moderate volume, practising evening recovery, gear drying and next-morning restarting, rather than relying on one long run. The recovery framework is in [Post-race recovery](/en/knowledge/post-race-recovery/).
2. **Dedicated mountain navigation.** Compass reading, offline track use, fog orientation and ridge identification need separate practice; the route does not depend on phone signal, and a navigation mistake in remote ground costs far more than at an ordinary trail race. Methods are in [Trail navigation](/en/knowledge/trail-navigation/).
3. **Climbing and descent ability.** The 16,400 m of ascent spreads over six days, and Day 1 alone is a 4,100 m high-mountain day; training must cover repeated climbs, long descent buffering and stride control on wet terrain. Points are in [Uphill and downhill](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/).
4. **Weather adaptation and wet-cold kit.** Prepare waterproof and warm layers for rain, low temperature and low visibility, and test the jacket/trousers, light and loaded-walking combination in training; gear choices are in [Trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/).
5. **Fueling and camp rhythm.** Practise fast eating between stages, dry-bag organisation and compressed sleep; plan fueling by stage rather than spreading it across a 100-mile figure. Strategy is in [Long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/); run through the [Race checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/) before the start.

## Race-day execution card

**Before the start (Conwy registration day).** Compiled basis: registration Sunday 5 September, 13:00-17:00 at Ysgol Porth Y Felin in Conwy, with a compulsory 17:30 briefing. Check kit, collect tracker and map, confirm overnight and resupply bag limits, download the day's track offline, and confirm camp location and next-morning start time with staff.

**Each morning before departure.** Check the 1.5 L water system, day's food, waterproof layers, warm layer, compass, head torch and red light, phone; confirm the day's checkpoints and cutoffs; refresh the offline track; lay out next-day clothing and the resupply bag in the dry bag before sleep.

**On stage.** Pace to cutoff margin; do not get dragged by fast early runners; top up water and carbs at water points and public shops; when foggy or unsure, stop and read the map rather than following feel along the ridge; control cadence on climbs and keep descent buffer. After finishing each day, refuel first, treat feet and friction, dry clothing, then rest.

**Withdrawal and medical.** Missed cutoffs, deformed gait or medical red flags go to on-site assessment at a checkpoint or camp; wait for organiser transport after retiring. Do not seek outside crew handovers or pacing outside the rules.

**Gear switch points.** Add warm layers on high ground; put on waterproof trousers in rain; stow poles on the rock-scramble sections; confirm torch and rear light work before nightfall; swap dry socks and worn items point to point at camp.
