## What this race is

The VMM 100km is the flagship distance of the Vietnam Mountain Marathon and the World Trail Majors scoring distance. It starts in Sapa Square in the middle of Sapa town, climbs Ham Rong Mountain, follows a new mountainside route above the Muong Hoa valley, takes a single-track climb to Mount Silverstone and drops into the Red Dao valley of Ban Moi (CP M4) before reaching the Topas Ecolodge checkpoint. It then passes Lao Chai (CP1), O Quy Ho (CP3), Cow Mountain, Ta Phin (CP4), 31 Bridge (CP5), Sau Chua and CP6–CP7, climbs Silverstone a second time and descends to the Topas Ecolodge finish.

## The course

The current official 100km page gives these points in order:

- Start in Sapa town and climb Ham Rong Mountain; after CP M2 a new route follows the mountainside with views over the Muong Hoa valley.
- A single-track climb to Mount Silverstone, explicitly described as an alternative to the usual VMM route to the peak; then the Ban Moi valley (CP M4), home of the Red Dao people.
- Cross the valley to the Topas Ecolodge checkpoint, climb back to Topas Ecolodge, run a road section, descend steeply to a Water Station in the valley, cross the river and climb very steeply on a traverse to Giang Ta Chai, then follow rice terraces to CP1 at Lao Chai.
- The CP1–CP2 climb is updated and extends higher up the mountainside past remote Hmong villages; CP2 leads to CP3 at O Quy Ho.
- After CP3 comes Cow Mountain (Núi Bò): farmland first, then a rugged ridge-line single track to the summit, with Fansipan visible on a clear day; the descent ends at CP4 in Ta Phin.
- From CP4 the character changes to a riverside trail and forest on the way to CP5 (31 Bridge); CP5 to CP6 goes through Sau Chua on good but very steep trail.
- CP6 to CP7 is mostly net downhill through small villages and bamboo; after CP7 comes the second steep Mount Silverstone climb, then the descent to the Topas Ecolodge finish.

The official description mixes three different landforms: a mountainside single track, a valley water station and village terraces. The photograph shows the valley and terraces only; it does not prove that the 2026 course passes this camera position, and it is not race-day weather.

## Start, finish and logistics

- Start: Sapa Square in central Sapa. The organiser gives a Google Maps link; the Vietnamese page's link carries coordinates of about 22.3356 N, 103.8418 E. The map anchor uses those coordinates — a town-centre square, not a surveyed start line.
- Finish: Topas Ecolodge. The FAQ says every distance finishes there and that the finish area has showers, a pool and food; the organiser runs buses from Topas Ecolodge back to Sapa for runners only.
- Bib collection: at Sapa Square; runners must collect their own bib with photo ID or passport and a signed indemnity. The legacy English page gives Friday 09:00–21:00; the current page does not restate the 2026 hours.
- Travel: the organiser suggests the Topas Mountain Express (USD 50 each way), a night train to Lao Cai or a bus to Sapa. No start-line parking plan is published.
- Drop bags: see above — three bags, with the finish bag at the finish before the first finisher.
- Support: the 100km allows one supporter with a supporter card at designated checkpoints only — CP Topas Ecolodge, CP1, CP2, CP4, CP5 and CP6; CP3 and CP7 are not on the list. Supporters may not run with the participant, give medical support, set up tables or tents, light fires or eat checkpoint food.
- Pacers: the organiser publishes pacer rules only for the 100 miles (final 70 km from CP Topas Ecolodge at km 86.3). The 100km page has no pacer clause; whether the 100km allows pacers is unknown, and the 100 miles rules are not applied here.
- Mandatory kit: a pack or belt able to carry all kit, 1 litre of water capacity, a head torch, a waterproof hooded jacket, a long-sleeve layer, a cap or buff, a survival blanket, a whistle, a phone and a basic first-aid kit. Missing key items means disqualification; missing other items costs 30 minutes. The rules require the kit to be carried at all times, including at the finish.
- Weather: the FAQ gives a September daily average of about 20°C in Sapa and warns that mountain weather can turn wet and colder; the level-B report mentions rain-season mud on parts of the 2026 descent. The organiser has published no monsoon- or park-access-related change to aid stations or river crossings, so nothing is entered in `courseChanges`; the point is noted in the body only.

The finish at Topas Ecolodge is fixed for every distance in the official FAQ, and the finish area has showers, a pool and food; the photograph shows only the terraced slope near the lodge and does not prove a finish gantry, the pool or race-day layout.

## Difficulty, withdrawal and risk

- **Difficulty:** 101 km, +5,500 m; the official 2026 profile prints a 28-hour limit. The difficulty is wet single track on Ham Rong, two Silverstone ascents, the Cow Mountain ridge, bamboo descents between CP6 and CP7, and monsoon mud and stream crossings; not altitude, but high climb density and technical descending. No per-segment technical grade is published, and this page does not spread 5,500 m into a per-kilometre number.
- **Support/bail-out points:** CP Topas Ecolodge, CP1 (Lao Chai), CP2, CP4 (Ta Phin), CP5 (31 Bridge) and CP6 allow one supporter; CP3 (O Quy Ho) and CP7 are not supporter points. The finish at Topas Ecolodge has an official runner bus back to Sapa; public transport from mid-course villages is not fixed, so evacuation follows the organiser, not hitchhiking.
- **Risk:** monsoon mud and slippery bamboo, stream crossings, cold and fog on the night ridge; the official FAQ warns the mountains can turn wet and cold quickly. Whether the 100 km allows a pacer is not stated, so it remains unknown; the 100-mile pacer rule is not applied.
- **Withdrawal:** report to the checkpoint captain and hand in chip/bib. CP3 and CP7 have no supporter access, so ask about the official shuttle before withdrawing. The 2027 sweep details are not published, and this page does not treat the 2026 arrangement as 2027 rules.

## Weather and environment: September rainy-season Sapa mountains

The official FAQ puts Sapa's September daily average at about 20 °C, and warns the mountains can turn wet and cold suddenly; third-party 2026 reporting notes muddy descents under the rainy season. The race is not high-altitude, but climb density is high and single-track and bamboo sections dominate, with slippery surfaces after rain.

## Entry and qualification

The 100km requires at least one previously completed chip-timed 50km+ mountain ultra with significant elevation; loop and virtual races do not qualify. A result link (VTS, UTMB Index, ITRA or similar) must be supplied at registration, and a qualifying race run after registering must be submitted by 30 days before race day. The minimum age is 18 on race day.

Registration closes one month before race day or when the quota is full; the 100km page does not give fees or a quota in this read, so they are not filled in. Entries can be transferred until 30 days before the race; there is no deferral to another year except for pregnancy; a distance change costs VND 500,000 and is not possible within 30 days or after sell-out.

Qualification benefits: the organiser states 4 ITRA points, ITRA National League, UTMB Index 100K and a Western States qualifying race, with the year disagreement described above.

## Training transfer

The 100 km is a 101 km, +5,500 m, 28-hour Vietnamese mountain ultra with two Silverstone ascents, the Cow Mountain ridge, rainy-season muddy descents and a night section. The table breaks the demands into trainable abilities; it lists directions, not an official plan.

| Ability | Course demand | Preparation direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ultra endurance | Finish 101 km inside 28 hours | Extend continuous running time gradually, keeping one session near 100 km or 50 mile mountain scale |
| Climb load | +5,500 m: two Silverstone ascents, Cow Mountain and the steep CP6–CP7 trail | Build vertical load on climb sessions; practise repeated summits fatigued |
| Slippery descents | Rain-season bamboo, stream and single-track descents muddy | Add wet-downhill running and quad strength; do not force wet surfaces |
| Night running | The 100 km runs through the night; headlamp mandatory | Do night runs; practise ridge and bamboo navigation under headlamp |
| Heat and rain | September Sapa daily average about 20 °C, mountains can turn wet and cold | Train layered clothing and rain gear; practise cooling and electrolytes per [racing in heat and humidity](/en/knowledge/heat-and-humidity/) |
| Long-duration fuelling | CP Topas, CP1–CP7 and several water stations | Train station-to-station eating per [fueling long runs](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/); read an authorised file with the [track viewer](/en/tools/track-view/) and plan segments with the [split pace tool](/en/tools/splits/) |

The entry threshold requires a prior chip-timed mountain ultra of at least 50 km with significant climb; loops and virtual races do not count.
