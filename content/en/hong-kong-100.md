## What this course is

The Hong Kong 100 (HK100 Ultra Marathon) starts at Pak Tam Chung in Sai Kung, in the eastern New Territories, and runs west along the MacLehose Trail and its scenic diversions. It crosses coastal paths, beaches, old villages, reservoirs and ridgelines, then finishes with the descent from Tai Mo Shan (957 m), Hong Kong's highest peak, and about 4 km of downhill running into Rotary Club Park on Tai Mo Shan Road. The organiser describes it as a 100 km trail race built around the MacLehose Trail, with diversions added so that runners spend more time in the trail's most scenic sections.

The race was first held in January 2011 with 175 starters. It was founded by two Hong Kong lawyers, Janet Ng and Steve Brammar. By 2026 the field had grown to 1,844 finishers, making it one of Asia's largest hundred-kilometre trail races and the opening event in the 2027 World Trail Majors calendar. The 2027 race week runs from 21 to 24 January and contains four categories:

- The Third: Thursday 21 January, 34 km, 1,558 m of climb, 10-hour limit.
- The Half: Friday 22 January, 53 km, 2,263 m of climb, 14-hour limit.
- HK100: Saturday 23 January, 100 km, 5,142 m of climb, 30-hour limit.
- The Grand Sam: 21–24 January, all three races back to back. The organiser's event card gives 183 km, 8,963 m of climb and a 54-hour limit; adding the three individual distances gives 187 km, 4 km more than 183. The card does not explain the difference, so both figures are recorded here.

[![Hong Kong 100 2027 four-category structure chart: The Third 34 km, The Half 53 km, HK100 100 km, and The Grand Sam at the organiser's 183 km versus the 187 km sum; bars are scaled to 187 km and this is not a map or elevation profile](/media/routes/hong-kong-100/hongkong100-four-races.svg)](/media/routes/hong-kong-100/hongkong100-four-races.svg)

Those four sets of figures come from the 2027 race cards on the organiser's home page. The Third and The Half both start at Pak Tam Chung. The transport page notes that the start and CP1 to CP5 arrangements also apply to The Half, and the start, CP1 and CP2 to The Third. Beyond that, the organiser gives no text description of exactly where the three races split, so this page does not infer it.

The geography is worth setting out because it explains the shape of the race. The first half follows the Sai Kung coast. The first 10 km are on Stage 1 of the MacLehose Trail, with the islands of the South China Sea on the right and High Island Reservoir on the left; the route crosses the dams that form the reservoir to CP1 at East Dam. It then climbs Sai Wan Shan, descends to Sai Wan village, crosses Sai Wan and Ham Tin beaches, skirts behind Ham Tin village to Tai Long Wan, turns inland about halfway along Tai Long Wan beach, and after a stretch of "jungle trail" rejoins Stage 2 of the MacLehose Trail. The middle section climbs from Pak Tam Au over Ngau Yee Shek Shan, descends the Cheung Sheung country trail to Hoi Ha Road, follows the road to Pak Sha O, then uses fast coastal path and dirt trail to Yung Shue O before climbing Kai Kung Shan to Kei Ling Ha. The second half runs from Kei Ling Ha over the Ma On Shan ridge, behind Lion Rock and over Beacon Hill into northern Kowloon, then down to Shing Mun, Golden Hill, Needle Hill, Grassy Hill and finally Tai Mo Shan. The organiser states plainly that most of the climbing is in the second half, so the first half has to be paced with something left in reserve.

Entry is capped by the Hong Kong Government: 1,800 for HK100, 500 for The Half and 500 for The Third. Places go through a ballot, with exemptions only for past winners, 2026 volunteers, runners who entered all 16 editions from 2011 to 2026, and sponsor nominees. Ballot results for the 2027 edition were released on Monday 17 August 2026; successful entrants had to complete registration by Sunday 23 August 2026, and unclaimed places were reallocated from Monday 31 August 2026.

The card at the top of this page uses the 2027 race card's **100 km / 5,142 m**, because it is the pair of distance and climb figures stated together on the official 2027 race card. The course page's "100km journey climbs over 5,000m" describes the same edition at lower precision; it does not contradict 5,142 m.

The 2027 checkpoint-table image is a third, more detailed and more conflicted data set. It adds its 11 cumulative distances to **96 km** and labels the last row "Finish 96"; its per-stage gain and per-stage loss each add up to **5,142 m**. That is **4 km** short of the race card and introductory text, but the organiser does not say whether the 4 km is an unlisted connecting section, a missing table row, or simply a brand distance. This page does not add the 4 km and does not relabel 96 km as "the official distance". The segment and checkpoint tables below use the 96 km table because it is the only official source that also gives per-stage distance, gain/loss and opening/closing times.

The table creates two further conflicts:

- It labels East Dam as **SP** and then starts numbering from **CP1 Sai Wan Pavilion**. The course text calls East Dam **CP1**, Sai Wan Pavilion **CP2**, and continues to **CP10 Lead Mine Pass**. From Sai Wan Pavilion onward, the table's CP number is one lower than the text's. This page keeps the course-text CP1–CP10 in prose, keeps the table's own label in the table, and shows both.
- The table makes CP10 Leadmine Pass to the Finish **9 km** (96 − 87). The course text says "just 10km to go" in the same section. Subtracting from the race card's 100 km gives **13 km**. All three figures are official and all three are reported; this page does not choose one for the organiser.

The third row needs its own explanation. UTMB Index records 92.9 km and 5,073 m for the 2026 edition. That is not a shorter 2027 course: it is the shortened course actually used in January 2026. Third-party race history records that Typhoon Wipha damaged the route in the summer before the race and that the January 2026 course was shortened by roughly 7 km. The 2026 finishing times and climb therefore cannot be compared directly with the 2027 100 km version, and 92.9 km must not be presented as "the official distance".

There is also a small internal inconsistency in the official text. At CP10 (Lead Mine Pass, 87 km) the course page says runners have "just 10km to go"; the checkpoint table says 87 → 96 = **9 km**; subtracting from the race card's 100 km gives **13 km**. All three official figures are reported here, and the organiser's text is not rewritten. The segment table uses the checkpoint table's cumulative marks, so CP10 to the finish is 9 km; the top card keeps the race card's 100 km.

The checkpoint table also publishes per-stage descent, summing to **−5,142 m**. This is the only formal per-stage gain/loss data; the race card still publishes no descent and the course text does not either. The segment table uses the table's figures directly rather than writing "not published", but it does not present the table's −5,142 m as the race card's figure.

Segment notes. Place names, hill names and terrain descriptions are taken from the 2027 official course page:

- **Segment 1 (0 → 10 km, East Dam).** Stage 1 of the MacLehose Trail, with the South China Sea islands on the right and High Island Reservoir on the left, crossing the dams to East Dam. CP1 sits at East Dam; the road to East Dam is closed on race days and no vehicles can reach the checkpoint. The organiser puts most of the climbing in the second half and advises saving energy for it.
- **Segment 2 (10 → 17 km, Sai Wan Pavilion).** A climb over Sai Wan Shan to Sai Wan Pavilion. For 2027 the organiser has relocated CP2 to Sai Wan Pavilion, before the beaches; the course page explicitly labels this as a 2027 change. CP2 positions in 2026 and earlier material therefore do not apply to 2027.
- **Segment 3 (17 → 28 km, Pak Tam Au).** A short climb gives views over Sai Kung Country Park, then the route descends to Sai Wan village, crosses Sai Wan and Ham Tin beaches, skirts behind Ham Tin village to Tai Long Wan, turns inland about halfway along the beach, follows a stretch of "jungle trail", rejoins Stage 2 of the MacLehose Trail and undulates to Pak Tam Au. CP3 is also relocated to Pak Tam Au for 2027.
- **Segment 4 (28 → 36 km, Pak Sha O).** From Pak Tam Au the route climbs steeply over Ngau Yee Shek Shan, with views to islands including Kau Sai Chau on the left at the summit. After the descent it turns sharply right at Cheung Sheung and drops down the Cheung Sheung Country Trail, which the organiser calls a soft and bouncy trail runner's dream. It turns left at Hoi Ha Road and follows the road to Pak Sha O. The organiser specifically requires runners to use the pavement and cross the road with care. CP4 is at Pak Sha O.
- **Segment 5 (36 → 44 km, Yung Shue O).** Pak Sha O to Yung Shue O mixes fast coastal path with dirt trail. Approaching the checkpoint, runners look across Kei Ling Ha towards Ma On Shan ("Saddle Hill").
- **Segment 6 (44 → 52 km, Kei Ling Ha).** The organiser states that after CP5 the real climbing starts. The route leaves via the back of Yung Shue O village, climbs the Cheung Sheung Country Trail, turns sharply right to rejoin the MacLehose Trail, and crosses Kai Kung Shan ("Rooster Hill") before descending to Kei Ling Ha. CP6 is the only mid-course checkpoint with drop bags.
- **Segment 7 (52 → 65 km, Gilwell Camp).** The organiser calls this the race's longest and hardest stage and tells runners to make sure they have enough liquids. The ridge after Ma On Shan gives views over Sai Kung and the hundreds of islands of the South China Sea, followed by smooth, runnable orange-coloured dirt trail and the welcome at Gilwell Scout Camp. The segment is 13 km (65 − 52).
- **Segment 8 (65 → 74 km, Beacon Hill).** From Gilwell Camp the route traverses behind Lion Rock, looking over Kowloon towards Hong Kong Island; the organiser calls it the best city view of the whole course. It then climbs to Beacon Hill. CP8 is generally accessible only on foot, about an hour's hike from the nearest road.
- **Segment 9 (74 → 80 km, Shing Mun).** A sharp descent into monkey country, then a stretch sharing the trails with macaques. Every runner must use the footbridge across Tai Po Road, which the organiser describes as required for both safety and fairness, before climbing the sealed road of Golden Hill. The organiser warns that monkeys will snatch food eaten here. CP9 is at Shing Mun Reservoir, reachable by car but with very limited parking.
- **Segment 10 (80 → 87 km, Lead Mine Pass).** Leaving Shing Mun, three climbs remain: the steep Needle Hill, the winding sealed road over Grassy Hill, and the sharp descent to Lead Mine Pass. CP10 is inaccessible to vehicles; the organiser's own advice is that it is easier to shuffle to the finish than to walk out of the country park.
- **Segment 11 (87 → 96 km, finish).** One last climb over Tai Mo Shan (957 m), then about 4 km downhill to Rotary Club Park. The official checkpoint table makes CP10 to the Finish **9 km**; the course text says "just 10km to go"; using the race card's 100 km gives **13 km**. All three figures are retained; this page does not choose one for the organiser. Whichever figure is used, this section contains the course's highest point, 957 m.

On the official segmentation, the first 52 km to CP6 are coastal paths, beaches, villages and country trails; after CP6 the route becomes a continuous sequence of ridges and sealed-road climbs: Ma On Shan, Lion Rock, Beacon Hill, Shing Mun, Needle Hill, Grassy Hill and Tai Mo Shan. That is where the organiser's "much of the climbing is in the second half" lands.

## Surface and terrain

The official pages publish no surface percentages and no "runnability" figures, so this page does not invent any. The material categories that the official text does establish are:

- **Coastal path and hill trail.** The first 10 km are on Stage 1 of the MacLehose Trail, followed by Stage 2.
- **Beaches.** Sai Wan, Ham Tin and Tai Long Wan must be crossed; the organiser describes "crossing Sai Wan and Ham Tin Beaches" and turning inland about halfway along Tai Long Wan beach.
- **Jungle trail.** A stretch of "jungle trail" after Tai Long Wan before rejoining the MacLehose Trail.
- **Soft country trail.** The Cheung Sheung Country Trail is described by the organiser as soft and bouncy.
- **Orange dirt trail.** After Ma On Shan there is a smooth, runnable orange-coloured dirt trail along the ridge.
- **Pavement and road.** The Pak Sha O section requires the pavement and careful road crossing. The climbs over Golden Hill, Needle Hill and Grassy Hill include sealed road, which the organiser names explicitly.
- **Steep and uneven ground.** The safety section states directly that the course is "rocky, uneven and steep in places" and that overtaking is only acceptable when it is safe.

The organiser does not name one long climb as the difficulty; it lists coastal path, beaches, jungle trail, soft country trail, orange dirt trail, pavement and sealed road.

## Risks and escape points

- **Night running.** HK100 takes place in late January with a 30-hour limit. The official pages do not give a start time; slower finishers will run at night. The mandatory kit requires a headlamp or torch with batteries, and this applies to HK100 and The Half. Headlamp, spare batteries and a spare light should be checked against the official list.
- **Heat and humidity.** January is Hong Kong's relatively dry season (Hong Kong Observatory). The course page repeatedly stresses drinking enough and carrying enough fluid to the next checkpoint.
- **Technical ground and road crossings.** The organiser describes the course as rocky, uneven and steep in places. The Pak Sha O pavement and the Tai Po Road footbridge are mandatory. Crossing roads while running is a foreseeable risk on this course.
- **Monkeys.** Macaques around Shing Mun and Golden Hill will snatch food; the organiser calls them "cheeky, thieving, mischievous" and warns that eating there invites theft. Keep food in the pack, not in hand.
- **Mobile coverage.** The organiser states that most of the course has coverage but that some areas have none or only intermittent signal. A phone is not a guaranteed rescue channel for the whole route; the emergency numbers are printed on the race bib.
- **First aid and rescue.** Emergency first aid is available at every checkpoint. A runner needing help should call the event numbers on the bib so that the organiser can retrieve them.
- **Escape points.** According to the official transport page: CP1 has a race-day road closure and no vehicle access; CP2–CP4 are reachable by taxi or public transport, with private cars requiring a permit; CP5 by taxi, with private cars requiring a permit; CP6 has taxi and public transport but extremely limited parking; CP7 has taxi with very limited parking; CP8 is reached on foot, about an hour; CP9 is car accessible with very limited parking; CP10 has no vehicle access and about an hour on foot.
- **Withdrawal procedure.** Sign the withdrawal form at the nearest checkpoint; if first-aid crews remove a runner, they handle it. The rules require runners to stay on the marked course and prohibit shortcuts.
- **Cancellation or course change.** The organiser reserves the right to cancel or shorten the event for adverse weather, landslides, rockfalls, hill fires, protests, blockage or force majeure. Hong Kong's typhoon season runs from May to November (Hong Kong Observatory); the rules list hill fires as a reason to cancel or shorten, and the Observatory says January–February can bring cold fronts.
- **Semi-self-sufficiency.** Mandatory kit must be carried at all times and can be checked at random; a missing item can lead to a time penalty or disqualification. Pacers and accompanying runners are strictly prohibited, and outside support is allowed only at checkpoints.

## Getting there

The start and finish require completely different plans. All of the following comes from the official 2027 transport page.

**To the start (Pak Tam Chung, Tai Mong Tsai Road)**

- **Taxi.** The transport page gives estimates: from Hong Kong Island under an hour and a little under HK$300; from Kowloon about 45 minutes and about HK$200. It gives the Chinese address 西貢大網仔路北潭涌 to show the driver.
- **Public transport.** The transport page lists bus and minibus routes to the start, all involving a change in Sai Kung Town. From Kowloon: bus 92 from Diamond Hill MTR bus terminus, from 6 am every 30 minutes; minibus 1A from Choi Hung MTR station, from 6.30 am, leaving when full. From Hong Kong Island: MTR to Hang Hau, then minibus 101M from 5.30 am, leaving when full; or MTR to Choi Hung, then minibus 1A.
- **Sai Kung Town to Pak Tam Chung.** Bus 94 from 6 am every 30 minutes; minibus 9 from 6.15 am every 30 minutes.
- **Official travel service.** GL Tours of Hong Kong is appointed as the official travel services partner for the 2027 Hong Kong 100, providing hotel accommodation, transfers and other travel services. The transport page recommends using it to help reduce traffic jams to the start.

**Leaving the finish (Rotary Club Park, Tai Mo Shan Road)**

- **Free shuttle.** Between the finish and Tsuen Wan MTR station, operating from 5.30 pm on 23 January 2027 to 2.30 pm on 24 January 2027, leaving when full. A later passage on the same page says the service runs from 5.30 pm Saturday to 11 am Sunday; the two end times disagree, so the official page should be rechecked before relying on it.
- **Road closure.** Tai Mo Shan Road to the finish is closed from 08:00 on 23 January to 17:00 on 24 January 2027. No participant cars or taxis may enter or stay on Tai Mo Shan Road or at the finish (Rotary Club car park) during that period.
- **Bus 51.** Stops at the junction of Route Twisk and Tai Mo Shan Road, about 3 minutes' walk from the finish, and runs to Tsuen Wan Railway Station. The transport page notes that it does not run overnight: it stops at around 11 pm and resumes at 6 am, every 30–40 minutes.
- **Spectating and support.** The start at Pak Tam Chung is car accessible with limited parking. CP1 East Dam has a race-day road closure and no vehicle access. CP3 Pak Tam Au, CP4 Pak Sha O and CP6 Kei Ling Ha are reachable by public transport or taxi. CP8 and CP10 are reached on foot, about an hour. CP9 Shing Mun is car accessible with very limited parking.
- **Self-driving.** The transport page lists limited parking at the start, no parking at the finish, and the closure of Tai Mo Shan Road during the race window; it recommends the official travel service.

**From the airport**

From Hong Kong International Airport (HKG), the airport's official transport pages list three ways into the city: the Airport Express (described by the airport as the quickest way to and from the city), public buses serving all of Hong Kong, and taxis. There is no direct rail link to Sai Kung. Putting the airport page and the race transport page together, the chain in which every leg can be traced to one of those pages is: airport → city by Airport Express or bus → Sai Kung Town → bus 94 or minibus 9 → Pak Tam Chung. The official race transport page does not list an airport shuttle; the only airport-adjacent option it names is GL Tours' paid transfer service.

## Season and weather

The 2026 edition was held on 24 January 2026. To give a reference for training and kit in the same window, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 17–25 January 2026 (seven days before to one day after the race, nine days in total) at a Hong Kong grid point:

- Grid point: 22.3902°N, 114.2236°E, elevation 380 m, timezone Asia/Hong_Kong.
- Daily maximum: 13.9°C to 23.4°C, median 18.2°C.
- Daily minimum: 6.5°C to 12.8°C, median 10.3°C.
- Precipitation: one day with any rain (0.1 mm on 23 January); no day reached 1 mm.
- Race day, 24 January: maximum 18.0°C, minimum 7.2°C, no precipitation.

These are **grid-point modelled historical values, not measurements on the course**, and they do not represent conditions on Tai Mo Shan. The grid point sits at 380 m while the course reaches 957 m; this page has no summit measurements, so the grid-point figures should not be read as summit conditions. The Hong Kong Observatory's long-term climate description points the same way: January and February are cloudier, occasional cold fronts bring dry northerly winds, urban temperatures commonly drop below 10°C, and sub-zero temperatures and frost occur at times on high ground and in the New Territories. January and December are usually the driest months. Taken together: the driest months still bring cold fronts and temperatures below 10°C. Kit should be planned for a conservative scenario of about 7°C at the start, wind on the summit and possible rain, not for an urban afternoon at 18°C.

The sample is also limited: nine days from a single year. It answers "what the most recent race week looked like", not "the probability distribution across many Januarys". For climate probabilities, the Hong Kong Observatory's long-term statistics are the appropriate source; this page does not turn a nine-day sample into a climate conclusion.

## Difficulty rating

The official pages do not publish a single difficulty grade. What follows is this site's rating, not the organiser's, and it rests only on verifiable dimensions:

- **Distance.** The 2027 race card says 100 km, the 2027 checkpoint table adds to **96 km**, and the 2026 course as run was 92.9 km. These are three different bases and must not be conflated. The top card uses the race card's 100 km; the segments and cut-offs use the table's 96 km.
- **Climb / descent.** 5,142 m on the 2027 race card; the checkpoint table's per-stage rows also total **+5,142 / −5,142 m** and are the only formal source for per-stage descent. The course page describes "over 5,000 m", with most of it in the second half.
- **Time limit.** 30 hours. The checkpoint table gives opening and closing clock times, but no start time; this page does not convert them into elapsed time.
- **Technicality.** The organiser explicitly describes rocky, uneven and steep ground, plus beaches, jungle trail, sealed road and ridge dirt.
- **Altitude.** The high point is 957 m (course page).
- **Escapability.** CP8 and CP10 require about an hour on foot, and CP1 is behind a race-day road closure.
- **Environment.** The organiser repeatedly stresses hydration and carrying enough fluid to the next checkpoint; monkeys steal food; parts of the course have no mobile signal.

**This site's rating, not the organiser's:** a hundred-kilometre-class trail race with 5,142 m of climb on the 2027 race card, a 30-hour limit, rocky and uneven ground in places, sealed-road climbs over Golden Hill and Grassy Hill, and two checkpoints (CP8 and CP10) that need about an hour on foot to leave. Editorial advice: do not pace the first half like a road marathon; save energy for Needle Hill, Grassy Hill and Tai Mo Shan. For pace arithmetic, this site's [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/) is available; for the difference between marathon distance and 100 km, read [the marathon distance](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/); for splitting target times by segment, use the [splits tool](/en/tools/splits/). These are site tools, not official pacing advice.

## Entry, ballot and quotas

Hong Kong 100 does not sell places on a first-come basis. The official 2027 entry page states that the Hong Kong government caps the field at 1,800 for HK100, 500 for The Half and 500 for The Third. Everyone must enter through the ballot except past winners, 2026 volunteers, runners who joined all 16 editions from 2011 to 2026, and sponsor nominees. Ballot results are released on 17 August 2026; selected runners must complete registration by 23 August 2026, and unclaimed places are reallocated from 31 August 2026.

| Item | Official 2027 rule | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Field cap | HK100 1,800; The Half 500; The Third 500 | Separate caps per distance |
| Entry route | Ballot; winners, 2026 volunteers, 16-year streakers and sponsor nominees exempt | Exempt places are not transferable |
| Ballot result | 17 August 2026 | Follow the official notice |
| Registration deadline | 23 August 2026 | Late places are forfeited |
| Unclaimed places | Reallocated from 31 August 2026 | A rejection can still become a place |
| Format | Solo or pair of no more than two | One distance per person, once |
| Distance | 100 km / Half / The Third | Chosen at entry, not changeable |

The 16-year exemption belongs only to runners who did every edition from the first in 2011. It is not a cumulative "many editions" credit; the page narrows it to consecutive years, so do not infer your own eligibility.

## Mandatory gear and checks

The Hong Kong 100 mandatory list is short and hard. The official gear page requires runners to carry at all times: a mobile phone, a headlamp or torch with batteries, a whistle, an emergency space blanket or equivalent, capacity for at least 1 litre of water, two energy bars or food portions, a ziploc bag for personal rubbish, the race bib, and some cash. The light applies to HK100 and The Half; for The Third it is optional, though the page suggests bringing one if you might take 10 hours.

## Training for 96 km and 5,142 m

The official 2027 checkpoint table runs from SP–East Dam at 10 km to the finish at a cumulative 96 km, with stage gain and loss each totalling +5,142 m and −5,142 m. The race card and introduction still say 100 km, and the organiser does not explain the 4 km difference. Train from the checkpoint segments, not from the round 100 km number, because the race you face is eleven segments, 5,142 m of climb and a 30-hour limit.

| Phase | Weekly volume | Weekly climb | Key session | Terrain/night |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base, 4 weeks | 50–65 km | 1,200–1,800 m | 90 minutes continuous climbing | One stair and one beach session |
| Specific, 5 weeks | 65–85 km | 1,800–2,600 m | 4–5 hour trail long run | 2-hour night run with headlamp |
| Segment simulation, 3 weeks | 75–95 km | 2,600–3,400 m | Rehearse CP5–CP7: 21 km / +1,403 m | Afternoon heat plus wet stairs |
| Peak, 2 weeks | 80–100 km | 3,000–4,000 m | 6–8 hours loaded, practise CP6 bag | 5 hours overnight |
| Taper, 2 weeks | 50–60% of peak | 40–50% of peak | Short hills at intensity | Gear and route check only |

The section to rehearse is CP5–CP7. From 52 km to 65 km the course climbs 794 m, and CP5–CP6 alone is 13 km with that gain; by 65 km the cumulative climb is 3,194 m. Many runners do not "stop running" here; they destroy their legs climbing. One long session with 800 m of climb after 50 km is closer to Hong Kong than stacking flat volume.

Train the beach and the stairs separately. The Sai Wan, Ham Tin and Tai Long Wan crossings break rhythm, and stone steps force walking uphill and forbid charging downhill; neither exists in a flat training block. Do at least two wet-stair descents and one 20-minute beach run in the race shoe.

## Race-day execution, bags and support

Hong Kong 100 restricts support more than many hundreds: the official rules allow outside assistance and support crews only at checkpoints, except in an emergency; pacers and support runners are strictly prohibited; checkpoint food and drink are for runners only. Buying food and drink from shops, restaurants, hawkers and vending machines along the route is allowed. Your crew cannot meet you mid-trail; it can only appear at a CP.

| Point | Action | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Race morning | Hand in two 45×50 cm drawstring bags at the start | One bag per point; extra baggage HKD 200 each |
| CP1 East Dam, 10 km | No change, top up water | Cut-off from the official table |
| CP3 Pak Tam Au, 28 km | First big refuel | Eat your own food; CP food is runners only |
| CP5 Yung Shue O, 44 km | Last big refuel before night | Light, spare batteries, warm layer |
| CP6 Gilwell Camp, 65 km | Collect bag, change socks and clothes | Bag forwards to the finish |
| CP7 Beacon Hill, 74 km | Enter the last 22 km | Water and food check |
| CP10 Leadmine Pass, 87 km | Last major climb | No vehicle escape; leave margin |
| Finish, Rotary Park, 96 km | Finish | 30-hour limit |

There are only two bag points: CP6 Gilwell Camp and the finish. The organiser provides two 45×50 cm drawstring bags, one per point per runner, handed in at the start; after CP6 the bag is forwarded to the finish. Extra baggage costs HKD 200 per item and goes only to the finish, not to CP6. Put the socks and warm layer you want at CP6 in the CP6 bag, not the finish bag.

CP10 is the point that needs the biggest buffer. The official page says vehicles cannot reach CP10 and it is about an hour's walk to the nearest road; CP8 has the same one-hour walk. Missing the CP10 cut-off is therefore not "wait for a car"; it is a walk out. Write "reach CP10 45 minutes before cut-off" on the race card, not "move fast".

## Results, records and history

The first Hong Kong 100 was held in January 2011 with 175 starters, founded by two Hong Kong lawyers, Janet Ng and Steve Brammar. It is now the opening round of the World Trail Majors and the largest hundred-kilometre trail race in Asia. The 2026 edition had 1,844 finishers; the January 2026 course was shortened by roughly 7 km after Typhoon Wipha damaged the route the previous summer, and produced the race's first dead heat: Veronika Leng and Vietnam's Hà Thị Hậu crossed together in 10:43:35.

| Item | Result/fact | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First edition | January 2011, 175 starters | Founded by Janet Ng and Steve Brammar |
| 2026 finishers | 1,844 | Course shortened by about 7 km |
| 2026 winners | Veronika Leng / Hà Thị Hậu, 10:43:35 | First dead heat |
| Men's course record | Qi Min 9:28:36 (2018) | Classic route |
| Women's course record | Yao Miao 10:40:52 (2018) | Classic route |
| Two-time winners | Yun Yanqiao 2013/2017; You Peiquan 2020/2023; Meng Guangfu 2024/2025 | Only three |

Read the 2026 dead heat and the shortened course together. A course 7 km short cannot be compared directly with the classic-route 9:28:36 and 10:40:52; calling 10:43:35 a new women's record is wrong because it is not the same distance. Before comparing any Hong Kong 100 result, establish which course version was run, then read the place and the time.
