## What this course is

The Pilolcura 100K is a point-to-point winter night race. It starts at Punucapa, north of Valdivia, crosses the Coastal Range over Oncol Hill, drops towards the Pacific coast, and converges on the city of Valdivia for the finish. The official English page puts it this way:

> Run 100 kilometers across the Coastal Range, navigating rocky and slippery trails that take you from Oncol Hill to the Pacific Ocean coast.

The photograph shows the rainforest and viewpoint setting around Cafetería Oncol only; it is not a 2027 course photograph and does not prove the aid-station position, night conditions or metre-by-metre course passage.*

**A note on the name.** The organiser calls this 101 km category **Pilolcura 100K**: the race page path is `/races/PILOLCURA100K` and the page title is `PILOLCURA 100K`. The same page still gives the start as **Punucapa**. As of 2026-11-08 the official pages do not separately explain the relationship between the two place names. This page does not infer that they are the same place; it records the official category name and the start printed on the same page separately, so readers who see "Pilolcura" do not mistake it for a different race.

The same page describes the finish as:

> the route converges with the city of Valdivia, where the main finish line awaits, bathed in the light of dawn.

The race is not designed to be run in daylight: the organiser sets a 22:00 start, titles the page "Run on the Longest Night of the Year" and writes that the route challenges runners to "conquer the darkest night of the year in southern Chile".

### What makes this South American race different

- **Temperate rainforest.** The official description names the trees lining the route: "olivillos, canelos, ulmos, laureles, and mañíos" — the Valdivian rainforest (Selva Valdiviana). The destination page lists that rainforest as one of the region's defining landscapes.
- **The Valdivia river network.** The destination page says the city was founded "a orillas del río Calle-Calle" and that its river network includes the Valdivia, Calle-Calle, Cau-Cau and Cruces rivers, forming a wetland landscape. The finish sits inside that city.
- **Southern-hemisphere winter in late June.** The race falls just after the June solstice, and the organiser frames it as the longest night of the year. The destination page adds that "en el sur de Chile, el invierno y la lluvia añaden una dimensión única a la experiencia del trail running", and quotes Parque Oncol as a coastal forest remnant with annual rainfall above 3,500 mm, strong winds and high humidity.

The difficulty is not altitude: the official profile tops out at 701 m and drops to 1 m. Night running, winter rain, wet rock and rainforest terrain carry the difficulty instead. The organiser explicitly describes "rocky and slippery trails", so this page does not present the course as fast.

## Mandatory kit and semi-autonomy

The 2027 regulations make semi-autonomy the basic rule of the race:

> Deben estar preparados para llevar sus propios líquidos, comida y equipo (incluyendo ropa para condiciones climáticas adversas) entre los avituallamientos.

The boundary at aid stations is equally explicit:

> Los puntos de avituallamiento disponen de bebida y comida para consumir in situ.. sólo suministra para llenar los bidones o bolsas de líquido.

Other regulation points that affect equipment:

- No disposable tableware is provided; runners who want hot food must bring their own utensils.
- Poles, if carried, must be carried for the whole race and cannot go in the drop bag.
- Listening to music with headphones is not allowed.
- Only organiser drop bags are transported, and they must be collected at the finish within two hours of race closure.
- Equipment checks take place at accreditation, before the start, during the race and after the finish.

## Risks and escape points

- **Night start.** The 22:00 start is the organiser's framing of the longest night of the year. Headtorch, spare batteries and night navigation are prerequisites; this page recommends no specific models.
- **Winter rain and wet ground.** The organiser explicitly writes "rocky and slippery trails" and lists cold and rain as conditions runners must be prepared for. Regional climate is in the next section.
- **Cold.** The Open-Meteo grid for 24–27 June has a median daily minimum of 5.3 °C and a minimum of −1.9 °C; these are regional grid values, not course measurements.
- **Long gap.** 18.6 km without hot food between Las Truchas 1 and Cafetería Oncol, on one of the main climbing sections.
- **Incomplete cut-off information.** The official pages do not yet provide 2027 checkpoint cut-offs (see below), so 2026 data cannot be used for pacing.
- **Withdrawal.** The regulations state that runners who withdraw at an aid station are repatriated to the finish "conforme a reglamento"; the exact procedure belongs to the runner guide, which the official pages did not provide as of 2026-11-08.
- **Communications.** The entry page lists LiveTrail and an SOS system among the runner benefits, but this page has not verified signal coverage along the course and records it as unknown.
