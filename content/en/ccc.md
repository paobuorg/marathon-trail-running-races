## What this course is

CCC is the **100K final of the UTMB World Series**. The organiser describes it as having grown from the "little sister of the UTMB" into one of the most prestigious races in the world. It starts in central Courmayeur, climbs quickly in the first kilometres to about 2,500 m facing Mont Blanc and the Grandes Jorasses, crosses the Grand Col Ferret into Switzerland, passes La Fouly, Champex and Trient, re-enters France at Vallorcine, climbs the Vallons des Chezerys and finishes in central Chamonix.

![Via Roma in central Courmayeur in the morning, with snow-covered Mont Blanc peaks behind the town](/media/routes/ccc/courmayeur-via-roma-2024.jpg)

Photo: Via Roma in central Courmayeur, 2024-06-16, Florian Pépellin, CC BY-SA 4.0. This is a location photograph of the start town, not a CCC race-day photograph; the square, white canopy and pedestrians are not evidence of race layout or field size.

**Series boundary**: CCC is the 100K final. It shares the race week with [UTMB (the 100M final)](/en/routes/utmb/), but the two are ranked separately; OCC is the 50K final, TDS is a series race and PTL does not count toward the series. Their distance, climb, services and cut-offs cannot be transferred to one another.

## Entry requirements and the 2026 lottery

CCC is not first-come, first-served. For the 2026 lottery a runner needed a valid UTMB Index in the 50K, 100K or 100M category plus at least one Running Stone; winning a UTMB World Series race gave direct entry. By comparison, OCC accepts any UTMB Index category and also requires at least one Running Stone. Pre-registration ran from **8 to 19 January 2026** and the lottery was held on **22 January**. Bibs cannot be transferred, deferred to another year or moved to another category.

The 2026 race started in three waves: Top Elites / Elites / CCC1 at **09:00**, CCC2 at **09:15** and CCC3 at **09:30**. The official rules calculate the maximum time and cut-offs from the start of the **last wave**. So 09:30 to 13:30 the next day is **28 hours**, while the first wave at 09:00 gets **28 hours 30 minutes** to the same finish cut-off. The header's 28 hours and the first-wave 28 hours 30 minutes are not contradictory; they use different waves.

What follows directly from the table:

- **The largest single climb is the start to Refuge Bertone**: 13.6 km with +1,436 m, about 106 m per km; it is also one of the steepest long segments.
- **The largest single descent is Arnouvaz to La Fouly**: 12.7 km with −1,042 m. La Fouly to Champex-Lac at −644 m is not the largest descent; reading it that way confuses the segment column.
- **The steepest segment is Martigny to Trient**: 9.9 km with +1,077 m, about 109 m per km, and it arrives after 69 km.
- **The final segment is the longest**: Vallorcine to Chamonix is 17.7 km and still includes +787 / −1,009 m, so it is not a pure descent.
- **At three points the "Slowest" time is later than the cut-off**: Trient (05:18 vs 05:15), Vallorcine (08:53 vs 08:30) and Chamonix (13:31 vs 13:30). The two columns are not the same basis; this page quotes only the cut-off column.

## Risks and escape points

- **Martigny is the lowest point** (507 m); from there it is 9.9 km and +1,077 m to Trient, the main climb of the second half.
- **Plan de l'Au closes at 00:15 on Saturday**, so most runners pass at night; Champex-Lac to Plan de l'Au is only 4.9 km with +74 m, the shortest buffer on the course, and is not a rest section.
- **Vallorcine to Chamonix still includes +787 / −1,009 m**; the final 17.7 km is not a downhill sprint.
- **The organiser does not publish a per-point medical list**; the Services column is empty, so the legend cannot stand in for a per-point configuration. Exact medical and aid locations are marked unknown here.
- **The high sections reach about 2,500 m**, so weather and visibility are the main risks; the official page gives no weather data for the Courmayeur start, and this page states no temperatures for it.
- **Official escape arrangements were not obtained.** This page does not apply Chamonix transport advice to the Courmayeur start and does not assume shuttle services at intermediate towns.

## Getting there

The official transport page covers only the **Chamonix (finish) side**:

- Geneva airport (GVA) is about 1 hour 30 minutes from Chamonix, with direct buses or a train from Geneva Cornavin.
- Lyon Saint-Exupéry (LYS) is about 2 hours 30 minutes away, via TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde and then train or bus.
- Milan Malpensa (MXP) has direct BlaBlaCar or FlixBus services to Chamonix.
- Zurich (ZRH) is reached by train through Switzerland.
- Paris CDG/ORY connects by TGV to Annecy or Bellegarde, then train or bus.
- The organiser discourages driving because central Chamonix car parks are saturated during the event.

**Courmayeur start side**: the official 2026 practical page says shuttles to the start are included in the bib for UTMB, CCC, OCC, TDS, MCC, ETC and YCC but **must be reserved**; in 2026 pre-shuttles ran from Cluses, Sallanches, Le Fayet, Servoz and the Chamonix valley. The exact CCC departure times and boarding points still sit in the runner guide; this page does not invent them.

## Check-in, drop bags and bib

- **Bib collection**: at the CCC start village or in Chamonix; the 2026 booking window ran from **1 July to 10 August**. Bring photo ID.
- **PPS health pathway**: mandatory for adults at €5; from 2026 no medical certificate is required, but the PPS must be completed before collecting the bib.
- **Insurance**: individual accident cover for search and evacuation in France, Italy and Switzerland is mandatory; helicopter evacuation is chargeable and the organiser chooses the means.
- **Drop bag**: CCC allows **one bag**, deposited at the start, collected once at **Champex-Lac** and again at the finish. Only the organiser's bag is carried; no poles and no valuables; collect within two hours of finishing.
- **Bib**: worn on the chest or stomach and fully visible throughout; no transfer, deferral or category change. A DNF bib is deactivated but remains the pass for official shuttles.

## Mandatory kit, semi-autonomy and penalties

CCC is built on **semi-autonomy**: between aid stations, runners must carry enough food, water and safety equipment for the next leg. The organiser publishes a base mandatory list plus additional items that can be activated individually for the weather; activated items are confirmed no later than 24 hours before the start. All mandatory kit must be carried for the whole race and be available for checks; sealed bags that cannot be reopened are forbidden. If poles are used, they must be carried from the start to the finish and cannot go in a drop bag; no disposable cutlery, cups or bowls are provided at aid stations.

**Assistance boundary**: at CCC, personal assistance is allowed only at designated aid stations, by one person, with no equipment other than a bag of at most 30 litres; medical help from a runner's own team is forbidden, and pacing or being accompanied outside marked spectator areas is prohibited. By comparison, OCC allows no personal assistance anywhere.

**Penalties**: missing a critical safety item (water reserve, hooded waterproof jacket, headlamp, survival blanket or phone) is a disqualification for both races; other missing kit is 1 hour at CCC and 30 minutes at OCC; accepting assistance outside authorised zones is 1 hour at CCC and 30 minutes at OCC; pacing is 15 minutes per notification at CCC and 10 minutes at OCC; starting before an assigned wave is 2 hours at CCC and 1 hour at OCC. Time penalties are served on the spot and the race clock keeps running; they are not deducted afterwards.

## Who it suits, and how to run it

CCC's entry standard is a UTMB Index category plus Running Stones, not a 100K finish certificate. The course is 107.8 km with +6,198 m and requires night travel through Plan de l'Au, Martigny and Trient; it suits runners who have already completed 50K–100K mountain races independently and can handle night, cold and long self-supported sections. A first 100K should not treat CCC as a low-risk entry point.

Do not pace off the winner's 10:21:48. The first 13.6 km climb 1,436 m, about 106 m per km, so the start is not a flat warm-up. The cut-offs that matter are Champex-Lac 54.2 km / 23:15, Plan de l'Au 59.1 km / 00:15, Martigny 69.2 km / 02:15, Trient 79.1 km / 05:15 and Vallorcine 90.1 km / 08:30. The 9.9 km / +1,077 m from Martigny to Trient is the hardest gate of the second half; the final 17.7 km still includes +787 / −1,009 m and is not a pure descent. A 30–60 minute margin at each point is more useful than calculating total time.
