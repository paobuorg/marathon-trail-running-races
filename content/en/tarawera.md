## What this race is (distances first)

Tarawera Ultra-Trail by UTMB takes place in Rotorua, in New Zealand's Bay of Plenty. The official site calls Rotorua the birthplace of New Zealand tourism. The TMiler starts at Te Puia and finishes at the Village Green in the city centre.

The card at the top of this page uses the official 163 km and 3,800 m. The body keeps the other figures. The four are not averaged and are not rounded into "about 163 km".

The GPX file name and track name say `2026 Tarawera Ultra-Trail 160km_V5`, three kilometres short of the 2027 card's 163 km. Inside the file the first point is timed 2019-12-18, the metadata is dated 2019-12-18 and the last point is timed 2025-10-09. Three different years cannot be reconciled, so this page cannot say which edition the file belongs to and does not treat it as 2027 geometry.

Horizontal 161.122 km and three-dimensional 162.806 km differ by 1.684 km. That gap comes from elevation: adding each segment's height difference lengthens the path. The official 163 km sits closer to the 3D figure but is still 194 m longer, and this page cannot determine which calculation produced that 194 m.

The gain differs more. The official card says 3,800 m+, the GPX point-by-point sum gives 5,500 m, and a 3 m threshold gives 4,555 m. With 19,757 points, sampling density and threshold together decide the result. The organiser does not publish its algorithm, so this page records the difference as unknown and does not explain it as "the organiser under-reports" or "the file is wrong".

Elevations inside the file run from 138.25 m to 730.4 m, with the first point at 310.2 m and the last at 285.2 m. There is no altitude issue on this course; the gain comes from repeated small rises rather than one long climb.

There are also inconsistencies inside a single page. The timing table puts Okataina at 118.6 km while the pacer hand-over table and the drop-bag table put it at 118.5 km. The timing table puts the finish at 163.0 km while the drop-bag table says 162.7 km. All three are official numbers, printed side by side and not merged.

This is not unique to the TMiler. The T102 page has the same mismatch between its timing table and its drop-bag table: Okataina 57.5 against 57.3, Tikitapu 84.8 against 84.5, finish 101.9 against 101.6. This page records it as a general state of the official site and does not infer course length from it.

Course shape has a conflict too. The homepage says "Courses are all point-to-point", while the TMiler page says it is a loop that "starts and finishes in Rotorua". Both are official, so both are printed and neither is merged. The start and finish are also not the same place: Te Puia at the start, the Village Green at the end, so "loop" means returning to the same city rather than leaving and entering by one gate.

The GPX linked from the 2027 TMiler page does not have coincident endpoints either. The first point is -38.163829, 176.251135 and the last is -38.131939, 176.252814, 3.549 km apart by Haversine distance. The file's edition cannot be determined: the filename says 2026, the metadata timestamp is 2019-12-18, and track points run from 2019-12-18 to 2025-10-09. This page therefore does not use it to settle loop versus point-to-point and records only the conflict. Read as a loop, a reader would assume the start and finish are in one place and could book a single town base; the official text gives Te Puia as the start and the Village Green as the finish.

The cut-off pace has an arithmetic gap as well. The official figure is 13:17 min/km; 36 hours over 163 km is 13.25 min/km, or 13:15. A pace of 13:17 corresponds to 162.6 km, 0.4 km short of the official distance. This page reads cut-offs from the official clock times, not backwards from the pace figure.

## Thirteen aid stations and one water point

The TMiler has 13 aid stations and 1 water point. Of the 14 intermediate stations in the table above, Te Ariki Isthmus lists only water and Näak with no food, while the rest list cold or hot food.

The aid-station food is a single list: lollies, potato chips, sandwiches (jam, honey, chocolate spread, marmite), fruit (bananas, oranges, watermelon), ice, water, Näak nutrition (drink mix, waffles, gels) and soft drinks (cola, ginger beer, mountain dew). The page also says some items may not be available at all aid stations, so the list is not a station-by-station supply table. The organiser does not publish a full per-station matrix, and this page does not invent one that looks complete.

The event is cup-less. Aid stations provide no disposable cups, so runners must carry a cup, soft flask or hydration vest.

Drop bags exist at six points only: Buried Village 29.9 km, Rerewhakaaitu 56.0 km, Outlet 88.8 km, Okataina 118.5 km, Tikitapu 145.7 km and the Village Green finish at 162.7 km. Bags must be dropped at the designated area at Athlete Check-In before 20:00 on Friday; there is a separate race-day start-line bag that is returned to the finish.

## Mandatory gear and entry qualification

The TMiler mandatory list has 13 items: long sleeve thermal top, waterproof and breathable jacket, beanie/balaclava/head sock, long leg thermal pants, full-fingered lightweight thermal gloves, headlamp, spare batteries or a second headlamp or a portable USB charger, mobile phone, water bottle/collapsible cup/hydration vest, emergency survival bag, high visibility safety vest, self-adhesive bandage, and a lightweight dry sack or zip-lock bags.

TMiler runners must carry the full list at all times, with no weather relaxation. For T102, T50 and T21 the weather-dependent items are confirmed at 12 noon on Wednesday 10 February.

The gear descriptions are hard requirements, not "something similar". The waterproof jacket must have fully taped (not critically taped) waterproof seams and a hood; the phone must switch on, hold charge and take a SIM card usable in New Zealand; the survival bag must be a bag rather than a blanket, large enough to climb into and pull tight around the shoulders; the self-adhesive bandage must be at least 40 mm wide and 2 m long unstretched.

The high visibility vest must comply with AS/NZS 4602:1999, 2010 or 2011 Class D/N and must be worn on the Tarawera Road section between the Blue/Green Lake Carpark and Buried Village.

Gear is checked before the race pack is issued. Early checks are available at gear-check stores in New Zealand or Australia until 11 February, or at Athlete Check-In. Failing the check means no race pack; a missing item during the race means you cannot continue until it is replaced, and a time penalty may apply. The official wording is direct: no mandatory gear, no start.

Entry qualification applies to the TMiler only. You must have completed an official 50 km or longer trail race between 3 January 2025 and 3 January 2027 and submitted the result by 3 January 2027; a backyard ultra of 8 yards or more also counts; the Blue Lake 24hr Miler Qualifier requires 10 laps (about 60 km) within 11 hours. Runners who do not qualify are moved into the T102.

The T102 has no pre-requisite event. The organiser only says ultra-trail experience helps with start-group seeding. That is not a claim that the T102 is easier; it is a different entry rule.

The minimum age is 18 on race day. Refunds are 45% before 22 November 2026, 22% before 3 January 2027 and none after. The TMiler deferral requires registering before 16 May 2026, requesting before 3 January 2027, and paying NZ$55 when entering the 2028 event.

## Support crews, pacers and spectators

The official site gives two different assistance radii. The race page says 200 m either side of a spectator aid station; the on-course page says 500 m either side. Both are official, so both are printed and neither is chosen. In practice, plan for the more conservative 200 m.

The pacer registration deadline also has two versions: 10 February 2027 on the race page and 11 February 2027 on the on-course page. Both are printed here, and the earlier date is the safer one to work to.

Pacers may join only from Okataina, which is after 118.5 km. Two pacers may be registered but only one may be on course at a time, and hand-overs must happen at an aid station. T14, T21, T50 and T102 have no pacers.

Pacers must carry their own full mandatory kit, may not carry extra water, food or equipment for the runner, and may not provide material assistance. They arrange their own transport, but must use the event bus to reach Okataina.

Spectators can watch at several points, but may enter only the stations listed above. Okataina is shuttle-only: Lake Okataina Road is closed to event traffic for the duration and the organiser explicitly says not to drive there.

The Puarenga spectator shuttle loops from the Waipa MTB Carpark from 7:15 to 10:30, takes 5 minutes one way and costs NZ$8 return. The Okataina shuttle leaves the Rotoiti Rugby Club every 30 minutes from 09:30 Saturday to 05:00 Sunday, with the last bus leaving Okataina at 06:00, taking 20 minutes one way and costing NZ$20 return.

Tarawera Road closes from 09:45 to about 11:00 for the T21 start. Parking at Buried Village is limited, and the organiser points to the Tarawera Trailhead 500 m down the road.

Supporters may join the final stretch from Redwoods. They must register with event staff there and collect a wristband; only TMiler and T102 supporters may enter the finish chute with their runner.

T21 and T14 runners may not receive material assistance from crew; spectators may cheer only. That is not the same as "spectators may watch".

## Risk and withdrawal

- **Two nights**: a 04:00 start and a 36-hour limit cover all of Saturday night and the small hours of Sunday. A headlamp and spare power are mandatory.
- **Lake crossing**: the official course description states there is a boat ride across Lake Rotomahana. The pages checked for this article publish no crossing timetable, boarding rule or delay procedure, so this page records it as unknown and does not fill the gap from previous editions.
- **Summer rain**: February is southern-hemisphere summer, but Rotorua is wet. Across 24 days (13-14 February, 2015-2026), 10 days reached 1 mm, 7 reached 5 mm, and the largest daily total was 64.8 mm.
- **Mostly runnable terrain**: the organiser describes mainly single track, wide trails, forestry and sealed roads. Runnable is not the same as easy: roots, wet surfaces and night running still count.
- **Low altitude, many small rises**: the GPX runs from 138.25 m to 730.4 m, so altitude is not a factor; the 3,800 m of gain is spread over 163 km, about 23 m per kilometre.
- **Private land**: the entire Tarawera Forest is private and out of bounds outside race day. Pre-race reconnaissance cannot include that section.
- **Limited crew access**: only eight points are open to spectators, and the official assistance radius is given as two different numbers. Crews should work to 200 m and should not hand up outside a spectator station.
- **Absolute cut-offs**: all 13 cut-offs require leaving before the stated time, with no buffer for late arrival.
- **Withdrawal**: the pages checked here publish no per-station transfer vehicle, evacuation time or sweep schedule, so this page records it as unknown.
- **Weather**: the actual weather on 13-14 February 2027 was unknown at the check date; check the forecast again before travelling.

## Getting there

## Difficulty

The hard numbers are 163 km, 3,800 m of gain and 36 hours. On the official distance and limit, the minimum average speed is 4.53 km/h and the minimum average pace is 13:15 min/km.

Gain density is about 23 m per kilometre, lower than a typical Alpine 100-mile, but the course is longer and more runnable. The organiser describes it as designed for achievability rather than impossibility, not as a technical mountain course.

Altitude is not a variable. The GPX tops out at 730 m and bottoms at 138 m, which puts it in a different class from high-altitude races. The real variables are night, rain, wet roots and the lake crossing.

Against the other races at the same event: T102 is 102 km with 2,300 m and 30 hours, point to point from Kawerau; T50 is 52 km with 1,150 m; T21 is 23 km with 400 m; T14 is 14 km with 161 m. None of these distances or gains can be substituted for another.

The pages checked for this article do not publish ITRA points, a UTMB Index threshold or a finish rate. This page records them as unknown rather than estimating a difficulty grade.

## How to spend 36 hours: target pace and cut-off pressure

Thirty-six hours is not a slogan; it is a hard average. 163 km divided by 36 hours is 4.53 km/h, or 13:15 min/km. That average includes aid stops, drop bags, clothing changes, queues and going off course. The official page also prints 13:17 min/km, two seconds per kilometre slower than 36 hours over 163 km; this page uses 13:15 as the arithmetic baseline and keeps the official 13:17 on the record.

The steady-state baselines for different finish times are below. They are arithmetic, not a training plan, and they ignore the distribution of climb, night slowdowns and weather.

| Target finish | Required average pace | Required average speed |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30 h | 11:02 min/km | 5.43 km/h |
| 32 h | 11:47 min/km | 5.09 km/h |
| 34 h | 12:31 min/km | 4.79 km/h |
| 36 h | 13:15 min/km | 4.53 km/h |

The cut-off table gives leave-by limits, not paces. Dividing the distance between consecutive cut-offs by the time between them produces 12 implied average speeds. Most intervals sit between 4.47 and 4.71 km/h, close to the 36-hour 4.53. There are two exceptions: Okataina to Millar at 3.83 km/h, and Millar to Tikitapu at 6.00 km/h.

Okataina to Millar is 15.3 km in 4 hours, an implied 3.83 km/h. Millar to Tikitapu is 12.0 km in 2 hours, an implied 6.00 km/h. Together they cover 27.3 km in 6 hours, an average of 4.55 km/h, almost exactly the 36-hour overall 4.53. The problem is not the average but the split: the first interval is about 16% slower than the overall figure and the second about 32% faster. Reading 6.00 as permission to speed up would be a mistake, because it follows a four-hour segment.

Okataina to Millar at 3.83 km/h is red and Millar to Tikitapu at 6.00 km/h is amber. It is not a target-pace table and not a course profile; cut-offs are administrative limits, and the tight-loose pattern comes from the published clock times. The organiser does not explain the pattern.

## Training conversion

The TMiler is a runnable 163 km, 3,800 m+ forest ultra with limited elevation range; preparation centres on long-distance durability, night sections and wet forest trail, not high-altitude mountaineering. The mapping below uses only verified facts and gives no individual training prescription.

| Verified fact | Training direction | Site reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 163 km, 3,800 m+, 36 h, minimum 4.53 km/h | Walk-run durability and progressive weekly volume | [Ultra-running basics](/en/knowledge/ultra-running-basics/), [Training volume and progression](/en/knowledge/training-volume-and-progression/) |
| Native bush, redwoods, lakeside trail, slippery roots | Forest-trail rhythm and downhill landing control | [Mountain path basics](/en/knowledge/hill-and-mountain-path-basics/), [Downhill running technique](/en/knowledge/downhill-running-technique/) |
| Overnight section, night forest trails | Night-run practice and lighting habits | [Night running and conditions](/en/knowledge/night-running-and-traffic/) |
| 13 aid stations plus 1 water point, 6 drop-bag points | Rehearse fuelling and drop-bag lists on training outings | [Long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), [Crew and pacer planning](/en/knowledge/crew-and-pacer-planning/) |
| Qualification: a 50 km+ trail race completed 3 Jan 2025 – 3 Jan 2027 | Submit proof before the deadline | This site does not replace race qualification review |

Decision branches:
