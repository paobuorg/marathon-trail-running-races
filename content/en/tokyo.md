## What this course is

The Tokyo Marathon is a point-to-point road course through central Tokyo. It starts at the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building in Shinjuku and finishes in front of Tokyo Station on Gyoko-dori, by the Wadakura-Gate intersection. The official 2026 race outline gives the order as Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building → Suidobashi → Ueno-hirokoji → Kanda → Nihombashi → Asakusa Kaminarimon Gate → Ryogoku → Monzen-nakacho → Ginza → Tamachi → Hibiya → Tokyo Station/Gyoko-dori Avenue.

This is not a loop in a park. It links Shinjuku, Akihabara, Asakusa, Ryogoku, Ginza, Tamachi and Marunouchi in one continuous city route. There are three turnarounds — Ueno-hirokoji, Tomioka Hachimangu and Tamachi — which let the course pass more landmarks but also mean that the same road is used by runners moving in different directions at different paces. The start and finish are separate, so race-week logistics, baggage and the journey home all need point-to-point planning.

The official 2026 distance is 42.195 km. The course is certified by the Japan Association of Athletics Federations (JAAF) and recognised by World Athletics and AIMS. The 2026 race outline also states that the edition held World Athletics Elite Platinum Label status. The 2026 field was 39,000: 38,500 in the marathon and 500 in the 10.7 km race. The results page records 38,472 marathon starters and 37,268 marathon finishers, a 96.9% finish rate.

A 10.7 km race is held on the same day, from the same start, finishing at Nihombashi. The organiser states that records for that race are not certified. The first 10.7 km therefore carries two events on shared roads. Corrals separate the fields at the start, but the early kilometres are still busier than the marathon field alone would suggest. Among 2026 marathon finishers, 2,070 (5.6%) broke three hours, 9,951 (26.7%) broke four hours, 11,389 (30.6%) broke five hours, 8,858 (23.8%) broke six hours and 4,967 (13.3%) finished inside seven hours. That distribution describes a quick course, but also a large group finishing near the limit.

Segment notes. All times come from the 2026 official program guidebook p.24 and are measured from the 09:10 gun:

- **Segment 1 (0 → 4.9 km).** The route leaves the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building and heads towards Ichigaya. The official profile shows a net descent through this opening, which makes it easy to start too fast. The 4.9 km cut-off is at 10:25, or 75 minutes, which averages about 15:18/km. That is the loosest checkpoint of the race, but the start corrals can absorb a surprising amount of that margin.
- **Segment 2 (4.9 → 9.2 km).** Cut-off 11:10, 45 minutes for 4.30 km, about 10:28/km. The route enters the Kanda and Akihabara area, with many junctions and road markings; crowd speed matters more than the nominal pace.
- **Segment 3 (9.2 → 15.4 km).** Cut-off 11:55, 45 minutes for 6.20 km, about 7:15/km. This is one of the tightest segments when the official cut-off times are converted to pace. A very conservative first 9 km can leave too little room here.
- **Segment 4 (15.4 → 20.9 km).** Cut-off 12:45, 50 minutes for 5.50 km, about 9:05/km. This passes the Asakusa Kaminarimon and Ryogoku side of the course. It is the densest landmark section and the easiest place for photographs and crowds to disturb rhythm.
- **Segment 5 (20.9 → 24.7 km).** Cut-off 13:20, 35 minutes for 3.80 km, about 9:13/km. Just after halfway, the route turns around at Tomioka Hachimangu and changes direction several times.
- **Segment 6 (24.7 → 29.2 km).** Cut-off 14:00, 40 minutes for 4.50 km, about 8:53/km. After the turnaround the course heads back towards Asakusabashi; this is where the route starts to feel repetitive.
- **Segment 7 (29.2 → 33.5 km).** Cut-off 14:35, 35 minutes for 4.30 km, about 8:08/km. Approaching Ginza, this is one of the tightest segments by cut-off arithmetic. Fatigue is accumulating and the margin should already be banked.
- **Segment 8 (33.5 → 38.5 km).** Cut-off 15:15, 40 minutes for 5.00 km, about 8:00/km. Ginza, Hibiya and the Tamachi turnaround all sit in this section. The road is narrow in places and spectator pressure reduces usable width.
- **Segment 9 (38.5 → 42.195 km).** Cut-off 16:10, 55 minutes for 3.695 km, about 14:53/km. The final run back to Tokyo Station is generous by comparison, but it is still a cut-off, not a guarantee; the earlier margin should not be spent twice.

These minute-per-kilometre figures are arithmetic derived from official cut-off times divided by official kilometre differences. They are not official pacing advice and not measured on the course. The organiser publishes no segment ascent, segment surface or segment field-size data.

## Surface composition

The organiser publishes no surface split and does not divide the course into percentages of asphalt, concrete, stone or gravel. What can be confirmed is that this is a city road course: the route uses ordinary streets in central Tokyo, crosses several bridges and junctions, and finishes on Gyoko-dori in front of Tokyo Station. Any "asphalt percentage" or "stone percentage" without an official source is left out.

The practical surface issues are not trail-style technical difficulty. They are:

- **Bridges and expansion joints.** The course crosses multiple bridges, including the Suidobashi and Ryogoku stretches, and bridge joints, metal covers and road markings become more slippery when wet. The organiser gives no count of joints and no anti-slip details.
- **Junctions and turnarounds.** The three turnarounds require braking, turning and re-accelerating. Around Ginza, Hibiya and Tamachi the road narrows and spectators or barriers reduce usable width.
- **Elevation.** The official profile shows a start at about 40 m, a descent to near 0 m before 5 km, then small undulations at low altitude. That is modest for a road marathon, but the organiser gives no cumulative ascent, so it cannot be converted into a precise climb category.

This page therefore describes the surface and terrain as "city road, low altitude, low technical difficulty". That is a descriptive judgement based on the official route and elevation profile, not an official grading.

## Risks and escape points

- **Uneven cut-off rhythm.** The nine checkpoints run from 10:25 to 16:10. Converted to pace, segment 3 (9.2 → 15.4 km) is about 7:15/km, segment 7 (29.2 → 33.5 km) about 8:08/km and segment 8 (33.5 → 38.5 km) about 8:00/km — clearly tighter than the overall average. Reading only "seven-hour limit" can lead to being cut off in the second half.
- **Heat and humidity.** The 2026 race started at 09:10. Early March in Tokyo can still be cold, but it can also produce a daily maximum above 20°C. The weather window in this page reaches a modelled 22.8°C maximum, but that is a grid value, not a course measurement. The start is usually cooler than the daily maximum; exposed sections in the afternoon can feel much warmer.
- **Cold rain.** The same window has a daily minimum of 2.2°C and a single day with 49.5 mm of precipitation. Wet cold magnifies the slipperiness of bridge joints and road markings, and can chill runners who wait a long time in the start area.
- **Crowds.** 38,472 marathon runners started in 2026. The start area and first 5 km directly affect whether a runner can pass the 4.9 km and 9.2 km checkpoints on plan. Advance boards warn of aid stations 150–300 m ahead, but queues still form at peak times.
- **Medical and withdrawal.** The organiser places 13 medical stations on the course, plus medical cover at the start, finish and 10.7 km finish. To withdraw, the official instruction is to notify a race official, remove the barcode from the bib and hand it to the official, then proceed to the nearest checkpoint.
- **Sweep bus.** Runners who are cut off or who withdraw are taken by sweep bus to the finish area, or may return home from the nearest station. If baggage was checked in, the official instruction is that the runner must board the sweep bus to recover it at the finish; going straight to a station is not an option.
- **Off-course risk.** The official traffic page states that roads are closed for long periods, and that pedestrians, bicycles and e-scooters may not cross the course; use footbridges or subway entrances instead. Do not cross closure lines to take a shortcut. **Verification status:** when re-checked on 7 November 2026 the traffic page redirected to the event homepage and is no longer accessible; the details above are as recorded on 6 November 2026.
- **Rule boundaries.** Personal drinks stations are for elite athletes only. The organiser accepts no responsibility for water or food taken outside official aid stations. Rubbish belongs in the bins at aid stations. Treating these as suggestions rather than rules can lead to being stopped by staff or leaving waste on the course.
- **Two events on the early course.** The 10.7 km race starts from the same point and finishes at Nihombashi. Corrals separate the fields, but the two flows can still interact before that finish; the first 10.7 km should not be assumed to be clear for goal pace.

## Getting there

The access information below comes from the organiser and transport operators. All times are operator estimates, not measurements made by this page.

- **Nearest station to the start.** Tochomae Station on the Toei Oedo Line, Exit A4, about one minute on foot to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building start area (GO TOKYO official tourism page). This is the nearest station named by the official tourism page, not a guarantee that the security entrance is beside the exit; a 39,000-runner field needs time for entry queues.
- **Finish.** Tokyo Station/Gyoko-dori Avenue. Tokyo Station is served by JR conventional lines and Shinkansen, and by the Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line; the Tozai Line serves Otemachi, a short walk away (Tokyo Station City official access page).
- **Narita Airport → Tokyo Station.** About 60 minutes by Narita Express, about 90 minutes by Express Airport Narita, or about 80–110 minutes by airport limousine bus to the Tokyo/Nihombashi area (Tokyo Station City official page).
- **Haneda Airport → Tokyo Station.** Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho, then about 35 minutes by Keihin-Tohoku or Yamanote Line; about 40 minutes by limousine bus to Tokyo Station Yaesu South Exit (same official page).
- **Packet pick-up.** Tokyo Big Sight South Exhibition Halls, 26–28 February 2026. The organiser does not allow race-day packet pick-up.
- **Race-day city transport.** The official traffic page advises against driving or cycling; the course and nearby roads are closed for long periods. Pedestrians must use footbridges or subway entrances to cross. The official site also has a separate page for shuttle buses serving the Akihabara and Ueno areas, but the timetable was not available in the pages retrieved here.
- **Driving.** The organiser explicitly discourages it. Some Metropolitan Expressway exits are regulated, detours must follow police and staff instructions, and a car parked inside the closure area may not be able to leave on race day.
- **After the finish.** The finish is in front of Tokyo Station, which is the nearest large rail hub; baggage retrieval, changing and crowd dispersal still take time. Leave at least an hour of buffer before a train or flight. That is a planning judgement based on the separate start and finish and a 37,268-finisher field, not an official guarantee.

## Early-March season and weather

The 2026 race started at 09:10 on 1 March. To describe the same time window, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for the Tokyo grid point at 35.6766N / 139.6911E (timezone Asia/Tokyo) from 2026-02-22 to 2026-03-02, nine days:

- Daily maximum: 12.1°C to 22.8°C, median 16.7°C.
- Daily minimum: 2.2°C to 11.8°C, median 7.8°C.
- One of the nine days had precipitation ≥1 mm and one had ≥5 mm; the largest daily total was 49.5 mm on 2026-02-25.
- On 2026-03-01 itself: maximum 16.7°C, minimum 6.9°C, precipitation 0.0 mm.

These four lines are **modelled historical values for a Tokyo grid point, not course measurements and not a race-day forecast**. A grid point cannot represent the different microclimates of Shinjuku, the Asakusa waterfront, Ginza and Tokyo Station, and the data includes no humidity, wind, sunshine or apparent temperature. A 09:10 start is usually close to the daily minimum-to-median range, but the daily maximum normally arrives in the afternoon, so the second half can still warm up.

The practical message is not to treat early March in Tokyo as one uniformly cool marathon. Within the same week the archive contains a 2°C morning and a 22.8°C afternoon; rain gear, a warm layer, gloves and sun protection should be packed as an adjustable combination. The official aid-station notice also asks runners to take water from the rear tables and to put paper cups in the designated bins.

## Difficulty

The organiser publishes no single difficulty grade, and this page does not invent an official one. On the verifiable dimensions, this is a low-altitude, low-technical-difficulty city road marathon:

- Distance 42.195 km, agreed by all four official documents.
- The official profile shows a start at about 40 m, a drop to near 0 m before 5 km, then small undulations at low altitude; cumulative ascent and descent are not published.
- Official limit seven hours, with nine checkpoints from 4.9 km to the finish.
- 38,472 marathon starters in 2026, with crowding at the start and in the first 5 km.
- Three turnarounds and multiple bridges and junctions, on a city-road surface.
- A wide early-March weather window, with both cold rain and warming possible.

The difficulty therefore sits in cut-off rhythm, city crowds, weather variation and point-to-point transport rather than in climbing or technical terrain. For a runner whose goal is to finish, the priorities are not to be dragged too fast in the first 5 km and to keep margin before the tighter checkpoints at 9.2, 15.4, 29.2 and 33.5 km. For a runner chasing a time, the three turnarounds and the usable width through Ginza will affect pace continuity more than the elevation profile. That judgement follows from the official numbers and route description, not from personal experience.

It is also not a course to prepare for with trail-race logic. There is no mandatory kit list, no technical section and no altitude acclimatisation requirement; road pacing, fuelling rhythm and heat adaptation are the relevant work. Treating a trail pack, poles or trail shoes as essential adds burden without evidence. Use the site's pace calculator for target-pace arithmetic and the marathon-distance entry for the training context.
