## What this race is (disambiguate first)

HOKA Ultra-Trail Australia by UTMB takes place in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, with the event hub and start/finish at Scenic World in Katoomba, operated by USM Events Pty Ltd. UTA100 is the 100 km race; the official description says it runs along cliff tops, through lush valleys and across scenic ridges before returning to Scenic World.

UTA belongs to the UTMB World Series. The UTA100 page defines the race as a "UTMB World Series 100K Event" and states that finishers receive 6 running stones; the Finals Access control on the same page is shown as SOLD OUT. This page records those two official fields only and does not explain Finals qualification, because the 2027 page does not.

The UTMB World Series page names HOKA Ultra-Trail Australia by UTMB as the 2027 Oceania Major, while the UTA100 race page calls the course a "UTMB World Series 100K Event". The first is an event tier and the second a race distance, so they sit on different levels, and the "We are major" line is only a site slogan. This page therefore writes that UTA is the 2027 Oceania Major and that UTA100 is its 100 km race, rather than excluding UTA from the Majors.

The official 2027 figures for all six races are below. The table exists only to establish which race is UTA100; this page does not mix any other race's distance or climb into UTA100's splits and cut-offs.

The cut-offs need explaining. UTA100 starts in waves between 05:00 and 08:30, and the cut-offs are absolute clock times, not "so many hours after your own start". The official final limit is 28 hours after the last starter crosses the mat, estimated at 12:30pm Sunday. A runner starting at 05:00 therefore has more than 28 hours available, while an 08:30 starter has exactly 28.

For the same reason, the 12:30PM cut-off at Tarros is 4 hours after an 08:30 start but 7.5 hours after a 05:00 start. Reading the chart as an individual time limit per split will give the wrong answer.

The Six Foot Track Water Point and the Emergency Aid Station have no cut-off. The first has water only and the second has limited food; neither is a full aid station.

The time chart footnote says the charts show estimated fastest and slowest splits and are a guide only, that cut-off times supersede the slowest estimate, and that cut-offs may be brought forward in bad weather. The table above therefore cannot be read as "if I reach the point before the cut-off I am safe".

The official text says UTA100 has 7 checkpoints, while the table has 10 rows. The difference is a counting convention: the Water Point and Emergency Aid Station do not count as checkpoints, and neither does the finish. This page prints all 10 rows and does not force 7 and 10 into one number.

## Mandatory gear, supplies and rules

UTA100 mandatory gear has three layers. The first applies to all five races: long-sleeve thermal top, waterproof and breathable jacket, beanie or buff, mobile phone, A5 emergency instruction card, whistle, space blanket or light bivvy, a compression bandage at least 7.5 cm wide by 2.3 m long, 2 litres of water capacity, two food portions per leg, a rubbish ziplock bag, race number, and a timing tag for the backpack.

The second layer applies to UTAMiler, UTA100 and UTA50: a high-visibility safety vest complying with AS/NZS 4602 and worn between 20:00 and 06:00, a headlamp, a small backup light, and a dry sack or sealed bags.

The third layer applies only to UTAMiler and UTA100: long thermal pants, full-fingered lightweight thermal gloves, and, weather permitting, long waterproof pants and a 100-weight long-sleeve fleece.

For UTA22 and UTA11 the thermal top and waterproof jacket are weather-dependent, and neither race needs a headlamp or a high-visibility vest. The four gear lists cannot be swapped between races.

The weather-dependent rulings have specific announcement times: UTA11 at 10:00 on 13 May, UTA22 and UTAMiler at 09:00 on 14 May, and UTA100 at 10:00 on 15 May. The UTA100 line says "Friday 15 May", but 15 May 2027 is a Saturday; this page keeps the published time and flags the weekday mismatch rather than correcting the official text.

UTA100 and UTAMiler runners must pass a mandatory gear check before collecting the race pack. There are two routes: an early check at a partner store until 12 May, or a check at race check-in. The official wording is "No mandatory gear = No start"; random checks happen during the race, and a missing item must be replaced and may bring a time penalty.

The waterproof jacket must have fully taped, not critically taped, seams and a hood. The phone must be charged and have a working SIM. These are inspection items, not advice.

Aid stations are cupless. Runners must carry a cup, soft flask or bladder, and the official page recommends carrying water between checkpoints as well.

The checkpoint food list is a mixture: Näak drink, gels, waffles, water, hot water, tea, coffee, soup, soft drink, lollies, fruit and bakery goods. The official text says not every item is available at every checkpoint and that exact stocks are in the Runner Guide. This page therefore does not say "this station always has hot food".

The first timing point, Narrow Neck (CP1), has no portable toilet. The start, every checkpoint except CP1 and the Emergency Aid Station have portable toilets, while Katoomba Aquatic Centre, Fairmont Resort and the finish have flushing toilets. Over 28 hours that is not a minor detail.

Drop bags: one bag per runner at each drop bag location, closable, soft and reasonably sized, with no plastic bags, plastic boxes or glass. Checkpoint drop bags go to the checkpoint trucks at race check-in on Friday between 10:00 and 19:00; finish-line drop bags are handed in at the KCC area on Friday only, also 10:00-19:00.

Drop bags are returned to the Scenic World marquee within four hours after the relevant cut-off. The page says bags must be collected by 2:00pm on Sunday 18 May, but the 2027 race week ends on 16 May; this page records the conflict and does not change the official date. Uncollected bags are donated or disposed of after 10:00 on Monday.

Course markings are white-backed red directional arrows, dark blue crosses on white and pink flagging tape, with reflective tape and arrows on night sections, markings at least every 500 m and at every junction, a cross for the wrong direction, and a distance sign every 5 km.

Outside supported checkpoints, spectators may offer moral support only; handing over material or physical assistance is not allowed, and crossing private land is explicitly prohibited. Anyone bringing a support crew should read the UTA100 Support Crew section first.

Bibs may not be passed to another person: the official text says that under no circumstances may a bib be given to someone else. Withdrawals and refunds follow the athlete withdrawal policy, with no refund after the final deadline.

Trekking poles: none of the six 2027 pages checked here (entry, UTA100, schedule, mandatory gear, on-course, transport) states a pole rule. This page therefore marks it unknown and does not say "allowed" or "not allowed"; check the Runner Guide and the pre-race briefing.

Medical certificate: none of the pages checked here requires one, and only UTAMiler needs proof of a 50 km or longer finish. That does not mean there is no health declaration; this page does not turn "no medical certificate" into "no health requirement".

## Risks and escape routes

- **Cold nights**: across the 48 days of 13-16 May from 2015 to 2026, the median daily minimum at Scenic World is 3.9 C, with a low of -3.4 C; near the high point the median is 4.15 C with a low of -2.7 C. A 28-hour race includes at least one full night.
- **Cool and wet by day**: the median daily maximum over the same 48 days is only 14.75 C at the start area and 13.9 C at the high point, and rain makes it feel colder. The thermal layers are mandatory gear, not advice.
- **Technical terrain**: the official description includes narrow walking tracks, very steep staircases and ladders, creek crossings, rock and rough ground. This is much slower than fire trail of the same length.
- **First aid**: the official page states that first aid is available at every checkpoint, the Emergency Aid Station and the finish, with roving first aid crews on course. Per-point evacuation capability is not published; see the retreat note below.
- **Cut-offs are absolute**: later starters have less time. Treating the chart as a personal split limit underestimates the risk, and cut-offs may move earlier in bad weather.
- **Cupless**: without a container there is no way to drink at aid stations, and this cannot be fixed on the day.
- **Drop bag date conflict**: the 18 May collection date does not match the 2027 race week; confirm with the Runner Guide or event app before planning the trip home.
- **Withdrawal**: the official pages describe first aid points, roving first aid crews and following official instructions, but publish no per-checkpoint evacuation transport or timing; this page marks that unknown.
- **Weather**: the actual weather for 13-16 May 2027 is unknown as of the check date; look at the forecast again before the race.

## Getting there

The official "How to get there" page says the easiest access from international ports to the Blue Mountains is to fly into Sydney International/Domestic Airport. The same page tells international visitors to check whether they need a visa before booking.

Regional public transport is provided by Transport for NSW, accepts Opal or cashless payment, and information is available on 131500; the transport page lists special event timetables 686 and 686G. This page has not checked individual departures and does not claim a specific service time.

Free runner shuttles run Thursday to Sunday: the Katoomba town centre loop to Scenic World takes about 10-15 minutes, and the Leura service via Fairmont Resort to Scenic World takes about 20-25 minutes. Katoomba is a loop and Leura runs to a timetable. The Blackheath/Medlow check-in and start shuttle times are "to be confirmed later".

New for 2027, spectators using official UTA buses must buy a Spectator Bus Pass. It gives unlimited travel from Thursday to Sunday to the expo, eligible start lines excluding UTA22, finish lines and named UTAMiler and UTA100 checkpoints. Runner shuttles are included in the entry fee.

Parking is not settled. The official text says a decision on KCC Oval parking will be made closer to the event depending on ground and weather, that wet weather has prevented its use in recent years, and that capacity is extremely limited. Do not assume KCC Oval parking as the default plan.

The event pages give no rail or bus connection, timetable or travel time from Sydney Airport to Katoomba. This page marks that unknown and keeps only the one official recommendation: fly into Sydney.

## Difficulty

UTA100's hard numbers are 101 km, 4,400 m of climb, 28 hours and a required cut-off pace of 3.84 km/h. This is a long mountain trail race, not a road ultra.

The official buckle standards are gold for the winning male and female, silver for male sub-14 hours and female sub-16 hours, bronze for male 14-20 hours and female 16-22 hours, with a finisher medal after those times. The official text says buckles are awarded based on start group times, not net time.

Prize money and placings use net time: the top 5 male and female overall receive prize money, the 10 fastest receive a trophy, the 5 fastest receive free entry the following year, first in each age division receives a trophy, and automatic UTMB Finals qualification is also on net time. The buckle and the prize use two different time bases; that is what the official page says, and this page shows both without choosing.

UTA100 is also a qualifier for the 2028 Western States 100: finishing the 2027 race in 19 hours or less puts a runner into the 2028 Western States lottery. The official text also says Western States reserves the right to determine qualifiers and change the criteria.

The final climb is 951 Furber Steps. The official description puts them after about 99 km, between the runner and the finish at Scenic World; this is the most commonly underestimated part of the course.

Against the other races: UTAMiler is 161.1 km with 7,200 m and 44 h 30 min, UTA100 is 101 km with 4,400 m and 28 h, and UTA50 is 51 km with 2,400 m and 14 h 30 min. Their distances and climbs cannot be swapped, and UTA100 should not be rounded to "100 km even".
