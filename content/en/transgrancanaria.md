## What this race is (distance disambiguation first)

The North Face Transgrancanaria takes place on Gran Canaria in Spain's Canary Islands and is organised by Arista Eventos S.L.U. The Classic is its flagship long-distance race, running from Las Palmas de Gran Canaria in the north-east, diagonally across the central mountains, to Maspalomas in the south.

The official Classic description places the race in the World Trail Majors circuit, not the UTMB World Series. Both the home-page card and the Classic page say so: the Classic is "part of the international World Trail Majors circuit" and the Marathon belongs to "The Short Series by Gran Canaria World Trail Majors". None of the four pages checked here (home, Classic, schedule, regulations) mentions the UTMB World Series.

The Classic exists in three sets of numbers. This page shows them side by side and does not choose one on the organisers' behalf:

The card at the top of this page uses the official 126 km and 6,764 m of gain; the body also keeps the 125 km card figure and the GPX's 126.092 km. None of the three is averaged.

The 125 vs 126 difference needs its own explanation. The home-page card carries a 2026 date (6 March, 23:59) and the Classic page carries a 2027 date (26 February, 23:59), so the two cards belong to two editions. But the file labelled 2026 measures 126.092 km, which does not match 125 km, so it cannot be asserted that "2026 was 125 km, 2027 is 126 km, and the course grew by one kilometre". The likelier explanation is that the home-page card rounds 126 down to 125. This page does not record it as an edition change and does not put it in the change log.

Gain also comes in three versions. The official race card gives 6,764 m and the same page's text says "nearly 7,000 metres"; those are two phrasings on one page. Summing every GPX point gives 7,568 m, and applying a 3 m threshold to suppress GPS jitter gives 6,552 m. This page uses the official 6,764 m and explains the two file-based calculations instead of presenting 7,568 m as official.

Loss is published only by the GPX: 7,539 m raw and 6,523 m with the 3 m threshold. No official page publishes a descent total, and the organisers do not write descent as "about equal to the gain". This page does not convert it.

The high point is also published only by the GPX: 1,731 m at about 71.9 km, between Artenara and Tejeda. No official page gives a high point. The low point is 0 m, at the sea-level start and finish.

How to read the cut-off column: the official table gives only a "Cut-off time" column and does not say whether it means arrival or departure; this page takes the conservative reading that a runner must leave the station by that time, subject to the regulations and the pre-race briefing. The first-runner time is the official predicted arrival of the leading runner, not a cut-off. The two must not be mixed.

The elevation column needs a footnote. The official technical-specification table gives altitudes for only two aid stations: CP1 Tenoya at 163 m and CP2 Arucas at 223 m. CP2 matches the GPX exactly at 19.3 km; CP1 does not, because the GPX reads 87 m at 10.7 km and only reaches 158–159 m at 11.3–11.5 km, which suggests the aid-station marker and the kilometre marker are a few hundred metres apart. The elevation column here is therefore terrain reference only, not a precise checkpoint spot height.

The shape of the course can be summarised in three parts. The first runs from sea level up through the low hills at Tenoya and Arucas to Teror and Fontanales. The second enters the central high country through El Hornillo, Artenara and Tejeda to El Garañón; El Garañón is the highest aid station at 1,679 m, while the GPX high point of 1,731 m sits between Artenara and Tejeda. The third descends south from El Garañón through Tunte and Ayagaures to the coast at Maspalomas, with the last 14 km almost entirely downhill.

Two points carry special rules. Tenoya is a liquid-only station, with water, cola and salts but no solid food. Tenoya and El Hornillo are both closed to spectators and assistance teams, so meeting a friend at those two points is not possible.

The organisers publish neither segment-by-segment gain and loss nor the specific food at each station. This page therefore does not invent "this segment climbs X metres" or "this station serves hot soup". Readers who need segment figures should use the official elevation profile and the GPX, remembering that the GPX is a 2026 file.

## Official supplies, assistance, bags and equipment

Outside assistance: the Classic allows it at Arucas (Barreto), Teror, Fontanales (Moya), Artenara, Tejeda, El Garañón and Tunte, and forbids it at Tenoya, El Hornillo and Ayagaures. The regulations tighten this further: assistance is allowed only within 100 m before and after an aid station, never inside it, and accompanying a runner counts as outside assistance whether or not the companion is racing.

Life bags: the Classic has two life-bag points, Fontanales and El Garañón. Runners hand in the items at bib collection, packed into one of the organisers' 35 × 50 cm bags and clearly separated and labelled. Uncollected items are returned to Expomeloneras and handed out only on Sunday from 09:00 to 14:00; they are not posted after the race.

Mandatory equipment: the official list includes a cold kit (hot gloves, windproof trousers, thermal T-shirt and long thermal pants), a 100 × 200 cm emergency blanket, a phone that works in Spain, 1.5 litres of water capacity, a headlamp of at least 200 lumens with spare batteries, a red tail light, a breathable waterproof jacket, a cap, ID and at least 50 euros in cash. The organisers decide two days before the race whether to keep the cold kit, and may drop it wholly or partly.

Medical certificate: the Classic, Advanced and Stages require one. The Classic page gives an upload deadline of 14 January 2027; Article 5.3 of the regulations gives 21 January 2027. Both dates are official and one week apart, so this page shows both rather than picking one. A registration without a valid certificate is not complete.

Aid stations provide no cups, as Article 10 states explicitly; every runner must carry an approved container.

Poles: Article 8.3 bans them for the first 2 km of every race except KV El Gigante, where they are allowed from the point the course enters the trail. Runners must carry them for the whole race in a way that cannot injure others.

Withdrawals: they must be made at an aid station with the bib handed in; if injury prevents movement, the runner calls the emergency number printed on the bib. After an aid-station cut-off closes, the organisers may help move a withdrawn runner to the finish area. Whether each individual point has an independent escape route is not published point by point, so this page marks it unknown.

## Risks and escape points

- **Day–night temperature gap**: the start and finish are at sea level, where the February median daily maximum is 19–21 °C; the grid point near the high point has a median daily maximum of 12.3 °C and a median daily minimum of 3.6 °C, with a historical low of −1.9 °C. The summit and the coast do not share one clothing plan.
- **Night**: a 23:59 start with a 30-hour limit means every runner spends at least one full night out; a headlamp and spare batteries are mandatory.
- **Long descent**: from El Garañón to Maspalomas the course keeps descending, with the last 14 km almost entirely downhill; downhill ability decides more of the second half than climbing does.
- **Assistance limits**: only seven points allow outside assistance, and only within 100 m either side of the station; Tenoya, El Hornillo and Ayagaures allow none, so "a friend waiting by the road" cannot be the plan.
- **No cups**: aid stations do not provide cups, so a runner without a container cannot drink.
- **Cut-off reading**: the cut-off is a leaving time, not an arrival time; lingering inside a station can still eliminate a runner.
- **Escape**: the organisers state only that withdrawals happen at aid stations and that help to the finish area may be given after a station closes; per-point transport and escape capability is not published, so this page marks it unknown.
- **Weather**: February on Gran Canaria can be wet; the summit grid point had 9 days at or above 5 mm in 84, with a maximum of 76.3 mm. Mountain fog and wind have no official statistics and are marked unknown.

## Getting there

Gran Canaria has one commercial airport, listed by Aena under the code LPA (Gran Canaria). The airport page states that the terminal is closed to the public at night, that only boarding-pass holders and staff may enter during that time, and that passengers may enter two hours before departure; this matters if a red-eye arrival is followed directly by bib collection.

The start is in the north-east at Las Canteras Beach, Saulo Torón Square, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria; the finish is in the south at Parque Sur, Maspalomas. The two are not on the same side of the island, and travel between them has to be arranged independently.

Bib collection and the Expo are at Expomeloneras in Maspalomas. The schedule gives the first block on 22 February, 16:00–19:00, to KV, Half, Open Agáldar, Stages and Promo only, not the Classic; from Tuesday 23 February the Classic can collect during the daily 10:00–14:00 and 16:00–19:00 blocks, with the Friday 26 February afternoon block ending at 18:00. Exact hours follow the schedule page.

The official 2027 pages publish no shuttle, transfer or parking plan, and the navigation has no transport or travel page. How to get from the start to the finish, how to recover a car afterwards, and how to return from Maspalomas to Las Palmas are therefore all marked unknown here; check the official schedule and the entry confirmation before travelling.

The airport page lists bus and taxi among ground-transport options, but it gives no route or timetable to the start or finish. This page does not print unverified route numbers or departure times.

## Difficulty

The official Classic page gives "Points 5" without naming the points system. This page does not explain what 5 points means for difficulty, and does not convert it into a "runnability" percentage.

What is certain: 126 km, 6,764 m of gain, 30 hours, ten aid stations and eleven cut-offs (ten stations plus the finish). This is a long mountain trail race, not a road ultra.

Against the other formats at the same event: the Classic is the only single race over 100 km; Advanced is 82 km and Marathon is 47 km. Their distances and gains cannot be swapped, and the Classic's 126 km cannot be written as "120 km" or "130 km" for convenience.

## Training transfer

The Classic is a 126 km, 6,764 m+, 30-hour World Trail Majors mountain race. Its demands are an island crossing, one full night, mountain-to-coast day-night temperature difference and a long final 14 km descent. The table breaks them into trainable abilities; it lists directions, not an official plan.

| Ability | Course demand | Preparation direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ultra endurance | Finish 126 km inside 30 hours | Extend continuous running time gradually, keeping one session near hundred-mile mountain scale |
| Climb load | 6,764 m+ through Teror, Fontanales, Artenara, Tejeda to El Garañón | Build vertical load on climb sessions; do not stack flat mileage alone |
| Night running | 23:59 start; every runner spends at least one full night out | Practise night runs, headlamp rhythm and night fuelling, following [night running and traffic](/en/knowledge/night-running-and-traffic/) |
| Downhill tolerance | Continuous descent after El Garañón, last 14 km almost all downhill | Add downhill running and quad strength; keep reserve for the long descent |
| Temperature adaptation | Mountain median daytime max 12.3 °C, coast 19–21 °C | Train layered clothing; prepare the cold kit per the organiser's decision two days before |
| Long-duration fuelling | Ten aid stations, no cups at stations | Train station-to-station eating and carry your own container and utensils per [fueling long runs](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/) |

Post-race recovery follows a [recovery day](/en/knowledge/recovery-day/); use the [split pace tool](/en/tools/splits/) to break a 30-hour target down to the stations. The tool does arithmetic only and does not replace island mountain mileage.
