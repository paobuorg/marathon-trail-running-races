## What this race is

Kodiak is Big Bear Lake's mountain trail weekend. The 2026 edition had five race distances plus a kids' race: 100M, 100K, 50K, 21K and 10K. The 100M leaves Rim Nordic Ski Area, circles the whole Big Bear Valley via the Pacific Crest Trail and the Sugarloaf summit and returns to The Village. The 100K uses the South Shore's Sugarloaf and Skyline trails; the 50K climbs the Snow Summit face before Skyline; the 21K and 10K stay in the Snow Summit ski area.

## The course

The organiser's 100M description runs in this order:

- **Rim Nordic Ski Area (0 miles)**: the start, climbing into the alpine forest on the outer ring of the Big Bear Valley.
- **Crab Flats (8.4) → Camp Whittle (19.4) → Van Dusen (26.8)**: the northern and western mountain sections, with the organiser noting a lot of running above 7,000 ft.
- **Doble Camp 1 (33.7) → Burns Canyon 1 (39.7) → Smarts Ranch (47.5) → Cactus Flats (51.4)**: the high-desert section with Joshua trees.
- **Doble Camp 2 (55.4) → Burns Canyon 2 (61.5)**: back into the mountains and around the valley.
- **Sugarloaf 1 (69.3) → Balky Horse (78.1) → Sugarloaf 2 (83.0)**: two passes through the Sugarloaf area, whose summit the organiser gives as 9,995 ft.
- **Bear Mountain (89.2) → Snow Summit (95.5) → The Village (100.1)**: the return to the Big Bear Lake finish.

![Aerial view of the Snow Summit ski area: snow-covered runs cut through conifer forest, with a small pond at the lower right and the Big Bear Valley beyond; the 100M descends from the Snow Summit summit on Going Green to the village finish, but this 2008 location photograph shows the mountain and descent direction, not the exact line or a race-day scene](/media/routes/kodiak/snow-summit-2008.jpg)

The terrain spans three climates: high desert, sub-alpine and semi-arid. The organiser gives no per-section technical grading or surface breakdown, so those are unknown. The 100K uses the South Shore's Sugarloaf and Skyline trails; the 50K climbs the Snow Summit face before rolling Skyline singletrack; the 21K and 10K stay in the Snow Summit ski area.

## Prize money and elite field

- **Prize money:** UTMB's official Majors table pays the same structure at 50K, 100K and 100M, with equal men's and women's purses: €5,000 for first, €3,000 for second, €2,000 for third, €1,250 for fourth and €1,000 for fifth. That is €12,250 per gender per distance, €24,500 per distance across both genders.
- **Finals places:** each Major gives the top 10 men and women plus age-group winners in the 100M, 100K and 50K direct entry; the four Majors together allocate 210 places. Kodiak's 100M feeds the UTMB Finals, the 100K feeds CCC and the 50K feeds OCC.
- **Elite field and testing:** before the 2026 race UTMB said there would be 20 anti-doping tests across the three ultra distances. With HOKA, the event supported 31 women from underrepresented groups, 10 of them in the 100K.
- **Field composition:** the 2026 pre-race figure was about 2,700 runners, 33% women; the 10K was nearly 62% women and the 20K/21K about 44%. These are pre-race official figures, not finisher statistics.

## Transport and accommodation

- **Access:** Big Bear Airport (501 W. Valley Blvd, Big Bear City) is the local general-aviation field; commercial flights usually go through Ontario (ONT), Los Angeles (LAX) or San Diego (SAN), then a drive into the mountains. The official visitor site lists LAX and other California airports as transport resources but publishes no race-day shuttle timetable.
- **Driving:** mountain roads climb from the Southern California lowlands; from November to April California requires tyre chains to be carried in mountain areas. The race is in October but early snow or ice is still possible, so check Caltrans road conditions.
- **Race shuttle:** the 2026 aid chart says Doble Camp 1/2 have no parking and supporters must use the official shuttle; the 2027 shuttle plan is not published, so this page does not treat the 2026 arrangement as 2027.
- **Accommodation:** The Village of Big Bear Lake is the start/finish for the 100K/50K/21K/10K and the 100M finish; staying in the village or near Snow Summit gives the shortest race-day travel.

## Withdrawal and risk

- **Crew/pacer points:** start, Doble Camp 1, Doble Camp 2, Sugarloaf 1, Sugarloaf 2, Bear Mountain, Snow Summit and finish. Cactus Flats lost crew/pacer access in 2026, a service change rather than a course change.
- **Pacers:** allowed from Doble Camp 2; Sugarloaf 1/2, Bear Mountain and Snow Summit also have pacer access. The 2027 rules are not published; this is read from the 2026 chart.
- **Risk:** exposure in the high-desert section, two Sugarloaf climbs, cold nights on the ridge and continuous running above 7,000 ft. The official page gives no per-segment technical grade, so this page does not invent one.
- **Withdrawal:** report to the aid-station captain and follow the organiser's instructions for handing in chip/bib. Doble Camp 1/2 have no parking, so self-evacuation is awkward; ask about the shuttle before withdrawing. No unified sweep policy is published, so it remains unknown.

## Training conversion: 36-hour multi-day mountain endurance

The challenge of Kodiak 100M is the full loop around Big Bear Valley, 161 km, about 4,876 m of repeated up-and-down, two passes through the Sugarloaf area, and 36-hour cross-night fatigue. Preparation centres on ultra-long mountain endurance, night rhythm and altitude adaptation, not raw road mileage. General methods are in [ultra-trail training plan](/en/knowledge/trail-ultra-training-plan/), [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), [recovery day](/en/knowledge/recovery-day/) and [load and overtraining](/en/knowledge/load-and-overtraining/).

| Phase | Focus | Practical content | Race-day constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-20 to T-14 weeks | Mountain aerobic base | One mountain long run plus easy runs a week; build weekly gain toward 2,000 m | A full-valley loop needs a steady aerobic base |
| T-14 to T-8 weeks | Long run and gain accumulation | Long run to 80–100 km; practise high-altitude ridges and stair sections | Repeated up-and-down at Sugarloaf and Snow Summit |
| T-8 to T-3 weeks | Loaded self-sufficiency and cross-night | Test carrying food and water; one cross-night or very long session | 36-hour race; night and early-morning sections need rhythm control |
| T-3 to race | Taper | Shorten distance and gain, keep short rhythm; no new shoes or kit | Recover fatigue before race day; see [taper and strength](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/) |

Boundary: this is a generic preparation framework, not medical advice. Runners with pre-existing cardiopulmonary conditions or recent injuries should consult a doctor first. The 100M 36-hour cut-off and the 100K Western States 18-hour qualifying line are different races under different rules; they cannot be applied to each other.
