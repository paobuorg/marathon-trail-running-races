## What this course is

The Western States 100 starts in Olympic Valley, California, and follows the middle section of the Western States Trail to the track at Placer High School in Auburn. The full Western States Trail runs from Salt Lake City, Utah, to Sacramento, California; the race uses only its middle portion. The start and finish are different points; the official recap describes the course as crossing the Sierra high country and the canyons of the American River.

First held in 1974, the event calls itself the world's oldest 100-mile trail race. The 2026 edition was the 53rd. The route climbs from 6,200 ft at the valley floor to Emigrant Pass at 8,750 ft within the first 4.5 miles, then crosses the Granite Chief Wilderness and Red Star Ridge, passes Robinson Flat, Last Chance, Devil's Thumb and Michigan Bluff, fords the American River at mile 78, and finally reaches Auburn by way of No Hands Bridge and Robie Point. The official trail history divides the route into six sections: the Wilderness, the Canyons, the Divide, the Rucky Chucky crossing, Darkness, and No Hands plus the climb to the finish.

The course crosses the traditional lands of the Nisenan, Washoe and other neighbouring Indigenous Peoples, as the event's home page and 2026 recap both state.

In the 2026 results, the top three men all broke 14 hours, with Bouillard becoming the first man to run under 14 hours at 13:46:15. Lichter ran 15:28:05 (the recap logs 15:28:06), beating Courtney Dauwalter's 2023 women's course record of 15:29:33. The official recap also records that several men broke the 2019 course record and that the morning was cool and windy.

Keep two versions separate: the event edition and the course version. The official course description promises the same basic route since 1986 unless snow forces a change. The home page describes a Granite Chief reroute with more than 7 miles of new and rehabilitated trail, with a significant portion hoped to open in 2026 and the balance in 2028. This page is pinned to the 2026 edition. It does not treat the 2027 GPX as 2026 geometry, and it does not treat a 1986 description as a complete 2026 technical file.

- **Official Course Description**: the valley floor at the start is 6,200 ft, Emigrant Pass is 8,750 ft, and the first 4.5 miles climb 2,550 ft. After the pass, runners climb another 15,540 ft and descend 22,970 ft to Auburn. Adding the two climb figures gives 18,090 ft; that is arithmetic on official segment values, not a separate survey by this page.
- **Official home page**: the overview says the trail climbs more than 18,000 ft and descends nearly 23,000 ft, and gives the distance as 100.2 miles. It does not repeat 18,090 or 22,970 exactly; this is the same official set rounded.
- **2026 race recap**: repeats 100.2 miles but publishes no gain or loss. It is a source for results and weather, not for elevation.

The entity card uses 100.2 miles and 18,090 ft because those are the two official figures that can be checked segment by segment. The home page's "more than 18,000 / nearly 23,000" is treated as an overview, not as the card value. The 22,970 ft descent is larger than the 18,090 ft ascent; the repeated climbs in the canyons are listed under Segment 3.

The official material reviewed for this page contains no per-segment ascent or descent table. If a source does not give an official segment figure, this page writes "not published" instead of inventing one.

Segment notes. Terrain descriptions come from the official trail history; elevation figures are quoted only where the event gives a local value.

- **Segment 1 (0 → 30.3 mi)** is the Wilderness. From the start the route climbs 4.5 miles to Emigrant Pass at 8,750 ft, then crosses the Granite Chief Wilderness, Red Star Ridge and Duncan Canyon to Robinson Flat. The official trail history says this stretch averages about 7,000 ft and in many years is covered entirely in snow. The checkpoints are Lyon Ridge (10.3 mi), Red Star Ridge (15.8 mi), Duncan Canyon (24.4 mi) and Robinson Flat (30.3 mi). The race starts at 5:00 a.m.
- **Segment 2 (30.3 → 43.3 mi)** leaves Robinson Flat, climbs Little Bald Mountain and descends 1,043 ft to Miller's Defeat (34.4 mi), then passes Dusty Corners (38.0 mi) to Last Chance (43.3 mi). The official history calls Last Chance the jumping-off point for the canyons. The course description warns of dry stretches of more than 8 miles during the Run.
- **Segment 3 (43.3 → 55.7 mi)** holds the two canyons. Deadwood drops about 2,000 ft and then climbs an extremely steep 1,500 ft to Devil's Thumb (47.8 mi). El Dorado is deeper: a 2,600 ft descent followed by an 1,800 ft climb to Michigan Bluff (55.7 mi). The official history says the most rugged 13 miles between Last Chance and Michigan Bluff are listed in the National Register of Historic Places, and that runners may meet 110°F (43°C) in the canyons.
- **Segment 4 (55.7 → 62.0 mi)** leaves Michigan Bluff, dips in and out of Volcano Canyon, reaches pavement for the first time on the outskirts of Foresthill, passes through "downtown" Foresthill and arrives at the aid station at 62.0 mi. The official table allows pacer changes at Foresthill, while Michigan Bluff allows them only after 8 p.m.
- **Segment 5 (62.0 → 78.0 mi)** follows the California Trail, which the official history describes as a 16-mile rolling descent to the bottom of the American River canyon. It passes Dardanelles (Cal-1, 65.7 mi), Peachstone (Cal-2, 70.7 mi) and Ford's Bar (Cal-3, 73.0 mi) before reaching Rucky Chucky at 78.0 mi. The segment ends with the river ford at Rucky Chucky.
- **Segment 6 (78.0 → 85.2 mi)** climbs out of the canyon to Green Gate (79.8 mi) and follows undulating terrain to Auburn Lake Trails (85.2 mi). The official history files this under Darkness; the course description says almost half the trail may be travelled at night and each runner should carry two LED flashlights.
- **Segment 7 (85.2 → 94.3 mi)** passes Brown's Bar, drops to the river again, climbs to the Cool limestone quarry and crosses Highway 49. Quarry Rd is at 90.7 mi and Pointed Rocks at 94.3 mi. The official history notes that after Highway 49 the finish is less than seven miles away.
- **Segment 8 (94.3 → 100.2 mi)** crosses Pointed Rocks, descends for the last time to the American River and crosses No Hands Bridge. The official history calls No Hands the lowest point on the entire trail, the longest concrete-arch bridge in the world when built, and a National Register property. The route then climbs to Robie Point (98.9 mi) and runs the final 1.3 miles onto the Placer High School track. The official history says this is the third and last time runners step onto pavement in 100 miles.

Several official details matter as much as the numbers:

- **A cutoff is a leaving time.** The official chart defines cutoffs as the deadlines for leaving the aid station, not for arriving. Anyone leaving after the cutoff is disqualified, and the rule is strictly enforced. An asterisk marks a default cutoff identical to the next station's cutoff.
- **Official pages also disagree.** The FAQ spectator section still gives Robinson Flat a 1:50 p.m. cutoff, while the current official checkpoint chart gives 2:10 p.m. This page follows the chart and leaves the discrepancy visible rather than choosing one official page for the reader. On race day, the current-year chart and race announcements govern.
- **Cutoffs are not a pacing plan.** The official chart states that cutoffs are deadlines for leaving a station, not a pacing guide.
- **The race is cupless.** Runners must carry a cup. The usual fluids are water, GU Hydration Drink Tabs, ROCTANE Energy Drink, Sprite or 7Up and Coke; night stations also have soup, hot coffee and hot chocolate. Solid food includes saltines, pretzels, chips, GU gels, oranges, bananas, melons, potatoes, cookies, candies and sandwiches. Hot soup is available at the River Crossing, Auburn Lake Trails and Pointed Rocks.
- **No pain medication is supplied.** The event does not provide ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) or naprosyn (Naprosyn); runners bring their own and carry the risk themselves.
- **Drop bags have a single collection window.** One bag per aid station, no longer than 16 inches, must fit through a 6 x 8 inch opening. Bags are collected on the Friday before the Run from 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. near the corner of Squaw Peak and Squaw Valley Road, returned to the Placer High School track, and must be claimed by 3:00 p.m. Sunday.
- Duncan Canyon and Dusty Corners allow one vehicle; Robinson Flat and Michigan Bluff use shuttles; Rucky Chucky has a near-side shuttle and drop bags on the far side; Green Gate, Pointed Rocks and Robie Point are on foot only. Actual withdrawal is governed by the official Drops rule.
- **Aid stations also close with the sweeps.** The official course description says aid stations close when the sweep arrives, so the published cutoff is not the only closing condition.

## Surface and terrain

The event publishes no distributable surface percentages, so this page does not invent a split between single-track, fire road and pavement. What can be confirmed comes from the official six-section description:

- **Wilderness**: the official history says Granite Chief Wilderness, Red Star Ridge and Duncan Canyon average about 7,000 ft, and that in many years the section is covered entirely in snow.
- **Canyons**: the Last Chance to Michigan Bluff section is steep gold-rush trail. Deadwood and El Dorado each drop and climb, with local descents and climbs of 1,500–2,600 ft, and the 13 most rugged miles are a National Register property.
- **Divide**: after Michigan Bluff the route enters Volcano Canyon and reaches Foresthill. The official history calls this the first pavement of the race, but it is only a brief pass through the town outskirts and "downtown" before the route returns to dirt on the California Trail.
- **River**: the California Trail is a 16-mile rolling descent to the bottom of the American River canyon. The Rucky Chucky ford is just below a class 6 rapid; the event reduces flow when possible, runs a cable from bank to bank, and uses boats in high-water years.
- **Final 22.2 miles (100.2 − 78.0)**: after climbing out of the canyon at Rucky Chucky the route is undulating. Highway 49 crosses the Cool limestone quarry. No Hands Bridge is concrete; after it the route climbs again until Robie Point, the third and last pavement section described by the event, before entering Auburn.
- **Finish**: the Placer High School track. The official checkpoint chart records it as the 100.2-mile finish with an 11:00 a.m. cut-off, medical cover, drop bags and crew access in multiple places; the official pages do not describe how runners cover the track itself.

In 2026 the new Granite Chief line sits "higher up on the ridge"; the event hoped a significant portion would open in 2026, with the balance in 2028.

## Risks and escape points

- **Heat and cold coexist.** The official course description says temperatures during the Run can range from 20°F to above 110°F, and the official trail history puts the canyons at 110°F (43°C). The 2026 recap records a cool, windy morning in the high country and a high of only 73°F at the Auburn finish, nearly 20°F below the historical average high for 27 June.
- **Night.** The event says almost half the trail may be travelled at night and requires each runner to carry two LED flashlights. Reflectors mark the last 38 miles. Keep the spare lamp within reach and check both before dark.
- **River.** The mile-78 ford sits just below a class 6 rapid. On race day a guide rope and staff are in place, and boats are used in high water. Do not attempt it alone on a training run; follow staff instructions.
- **Remoteness and medical cover.** The event repeats that much of the territory is accessible only by foot, horse or helicopter. Medical staff are experienced, but delay is a real risk. The official Medical and Other Risks page lists hypothermia, hyperthermia, dehydration, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, disorientation and mental and physical exhaustion, and says the runner assumes all financial obligations for an emergency ground or helicopter evacuation.
- **No weigh-ins.** The official Medical and Other Risks page states that runners are not weighed at check-in or at aid stations during the race. Medical staff assess how the runner feels and looks and their mental status rather than a weight number. A stable weight is not proof of correct hydration.
- **Drops.** The official Drops rule says runners who drop before Foresthill are taken to Foresthill, and those who drop after Foresthill are taken to the finish. Non-emergency evacuation may take several hours. Aid stations close when the sweeps arrive.
- **Dry stretches.** The official course description says there are dry stretches of more than 8 miles during the Run and 16 or more miles during training. With a cupless race, runners need to leave each station with enough water for the next one.
- **Markings.** Official markings are pink surveyor's tape, W.S. TRAIL signs, arrows and reflectors.
- **Crew-accessible escape points.** The official chart marks crew access at Duncan Canyon, Dusty Corners, Robinson Flat, Michigan Bluff, Foresthill, Rucky Chucky near side, Green Gate (on foot), Pointed Rocks (on foot), Robie Point (on foot) and the finish.
- **Snow.** The official history says the high-country section is covered entirely in snow in many years.

## Getting there

The official FAQ gives the travel baseline:

- **Reno–Tahoe Airport (RNO)**: about 50 miles from Olympic Valley, the closest airport.
- **Sacramento Airport (SMF)**: about 100 miles from Olympic Valley.
- **San Francisco Airport (SFO)**: about 200 miles from Olympic Valley.
- **Lodging**: plenty of accommodation near the start in Olympic Valley, home of the Palisades Tahoe ski resort; Truckee and Tahoe City also have lodging. The event does not provide a single shuttle for all runners.
- **Start**: the west end of Olympic Valley, 5:00 a.m. The official FAQ says spectators can watch check-in and runner briefings on Friday and the 5:00 a.m. start on Saturday.
- **Finish**: the Placer High School track in Auburn. The official FAQ says the race does not provide a shuttle back to the start; rides after the awards ceremony, Uber or Lyft can be used.
- **Spectator limits**: many checkpoints are restricted to race officials because of limited parking and the need to keep narrow access roads clear for fire and rescue equipment. The main spectator points named by the official FAQ are the start, Robinson Flat, Foresthill and the finish. Robinson Flat is reached only by the Foresthill Divide Road out of Foresthill, about an hour by road, narrow and partly steep; parking is about a mile short at Sailor Bar, with a shuttle bus to the aid station. Foresthill has more parking in town, and the latest runners reach it around midnight. The first finisher may arrive as early as 7:00 p.m., 24-hour runners must arrive by 5:00 a.m. Sunday and 30-hour runners by 11:00 a.m.
- **Spectator conduct**: spectators are asked to stay within 1/4 mile of the checkpoints and not to provide food, beverages or other assistance outside the main checkpoint areas.
- **Next edition**: the 54th edition is scheduled for 26–27 June 2027. The lottery is held on the first Saturday in December in the Placer High School auditorium at 123 Agard St., Auburn. A valid qualifier is required, and the official FAQ says there are no exceptions.
- **Training runs**: the event offers Memorial Day weekend training runs in late May.

The race starts in Olympic Valley and finishes in Auburn, and provides no return shuttle; runners need a crew driver, a rental car, or a ride-hailing plan from Auburn back to Olympic Valley.

## Difficulty

The official documents publish no single difficulty grade, and this page does not invent one. **This site's rating (not official)**: a high-altitude, point-to-point 100-mile mountain trail race, rated high difficulty on the combination of climb and descent, repeated canyon drops, a 30-hour cutoff and long dry stretches. The verifiable dimensions:

- 100.2 miles (161.3 km), point to point.
- 18,090 ft (5,514 m) of climb and 22,970 ft (7,001 m) of descent.
- Start at 6,200 ft, high point at Emigrant Pass 8,750 ft, canyon repeats of 1,500–2,600 ft.
- Official 30-hour cutoff; runners must reach the finish by 10:59:59 the next morning to be eligible for an award.
- 20 aid stations and 10 major medical checkpoints; dry stretches over 8 miles; a river ford at mile 78.
- Almost half the route may be at night; the event requires two headlamps.
- Entry by qualifier and lottery; 370 started and 322 finished in 2026.

The 2026 finish rate of 87% was an all-time high, and the recap records both cool weather and record-breaking competition. The same recap states that the top three men and fourth place all broke the 14:09 men's course record set in 2019.

The sub-24-hour silver buckle and sub-30-hour bronze buckle are the event's award rules, not a difficulty scale. In 2026, 136 runners earned silver. When turning a target finish time into segment splits, the [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/) handles the arithmetic and the [split planner](/en/tools/splits/) compares each segment with the official cutoff; neither tool replaces the official checkpoint chart.
