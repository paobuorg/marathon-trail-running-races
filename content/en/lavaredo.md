## What this race is (disambiguate first)

La Sportiva Lavaredo Ultra Trail by UTMB takes place in Cortina d'Ampezzo, province of Belluno, Veneto, Italy, with start and finish on the Corso Italia in the town centre. The official page calls Lavaredo 120K the "queen race", says it starts at 11pm, passes the Tre Cime di Lavaredo at sunrise, and labels the race the 20th edition.

The official site calls the event "A UTMB® World Series Event". The page's data fields give the 120K a Race Category of `ws100M`, 4 Running Stones and Finals Access `100m-direct-entry`. The same series' official page lists the 2027 Majors as exactly six: Kodiak and Canyons in the Americas, Val d'Aran and Wildstrubel in Europe, Chiang Mai in Asia-Pacific and UTA in Oceania. Lavaredo is not on that list, and the word "Major" does not appear anywhere on the 120K page. This page therefore positions it as a regular UTMB World Series stop (a UTMB World Series Event), not a Major, and does not place it on any global list.

The official 2027 figures for all five races are below. The table exists only to establish which race is the 120K; this page does not mix any other race's distance or climb into the 120K's splits and cut-offs.

The table lists only the distance, climb, start, limit, Running Stones and category code given on each race card. For every race except the 120K, this page has not checked aid stations, cut-offs or gear differences and does not fill them in.

The numbers are not interchangeable. The 120K is 120 km, the 80K is 80 km and the 50K is 50 km; they are not one course with different start and finish points, and the 80K even starts at Val Marzon in Auronzo di Cadore. Every split figure on this page belongs to the 120K only.

The 10K data fields carry no Running Stones and no category code, so this page leaves them blank rather than guessing whether it counts towards the series.

The two water points are Rifugio Auronzo at 49.522 km and Malga Travenanzes at 89.700 km; the race page singles out the latter as a fountain. Neither provides food.

All eight aid stations and both water points list the same drinks: hot tea, Coca-Cola, cold tea, water and sparkling water. Hot food and staples are not at every station, and the difference is what the food column records.

Only runners with a visible bib may enter an aid station. Food and drinks are served by station personnel and rubbish must go into the containers there; the penalty chart sets one hour for littering.

Neither aid stations nor life bases hand out disposable tableware, so runners must bring their own cup, bowl and cutlery. If you want hot food, you carry the container. This is not a suggestion.

Buying food and drinks in mountain huts is at the runner's own expense. The mandatory gear list requires at least EUR 20 in cash for exactly that situation.

Assistance is allowed only at Cimabanche and Rifugio Col Gallina, inside the designated area, with the station head's agreement and one assistant per runner. The other six aid stations allow none.

There is a refreshment station at the finish, and the hot meal is served separately at the Olympic Ice Stadium. The page does not give serving times for the hot meal, and this page does not copy times from previous editions.

## Mandatory gear and rules

Lavaredo 120K and 80K share one mandatory list; the 50K has another. The 120K/80K list is: a race backpack; a long-sleeved sweater; long trousers or tights; a hat or bandana; warm and waterproof gloves; at least 1 litre of water capacity; a beaker of at least 15 cl; a visible bib throughout; an emergency thermal blanket of at least 2.00 x 1.40 m; a whistle; a switched-on phone with emergency numbers stored; a waterproof jacket with an integrated hood and a membrane of at least 10,000 Schmerber; a headlamp with spare batteries or two headlamps with batteries; footwear suitable for Dolomite terrain (stones, rocks, gravel); energy bars or solid food; at least EUR 20 in cash; and elastic bandages.

The 50K list is essentially the same but has no headlamp and spare-battery row. The two lists cannot be applied to each other.

Recommended gear is waterproof trousers, mineral salts, sunglasses, a warm long-sleeved sweatshirt, sunscreen and a third layer (fleece or quilted jacket) for particularly cold weather. Recommended is not checked.

Trekking poles are permitted, but must be carried from the start. The regulations explicitly forbid starting without poles and collecting them later, and forbid putting poles in spare bags; the organiser may also ban poles in marked areas.

The list can be changed on the basis of the weather up to 12 hours before the start. Re-read the official list before packing; do not rely on the static list on this page.

Equipment may be checked before or during the race. Missing a key safety item (minimum water, hooded waterproof jacket, headlamp, survival blanket, smartphone) is disqualification; missing other mandatory items is a one-hour penalty; refusing a check is disqualification.

The medical certificate is a hard requirement of Italian law for every distance. Non-Italian athletes must provide a certificate under DM 18/02/1982 or the official downloadable form, issued no more than 12 months before, uploaded to the personal dashboard between 10 January and 15 May 2027, and valid on 27 June 2027.

The consequence of missing the deadline is stated plainly: the entry is annulled and no refund is due. It is not a soft requirement that can be fixed on race week.

Bib collection is at the Olympic Ice Stadium, via Alberto Bonacossa 1. Bibs 1-200 are collected on 24 June from 17:00 to 19:00, the rest on 25 June from 10:00 to 19:00; athletes must come in person with a valid ID and may not delegate.

Bag rules: at least one 30-litre bag per runner, only UTMB World Series bags accepted, returned on presentation of the bib, collected within two hours of the finish, only official bags transported, and no poles inside.

Aid stations have bins and runners must carry waste to the next one. A plastic bag for soiled paper is handed out with the bib; that is the official method, not an option.

Medical stations are linked to a command centre with staff on the course. Medical staff may withdraw a runner, demand mandatory gear, evacuate or refer; a runner treated with an intravenous drip must withdraw. There is a medical centre at the finish.

A dropout must be registered at a checkpoint and the bib is cut. The bib then gives access to transport, meals and treatment rooms. Some aid stations have repatriation vehicles; elsewhere runners must reach the nearest repatriation point by their own means.

Some aid stations have rest areas with camp beds and blankets. The regulations do not list which ones, so this page marks the detail unknown.

The 100K/100M penalty chart applies: short-cutting is at the race director's discretion; missing a key safety item, refusing a check, not assisting a person in need, disrespect and refusing an instruction are disqualification; littering, assistance outside authorised zones and poles not carried from the start are one hour; a phone in airplane mode, being accompanied outside tolerance zones and a non-visible bib are 15 minutes; cheating is lifetime disqualification; starting in an earlier wave than assigned is two hours.

At UTMB World Series Events the top five men and women overall and the first man and woman in each age category receive a trophy; the prize-giving must be attended and awards are not sent later. Age categories run 16-17, 18-19, 20-34, then five-year bands to 85+.

## Risks and withdrawal

- **Two nights**: a 23:00 start and a 05:00 Sunday cut-off mean at least two full nights on the course. A headlamp and spare batteries are mandatory, not optional.
- **Altitude and cold**: the highest timing point is Forcella Lavaredo at 2,449 m, and the GPX maximum is 2,451 m. On the 2,300 m grid point in late June the median daily minimum is 4.65 C and the minimum reached -0.8 C, while Cortina's median daily minimum is 11.35 C. The ridge and the town do not feel the same.
- **Rain**: at the Cortina grid point 22 of 36 days reached 1 mm and 14 reached 5 mm, with a maximum daily total of 34.1 mm. Dolomite storms arrive fast and exposed sections offer no shelter.
- **Technical terrain**: the gear page specifies footwear for Dolomite terrain (stones, rocks, gravel). This is not road or fire trail.
- **Semi-autonomy**: the organisation supplies only water and sports drink for refilling; food is carried or eaten at stations, and hut purchases are at your own expense. Treating aid stations as a full buffet will underestimate the load.
- **Restricted assistance**: only Cimabanche and Col Gallina allow an assistant, one per runner. Crews must read the rule before planning to meet anywhere.
- **Cut-offs are absolute**: arriving late does not buy a personal pace; every gated station must be left before its time.
- **Withdrawal**: some aid stations have repatriation vehicles, elsewhere runners must reach the nearest repatriation point themselves; the organiser does not publish per-point evacuation times or transport, so this page marks it unknown.
- **Medical certificate**: an Italian statutory requirement, not an event option. Without a valid certificate the entry is annulled after the deadline with no refund.
- **Weather**: the actual weather for 23-27 June 2027 was unknown at the check date; check the forecast again before travelling.

## Getting there

Cortina d'Ampezzo is in the north-east of Veneto, province of Belluno, about 72 km from Belluno and only 44 km from the Austrian border. The official page calls it one of Italy's most famous mountain destinations.

The closest airport is Venice. The official page says a daily bus service and private shuttles run from Venice airport to Cortina in a little less than two hours.

The three operators named by the event are Flixbus, Cortina Express and ATVO. Summer schedules from Venice airport are published at the end of April, so check again before travelling.

By car from the south: A13 Bologna-Padova, A4 Milano-Venezia, A27 to Belluno, exit Pian di Vedoia, then SS 51 Alemagna towards Dobbiaco and Cortina. The official page prints the exit as "Pian di Vedoao"; that is a typo and this page records the correct place name.

From the north: A22 Brennero south, exit Bressanone, then towards Dobbiaco and Cortina.

The travel page's introduction says it covers car, train and plane, but the body has only By plane, By bus and By car, with no station name, timetable or journey time. This page marks rail as unknown rather than inventing a route.

No page checked for this edition publishes race-week shuttles, parking or road closures. Check the official Runner Guide and race app; this page does not turn "there were shuttles before" into a 2027 fact.

## Difficulty

The hard figures for Lavaredo 120K are 120 km, 5,800 m of climb, 30 hours and a minimum average pace of 4.0 km/h. This is a long mountain ultra, not a road ultra.

The course tops out around 2,450 m and spends two nights on the mountain. Altitude, night cold, rain and technical terrain stack; difficulty is not one number.

Against the other races in the same event: the 80K is 80 km, 4,600 m and 22 hours and starts at Val Marzon in Auronzo di Cadore; the 50K is 50 km, 2,600 m and 12 hours; the 20K is 20 km, 1,000 m and 4 hours; the 10K is 10 km, 300 m and 2 hours. None of these figures can be swapped.

No page checked here publishes ITRA points, a UTMB Index threshold or a finish rate. This page marks them unknown and does not guess a difficulty grade.
