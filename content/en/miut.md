## What this race is

MIUT is a five-race linear trail event organised by Clube de Montanha do Funchal. Starts and finishes are spread across Madeira, with every race finishing in Machico. The 2027 fields:

| Race | Distance / gain / loss | Limit | Age | Places | Fee (early / late) | Start |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| MIUT Legend | 110 km / +7,200 / −7,200 m | 32 h | 21 | 1,000 | €180 / €195 | Porto Moniz, 24 Apr 00:00 |
| MIUT Advanced | 80 km / +4,760 / −4,760 m | 25 h | 20 | 600 | €140 / €160 | São Vicente, 24 Apr 07:00 |
| MIUT Discover | 56 km / +2,800 / −3,060 m | 15 h | 20 | 600 | €110 / €120 | Boaventura, 24 Apr 09:00 |
| MIUT Marathon | 40 km / +1,450 / −2,000 m | 11 h | 20 | 800 | €90 / €100 | Monte, 24 Apr 11:00 |
| MIUT Starter | 17 km / +510 / −520 m | 3 h 30 min | 18 | 800 | €40 / €55 | Porto da Cruz, 25 Apr 08:00 |

The total is about 3,800 places. **This is the 2027 official table, not the 2026 one**: the old page gave Advanced 20 hours, Discover 16 hours, Marathon 10 hours and Starter 5 hours, and started Advanced at 06:00, Discover at 07:00 and Marathon at 08:00. None of those match the 2027 regulations; every figure above has been rewritten from the 2027 table. The Legend minimum age is 21, not the 20 shown previously.

Identity: MIUT is one of the ten races in the 2026 World Trail Majors, not a UTMB World Series stop and not a UTMB Finals race; the official Legend page also lists it as a UTMB 100M qualifier that does not award Running Stones. The first edition was in 2008; 2026 was the 17th and 2027 the 18th. After the 2024 wildfires the race was rerouted for two editions and returned close to its original layout in 2026. Elite and ranking rules follow ITRA and WTM.

## The course

The Legend starts on the northwest coast at Porto Moniz, climbs into the Laurissilva cloud forest at Fanal, descends to Chão da Ribeira, climbs to the Estanquinhos plateau, crosses Chão dos Louros, drops into the Curral das Freiras valley, climbs Pico Ruivo (checkpoint at 1,760 m; the summit is 1,862 m), then follows Chão da Lagoa, Portela and the Larano coastal ridge before descending to Machico. It passes through the UNESCO-listed Laurissilva forest and the exposed rock ridge around Pico do Areeiro.

The official checkpoint table gives this geometry (cumulative kilometres; gain and loss are **for that segment**, not cumulative):

| Checkpoint | Cumulative km | Altitude m | Segment +m | Segment −m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Porto Moniz | 0 | 15 | — | — |
| Fanal | 12.3 | 1,160 | 1,580 | 435 |
| Chão da Ribeira | 20.2 | 440 | 130 | 850 |
| Estanquinhos | 29.9 | 1,580 | 1,400 | 260 |
| Chão dos Louros | 43.9 | 820 | 490 | 1,270 |
| Curral das Freiras | 58.4 | 670 | 1,295 | 1,445 |
| Pico Ruivo | 69.4 | 1,760 | 1,430 | 320 |
| Chão da Lagoa | 79.0 | 1,480 | 480 | 760 |
| Portela | 90.9 | 600 | 95 | 975 |
| Larano | 96.0 | 320 | 35 | 315 |
| Machico | 108.9 | 5 | 230 | 545 |

Adding the segments gives **108.9 km, +7,165 m and −7,175 m**. The three hardest blocks are Fanal (1,580 m up in 12.3 km), Curral das Freiras to Pico Ruivo (1,430 m up in 11 km) and the repeated climbs and descents from Pico Ruivo to Portela on an exposed ridge. The final 12.9 km from Larano to Machico still has +230 / −545 m; it is not a pure downhill finish.

## Checkpoints, cut-offs and aid

The ten Legend checkpoints from the official 2027 table. `L` = liquids, `S` = solids, `+` = nurse, `Méd` = medical, `AP` = personal assistance, `BAG` = drop bag:

| Checkpoint | Cumulative km | Fast | Slow | Cut-off | Services |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Porto Moniz | 0 | — | — | 00:15 | Start |
| Fanal | 12.3 | 1:35 | 3:25 | 03:30 | L S + |
| Chão da Ribeira | 20.2 | 2:20 | 5:10 | 05:15 | L S + |
| Estanquinhos | 29.9 | 3:50 | 9:10 | 09:15 | L S AP + Méd |
| Chão dos Louros | 43.9 | 5:30 | 12:20 | 12:30 | L S + |
| Curral das Freiras | 58.4 | 7:30 | 16:20 | 16:30 | L S AP + Méd BAG |
| Pico Ruivo | 69.4 | 9:15 | 20:20 | 20:30 | L S + |
| Chão da Lagoa | 79.0 | 10:25 | 23:20 | 23:30 | L S AP + Méd |
| Portela | 90.9 | 11:25 | 2:25+1 | 02:30+1 | L S + |
| Larano | 96.0 | 11:55 | 4:55+1 | 05:00+1 | L S + Méd |
| Machico | 108.9 | 13:00 | 7:55+1 | 08:00+1 | L S + Méd |

- **Cut-offs apply at departure.** The regulations say the time of leaving a checkpoint determines compliance. Arriving at or trying to leave after the cut-off means disqualification and surrender of the bib; it is not enough to have "checked in" before the time.
- **The overall limit is 32 hours**, and the published slow schedule reaches the finish at 31:55 — only five minutes inside the limit. The fast schedule is 13:00. Do not turn 32 hours into a flat 17:38/km target: the first 12.3 km allow 17:04/km, while the first 29.9 km allow 18:34/km, because the organiser built more margin into the climb.
- **Aid:** the organiser promises water, isotonic drink, soft drinks, fruit, sweet and savoury food and hot broth at the main stations. There are **no disposable cups**; every runner must carry a cup or bottle. The exact products at each station belong to the official per-race page; this page does not invent a menu.
- **Personal assistance:** allowed only in Legend, Advanced and Discover, within 20 m before and after an official aid station and with one assistant per runner. At Curral das Freiras the assistant must show the access card issued at check-in. Marathon and Starter forbid personal assistance and pacing.
- **Drop bags:** Legend and Advanced bags go to Curral das Freiras, then to the finish; the other races go directly from start to finish. Do not put poles, documents, cash, medication or electronics inside. Bags not collected by 12:00 on Sunday are treated as abandoned; later shipping costs €15 plus postage.

Section 8 of the 2027 regulations sets different requirements by race. **Legend and Advanced are the strictest:**

| Waterproof jacket | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Working mobile phone | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Reserve food | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Whistle | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Personal cup | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Bowl/container plus utensil | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — | — |
| 2 headlamps plus spare batteries/power bank | ✓ | ✓ | — | — | — |
| 1 headlamp plus spare batteries/power bank | — | — | ✓ | — | — |
| Flashing red rear light, on at night | ✓ | ✓ | — | — | — |
| ID document | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

All runners must also carry a thermal blanket (at least 100 × 200 cm) and an elastic bandage (at least 100 × 6 cm). If the organiser activates the cold-weather kit, it adds a long-sleeved thermal top, waterproof trousers, a hat or buff covering the ears and waterproof gloves. Missing a critical item (bib, backpack, water capacity, thermal blanket, whistle, waterproof jacket, phone, headlamp or ID) can mean immediate disqualification. Missing a non-critical item (one of two headlamps, bandage, reserve food, cup or rear light) costs one hour; running with a headlamp or rear light off at night costs 15 minutes; an invisible bib costs five minutes.

## Entry, fees and qualification

The 2027 field is about 3,800: 80% direct entry and 20% lottery. Direct entry opened race by race at 15:00 local on 12–16 October 2026. Lottery pre-registration ran from 26 October to 9 November, with the draw on 14 November. Places and both price tiers are in the table above; early pricing ends on 30 November 2026 and the late tier runs to 15 March 2027.

Qualification:

- Legend: at least 4 ITRA points between 1 January 2024 and the entry date, or 115 km-effort; minimum age 21.
- Advanced: at least 2 ITRA points or 45 km-effort; minimum age 20.
- Discover: at least 1 ITRA point or 25 km-effort; minimum age 20.
- Marathon and Starter: no points requirement; minimum age 20 and 18 respectively.
- Every runner must upload a medical certificate and complete the medical questionnaire by 20 March 2027, or will not be allowed to start.
- Refunds: 85% until 30 November 2026; 50% from 1 December 2026 to 31 January 2027; 30% from 1 February to 15 March 2027; nothing from 16 March. A distance change costs €15 and is allowed until 20 March 2027.
- Places are not transferable and cannot be deferred; pregnancy is handled under the regulations' retention clause.
- Elite: men with ITRA ≥825 and women ≥700 can apply for a complimentary entry; men ≥775 and women ≥650 can apply for a guaranteed paid place after sell-out. Deadline 28 February 2027.
- Charity: a €300 donation to one of four partner institutions plus the entry fee, confirmed by 31 January 2027. Travel: at least five nights through the official travel agency, 20 places, deadline 28 February 2027.

## Pace and split plan

The averages below divide 108.9 km by a target finish time. They exclude aid stops, queues, navigation errors and night-time slowdowns, and they are not the organiser's cut-off table:

| Target finish | Average pace | What it means |
|---:|---:|---|
| 13 h | 7:10 /km | The organiser's "fast" finish; podium/front-group rhythm |
| 20 h | 11:01 /km | Requires sustained running; do not blow up before Pico Ruivo |
| 24 h | 13:13 /km | Solid mid-pack; still needs a buffer in the first 30 km |
| 28 h | 15:25 /km | Mostly fast hiking with some running |
| 32 h | 17:38 /km | Official limit — but **you cannot run the first 20 km at this average** |

The counter-intuitive part: 32 hours is 17:38/km, yet Fanal at 12.3 km closes at 3:30; at 17:38/km you arrive at 3:37, seven minutes outside. Chão da Ribeira at 20.2 km closes at 5:15; at 17:38/km you arrive at 5:56, 41 minutes outside. **The first 20 km must be faster than the overall average** to bank a buffer. By Estanquinhos at 29.9 km the cut-off is 9:15 against an 8:47 even-pace arrival, so 28 minutes of cushion reappear. Treating 32 hours as "17:38 everywhere" fails at the first two checkpoints.

A workable budget: run the first 20.2 km at 15:15–15:30/km to reach Chão da Ribeira in 5:08–5:13; use 16:30–17:30/km from 20.2 to 58.4 km to reach Curral before 16:30; allow 18:30–20:00/km on the Pico Ruivo climb but leave Chão da Lagoa before 23:30; then use 17:00–17:30/km from 79 to 108.9 km, pass Larano before 05:00 and finish before 08:00. These are planning figures, not official splits.

## Difficulty: official 4/5, and why

The organiser rates the Legend 4/5, Trail Ultra Endurance. Using the checkpoint table, the main demands are:

1. **1,580 m of climbing in the first 12.3 km.** From near sea level at Porto Moniz straight up to Fanal, in the dark. The 3:30 cut-off looks generous, but this is one of the densest climbs of the race.
2. **1,430 m of climbing in 11 km from Curral das Freiras to Pico Ruivo.** The Pico Ruivo cut-off is 20:30, followed by the exposed Areeiro ridge with wind and technical rock.
3. **Repeated descents.** The course loses 800–1,445 m in single segments before Fanal, Chão dos Louros and Curral. At night and under fatigue, the descents are where most mistakes happen.
4. **32 hours is not as generous as it looks.** The published slow schedule finishes at 31:55, leaving five minutes. One navigation error, queue or stomach problem can consume the buffer.
5. **Long gaps between aid.** The longest is 14.5 km from Chão dos Louros to Curral, and there are no disposable cups. At least 1 L of water capacity and reserve food are hard requirements, not suggestions.

Weather and kit must be planned for "midnight plus ridge". In 2026 it was about 16°C at night and low 20s°C by day, with drizzle and sun alternating on the ridges; that is not a 2027 forecast, only the range a runner may meet in the same season. After the 00:00 start, the coldest sections are the Fanal and Estanquinhos plateau; the waterproof jacket, long-sleeve layer, ear-covering hat and gloves need to be within reach. Two headlamps are not "a spare": one is the primary and one the backup, and the red rear light must be on on road sections. Madeira rain arrives quickly, and wet roots and rock in the Laurissilva make descents much slower than in the dry; build that loss into the pace budget.

Withdrawal and medical: formalise a withdrawal at an official checkpoint/aid station or by phone to the organiser; do not cut down an unofficial path. The organiser provides transport back to Machico from checkpoints, subject to seats and schedules. Medical stations and mountain rescue are placed along the course; the race doctor can remove a runner for hypothermia, dehydration or altered consciousness. Rescue costs caused by ignoring instructions or reckless behaviour are charged to the runner. The emergency number is given at check-in; it is not a substitute for 112.
