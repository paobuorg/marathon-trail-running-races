## 60-second verdict

- **Next edition:** Sunday 4 April 2027, 50th edition, branded the ASICS Marathon de Paris. As of the check date (2026-11-25), the 2027 course, start and finish lines and detailed participant guide are **not yet published**; the route description below uses the verified 2026 edition as its baseline, and every cross-edition assumption is labelled.

- **Easiest mistakes:** separated start and finish (start on the Champs-Élysées; bag drop and finish on the Avenue Foch side); bibs are collected only at the three-day Run Experience expo and are **not handed out on race day**; no cups or bottles are distributed on course, so a own container is mandatory; runners in late waves must calculate cut-off margin from their own wave start, not from the 08:00 first gun.

## What this course is

The Paris Marathon is the largest city road marathon in France and a World Athletics Label road race. Under the 2026 official rules it runs on closed Paris roads over the standard marathon distance of 42.195 km, linking the Champs-Élysées, Place de la Concorde, Rue de Rivoli, Opéra Garnier, Place de la Bastille, the Bois de Vincennes, Notre-Dame, the Eiffel Tower and the Bois de Boulogne into one loop. The organiser's event page describes it as a race across the capital's most famous avenues and squares, and certifies it as an FFA Gold Label road race.

The 2026 field shows what kind of race this is: of 58,853 starters, 49% were first-time marathoners, 33% were women, 29% came from outside France, and the average age was 38. Among international runners, the United Kingdom supplied the largest group after France, followed by the United States. This is therefore a race where elite racing and large first-marathon fields share the same course: a fast front group in the early kilometres, followed by a large mass of runners finishing between roughly four and five hours. Road width, station queues and crowd merges are real planning issues.

The route is a loop rather than an out-and-back: the 2026 start was at the foot of the Arc de Triomphe on the Champs-Élysées, and the finish was on the western edge of the Bois de Boulogne on Avenue Foch. The organiser states that the 2027 start and finish lines will be announced later; this page does not treat the 2026 locations as a 2027 commitment.

## Eligibility and entry

### Age and medical documentation

The 2026 rules required runners to be born in 2006 at the latest; the 2027 official FAQ states runners must be born on 31 December 2007 at the latest. Since 2024 the French medical certificate has been replaced by the online Health Prevention Pathway (Parcours de Prévention Santé, PPS): runners complete the PPS online, download the Attestation PPS certificate and upload it to the timeto registration account; the certificate is valid for 3 months. If the PPS refers the runner to a doctor, a medical certificate less than 1 year old in French, English, Spanish, German, Italian or Portuguese is required. Holders of an FFA licence or another athletics federation licence may use the licence under the rules. Foreign licence holders must still provide one of the listed documents; a foreign licence does not grant an exemption.

Documents must be uploaded online at least 7 days before the event; runners who miss this deadline must hand them over at bib collection. The 2027 medical requirement follows the 2027 race regulations, which the FAQ says are not yet finalised.

The rules contain no general clause for transferring one's own place to a friend; travel services sold by V.S.O. may be transferred under the rules, but the bib itself is not transferable.

### Fees, refunds and deferral

The 2026 edition opened on the timeto platform: pre-registered runners from 22 April 2025, the general public from 24 April 2025. The fee bracket table in the 2026 rules PDF exists as a tabular image that this check could not extract as text, so this page does not cite unverified 2026 bracket prices; the 2027 flat €169 comes from the official FAQ.

Refund position: registration is personal, firm and final, and is non-refundable except where the rules stipulate otherwise. Under force majeure the rules offer substitution, postponement or a refund of the bib price only (excluding other sums paid); the Pass Event FFA cancellation insurance covers listed reasons: death, accident or illness, family events, visa refusal, theft of identity papers within 48 hours before the event, and court summons. Outside the rules and insurance terms there is no general deferral to the next edition.

## Course and geometry

### 2026 route sequence

According to the organiser's event page and the ASO course map published by the city council, the 2026 route runs from the Champs-Élysées at the foot of the Arc de Triomphe down to Place de la Concorde, along the Rue de Rivoli past Opéra Garnier, then east to Place de la Bastille; it then loops through the Bois de Vincennes, returns through views of Notre-Dame and the Eiffel Tower, and finishes on the western Bois de Boulogne edge on Avenue Foch. Other landmarks marked on the map include the Grand Palais, the Carrousel du Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay, Place de la Nation, INSEP and the Hippodrome d'Auteuil.

The 2027 course is described by the organiser as still under preparation; start/finish lines, access routes and race-day instructions will be published in the run-up to the event. The segment descriptions below apply only to the 2026 edition and are not a 2027 commitment.

## Timing and cut-off

### Timing system

The bib carries the timing chip; detection points are at the start, finish, KM 1, KM 21.1 and every 5 km. The chip travels with the bib and cannot be handed to another runner. The FAQ states that every finisher can look up the official time in the results section.

### Cut-off rules

The rules state one total cut-off: **6 hours from the last participant crossing the start line**. Runners overtaken by the end-of-event vehicle/system are removed from the course, unranked and do not receive a medal. The rules do not publish per-kilometre or per-station cut-off times; the wave start times available come from a third-party recap of the official participant guide, whose PDF this check did not obtain. ## Refreshment and support

### 2026 refreshment layout

The official refreshment news page lists the 2026 configuration: **13 refreshment points** in total — 9 full stations (food + drinks), 4 water points, 1 full station at the finish, and 2 Ta Energy electrolyte stations. Full stations are about 160 m long; the 2026 edition added stations and shortened gaps between them, especially in the second half, and cooling systems are set up for high-temperature days.

The official page does not publish per-station km, so this page treats that position list as third-party and defers to the official guide before race day.

### Own-container rule and station strategy

**No cups or bottles are distributed on course**; runners must bring their own container (foldable cup, soft flask or hydration pack). This is a standing requirement under the City of Paris single-use plastic restriction, not a temporary 2026 measure. Volunteers and refill stations support refilling. Runners targeting under 2:50 may use an elite-style bottle system: it requires starting in the preferred wave (start by 08:03) and holding a 4:01/km pace; used bottles are collected and reused, and bottles may be discarded only in designated zones 150 m after stations, under disqualification risk elsewhere.

Toilets are available at the start, finish and next to feed stations; no showers are provided. The course features live bands, drumming groups and entertainment zones: in 2026 there were 40 animation zones and about 200,000 spectators. Companions without a bib may not run alongside or cross the finish with a runner.

## Bib collection, bag drop and transport

### Bib collection (Run Experience / Salon du Running)

Bibs are collected at the **Run Experience** running fair, Parc des Expositions – Porte de Versailles, Hall 1, 75015 Paris. The 2026 opening hours were Thursday 15:00–20:00, Friday 10:00–20:00, Saturday 09:00–18:00; **bibs are not handed out on race day**. The fair hosts more than 150 exhibitors.

Collection materials: the PPS number (or FFA licence) uploaded to the timeto account; the QR code from the confirmation email or dashboard; an ID document. If your target time has changed and you want to switch pens, request it at the information point during expo opening with a performance certificate **less than 4 years old** from an official race; Elite-range requests go to the SOS RUNNERS desk.

### Bag drop and the separated start and finish

Luggage lockers are on **Avenue Foch**, opening from 7:00 a.m.; bags must be no larger than **45×36×20 cm**, carry the luggage label from the bib envelope, and are retrieved by showing the race number. This is the start/finish separation that catches many first-timers: the start is on the Champs-Élysées while bag drop and finish are on the Avenue Foch side. Plan the morning route from accommodation to the start, and allow time for bag-drop queues and the walk to the corral; the FAQ itself warns that queues can be long, so arrive early.

### Public transport and traffic restrictions

Expo access: metro line 12 Porte de Versailles; tramway T3a and T2; bus 39, 42 and 49; Vélib' stations nearby. For spectators on race day, RATP's #Line42km special route runs along 34 metro stations close to the course; printed maps are available at the Run Experience fair.

Traffic restrictions follow Préfecture de Police arrêté n°2026-00388 of 3 April 2026: on 12 April 2026 the Champs-Élysées was fully closed 07:00–15:00; Place de la Concorde and the George Pompidou underpasses were restricted in stages until 13:30/16:00; the Alma underpass until 18:30; Périphérique exits at Porte Dauphine, Porte d'Auteuil and Porte de Passy were closed 07:00–18:00; A13 exit 1 (Porte d'Auteuil, province→Paris direction) was closed 07:00–18:00. On race-day morning, driving or taking a taxi into central Paris must be reconfirmed against the current edition's notice; metro is the preferred option.

## Gear and rules

- **Bib:** worn visibly on the chest/abdomen; the chip is on the bib and cannot be transferred or cut off.
- **Own container:** no cups or bottles on course, so a foldable cup, soft flask or pack is essential; practise grabbing and drinking from your own container on long training runs.
- **Headphones:** the official FAQ allows running with your own music or podcast; still listen for volunteer instructions, the end-of-event vehicle and station calls.
- **Prohibited equipment:** bikes, wheeled devices and motorised devices are prohibited; wheelchair and para athletes start in their own dedicated groups under their own rules.
- **Accompanying runners:** companions without a bib cannot run alongside or cross the finish; security enforces this along the course.
- **Bottle waste:** sub-2:50 bottles may be dropped only in designated zones 150 m after stations; dropping elsewhere risks disqualification.
- **Bag prohibited items:** the bib envelope lists prohibited storage items; valuables and electronics are at own risk, and the organiser disclaims locker loss liability.
- **Pen changes:** a switch to a faster pen must be requested at expo with a performance certificate less than 4 years old; the rules require Elite/Preferential/Red runners to register in the base Yellow wave first, then email proof to parismarathon@aso.fr, with the pen-change deadline roughly two months before the event.

### Environmental risks

Paris April weather swings: race days have ranged from cold and wet at 7–9 °C to a warm afternoon above 23 °C. The course is mainly paved roads, with wind on some square and riverside sections; wet cobbles and speed bumps are slippery after rain. For warm-day planning, adjust pace and hydration using the heat-adjustment tool on this site.

## Training transfer

1. **Nearly half the field are first-timers: keep the first half controlled.** With 49% first marathoners in 2026 and a wide, slightly downhill opening on the Champs-Élysées, starting too fast is the classic mistake. Following the [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) guidance, run the first half slightly under target pace and save sensation for after 30 km.
2. **The own-container rule changes fuelling rhythm.** With no cups distributed, you cannot rely on grabbing a cup at every station; practise refilling and drinking from your own container in long runs. Combine this with the [race-day fuelling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/) and [hydration](/en/knowledge/hydration/) guidance to set a station-by-station water and gel schedule.
3. **Derive pen choice and cut-off margin from your predicted result.** If unsure which target pen fits, estimate the full marathon with the [race predictor](/en/tools/race-predict/), then check per-5-km pace and cut-off positions for a 3:30 or 4:00 goal with the [splits tool](/en/tools/splits/).
4. **Plan for morning cool and possible afternoon warmth.** April Paris starts cold and can turn mild; include one mid-length long run near target pace in your cycle, and assess warm-day pace reduction with the [heat-adjust tool](/en/tools/heat-adjust/). For the cycle and taper baseline, see the [marathon-distance training fundamentals](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/).
5. **Correct the late-wave time illusion.** Runners in 4:00 and later waves start late with similar-paced people around them, but the cut-off runs from the last gun plus 6 hours; use the [splits tool](/en/tools/splits/) to check remaining margin after 30 and 35 km, and do not relax just because everyone nearby is slow.

## Race-day execution card

**The day before (Saturday)**
- Collect the bib from Hall 1, Porte de Versailles from 9 a.m.; check the QR code, ID and PPS/FFA materials are in order, and confirm your corral and start time.
- Pack the bag within 45×36×20 cm and attach the luggage label; put the own container, gels and anti-chafe balm in the race bag.
- Check next morning's temperature and precipitation; prepare a start warm layer and post-race dry clothes; note the return route from the Avenue Foch finish to accommodation.

**Race morning**
- Arrive ahead of the corral entry requirement; bag drop is on the Avenue Foch side, so allow time from the lockers to the Champs-Élysées corral entrance.
- Warm up with the coaches leading group warm-up in the pen; pacers are assigned by corral, and ask volunteers if you cannot find your target pacer.
- Don't follow the fast group in the opening kilometres; remember stations hand out no cups, and move toward the volunteer side early to get your refill.

**Mid-course and finish**
- Check splits at every 5 km timing point; after 30 km compare against the cut-off margin and walk through stations or adjust the goal if time is tight.
- Stations are 160 m long; don't crowd the entrance; drop used bottles/containers only in the designated zones.
- After crossing the line, move through the finish refreshment area and collect the finisher medal; return to the lockers with your bib to collect your bag.

**Spectators**
- RATP's #Line42km line with 34 nearby metro stations supports multiple crossings; agree fixed meeting points with family and friends in advance. With about 200,000 spectators in 2026, landmark sections are crowded, so leave phone-contact slack.
