## What this race is

The South Downs Way 100 is Centurion Running's 100-mile race and one leg of its 100-mile Grand Slam. It starts at Matterley Bowl near Winchester, makes a loop inside the estate, joins the South Downs Way and runs east through Beacon Hill, Queen Elizabeth Country Park, Cocking, Washington, Southease, Alfriston and Jevington before finishing with a lap of the track at Eastbourne Sports Park.

The 2027 official Route section says "a 4 mile loop through Matterley Estate", then "final 96 miles remain identical to that of all previous years". The same page says the first 5 miles are entirely on private land and must not be recce'd. Read together, 4 miles is the length of the loop inside the estate and 5 miles is the private-land envelope from the start to the point where the route properly joins the National Trail. The organiser does not publish a 4-versus-5 split, and this page does not invent one.

## The course

The official course notes, in mile order:

- **Mile 0**: the start is at Matterley Bowl. The race first makes a loop inside the estate and then joins the South Downs Way. The official page says the first 5 miles are entirely private land and must not be recce'd; the Route section describes the estate loop as 4 miles. See the nesting note above.
- **Mile 23.4**: QECP is the second numbered aid station and the first crew-accessible station. The 2025 edition moved the checkpoint from the 2024 position at mile 22.6 (Bottom Field / The Meadow) to mile 23.4 at The Event Field, with the note "This check point has moved from its 2024 location". The 2026 and 2027 pages keep The Event Field. The new checkpoint is on the other side of the A3 from the main visitor centre, with free parking on the field.
- **Mile 53**: the route does not take the short line straight across the A24; it follows the South Downs Way spur through Washington village because the direct A24 crossing is "extremely fast and dangerous" and would miss the village aid station. The official page is blunt: miss the detour and fail to check in at Washington and you will be disqualified.
- **Mile 91**: after entering Alfriston, the South Downs Way turns left at the T-junction opposite the George pub. The race route turns right instead, then after 30 metres follows the brown arrows (including St Andrews Church among four destinations) down the narrow lane to the Old Chapel Centre aid station.
- **Mile 91.1**: the key navigation point of the whole race. After the white bridge, take the blue "Jevington 4 miles" South Downs Way bridleway, not the yellow "Exceat 3 miles" path. The yellow route drops to Littlington and West Dean and goes over the Seven Sisters and Beachy Head to Eastbourne, off the race route.
- **Mile 91.6**: Alfriston is the 11th numbered aid station, at the Old Chapel Centre. The photograph shows St Andrew's Church and the adjoining Clergy House in the village centre; it is a location photograph, not the inside of the aid station or race-day coverage.
- **Mile 96**: at Jevington, turn left through the churchyard before the main road, cross to the Village Hall aid station, then run down the road and back onto the South Downs Way after 100 yards.
- **Mile 98**: the trig point (a waist-high concrete pillar) is the point to leave the South Downs Way. Follow the markers downhill, past the right side of Willingdon Golf Club, via Linkway, Park Lane and Kings Drive, then along Cross Levels Way to the track. The trig point to road section is 0.61 miles. Enter the track gate, turn right onto the track and complete one lap.

The official course notes say 93% of the route is on the South Downs Way National Trail; the FAQ and vital stats say 85% trail and 15% road. These are different measures (proportion on the National Trail versus surface type), and both are kept.

On terrain, the FAQ says no single climb or descent exceeds 800 ft and the highest point is at that height. The surface includes grass, woodland single and double track, gravel and earth paths, and some pavement. The Downs are chalk, so after rain parts of the route are muddy and exposed chalk can be slippery; the ridge is exposed to wind, rain and sun. There are 95 gates or stiles, and runners must close every gate behind them. Road crossings are not marshalled; runners cross at their own risk.

## The 14 points and 13 segments

The official aid chart counts the start and finish as route points, so the course has 14 points and 13 segments. Numbers `01–12` are the official aid-station numbers; Start and Finish are unnumbered. Each segment is the difference between consecutive cumulative mileages. Kilometres use 1 mi = 1.609344 km and are rounded to 0.1; adding the rounded segment kilometres gives about 160.8 km. The difference from the official 161 km comes from the official mileages being published to 0.1 and the finish being rounded. This page does not back-calculate the official total from the segments.

The `implied speed` column is "segment distance ÷ the entire interval between the two Wave 1 cut-offs". It is not a target pace and not an arrival time. A cut-off is a leave-by limit; arrival must be earlier.

`4.49` and `7.43` are the direct result of dividing the published interval by the published distance. They are not target paces, and this page does not claim the official table has an error. They appear in adjacent segments because the 2027 Houghton Farm move changes Cocking→Houghton to 10.7 miles / 3:50 and Houghton→Washington to 7.7 miles / 1:40. If you treated Houghton's 14:50 elapsed time as a target, you would have only 1:40 to reach Washington by 16:30, an implied 7.43 km/h, far above the 5.37 km/h overall average. The "easy then hard" shape is a property of the 2027 table, not a uniform pacing plan.

## Splitting 30 hours

The official 30 hours is not "30 hours of walking". 100 miles / 30 hours = 3.33 mph = **5.37 km/h**, and that is the overall average including stops, eating, changing, queues and navigation. Every stop pushes the moving speed higher. As arithmetic examples: if the 12 numbered stations average 10 minutes each, that is 2 hours, leaving 28 hours for 161 km, or **5.75 km/h** moving average; at 15 minutes each, 3 hours, the required moving average is **5.96 km/h**. These are arithmetic illustrations, not official pacing advice.

The Wave 1 30 hours is cut into 13 intervals by the official cut-offs: 8:25 elapsed at South Harting, 14:50 at Houghton Farm, 16:30 at Washington, 25:10 at Southease, 27:30 at Alfriston, 30:00 at the finish. The first three segments to South Harting are not generous; the real trap is Houghton→Washington in 1:40. Leave Houghton at 14:50 and you must cover 7.7 miles by 16:30, an implied 7.43 km/h, far above the 5.37 overall average, before any stop. Treating Houghton's cut-off as a target will blow up at the next station.

The relatively loose interval is Cocking→Houghton: 10.7 miles in 3:50, an implied 4.49 km/h. It is not permission to walk; it is the buffer left when the 2027 table moved Houghton 2.3 miles later. The organiser does not explain the same-address change. The practical response is to arrive at Houghton early, not to treat 4.49 as a usable slow segment.

The night section starts after Washington: the official rules require a 100-lumen headtorch and spare batteries from Washington, and Botolphs 23:50, Saddlescombe 01:30 and Housedean 04:25 are all in darkness. Put the sleepiest hours before Washington and do not spend the Houghton buffer in daylight. Wave 2 starts one hour later and finishes at 12:30; the 30 hours are unchanged and the relative timetable is the same.

For a sub-24 "100 miles - One Day" buckle, 161 km / 24 h = **6.71 km/h**, again including stops, not a sprint for one segment. The official page states the buckle conditions but gives no pacing advice. The difference between the 30-hour finish buckle and the 24-hour one-day buckle is mostly about shortening stops and the night, not about how fast you run the first 50 miles.

## Start, finish and logistics

- Start: Matterley Bowl, Matterley Estate, SO21 1HW. The official page says to enter the estate from the A31 at Avington Cross Roads; parking is on site and free. Camping and parking open at 14:00 Friday and parking remains until 17:00 Sunday.
- Registration: Friday 11 June 16:00–19:30, or from 04:15 Saturday, both at Matterley Bowl. Bib and tracker collection is in person; crew passes and pacer bibs are issued at the same time.
- Start: Saturday 05:15 Wave 1 briefing, 05:30 start; 06:15 Wave 2 briefing, 06:30 start. Waves are assigned by predicted finish time and cannot be changed after publication.
- Finish: Eastbourne Sports Park, Cross Levels Way, BN21 2UF, behind Eastbourne District Hospital but with no access through the hospital. Free parking, toilets, showers, male and female changing, and hot food (runners only, vegetarian included); there is no sleeping area. Finishers complete one lap of the track.
- Shuttle: two buses from the finish back to Matterley Bowl, at 06:30 and 12:45 Sunday; the official page says parking remains until "bus returns with finishers at 1700 on Sunday".
- Mandatory kit: a fully charged mobile phone with the Race Medic and Race Director numbers, at least 1 litre of water capacity, a 1.4 m × 2 m emergency blanket, a waterproof jacket with taped seams of at least 10,000 mm, a whistle, a 100-lumen headtorch and spare batteries (required from Washington), a long-sleeved base layer or fleece carried separately and kept dry, and a cup. A missing item carries a minimum one-hour penalty; kit may be checked at the start, on course and at the finish, and refusing a check is a disqualification.
- Age: minimum 20 on race day; age falsification on the entry form carries a lifetime ban.
- Weather: official averages 15°C, 52 mm rain and 60% humidity; chalk sections are slippery when wet and the ridge is exposed. The official page does not state the averaging period, station or hourly window, and there is no published course change for weather, national-park access or stream crossings, so this page treats the figures as the organiser's June averages, not a race-day forecast, and does not put them in `courseChanges`.

Other route rules: follow the course markings and the pre-race GPX; cutting the course carries a time penalty or disqualification; storing supplies along the trail carries a two-hour penalty; leaving a checkpoint after its cut-off without handing in bib and tracker leads to disqualification and a lifetime ban. The first 5 miles at Matterley Estate are entirely private land and must not be recced.

## Entry and qualification

2027 entry opens on 19 July 2026 at 19:00, first come first served. The entry fee is £225, or £227 for non-UK Athletics affiliated runners; the official "No. of Starters" is 600. There are 100 free volunteer places. Elite applications reference an ITRA Index of 750+ for men and 600+ for women, with case-by-case review for runners not on ITRA. Entry is also possible through the 100-mile Grand Slam package.

Qualification: a previous 50-mile-or-longer race finished within 15 hours. The official home page says the qualifying window starts on 1 January 2020; the FAQ says 1 January 2021. Both are kept. A runner who does not yet have the qualifier may enter, but the organiser will contact them about their qualifying plan about eight weeks before the race.

Waiting list: the register page says "a waiting list will be in operation" once places sell out; the FAQ says "There will be no waiting list for this event". Both are kept and this page does not decide which is live.

Refunds and deferral: withdrawal more than four weeks (28 days, Friday) before the race gives a 70% refund (30% admin fee), or a deferral to 2027 with a 15% fee; within four weeks there is no refund and no deferral; pregnancy or postnatal withdrawal with written notice before four weeks gives a 100% refund. XCover refund protection can be purchased at entry and is underwritten by XCover. The 2027 register page's refund section still says "2026 place" and "2026 fee", and the deferral target also says 2027, the same year as the race; this page records the page as written and does not treat it as the operative policy.

## Difficulty and risks

In this site's editorial rating, this is a **4/5 continuous-runnable 100-mile race** (not an official grade), not a technical mountain race. The 100 miles, 12,700 ft / 3,800 m gain and 30 hours, with no single climb or descent over 800 ft, make the route broadly runnable; the real difficulty is cumulative fatigue from running so much of it, chalk trails that turn slippery after rain, an exposed ridge, 95 gates and road crossings, and the mile 91.1 navigation point, where the wrong branch drops to the Seven Sisters and Beachy Head, off the race route.

The overall 30-hour speed is 5.37 km/h, not the 3.2 km/h written on the earlier page; 3.33 mph and 5.37 km/h are the same number. That speed includes stops, so it is not a "walking is enough" floor. The official weather averages are 15°C, 52 mm rain and 60% humidity; a June day on the South Downs can be sunny and exposed or wet, windy and muddy. It is not a technical mountain race, but it is not a road ultra moved onto a trail, either.

Withdrawal and risk: the rules say that leaving an aid station after the cut-off without handing in your number and tracker leads to disqualification and a lifetime ban; conversely, withdrawing means handing in number and tracker at an aid station and being recorded. The official pages do not publish a withdrawal shuttle or a lift to the nearest station. The longest of the 13 segments is 12.0 miles (Start→Beacon Hill 11.4 and Beacon→QECP 12.0), the shortest 4.1 miles (Alfriston→Jevington); most are 4–10 miles, not "an aid station every 8–11 miles". Treat "reach the next aid station" as the withdrawal route and have an independent plan to get back from Eastbourne or a mid-course station.
