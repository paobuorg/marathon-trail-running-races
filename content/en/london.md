## 60-second verdict

The TCS London Marathon is a point-to-point city road race and one of the Abbott World Marathon Majors. The 2026 edition started on Sunday 26 April in the Greenwich/Blackheath area and finished on The Mall next to St James's Park. The organiser describes the terrain as "road, fast and flat"; the official route measures 26.2 miles, and the event overview lists 246 feet (about 75 m) of elevation gain. The course runs through south-east London, the city centre, north of the Thames and around the Isle of Dogs, and the start does not return to the finish.

Three judgements to hold first. **This is a flat city course decided by pacing control**, not climbing: the hard parts are the crowds after the three start lines merge, the wind tunnels and GPS drift around the Canary Wharf towers, and fatigue management after 30 km. **Entry channels and qualification standards are independent**: the public ballot, Good For Age age qualification, charity places, Championship places and club places follow separate review paths and must not be mixed. **The eight-hour cut-off runs from the last runner's 11:30 start, not from the elite gun.** Runners predicting more than seven hours are assigned to the Pink Start; anyone outside the eight-hour pace must be self-sufficient after drinks stations close.

Who this suits: runners with a complete marathon training background who can hold pace in dense crowds and fuel to plan. Who should not enter: runners without long road mileage who expect to cram after a ballot win, and anyone treating a Major as a sightseeing trip without reserving time for Expo collection and travel. This page is fixed to the 2026 edition, checked 2026-11-25; the 2027 edition (24–25 April 2027) must be rechecked against the organiser's current pages.

## Course and geometry

The London Marathon is a point-to-point city road race: it starts in the Greenwich/Blackheath area, runs through south-east London, the centre, north of the Thames and around the Isle of Dogs, and finishes on The Mall; it does not return to the start. The course page calls the whole route 26.2 miles, and the overview page labels the terrain "Road - fast and flat".

The published route order: runners from the three start lines merge for the first time near Woolwich before Mile 3; then run west through Charlton, Greenwich and Deptford, looping the Cutty Sark between Miles 6 and 7; onward via Rotherhithe and Jamaica Road in Bermondsey toward Tower Bridge. After crossing the bridge they are north of the Thames at 13 miles; then along The Highway east to Westferry, and around Mudchute, Docklands and Canary Wharf. Just past Mile 21 comes the 250-metre Rainbow Row; the last five miles run along The Highway, Tower Hill and Victoria Embankment, turn through Parliament Square and Birdcage Walk, and take the final turn before Buckingham Palace into The Mall to the finish.

- **Segment 1 (start → Mile 3):** each start line sets off separately and merges near Woolwich. The official advice describes a wide road with an overall net descent but real crowding; do not burn energy weaving, and do not accelerate on the Mile 3 descent.
- **Segment 2 (Mile 3 → Mile 6):** after the merge, west through Charlton, Greenwich and Deptford. The official advice is to settle into a straight line and marathon pace here.
- **Segment 3 (Mile 6 → Mile 7):** the Cutty Sark loop; the spectator guide lists it as a named viewing point.
- **Segment 4 (Mile 7 → Mile 13):** via Rotherhithe, Jamaica Road and Bermondsey to Tower Bridge. Miles 4–14 are described as relatively flat with small net negative climbs, with an "uncomfortable little uphill" before the bridge; cross and you are at 13 miles, north bank.
- **Segment 5 (Mile 13 → Mile 21):** east along The Highway to Westferry, around Mudchute, Docklands and Canary Wharf. The official advice flags that the Canary Wharf towers and narrow streets, and the Westferry underpass, cause GPS drift, and that towers and narrow streets form wind tunnels.
- **Segment 6 (Mile 21 → Mile 25):** Rainbow Row, the 250-metre celebration stretch, just past Mile 21; then via Tower Hill onto Victoria Embankment, which the official advice calls "kind to tired legs".
- **Segment 7 (Mile 25 → finish):** turning near Westminster Bridge with about a mile left, Birdcage Walk with about 1 km left; the last turn before Buckingham Palace delivers you to The Mall and the finish.

The table only says which split points the organiser published; it does not say what the elevation change is on each. Splitting 246 ft or 278 ft evenly across seven segments would be a self-made figure, and this page does not do it.

## Timing and cut-off

London publishes only an overall cut-off, not separate station cut-off times. Overall limit: eight hours from the last runner's 11:30 start, i.e. The Mall closes at 19:30; after that the finish moves to St James's Park until midnight. Anyone finishing after 19:30 must continue on the pavement. Tailwalkers and cycle marshals maintain the eight-hour pace; runners predicting over seven hours start from the Pink Start; anyone outside eight-hour pace must be self-sufficient after drinks stations close, and toilets remain available until midnight. The start lines themselves close at 11:30 and late arrivals cannot start.

Cut-off feasibility is worked from gun time in the table below. In 2026 the last wave started 11:30 and The Mall closed 19:30, an overall limit of 8:00:00 (480 minutes).

**Bag drop and logistics.** Free bag drop is provided; only the official event kitbag is accepted, with nothing attached to it. There is no bag service in the Green assembly area; do not put phones or valuables in the drop bag. Riders travel free to the start on designated public transport with the bib, and on designated routes after the finish until 20:00; see the transport section.

**Official pacers.** Event pacers cover target finishes from 3:00:00 to 7:30:00 and appear in the corresponding waves. Pacers are contracted event staff; runners may follow an official pacer, but may not bring private friends onto the course to act as pacemakers.

**Crew and pacing boundary.** The entry conditions prohibit any artificial aid or wheeled device on course (the wheelchair division follows separate rules) and prohibit bringing pets; places are personal and may not be transferred or worn by another person. A wheelchair team may have four support members, at most two pushing at any time, and the chair may have no gears or any mechanical, powered or electronic assistance. Beyond the wheelchair support team and official pacers, the official terms reviewed for this page do not authorise private crew to hand supplies, pace runners or pass information on course. Mass-participant runners should not import trail-race-style private aid stations or roadside pacing into London. ## Training transfer

What this course means for training, four points:

1. **A flat city course still requires long runs.** The official climb figure is small, but the repeated impact of 42.195 km and late-race fatigue remain; training must cover continuous running of comparable duration, not only short intervals for speed. Long runs: [long run](/en/knowledge/long-run/) and [marathon training plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/).
2. **Pacing control is the first training target.** The first three miles are crowded with a net descent and easy to run too fast; fix target pace with tempo runs and pacing practice beforehand, and follow your own plan on race day; whether to follow an official pacer depends on your target band. Pacing zones: [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) and [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/).
3. **Fix fuelling habits in training.** Race day provides water, Lucozade Sport, gels, hydration stations and Vaseline, but your long-standing personal supplies still need to come from you; rehearse hourly calories, fluid and salt in long runs, and arrange carb loading through [long-run fuelling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/) and [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/) — do not change diet at the last minute.
4. **Plan for the wall after 30 km.** The Canary Wharf to Rainbow Row section stacks crowds and wind; arrange volume and taper through [periodization](/en/knowledge/periodization/) and [taper](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/), and avoid adding volume in the final two weeks.

## Three native diagnoses: real hard points, who should not come, the most misread numbers

**Real hard points.** On a flat city course the difficulty is not climbing but three things: early crowds stacked on a descent, with the three start lines merging at Mile 3 and the first five miles easy to run too fast; mid-late GPS drift and wind tunnels in the Canary Wharf towers and narrow streets, where watch trace is unreliable and mile markers and pacing plan must be trusted; and fatigue after 30 km, when the late section passes Tower Bridge, the Embankment and Birdcage Walk, with strong emotional swings and an easy pace fade.

**Who should not come:** runners without a marathon or equivalent long-distance volume who expect to cram; those who cannot hold pace in dense crowds and get pulled into the group rhythm; those unprepared for late-April temperature swings and wind or rain; and anyone treating a Major as a sightseeing trip without reserving time for the Expo (four days before) and the walk to the start. GFA and Championship run on certified times, the public ballot on luck, charity places on fundraising with the charity; the channels cannot be swapped.

**Most misread numbers:** first, elevation — 246 ft and 278 ft are two official display figures and must not be averaged into one "official climb"; second, the cut-off — eight hours runs from the last runner's 11:30 start, not the elite 09:30 gun; third, "26.2 miles" versus the embedded route's "26.1 miles" — the former is the event body figure, the latter the embedded route's own measurement; fourth, meeting GFA standards only grants an application; 6,000 places are allocated fastest-first, so qualifying is not the same as being selected.

## Risks and exit points

- **Overall cut-off:** eight hours from the last runner's 11:30 start; The Mall closes 19:30, then the finish moves to St James's Park until midnight. After 19:30 finishers must use the pavement. Start lines close 11:30; late arrivals cannot start.
- **Back-of-pack:** tailwalkers and cycle marshals hold the eight-hour pace; predictions over seven hours start Pink; outside eight-hour pace you are self-sufficient after stations close, toilets until midnight. Behind eight-hour pace, the team may run occasional manual bib scans.
- **Dropping out:** the official exit path is to tell the nearest First Aid Point staff; wait for a sweep bus to the finish, or travel free on public transport with the bib; the bus may take a while. No point-by-point withdrawal table is published; do not treat every metro station along the route as an official exit.
- **Medical:** enter only if medically fit; running injured or ill is dangerous; complete the medical information on the bib back; the guide points to the medical advice from race medical director Professor Sanjay Sharma; call 999 in an emergency.
- **Crowds and navigation:** the first three miles are crowded with a net descent — do not accelerate downhill; there is an uphill before Tower Bridge; towers, narrow streets and the underpass cause GPS drift, so use mile markers; wind tunnels form between towers.
- **Spectators and finish:** spectators must not go to the start, which is open to runners only; Meet and Greet is up to about 30 minutes' walk from the finish, with alphabetical points; phone signal may be unstable in crowded areas; Westminster station is exit-only 07:00–19:00.

## Travel and Expo collection

- **Planning tools:** the guide routes public transport to the public planners of TfL and National Rail; assembly area and wave are emailed about three weeks before Marathon Day.
- **Free travel:** runners travel free from London stations to the start on Southeastern trains, and on the DLR, Elizabeth Line, London Underground, Overground and buses with the bib; the official note says explicitly "do not tap in or out at any London station". Free travel applies to the runner only, not family, friends or spectators.
- **Nearest stations:** Blue and Yellow assembly areas map to Blackheath, about a 10-minute walk; Pink to Maze Hill, about 10 minutes; Red to Greenwich, about 20 minutes. The Green runner's nearest station is left blank in the guide table; this page does not guess.
- **DLR:** on 26 April 2026 DLR services from Tower Gateway and Lewisham start about 05:30; services from Bank and other DLR routes start about 07:00.
- **River:** Thames Clippers run from central London to Greenwich Pier every 20 minutes; a short walk reaches the assembly areas.
- **Spectator movement:** spectators must not go to the start; the guide suggests walking between route positions and following marshals. Crowded points — Cutty Sark, Canada Water, Bermondsey, Tower Bridge, Canary Wharf, Limehouse, Tower Hill, Westminster — have alternative stations; Cutty Sark and Limehouse may queue 90 minutes at peak.
- **After the finish:** free travel on the Underground, Overground, Elizabeth Line, buses and DLR until 20:00; no free Southeastern travel after the race; Westminster is exit-only. Meet and Greet sits around Whitehall, Horse Guards Parade and Horse Guards Road, by letter.
- **Airport and driving:** the guide and spectator guide publish no airport shuttle, transfer time or fare; this page does not write "how long Heathrow/Gatwick takes to the start". The organiser publishes no runner parking plan, and the spectator guide confirms road closures on race day. Check airport connections yourself through the TfL and National Rail public planners.
- **Expo:** the bib (with timing chip) is collected in person at the ExCeL London Event Running Show over the four days before; no race-day collection, no mailing.

## History and origins

The first London Marathon was held on 29 March 1981: more than 20,000 people wanted to run, 7,747 were accepted and 6,255 finished, led home jointly by Dick Beardsley (USA) and Inge Simonsen (NOR); Joyce Smith won the women's race. The event was founded by Chris Brasher and John Disley after the New York Marathon inspired them, and it is one of the younger city stops among the Majors.

Historical title counts: Ingrid Kristiansen (NOR) and Eliud Kipchoge (KEN) each won four men's titles; Tanni Grey-Thompson won the women's wheelchair race six times and David Weir the men's wheelchair race eight times. Broadcast reaches more than 196 countries and territories, with four to five million UK viewers on the BBC; elites compete for US$308,000 in prize money. In 2024 the event received the World Athletics Heritage Plaque, recognising its outstanding contribution to the worldwide history and development of the sport.
