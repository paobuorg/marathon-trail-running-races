## What this course is

The London Marathon is a point-to-point city road race: it starts around Greenwich and Blackheath, crosses south-east London, the centre, the north bank of the Thames and the Isle of Dogs, and finishes on The Mall beside St James's Park. The official course page calls the route 26.2 miles, and the event overview describes the terrain as "Road - fast and flat". The start is around Greenwich and Blackheath and the finish is on The Mall; the route does not return to the start.

The official route sequence is: runners from the three start points first meet just before Mile 3, around Woolwich; the route then turns west through Charlton, Greenwich and Deptford, loops around Cutty Sark between Miles 6 and 7, and continues through Rotherhithe and Jamaica Road in Bermondsey towards Tower Bridge. After crossing the bridge, runners are north of the river and have clocked 13 miles; the route then heads east along The Highway to Westferry and circles through Mudchute, Docklands and Canary Wharf. Just past Mile 21 is Rainbow Row, a 250-metre stretch in Limehouse. The final five miles take in The Highway, Tower Hill and Victoria Embankment before Parliament Square, Birdcage Walk and the last corner in front of Buckingham Palace lead onto The Mall and the finish.

At the 2026 edition, Sabastian Sawe won the men's race in 1:59:30, becoming the first man to run an official marathon inside two hours. Tigst Assefa won the women's race in 2:15:41; World Athletics lists both performances as world and area records. Yomif Kejelcha was second in 1:59:41 and Jacob Kiplimo third in 2:00:28, so the top three all beat the previous world record.

The participant guide places runners in five assembly areas and allocates start waves by predicted finish time; runners from the three start points merge before Mile 3. The course page warns that the first three miles have wide roads and a net downhill, but are fairly crowded: the descent is not a sprint section.

The card at the top of this page uses 42,195 m and 75 m. 42,195 m is the standard marathon distance, and the official "26.2 miles" is the usual rounded form of that distance; the official pages do not state the full distance in kilometres. The participant guide says distance markers and timing clocks are at "every mile and every 5K point", and the course-map legend includes a KM marker; that proves the marking system exists, not that an elevation profile was published. Segment notes, with positions from the course page and pacing descriptions from the organiser's coach advice on the same page:

- **Segment 1 (start → Mile 3)**: the three start points run separately and merge for the first time near Woolwich. Official pacing advice calls this section wide, net downhill and fairly crowded; do not waste energy weaving, and do not pick up speed on the descent around Mile 3.
- **Segment 2 (Mile 3 → Mile 6)**: after the merge, the route runs west through Charlton, Greenwich and Deptford. The official advice for this section is to find a straight line and settle into marathon pace.
- **Segment 3 (Mile 6 → Mile 7)**: the loop around Cutty Sark. The official spectator page lists it as a named viewing point.
- **Segment 4 (Mile 7 → Mile 13)**: Rotherhithe, Jamaica Road and Bermondsey lead to Tower Bridge. The organiser describes Miles 4–14 as "relatively flat (still with a small net negative elevation)" and warns of a "slightly uncomfortable incline just before Tower Bridge"; beyond it runners are north of the Thames at 13 miles.
- **Segment 5 (Mile 13 → Mile 21)**: east along The Highway to Westferry, then Mudchute, Docklands and Canary Wharf. The official advice warns that tall buildings and narrow streets in Canary Wharf, and the Westferry Underpass, can cause GPS problems, while the buildings and streets act as wind tunnels; there are also several twists and turns.
- **Segment 6 (Mile 21 → Mile 25)**: just past Mile 21 is Rainbow Row, described by the organiser as a 250-metre celebration stretch; then Tower Hill leads into Victoria Embankment. The organiser calls the Tower Hill to Victoria Embankment section favourable for tired legs.
- **Segment 7 (Mile 25 → finish)**: a right turn by Westminster Bridge with about one mile to go, Birdcage Walk with about one kilometre to go, and the final turn at Buckingham Palace into The Mall. The finish is on The Mall.

This table shows only which split points the organiser publishes. It does not show elevation by segment. Dividing 246 feet or 278 feet evenly across seven segments would be a number invented by this site, and this page does not do it.

Five things matter when reading the table:

- **The water pattern**: the organiser says Buxton water is available "every three miles from Mile 3 to 12, then every two miles from Mile 12 to 24". Literally that means 3, 6, 9, 12, then 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24; Mile 12 belongs to both rules. The table lists each mile once rather than writing Mile 12 twice.
- **Sports drink and gels**: Lucozade Sport is at Miles 7, 15, 21 and 23; Lucozade Sport energy gels are at Miles 13 and 19. The organiser lists sports drink and gels as separate products from water.
- **Refill points**: Water Refill Stations are at Miles 5, 10, 14, 17 and 20 for bottle belts and hydration packs. The organiser also has refill stations in the Start and Finish Areas and along the route.
- **Vaseline**: Miles 14, 17, 20 and 22. The official pages publish no mile-by-mile medical-point list, only "the nearest First Aid Point", so this table does not invent medical locations.
- **Toilets**: at the Start, every mile from Mile 1 to Mile 24, and at the Finish. Accessible toilets are at the Start, the Finish, Miles 1 and 2, and every even mile after that up to Mile 24.

The official pages publish no individual cut-off for any aid point. The overall cut-off is eight hours after the last runner starts at 11:30, which puts the closure of The Mall at 19:30; after that the finish moves to St James's Park until midnight. Anyone finishing after 19:30 must move onto the pavement. The guide also says runners predicting a finish over seven hours start on the Pink Start, and runners outside eight-hour pace must be self-sufficient after Drinks Stations close; toilets remain available until midnight. That is why every row's cut-off reads "not published": the total cut-off cannot be copied onto each station as if it were a station time.

The official pages state that drinks stations are accessible for wheelchair users, and volunteers hand out drinks and can open bottles; bins are provided at every Drinks Station for gel wrappers, cups and other litter; runners are asked to drain bottles before discarding them. The participant guide, page 14, says runners receive Buxton natural mineral water and Lucozade Sport at the finish.

## Surface and terrain

The event overview describes the terrain as road, fast and flat. The official pages publish no split between asphalt, concrete, paving or bridge deck, no roughness figure, no gradient distribution and no elevation profile. This page writes no percentages and does not translate "city marathon" into a list of unsourced surface numbers.

The organiser's own pacing advice gives these qualitative descriptions: the first three miles have wide roads, a net downhill and crowding; Miles 4–14 are relatively flat with a small net negative elevation; there is a slightly uncomfortable incline before Tower Bridge; the tall buildings and narrow streets of Canary Wharf and the Westferry Underpass cause GPS problems and wind tunnels; Tower Hill to Victoria Embankment is favourable for tired legs; Birdcage Walk and the corner at Buckingham Palace are the final emotional section. Treat those as the character of the route, not as gradient data.

The organiser gives two gain figures, 246 feet and 278 feet; the official pages publish no loss, high point or low point. This page does not state an elevation that the organiser has not published. The course page also notes that a blue line marks the shortest route, faster runners should be allowed to use it, walkers should move left, and distance markers and timing clocks appear every mile and every 5K. The organiser defines the surface as road.

## Risks and escape points

The official hard rules are:

- **Overall cut-off**: eight hours after the last runner starts at 11:30, The Mall closes at 19:30; the finish then moves to St James's Park until midnight. Anyone finishing after 19:30 must continue on the pavement. The start lines themselves close at 11:30, and late arrivals may not start.
- **Back of the pack**: tailwalkers and cycle marshals hold an eight-hour pace; runners predicting over seven hours start on the Pink Start; anyone outside eight-hour pace must be self-sufficient after Drinks Stations close, with toilets available until midnight.
- **Dropping out**: the official escape instruction is to tell staff at the nearest First Aid Point. A runner can wait for a sweep minibus to the Finish or use public transport free by showing the bib; the organiser warns that the sweep minibus may take a long time. The official pages publish no point-by-point escape table, and this page does not treat every Underground station along the route as an official exit.
- **Medical**: only take part if fit and well; running with illness or injury is dangerous. Medical information goes on the back of the bib; the guide directs runners to the event Medical Director Professor Sanjay Sharma's advice; call 999 in an emergency.
- **Headphones and equipment**: non-noise-cancelling or bone-conduction headphones are allowed, or one earpiece, but runners must hear safety instructions. Handbikes, rollerblades, skateboards, scooters and motorised gear are prohibited.
- **Crowds and navigation**: the first three miles are crowded and net downhill, and the official advice is not to accelerate on the descent; there is an incline before Tower Bridge; the tall buildings, narrow streets and underpass in Canary Wharf cause GPS drift, and the organiser recommends mile markers rather than the watch; the buildings also create wind tunnels.
- **Spectators and finish**: spectators should not travel to the Start, which is for participants only; the Meet and Greet Area is up to about 30 minutes' walk from the Finish, with lettered meeting points; phone reception may be patchy in crowded areas, so do not rely on a phone alone; Westminster station is exit-only from 07:00 to 19:00.
- **Baggage**: only official event kitbags are accepted, with nothing attached; there is no bag drop in the Green Assembly Area. Do not put phones or valuables in the kitbag.

## Getting there

The participant guide and spectator guide give transport information for rail, Underground, DLR and river boats.

- **Planning tools**: the participant guide points public transport planning to TfL and National Rail. Assembly-area and wave details are emailed about three weeks before race day.
- **Free travel**: runners showing their bib travel free from London stations to the Start on Southeastern services, and on the DLR, Elizabeth line, London Underground, Overground and buses. The guide specifically says not to tap in or out at any London station. Free travel is for the runner, not family, friends or spectators.
- **Nearest stations**: Blue and Yellow assembly areas use Blackheath, about a 10-minute walk; Pink uses Maze Hill, about 10 minutes; Red uses Greenwich, about 20 minutes. The nearest-station cell for Green is blank in the guide's table, and this page does not guess it.
- **DLR**: on Sunday 26 April, DLR services start at about 05:30 from Tower Gateway and Lewisham, and at about 07:00 from Bank and all other DLR routes.
- **River**: Thames Clippers run every 20 minutes from central London to Greenwich Pier, a short walk from the assembly areas.
- **Spectators**: spectators should not travel to the Start. The spectator guide recommends walking between parts of the route and following marshals, and gives alternative stations for busy points at Cutty Sark, Canada Water, Bermondsey, Tower Bridge, Canary Wharf, Limehouse, Tower Hill and Westminster. Queues at Cutty Sark and Limehouse can reach 90 minutes at peak times.
- **After the finish**: official post-event travel is free until 20:00 on the Underground, Overground, Elizabeth line, buses and DLR; Southeastern does not offer free post-event travel; Westminster is exit-only. The Meet and Greet Area is up to about 30 minutes' walk from the Finish, with lettered meeting points at Whitehall, Horse Guards Parade and Horse Guards Road.
- **Airports**: the official participant and spectator guides publish no airport transfer routes, journey times or fares, so this page gives no "how long from Heathrow or Gatwick" figures. For airport connections, use the TfL and National Rail planning tools and check them yourself.
- **Driving**: the official pages publish no participant parking plan, and the spectator guide states that road closures will be in place on race day. This page offers no unverified car park.

## Season and weather

The 2026 race was held on 26 April. To prepare for the same window, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 19–27 April 2026, nine days, at a London grid point. The API returned the grid point 51.4938 N, 0.1630 W at 16 m elevation; these are modelled/reanalysis grid values, not measurements on the course and not an official race-day meteorological record.

- Daily maximum: 13.4 °C to 21.7 °C, median 18.1 °C.
- Daily minimum: 4.6 °C to 7.9 °C, median 6.4 °C.
- Total precipitation over nine days: 0.6 mm, all on 27 April; the other eight days recorded 0.0 mm, and no day reached 1 mm.
- Race day, 26 April, at the grid point: maximum 19.5 °C, minimum 7.5 °C, precipitation 0.0 mm.

These figures describe one nine-day window, not a climate guarantee. Start waves run from 09:30 to 11:30. The tall buildings and narrow streets of Canary Wharf also create wind tunnels, a point the organiser's pacing advice makes explicitly.

The official pages fetched for this page publish no heat or cold cancellation threshold and no wet-bulb rule, so this page does not state "above a certain temperature, X happens". The confirmed official measures are hydration, medical information on the bib, and asking for help at a First Aid Point if unwell.

## Difficulty

The official pages publish no single "difficulty grade", and this page does not invent one. **This site's rating**: a fast, flat urban road marathon. The verifiable dimensions:

- 26.2 miles; this site's entity stores the standard marathon distance of 42,195 m.
- Official gain 246 feet (about 75 m), with the embedded route giving 278 feet (about 85 m); the official pages give no official loss figure.
- Terrain defined by the organiser as road, fast and flat; this page does not add trail, altitude or technical ground.
- Overall cut-off eight hours after the last runner starts at 11:30; The Mall closes at 19:30, with the St James's Park finish until midnight.
- Three start lines, five assembly areas and wave starts; a merge before Mile 3 and GPS and wind-tunnel issues in Canary Wharf.
- The 19–27 April 2026 grid window had daily maxima of 13.4–21.7 °C and minima of 4.6–7.9 °C.

The official figures support low climb and a road surface; the site rating above is an editorial judgement, not an official grade, and does not replace the official pages.
