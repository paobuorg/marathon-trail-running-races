## What this race is

QMT135 is the flagship race of the Québec Mega Trail. It starts in Baie-Saint-Paul in Charlevoix, follows the St. Lawrence on the La Baie, Gabrielle Roy Est, Louise-Gasnier and Gabrielle Roy Ouest trails to Mont à Liguori (Le Massif de Charlevoix), then runs almost entirely on the Sentier des Caps de Charlevoix through Cap Brûlé to Saint-Tite-des-Caps. It continues through the Dalle Humide into the Sainte-Anne river canyon and the Mestachibo trail to Jean-Larose falls, climbs Mont-Sainte-Anne almost twice, and spends its last 20 km on the north side of the mountain before returning south to the finish.

## The course

The organiser's description gives these points in order:

- From the Hôtel & Spa Le Germain Charlevoix in Baie-Saint-Paul, the route follows the St. Lawrence on the La Baie, Gabrielle Roy Est, Louise-Gasnier and Gabrielle Roy Ouest trails to Mont à Liguori, the Le Massif de Charlevoix.
- It then runs almost entirely on the Sentier des Caps de Charlevoix. The French page says this section takes runners through Cap Brûlé; the English page does not name the new section in the same paragraph.
- It reaches the village of Saint-Tite-des-Caps, the one aid station the organiser ties to a kilometre: KM 80.
- From there it crosses the Dalle Humide trail into the Sainte-Anne river canyon and the Mestachibo trail to Jean-Larose falls. Poles are banned in the Mestachibo section.
- Mont-Sainte-Anne follows and must be climbed almost twice. The last 20 km run on the north side of the mountain before returning south to the finish.
- The finish is at Mont-Sainte-Anne; the official shuttle leaves from the main parking lot there, which places the finish area at that lot.

The graphic uses equal-width cards, so the 134 km are not compressed into a true distance scale. It answers three table questions: which segments are steepest, how long each one is, and where the aid station, drop bag and crew points sit. Every gain figure comes from the official static profile. The 11 segments add up to **5,944 m**, **56 m** short of the official **6,000 m**; the difference is not spread across any segment.

Using "segment gain ÷ segment distance" gives a simple climbing density in m/km. That is this page's arithmetic, not an official gradient, and it ignores descent:

- **Sommet 1**: 4.2 km / +633 m = **150.7 m/km**, the highest of the 11. **Sommet 2**: 4.6 km / +393 m = **85.4 m/km**.
- **Cap Gribane**: 13.7 km / +669 m = **48.8 m/km**; **Le Massif**: 19.3 km / +921 m = **47.7 m/km**; **Halte de l'Arche**: 19.8 km / +922 m = **46.6 m/km**.
- The lowest is the final 9.1 km / +110 m = **12.1 m/km**, but it arrives after 125 km, so it cannot be paced as "the flattest segment".

A 20:00 start puts the first 39.1 km to Le Massif in the dark; the Sentier des Caps cliff-edge section then crosses midnight into the morning, so headlamp and spare power cannot be planned for "the first segment" only. Three task blocks are easier to use than 11 separate rows:

1. **0–39.1 km (segments 1–2)**: town to Le Massif, 1,843 m of climbing; Le Massif has drop bag #1 and crew access.
2. **39.1–101.5 km (segments 3–7)**: Cap du Salut, Cap Gribane, Saint-Tite-des-Caps, Mestachibo and MSA; the first three cliff-edge stations ban crew, Saint-Tite has drop bag #2 and MSA has drop bag #3.
3. **101.5–134 km (segments 8–11)**: the two Sommet climbs, Fondeur and the finish; Sommet has no vehicle access, Fondeur allows crew, and 9.1 km remain after Fondeur.

## Start, finish and logistics

- Start: the Hôtel & Spa Le Germain Charlevoix in Baie-Saint-Paul, with the start in the hotel's interior courtyard. The schedule links a Google Maps place but gives no start-line coordinates; the map anchor here is a town-level geocode for Baie-Saint-Paul, not the hotel door.
- Finish: Mont-Sainte-Anne, with the shuttle leaving from the main parking lot. The FAQ says runners may park free at the Mont-Sainte-Anne main lot but may not leave a car at Baie-Saint-Paul or the other start sites.
- Shuttle: free and for runners only. Boarding at 16:30 and departure at 17:00 on 2 July from the Mont-Sainte-Anne main parking lot, about one hour. Runners who drive themselves must be self-supported and cannot park at the start.
- Bib pickup and gear check: 17:00-19:00 on 2 July at the start hotel. QMT135 is the only race with a mandatory pre-race gear check. Each missing item adds 30 minutes; more than two missing items means disqualification.
- Crew: one accredited person per runner, requested at bib pickup. Crew may assist only at Massif, Saint-Tite-des-Caps, MSA, Summit 1 & 2 and Auberge du Fondeur. Summit has no vehicle access; a gondola ticket can be bought at MSA customer service, running 10:00-15:30, and outside those hours the station is reached on foot only.
- Drop bags: three 20-litre bags issued by the organiser and collected at the start, going to Massif (#1), Saint-Tite-des-Caps (#2) and MSA (#3). They return progressively to the convention centre near the finish.
- Toilets: only at Massif, St-Tite-des-Caps, Sommet and Fondeur, plus the start and finish areas.
- There are no lockers. Pacers are allowed on QMT135 only, over the last 32 km, starting at MSA (km 104.2) or Auberge du Fondeur (km 125); they must be registered at bib pickup, pay CAD 10 in cash for a special bib, and carry the same mandatory gear as the runner.
- Poles: allowed except in the Mestachibo section (135/80/50 km); they must be carried for the whole race, cannot be picked up mid-race and cannot go in a drop bag.

## Entry and qualification

Entry requires a previous ultra trail race worth at least 75 points, calculated as 1 km = 1 point and 100 m of gain = 1 point. Road races and backyard ultras do not count. The organiser's example: 60 km with 1,500 m of gain = 60 + 15 = 75. QMT's own 50 km, the 48 km Trail du Coureur des Bois and the 50 km Trail La Clinique du Coureur are listed as upgraded races that qualify directly.

The 2027 registration route is inconsistent across official pages: the race-page button goes to `runreg.com/qmt2026`, the FAQ points to `raceroster.com/events/2027/130223/quebec-mega-trail`, and the FAQ's pacer link returns to `runreg.com/qmt2026`. The race page footer shows "223 remaining places" without a stated edition or update date, so it is not treated here as a live 2027 figure.

There is no waiting list. Places freed by cancellations and refunds are released in batches after the 16 December, 16 March and 16 May refund deadlines. Bib transfers are no longer allowed. The minimum age for QMT135 is 18. Entry fees, field caps and lottery ratios are not given on the pages read and are not filled in here.

On qualification, the official news page names QMT135 as a Western States 100 qualifier and says finishers earn 5 ITRA points; the QMT135 page carries an unnamed "Qualifying race, 32:00:00 or less" badge. The year-by-year lottery relationships are not spelled out on the official pages and are not inferred here.

## Season and weather

Open-Meteo's Baie-Saint-Paul town record for 2–4 July, 2015–2025 (33 days): daily maximum 16.4–32.5 °C, median 24.8 °C; daily minimum 8.9–21.2 °C, median 14.3 °C; 11 of 33 days had at least 1 mm precipitation, 4 had at least 5 mm.

This is the Baie-Saint-Paul grid, not Mont-Sainte-Anne, Saint-Tite-des-Caps or the Sentier des Caps ridge. July in Charlevoix can be 30 °C by day and about 10 °C at night, or continuously wet enough to make roots, rock slabs and the Mestachibo gorge slippery; a 20:00 start means the first night and the following morning feel different. The organiser publishes no race-period ridge measurements, and this page does not treat town data as ridge data.

## Difficulty and risk

QMT135's official technical grade is 5/5, with +6,000 / −5,859 m, a 37-hour limit and a 26-hour average finish. The difficulty is not altitude but 134 km of continuous undulation, the cliff-edge single track of Sentier des Caps, the new Cap Brûlé section, wet rock in the Mestachibo gorge (poles banned), and two final climbs of Mont-Sainte-Anne. The 20:00 start puts much of Sentier des Caps in the dark, raising headlamp and navigation load; no per-segment technical grade is published, and this page does not turn the official 5/5 into "unrunnable".

Retreat and risk: the official rules only say that a runner arriving after the cut-off must hand in the bib at a feed zone; no evacuation vehicle, shuttle or transfer time is published. In practice, withdraw at the nearest staffed checkpoint and hand in the bib there; do not leave the marked route to find a road through the forest. Medical decisions belong to the official medical team. Night-time and cliff-edge rescue access is poorer than on a road race, so plan on reaching the next accessible point rather than being collected by a car at any moment.
