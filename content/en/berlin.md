## 60-second verdict

The BMW BERLIN-MARATHON is a city road race and one of the Abbott World Marathon Majors. The 2026 edition was the 52nd BMW BERLIN-MARATHON, held on Sunday 27 September 2026 on Straße des 17. Juni in the Tiergarten, with the finish behind the Brandenburg Gate. The route measures 42.195 km; the SCC course page lists elevation as "tba", while the Abbott World Marathon Majors official magazine reports about 73 m of gain and 79 m of loss. The organiser calls it the flattest course among the Majors.

Three judgements to hold first. **This course is defined by long straight roads, low climb and pace friendliness — not by zero undulation.** The organiser publishes no per-segment gradient; the 73/79 m figures come from the Majors magazine and must not be split into per-kilometre climb numbers. **Entry runs on lottery plus a fastlane qualifying track, not first-come first-served.** Public lottery, Fastlane time qualification and the Sozialticket S discounted places run in parallel; the 2027 fee is €215, charged only after a place is assigned. **Only two official cut-off gates exist:** km 33 and the finish, with an overall limit of 6:15 hours. In 2026 the km 33 gate was 3:40 pm and the finish 5:00 pm; in 2027 they are 3:50 pm and 5:15 pm. The two editions' numbers must not be mixed.

Who this suits: runners who have already completed a road marathon, can hold steady pace on long straight roads, and will not be pulled along by an elite pace group. Who should not enter: those without continuous long-distance training who expect to cram after a lottery win, and anyone who reads "world-record course" as "an easy finish for everyone". This page is fixed to the 2026 edition, checked 2026-11-25; the 2027 edition (26 September 2027, the first running under the GENERALI BERLIN-MARATHON name) must be rechecked against current official pages.

## Training transfer

What this course means for training, four points:

1. **Long-road pacing is the core.** Long straights, few turns and a small official climb figure; training should fix target pace over long distances rather than practise climbing. Long runs: [long run](/en/knowledge/long-run/) and [marathon training plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/).
2. **Do not get pulled by elite pace.** Berlin is a world-record course and the start crowd can run fast; fix your own target pace with tempo runs beforehand and follow your own plan on race day. Pacing zones: [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) and [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/).
3. **Fuel to the 15-station rhythm.** Combined and plain-water stations alternate, with a gel at KM 27.5; rehearse personal supplies in long runs, and own-refreshment delivery only at the eight designated points. Fuelling: [long-run fuelling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/) and [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/).
4. **Plan for late-September temperature swing.** Morning cold and afternoon warming must be covered in training; arrange volume and taper through [periodization](/en/knowledge/periodization/) and [taper](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/).

## Three native diagnoses: real hard points, who should not come, the most misread numbers

**Real hard points:** on a low-climb, long-straight course the difficulty is not climbing but three things: crowding and road-width changes across six start waves, where the early section can pull you fast; the late-September temperature swing with possible afternoon warming, requiring start warmth and late-race cooling; and the mental fatigue of long straights after 25 km, where pace can silently fade on a flat surface.

**Who should not come:** runners without a marathon or equivalent long-distance volume who expect to cram; those who treat a "world-record course" as an easy course and lack pacing discipline; those unprepared for both early-morning cold and afternoon warming; and those who cannot refuse roadside aid outside the eight designated points or do not understand the crew boundary. Fastlane runs on a two-year certified time, the lottery on luck, Sozialticket on discounted status; the three tracks cannot be swapped.

**Most misread numbers:** first, climb — 73 m / 79 m is the Majors magazine figure while the SCC course page lists elevation tba; do not treat 73 m as the SCC figure or average it over each kilometre. Second, cut-off — the 2026 3:40 pm / 5:00 pm and 2027 3:50 pm / 5:15 pm gates must not be mixed. Third, the GPX — the 2025 file has planar 42.1836 km, 508 points and zero elevation; it cannot serve as 2026 geometry or produce a climb figure. Fourth, "15 aid stations" comprises 9 plain water points and 6 combined points; the KM 27.5 gel does not count as an extra station.

## Risks and exit points

- **Cut-off:** 2026 overall limit 6:15, km 33 gate 3:40 pm, finish close 5:00 pm; beyond either gate you must leave the course and be taken to the finish by sweep bus — continuing is not permitted. 2027 moves the gates to 3:50 pm and 5:15 pm; do not mix editions.
- **Mid-race withdrawal:** official pages publish no trail-style exit-point list; the race-day page describes only the sweep bus. Do not treat metro stations near start/finish as mid-race exits; the listed U-Bahn/S-Bahn stations serve the start/finish area.
- **Medical removal:** the medical team and paramedics are authorised and obligated to remove injured or overexerting runners; the course has medical teams, cycle doctors, aid organisations and Berlin Fire Brigade patrols, which does not mean a fixed medical point at every location.
- **Weather does not auto-cancel:** unless the German weather service issues a warning, the race proceeds; schedule and course may still change.
- **Heat precedent:** the Majors magazine notes the previous edition met unusually warm weather and encourages carrying a water bottle; an organiser statement, not a 2026 measurement.
- **Temporary re-routing:** the race-day page states "Schedule and course are subject to change"; this page fixes the 2026 version and does not guarantee 2027 identity.
- **Wheelchair division:** wheelchair and handcycle athletes enter from 8:00 and must wear a helmet; mass runners should not treat wheelchair access arrangements as their own exit plan.

## Travel and Expo collection

- **Airport (BER):** the train station sits underground at U2 level under Terminal 1; the airport express FEX links Hauptbahnhof, Potsdamer Platz, Südkreuz and BER every 15 minutes from 4:00 to 1:00; about 23 min to Hauptbahnhof, 19 min to Potsdamer Platz, 14 min to Südkreuz. Regional trains RE20, RB22, RB24, RB32 also stop at BER; S9 and S85 every 20 minutes; X7 express bus runs from U Rudow to the airport every 5–10 minutes. All from the BER official public transport page.
- **Rail:** S-Bahn stations near start/finish are Brandenburger Tor, Berlin Hauptbahnhof and Potsdamer Platz; regional trains also stop at Hauptbahnhof and Potsdamer Platz; take FEX or a regional train from BER to one of these, then S-Bahn or walk into the area. The organiser advises public transport and discourages driving.
- **City movement:** nearby U-Bahn stations Unter den Linden, Bundestag, Brandenburger Tor, Potsdamer Platz; runner entrances Platz der Republik (all) and Bremer Weg (purple bib, poncho marker, non-K).
- **Bib as ticket:** the 2026 briefing says the bib works as an ABC-zone public transport ticket from 24 Sep 0:00 to 28 Sep 3:00; the 2027 page lists 23–26 September. The windows differ; follow your edition's start card / briefing. The fee includes a four-day ABC transport ticket.
- **EXPO:** two official pages disagree — the 2026 briefing places the MARATHON EXPO by BRITA at Flughafen Tempelhof, Platz der Luftbrücke 5 (U6 to Platz der Luftbrücke plus about a 10-minute walk, or bus M43/248); the entry page writes EXPO by BRITA at Messe Berlin. Both official statements are kept side by side; confirm on the start card and current announcement before travelling. The EXPO has no parking; public transport advised.
- **Driving:** only bib and wristband holders may enter the start/finish area; accessible parking is limited to vehicles with an official special permit, on Altonaer Str. (toward Hansaplatz from Großer Stern). Park at your accommodation and take metro or S-Bahn to the start/finish, the arrangement official guidance points to.

## History and origins

The Berlin Marathon was first held in 1974, making it one of the older city stops among the Majors; the event is organised under DLV and World Athletics rules and supervised by BLV. The official About page counts 13 world records broken on Berlin's fast pavement to date, more than at any other marathon. The six-race Majors series began in 2006 (Tokyo, Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago, New York); Sydney became the seventh member in 2025, and finishing six AbbottWMM events earns the Six Star Medal. The event holds the World Athletics Elite Platinum Label, received the WA Heritage Plaque in November 2023, and was a 1986 AIMS founding member.
