## 60-second verdict

The Bank of America Chicago Marathon is a city road race and one of the Abbott World Marathon Majors. The 2026 edition was the 48th running, held on Sunday 11 October 2026 with start and finish in Grant Park, 26.2 miles through 29 neighbourhoods. The organiser describes the course as flat and fast; the official materials publish no cumulative elevation gain or loss. The 2026 field included more than 54,000 participants, supported along the route by about 1.7 million spectators and 10,000 volunteers.

Three judgements to hold first. **This is a dense city loop with packed aid and medical support; the difficulty is pacing control, late-race neighbourhood turns and possible October warming, not climbing.** **Entry splits a non-guaranteed Drawing from several guaranteed tracks:** Time Qualifier, Legacy Finisher, Charity, High Performance Program, Chicago Distance Series, 2026 cancelled entry and Para Athletics Program; each track's eligibility is independent and cannot be swapped. **The crew/pacer boundary is among the strictest in the Majors:** the rules state that receiving any assistance from another person — including advice, information, technical communication or unofficial pacing — can disqualify you; the only exceptions are official Guide Runners, Support Runners and contracted pacesetters who start in the Event.

Who this suits: runners with a complete marathon base who can fuel to plan in city crowds and understand the private-aid and pacing limits. Who should not enter: those without long-distance training who expect to cram after a Drawing win, and anyone planning to bring a friend onto the course to pace or hand supplies. This page is fixed to the 2026 edition, checked 2026-11-25; the 2027 edition (Sunday 10 October 2027, hosting the 2027 USATF Marathon Championships) must be rechecked against current official pages.

**Crew and pacing boundary (the easiest rule to get wrong on this page).** The rules page states a participant who receives any assistance from another person may be disqualified; assistance includes conveying advice, information or direct/indirect help by any means, including technical communication devices, and pacing by persons not officially participating. The only exceptions are officially designated Guide Runners, Support Runners and contracted pacesetters who start in the Event. No person without an official bib may be on the course, and no participant may receive assistance or refreshment from anyone without event management permission; a participant who leaves the course may not rejoin to gain a place or to pace or assist another runner. In other words, mass-participant runners get no roadside private aid, no friend pacing alongside, and no remote pacing advice by phone. This is entirely different from trail races that allow crew zones. ## Training transfer

What this course means for training, five points:

1. **City-loop long runs are the base.** The organiser publishes no climb figure, but the repeated impact of 26.2 miles and late-race fatigue remain; training must cover continuous running of comparable duration. Long runs: [long run](/en/knowledge/long-run/) and [marathon training plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/).
2. **Pace and fuel to the 20-station rhythm.** Stations sit about 1–2 miles apart with Gatorade and water; rehearse personal supplies in long runs. Note hydration backpacks are banned — carry on a belt, handheld or soft flask. Fuelling: [long-run fuelling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/) and [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/).
3. **Plan for late-race turns and crowds.** After 20 miles the route passes Pilsen, Chinatown, Bridgeport and Bronzeville with many junctions and turns; pacing zones: [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) and [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/).
4. **Plan for the October temperature swing.** Morning cool and possible afternoon warmth must be covered in training; arrange volume and taper through [periodization](/en/knowledge/periodization/) and [taper](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/).
5. **Strength and injury readiness track the long runs.** Repeated road impact loads the lower legs, ankles and hips; outside long runs, keep base strength, mobility and running stability work inside the periodised plan, and keep low-intensity stimulus through the taper instead of cramming volume before race day. Injury-risk self-check: [injury risk checker](/en/tools/injury-risk-check/); strength and taper principles: [taper](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/).

## Three native diagnoses: real hard points, who should not come, the most misread numbers

**Real hard points:** on a city loop the difficulty is not climbing but three things: three start waves and 20-station crowds that can pull the early miles fast; neighbourhood turns, junctions and wind shifts between lakefront and inner blocks that disrupt late rhythm; and the fatigue zone after 20 miles through Pilsen, Chinatown and Bridgeport, where fuel and pacing discipline decide whether you finish inside the cut-off.

**Who should not come:** runners without a marathon or equivalent long-distance volume who expect to cram; anyone planning to bring a friend onto the course to pace or arrange roadside private aid — the rules can disqualify directly; those unprepared for both October morning cold and afternoon warmth; and anyone reading "flat and fast" as "no undulation, easy for everyone". The Drawing runs on luck, TQ on certified time, HPP on elite standards, charity on fundraising; the tracks cannot be swapped.

**Most misread numbers:** first, elevation — none of the five official materials publishes cumulative climb; do not treat a third-party elevation map as official. Second, the cut-off — 6:30:59 is the overall limit, and finishers after 4 p.m. are not recorded officially; these are not the same rule. Third, records — 2:00:35 is the men's running course record held by Kiptum; the men's wheelchair record has two conflicting official versions, 1:21:34 / 1:21:24, which this page does not adopt. Fourth, charity fundraising — the 2026 minimum $2,200 and 2027 minimum $2,500 must not be mixed. Fifth, "20 aid stations" does not include Culligan refill points, Maurten gel depots or the Biofreeze pain-relief zone.

The Orange Line runs direct to Midway; the station is just east of the terminal with an enclosed walkway, about 20–25 minutes to downtown, fare $2.50 or less; Orange Line does not run overnight, with the N62 Archer bus as overnight alternate. Trains from both O'Hare and Midway go through downtown.
- **City movement:** the official guide recommends CTA, Metra, the South Shore Line, Divvy, free bike valet and Millennium Garages; the spectator transport page encourages public transport. Start and finish are in Grant Park, finish stretch on Columbus Drive.
- **Spectator stations:** Grand (mi 1, 3, 12.5), Jackson (mi 2), Chicago (mi 3.5, 12.25), Clark/Division (mi 4, 12), Sheridan (mi 8), Addison (mi 8.5), Sedgwick (mi 11), Washington/Wells (mi 13.5), UIC-Halsted (mi 14, 17), 18th (mi 19.25), Cermak-Chinatown (mi 21.5), Sox-35th and 35th-Bronzeville-IIT (mi 23, 23.25, 23.5), Cermak-McCormick Place (mi 25), Roosevelt (finish).
- **Expo:** Abbott Health & Fitness Expo, McCormick Place; collect in person against photo ID; no race-day collection, mailing or proxy.
- **Parking:** the guide lists Millennium Garages parking options; no-parking zones are towed from 1 a.m., and the start/finish area is crowded, so public transport is advised.

## History and origins

The Chicago Marathon was first held in 1977; the results page counts more than one million finishers since founding, with online historic results covering 1993–2019 and 2021–2025; 2020 is absent from the online results list, and any earlier gaps follow the official results page. The event is one of the Majors series, whose inaugural season ran from Boston in April 2006 to New York in November 2007; after Sydney joined in 2025, the seven stops are Tokyo, Boston, London, Sydney, Berlin, Chicago and New York. Seven world records have been set on the course to date (official amenities figure). Age-group awards cover men, women and non-binary divisions, spanning 16–19 through 80 and over. The 2027 event, the 49th running, will also host the 2027 USATF Marathon Championships; the 2026 press release counts more than 54,000 participants through 29 neighbourhoods, while the press-center figure is about 55,000 runners — the two scale figures are labelled separately and not mixed.
