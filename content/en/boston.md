## What this course is

The Boston Marathon is a **point-to-point road race**. It starts on Main Street in Hopkinton and finishes on Boylston Street in Boston, near the Boston Public Library in Copley Square. The official course map describes the full line: Main Street in Hopkinton, then Route 135 through Ashland, Framingham, Natick and Wellesley; where Route 135 meets Route 16 the route continues on Route 16 through Newton Lower Falls; at the fire station it turns right onto Commonwealth Avenue (Route 30) and runs through the Newton Hills; near the reservoir it bears right onto Chestnut Hill Avenue to Cleveland Circle; then left onto Beacon Street through Brookline, Kenmore Square and under Massachusetts Avenue; right onto Hereford Street against normal traffic flow; and left onto Boylston Street to the finish near the Boston Public Library in Copley Square.

Because the race is point to point, race-day logistics are built around moving people to Hopkinton rather than around a loop. The official spectator page says so directly: spectators who want to see the same runner in more than one place must plan carefully because the course is point to point. Hopkinton has hosted the start since 1924, according to the same official page.

The 130th edition in 2026 is listed by the B.A.A. as 30,000 participants and nearly 10,000 volunteers, with US$57.3 million raised through the Bank of America Official Charity Program. Qualifier registration for the 131st edition in 2027 ran from 14 to 18 September 2026 and drew 33,656 qualifier applications; acceptances began to be sent on 29 September 2026. Those numbers matter to the shape of the day: six waves start between 10:00 and 11:21, and the B.A.A. schedule puts every athlete across the starting line by 11:30 a.m.

## Official figures

Boston's official material uses two distance conventions and does not publish a single cumulative gain, loss or net-drop figure for the course. The 2026 official map prints `26.2 M / 42.2 K`; World Athletics defines a marathon as 42.195 km; and the B.A.A. qualifying page discusses net drop only when it adjusts times from **other** qualifying races. The table keeps these claims apart. Anything the B.A.A. does not publish is marked "not published".

| Source | Distance | Ascent | Descent | Net drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B.A.A. 2026 official course map, printed on the map | 26.2 mi / 42.2 km | Not published | Not published | Not published (profile shows shape only, no cumulative values) |
| B.A.A. course page and 2026 participant guide (mile convention; figure matches the map's 26.2 M) | 26.2 miles | Not published | Not published | Not published |
| World Athletics marathon definition | 42.195 km | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| B.A.A. qualifying net-drop bands (for other qualifying races) | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | 1,500–2,999 ft adds 5:00; 3,000–5,999 ft adds 10:00; 6,000 ft or more not accepted |

The last row needs reading in full. It says that when **another race** has a net drop in those bands, the B.A.A. indexes the qualifying time. It is not a measurement of the Boston course. Treating 1,500 ft as Boston's official net drop mistakes the object of the rule.

The official map does include an elevation profile: the left axis is labelled in feet and the right axis in metres, with the start at the high end and the finish at the low end. But the map prints no cumulative ascent, cumulative descent or net-drop number, and neither the course page, the readably extracted text of the participant guide nor the qualifying page supplies one. This page therefore leaves the card's ascent field empty and uses only the official distance of 42.195 km. Third-party GPS elevation totals are not official figures and are not used here.

What the official pages do support, segment by segment:

- **Hopkinton (start → 1.90 mi).** The start is on Main Street, and Hopkinton has hosted the start since 1924. The official spectator page gives a road-closure window of about 7:00 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. Athletes also walk 0.7 mi from the Athletes' Village to the start; that walk is outside the race distance but consumes time and energy before the gun.
- **Ashland (1.90 → 4.95 mi).** The official spectator page names Ashland station as an MBTA Commuter Rail stop. Road closure is about 7:00 a.m. to 1:45 p.m.
- **Framingham (4.95 → 7.52 mi).** Framingham station is named as a Commuter Rail stop. The town-by-town section gives a closure window of about 7:30 a.m. to 2:15 p.m., while the road-closure table on the same page gives 7:45 a.m. to 2:15 p.m. Both are official; this page shows both and does not pick one.
- **Natick (7.52 → 11.72 mi).** West Natick and Natick Center are named as Commuter Rail stops. The closure table gives 8:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.
- **Wellesley (11.72 → 15.93 mi).** The halfway point at 13.1 mi sits inside this segment. The official page describes the Scream Tunnel near Wellesley College as the loudest stretch just before halfway, and places a sensory-friendly viewing area around mile 13.5. The closure table gives 8:30 a.m. to 4:15 p.m.; the town-by-town section gives 8:00 a.m. to 3:35 p.m. The two official versions disagree.
- **Newton (15.93 → 21.35 mi).** The official page places the Newton Hills at miles 17.5 to 21. This is the most concentrated climbing of the race and the section runners remember. Closure is about 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m.; named rail access includes the Green Line at Woodland and Boston College.
- **Boston, first section (21.35 → 22.45 mi).** This piece lies between Newton and Brookline but is grouped under Boston in the official mileage. The closure table gives Boston 9:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.
- **Brookline (22.45 → 24.70 mi).** The official map puts Beacon Street through Brookline. The closure table gives 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.; the town-by-town section gives 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Again, both are shown.
- **Boston, finish section (24.70 → 26.20 mi).** The route turns right onto Hereford Street against normal traffic, then left onto Boylston Street, finishing near the Boston Public Library in Copley Square. Boston's road reopening is listed as late as 7:00 p.m.

The table answers only "where the B.A.A. cuts the course". It cannot answer "how much each segment climbs", because the B.A.A. publishes a continuous profile rather than town-by-town cumulative figures.

## Aid stations and cut-offs

Boston's hydration structure is unusual: it does not publish a 5 km station list. Instead, the B.A.A. promises Poland Spring water and Lemon Lime Gatorade Endurance Formula from mile 2 onward at **every mile mark**, plus water and Gatorade at the finish. Three Maurten Hydrogel stops are added on top. The 2026 participant guide and the official map both confirm the every-mile promise, and the official map adds that portable toilets are located at aid stations. There are also 26 medical stations along the course, each served by sweep buses that take runners to the Finish Area medical tent.

| Location | Distance · elevation · cumulative ascent | Service / cut-off |
|---|---|---|
| Athletes' Village (Hopkinton Middle/High School) | 0.0 mi · elevation not published · ascent not published | Water, Gatorade, portable toilets; cut-off not published |
| Every-mile water station (from Mile 2) | From 2.0 mi at every mile mark · elevation not published · ascent not published | Water and Gatorade; official page says stations close at staggered times, with no per-station times published |
| Maurten stop, Wellesley town line | 11.8 mi · elevation not published · ascent not published | Maurten Gel 100 and Gel 100 CAF 100; cut-off not published |
| Maurten stop, Newton | 17.0 mi · elevation not published · ascent not published | Same; cut-off not published |
| Maurten stop, just after Boston College | 21.5 mi · elevation not published · ascent not published | Same; cut-off not published |
| 26 medical stations | Exact locations not published · elevation not published · ascent not published | Medical staff and sweep buses to the finish medical tent; cut-off not published |
| Finish, Boylston Street | 26.2 mi · elevation not published · ascent not published | Water, Gatorade, medical; finish area closes at approximately 5:30 p.m. |

Points that need to be stated plainly:

- **Every-mile water is not every-mile food.** The B.A.A. names water and Gatorade; solid food and gels are named only at the three Maurten stops. No other food plan is published. Do not read "hydration every mile" as "energy gel every mile".
- **A medical station is not an aid station.** The 26 medical stations have staff and sweep buses, but the B.A.A. does not publish their mile positions. This page cannot give a "you can drop out at mile X" list.
- **The cut-off is a road-reopening rule, not a per-station timetable.** The official wording is that when officials decide it is time to reopen the roads, runners still on the course are instructed to move to the right; the pace is described as approximately 13:44 per mile. Timing systems, course clocks, medical stations and water stations then begin to close and cease operation. At 13:44 per mile, 26.2 miles takes about 5:59:48 (this page's arithmetic from the official pace, not an official finish limit). The B.A.A. separately says aid stations close at staggered times and finish-area facilities close at approximately 5:30 p.m.
- **Sweep buses go to the finish.** The official text says sweep buses pick up runners at every medical station and drop them at the Finish Area medical tent, not back at Hopkinton. Friends and family should plan their pickup at the finish side.
- **Start-area services are outside the race distance.** The Athletes' Village provides water, Gatorade and portable toilets to bib holders; that is pre-race service, not a course station.

## Surface and terrain

The B.A.A. does not publish a percentage breakdown of asphalt, concrete or any other surface, and this page does not invent one. What the official map does establish is the road line itself: Route 135, Route 16, Commonwealth Avenue (Route 30), Chestnut Hill Avenue, Beacon Street, Hereford Street and Boylston Street. The map draws one continuous road course and marks no trail section.

On terrain, the official elevation profile shows a high start and a low finish, so the overall shape is net downhill. The same profile shows continuous rolling ground, with the Newton Hills concentrated between miles 17.5 and 21. Because no cumulative ascent or descent is published, this page does not state a climbing total, and it does not reduce "net downhill" to "all downhill". Two things are true at once: the first half trends low, and the second half climbs repeatedly from 17.5 to 21. Judging the course by net drop alone understates the second half.

Two official details are easy to miss. The last stretch on Hereford Street runs **against normal traffic flow**, and the race closes the road for it. The final mile also turns frequently — Beacon Street into Hereford, then left onto Boylston — so runners should read the official map rather than rely on memory. The map's copyright notice also means the map can be viewed and linked, but not copied into this page.

## Risks and exit points

- **Medical and withdrawal.** There are 26 medical stations, each with sweep buses to the finish medical tent. The B.A.A. participant guide asks runners to complete the medical and emergency-contact information on the back of the bib. Because the station mile positions are not published, a runner cannot mark "the nearest medical point" before the race.
- **After the roads reopen.** The official rule is that runners still out when officials decide to reopen the roads are moved to the right side. Once roads open, timing, clocks, medical stations and water stations begin to close and stop operating; runners after that point are on their own. The B.A.A. puts a team of cyclists behind the pack to assist runners at or slower than 13:44 per mile. That is a safety presence, not a guaranteed pickup.
- **Finish-area closure.** Finish-area facilities officially close at approximately 5:30 p.m. The last wave starts at 11:21, so a late finisher has limited time for bag collection, which is listed as 11:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., and for the family meeting area.
- **Point-to-point baggage risk.** The official guide is explicit that no bags are transported from Hopkinton to Boston and that clothing left in Hopkinton is donated to local organizations. Leaving a jacket at the start means giving it away.
- **Weather risk.** Late April can still deliver a near-freezing morning and a day around 10°C; the grid data below shows both. The B.A.A. does not publish a segment-by-segment micro-forecast, and the start valley and the harbour-side finish can feel different.
- **Route changes.** The official spectator page states that, for public safety, the route may be changed and the race may be paused or cancelled before or during the event. The course is a No-Drone Zone, and spectators may not enter the course or pace a runner.
- **The only documented exit is the medical station.** There is no official mile-by-mile list of ambulance access points, family pickup points or rail evacuations. This page does not turn "a station is nearby" into "you can leave the course there", because road closures and station access are separate questions.

## Getting there

Official transport information is organised around getting people to Hopkinton, not around the airport:

- **Official buses.** Buses load on Charles Street in Boston, between the Public Garden and Boston Common. They are for official participants only, and a bib must be shown.
- **Loading by wave.** Wave 1 (red bibs, 201–8199) loads from 6:45 a.m.; Waves 2 (white, 8200–11999) and 3 (blue, 12000–15999) from 7:30 a.m.; Waves 4 (yellow, 16000–21299) and 5 (green, 21300–26699) from 8:00 a.m.; Wave 6 (orange, 26700–33699) from 9:00 a.m. The B.A.A. asks runners not to arrive at Boston Common before their wave and says early arrivals will be asked to return.
- **From bus to start.** Buses drop runners at the Athletes' Village next to Hopkinton Middle/High School. The start is a 0.7-mile walk from the Village. The B.A.A. publishes village-exit times by wave, with the schedule putting all runners across the start by 11:30 a.m.
- **After the finish.** Limited free buses run from 12:00 to 7:00 p.m. from Columbus Avenue between Berkeley and Clarendon Streets. This is limited capacity, not on-demand service.
- **Driving and parking.** Participant parking is limited to the 52 and 80 South Street lots, with a bib required for the participant shuttle. Spectator parking is limited to Hopkinton State Park, with race-morning shuttles. This page found no official encouragement to drive.
- **Rail and city transport.** The official spectator page lists MBTA Commuter Rail stops at Ashland, Framingham, West Natick, Natick Center, Wellesley Square, Wellesley Hills and Wellesley Farms. On the Newton side it names the Green Line at Woodland and Boston College; on the Brookline side, Cleveland Circle. It notes that Hynes Convention Center is not accessible-friendly.
- **Airport.** None of the fetched B.A.A. pages — course, participant guide or spectator — publishes airport transfers. This page does not fill that gap with Logan routes, fares or timings; airport information should come from the airport or MBTA in the relevant year, not from a generic guide presented as official race information.
- **Finish parking.** The spectator page says there is no parking near the finish and lists four suggested garages: 100 Clarendon, Prudential Center, Copley Place and Boston Common Garage, the last closed on Marathon Monday morning. These are suggestions, not guarantees.
- **Two official closure versions.** The town-by-town section and the road-closure table disagree for Framingham (7:30 vs 7:45), Wellesley (8:00 vs 8:30) and Brookline (8:00 vs 9:00). Plan with both and arrive by the earlier time.

## Season and weather

The 130th edition is on 20 April 2026. The first starters are the wheelchair men at 9:06, followed by wheelchair women at 9:09, handcycles/duos at 9:30, pro men at 9:37, pro women at 9:47, para athletics at 9:50, and six citizen waves from 10:00 to 11:21; everyone is across the line by 11:30. That puts most runners on the course between late morning and early afternoon, when temperature can rise and rain can arrive.

This page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 13–21 April 2026 (nine days including race day) at two grid points, not at course measurement sites:

- **Hopkinton grid** (returned point 42.21441N / 71.57895W, elevation 134 m): daily maxima 9.8–28.5°C, median 19.2°C; daily minima −1.6–14.0°C, median 6.9°C; six of nine days had at least 1 mm precipitation and two had at least 5 mm.
- **Boston grid** (returned point 42.35501N / 71.12906W, elevation 9 m): daily maxima 9.2–25.4°C, median 16.6°C; daily minima −0.4–14.0°C, median 6.9°C; four of nine days had at least 1 mm precipitation and four had at least 5 mm.
- **Race-day grid values for 20 April 2026:** Hopkinton maximum 9.8°C, minimum 0.3°C, 0.1 mm precipitation and maximum wind 17.6 km/h; Boston maximum 10.1°C, minimum 1.4°C, 0.4 mm precipitation and maximum wind 15.7 km/h.

These are **modelled grid values, not course measurements**. The grid elevation does not coincide exactly with the road, and the Hopkinton start, the shaded Newton Hills and the harbour-side finish can all differ from the grid. What the window shows is range: one day near 28.5°C and a race morning near freezing. Preparing for "spring" is not enough. The 2026 race-day grid is cold, with minima close to freezing, so the start corral will feel much colder than the daytime maximum.

## Difficulty

The B.A.A. does not publish a single difficulty grade, and this page does not invent an official one. On the verifiable dimensions, this is a **moderately hard road marathon**:

- 42.195 km, point to point, with separate start and finish.
- The official profile is net downhill overall but continuously rolling, with the Newton Hills at miles 17.5–21.
- The road-reopening pace is about 13:44 per mile, which works out to about 5:59:48 for 26.2 miles (this page's conversion, not an official finish limit).
- Six waves, the last at 11:21, everyone across by 11:30; 30,000 runners create start wait, hydration queues and finish congestion.
- Late-April weather spans a 28.5°C maximum and a −1.6°C minimum in the same nine-day window.
- Qualifying standards are demanding: for 2027, men aged 18–34 need 2:55:00 and women and non-binary entrants 3:25:00; the 80+ standards are 4:50:00 and 5:20:00.

Taken together, the difficulty is less about a published climbing number and more about **late-race rolling terrain, point-to-point logistics and weather uncertainty**. Starting at net-downhill pace and ignoring miles 17.5–21 costs time; assuming a qualifying time guarantees an easy day ignores the waiting and congestion of a 30,000-person event. This grade is derived from official figures and rules; it is not a runner's review and not a B.A.A. rating.
