## 60-second summary

- **This is a point-to-point road marathon, not an out-and-back loop.** The start is Main Street in Hopkinton and the finish is Boylston Street in Copley Square, Boston; the full distance is 42.195 km (the B.A.A. map marks 26.2 M / 42.2 K). Bag transport, shuttles and post-race meet-up all have to be planned around the rule that people go to Hopkinton while bags and runners return to Boston.
- **Qualifying does not guarantee entry.** The standard only permits applying; the 2027 field drew 33,656 qualifier applications and the acceptance cut-off was 5:17 below the age/gender standard, with about 1,000 additional random selections below the line. The full 2027/2028 age-band standards are in "Qualifying and entry".
- **The two "cut-offs" are two different clocks.** The qualifier cut-off is an entry-admission rule; the on-course road-reopening pace is about 13:44 per mile, a safety sweep cue rather than an official finish limit. This page keeps the two apart.
- **Three native judgments on this race.** First, the difficulty sits in the back-half Newton Hills (miles 17.5–21) and point-to-point logistics, not in climbing. Second, the official profile is net downhill overall, but the course is ineligible for world records (net drop, 91% start-to-finish separation). Third, qualifying times come from other races; courses with more than 1,500 ft of net drop have an index adjustment applied when the B.A.A. reviews the time, which has to be accounted for when choosing the qualifying race.

## History

- **Founding.** The Boston Athletic Association was established in 1887. The first Boston Marathon was run on 19 April 1897; John J. McDermott of New York won from a 15-member field in 2:55:10, from Metcalf's Mill in Ashland to the Irvington Oval near Copley Square, over 24.5 miles.
- **Women and wheelchair divisions.** Roberta Gibb was the first woman to run the race in 1966, finishing in 3:21:40; Kathrine Switzer was the first woman to complete with a bib in 1967; the women's division was officially established in 1972. The wheelchair division was officially recognised in 1975 with Bob Hall, who finished in 2:58.
- **Two special editions.** The 124th race (2020) was postponed from April and held as a fully virtual event over a 5–14 September window; the 125th (2021) was the first fall edition.
- **Start history.** Hopkinton has been the start line since 1924.
- The history wording comes from the B.A.A. official history page; complete year-by-year winner lists from earlier decades are not transcribed here.

### Registration window and admission rules

- **2027 registration window:** Monday 14 September 2026 10:00 a.m. ET through Friday 18 September 2026 5:00 p.m. ET, submitted on the Athletes' Village platform; it is not first-come, first-served. The 2027 qualifying window began 13 September 2025; the 2028 window began 19 September 2026.
- **Cut-off:** field size limits mean the standard only permits applying; within each age/gender group the fastest applicants are admitted first. The 2027 cut-off was 5:17 below the standard; about 1,000 qualifiers below the line are randomly selected through the Boston Qualifier Selection, with notifications by early October.
- **Qualifying race recognition:** the race must be an outdoor full marathon certified by USATF, AIMS or the national governing body, use transponder timing and have at least three officially entered competitors; indoor, virtual, treadmill, time-trial and handicapped marathons are not accepted.
- **Net-downhill index:** starting with 2027 registration, qualifying times from courses with 1,500–2,999 ft net drop receive a +5:00 adjustment, 3,000–5,999 ft a +10:00 adjustment, and courses at 6,000 ft or more are not accepted.

### Route

The official map gives the full route: from Main Street in Hopkinton along Route 135 through Ashland, Framingham, Natick and Wellesley; at the Route 135 / Route 16 junction onto Route 16 through Newton Lower Falls; right at the fire station onto Commonwealth Avenue (Route 30) through the Newton Hills; right near the reservoir onto Chestnut Hill Avenue to Cleveland Circle; left onto Beacon Street through Brookline and Kenmore Square, passing under Massachusetts Avenue; right onto Hereford Street against normal traffic flow; left onto Boylston Street, finishing in Copley Square.

The route is not a loop: start in Hopkinton, finish in Boston. The official spectator page warns directly that, because the race is point-to-point, spectators wanting to see a runner in multiple places must plan the route carefully.

## Timing and cut-offs

## Common misread: the two cut-offs are not the same thing

Two ideas in Boston wording are easily translated into the same "cut-off" concept; this page keeps them apart.

- **Qualifier cut-off (entry context):** how much faster than one's age/gender standard an applicant must be to be admitted. It is a field-allocation rule that floats with the applicant pool each year (see the year-by-year history under "Qualifying and entry"); it has nothing to do with whether a runner finishes on race day.
- **Road-reopening pace (race context):** when officials decide to restore traffic, runners still on the course are asked to keep right; the organiser describes this pace as about 13:44 per mile. It is not a station-by-station cutoff table, and the B.A.A. does not publish an ultra-style finish limit.
- **A third easy mix-up:** the net-downhill index for other qualifying races (add time above 1,500 ft) is a qualifying-time review rule, neither the Boston course's net drop nor a race cutoff.

Treating the admission cut-off as a race finish limit underestimates the time available on course; treating the road-reopening pace as an official finish limit suggests results beyond it are not recorded. Each follows its own official page.

Points that have to be explicit:

- **Water every mile does not mean food every mile.** The organiser commits to water and Gatorade only; solid food and gel are explicitly listed at the three Maurten points, and other food arrangements are not published.
- **Medical stations are not aid stations.** The 26 medical stations have staff and sweep buses, but their mile positions are not published, so this page cannot give a "which mile you can drop" list.
- **Point-to-point bags:** the organiser states directly that no bags are transported from the Hopkinton start to the Boston finish; clothing left in Hopkinton is donated to local organisations. Gear check accepts only the clear plastic B.A.A. gear bag.
- **Crew and pacing:** the course is a No-Drone Zone; spectators may not enter the course or pace a runner. Official volunteers are organised in Pre-Race, Hopkinton, Boston, Medical and Amateur Radio groups; this is not a personal-crew channel. Family meet-up should be planned on the finish side.

## Gear and rules

Points from the official participant guide and rules:

- **Bib information:** athletes must complete the medical and emergency-contact information on the back of the bib.
- **Pick-up identity:** government-issued photo ID plus digital pick-up pass; bibs must be collected in person.
- **Qualification verification:** times are reviewed by the B.A.A. after registration; the runner is not officially entered until verification completes.
- **Entry terms:** non-refundable, non-transferable, non-deferable, except under the pregnancy and postpartum policy.
- **Course conduct:** the course is closed; spectators may not enter or pace; drones are banned throughout; for public safety the route may be changed and the race paused or cancelled before or during the event.
- **Gear level:** the official pages do not publish a mandatory gear list (road marathon); given the wide weather range, see "Weather and environment" and the site's [apparel and chafing knowledge](/en/knowledge/running-apparel-and-chafing/).
- **Chip timing:** both qualifying and race results are recorded by net (chip) time.

Earlier editions' handbooks were not obtained, so the course-change history is not verified. This page has no verified official route change, rescheduling or rule change to log; `courseChanges` is an empty array rather than a claim that the course never changed. The rule-level changes known are the qualifying standards and net-downhill index: under-60 standards were five minutes faster from the 2026 race, and the net-downhill time adjustment applies from 2027 registration.

## Risks and escape points

- **Medical and withdrawal:** each of the 26 medical stations has a sweep bus to the finish medical tent; mile positions are not published, so the nearest medical point cannot be mapped before race day.
- **After reopening:** once roads reopen, timing, clock, medical stations and aid stops stop in turn; sweep cyclists assist runners at 13:44 per mile or slower, a safety cue rather than a sweep guarantee.
- **Finish-area closure:** facilities close about 5:30 p.m.; the last wave starts 11:21, so late finishers reserve time for bag pick-up and family meet-up.
- **Point-to-point logistics risk:** no bags from the start; clothing left in Hopkinton is donated.
- **Weather risk:** late April dawn can be near 0 °C; no segment-by-segment microclimate forecast is published.
- **Temporary route changes:** for public safety the route may be changed, paused or cancelled before or during the event; drones are banned and spectators may not enter or pace.
- **Withdrawal method:** get to a medical station and board a sweep bus; the organiser does not publish an ambulance-access or family shuttle list by mile, and "a subway station is nearby" is not written here as an evacuation option.

## Getting there

The official travel information mainly covers getting people to the Hopkinton start (shuttle details in the "Aid, logistics and crew/pacer support" table). City-level additions:

- **Rail:** commuter rail and Green Line stops are listed in the logistics table; the Hynes Convention Center station is not suitable for accessible use.
- **Airport:** the B.A.A. course page, participant guide and spectator page do not publish airport transit; for airport information rely on the airport or MBTA announcements for the current year.
- **Two closure-time versions:** the town section and road-closure table disagree in Framingham, Wellesley and Brookline (e.g. Framingham 7:30 vs 7:45, Wellesley 8:00 vs 8:30, Brookline 8:00 vs 9:00); check both when planning travel and arrive to the earlier closure time.
