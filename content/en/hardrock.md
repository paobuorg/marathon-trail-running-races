## 60-second summary

- **A high-altitude mountain hundred, not a city marathon.** The 2026 edition starts at the Silverton School Gym in Silverton, Colorado, loops the San Juan Range and returns to the same town. The official distance is 101.8 mi (about 163.8 km), with 33,441 ft of climb and the same descent, an average elevation over 11,000 ft, thirteen crossings above 12,000 ft, and a high point at Handies Peak, 14,048 ft.
- **The cut-off is 48 hours, not a leisurely finish window.** The official average finishing time is 39:52:11, about 8 h 08 min inside the cut-off. Cut-off times are published at only eight aid stations; the other six hike-in or road-side stations have no published cut-off.
- **"Official finisher" and the Hardrock circle are two layers of status.** Crossing back into Silverton before 06:00 on Sunday and completing the finish procedure earns the official Finished status; runners who have started but never finished sit in the Never Finished lottery pool. The finish ritual is kissing the Hardrock, a ram's head painted on mining debris; the kiss itself does not confer finish status.
- **Three native judgments.** First, the difficulty comes from the stacking of altitude, technical terrain and remoteness rather than distance alone: thirteen passes above 12,000 ft, exposed terrain above treeline, and a daily afternoon monsoon thunderstorm cycle. Second, the aid structure runs big stations feeding small pack-in ones; pack-in stations have a limited range and cannot restock a full food plan. Third, the direction reverses every year: clockwise in 2026, counter-clockwise in 2027; segments, climb and pacer start points cannot be mixed across years.

## Qualifying and entry

Hardrock has no time-based qualifying standard; entry runs mainly through the lottery and the historical-finisher pools. The wording below comes from the official lottery page and the entry and deferral page:

| Item | Official wording |
|---|---|
| Application window | First two weeks of October on UltraSignup; exact dates announced in September |
| Lottery timing | First weekend of December; the 2026 lottery was held 6 December 2025; the 2027 lottery is set for 5 December 2026 |
| Application size | About 3,000 applicants each year |
| 2026 selection | 147 entrants: 64 Finished pool, 83 Never Finished pool; women were 22.2% of applicants, and the share of selected women will not be below the share of women applicants |
| Lottery tickets | Never Finished pool: 2^N tickets, N being the number of DNSs since the applicant last started; Finished pool: one ticket for applying, one extra per finish, one per DNS since last start |
| Director picks | The Run Director may select six extra runners in 2026, evenly split between men and women |
| Entry fee | 450 USD plus UltraSignup processing fees |
| Refund | 50% refund for withdrawal before 1 June; no refund after 1 June; a waiting list is maintained after the field fills |
| Deferral | Five-year deferral for pregnancy; one-year deferral for specified parental circumstances |
| Pre-race duty | Every runner must complete a service requirement before the start; check-in 12:00–16:00 on 8 July and 08:00–11:00 on 9 July; mandatory briefing 12:00 on 9 July |

The pool mechanics deserve a separate note: **Finished and Never Finished are two independent ticket pools.** First-time applicants and repeat DNSs get exponentially weighted tickets in the Never Finished pool by DNS count; finishers accumulate tickets in the Finished pool by finish count. This is not admission by age-group pace, and no published time standard exists. The 22.2% women applicant figure is the 2026 lottery number, not a historical average.

The four legs are set out below; pass names, mileages and elevations come from the 2026 official course description:

- **Leg 1 (0–27.8 mi, Silverton → Telluride).** Leaving town, the route follows the Rainbow Trail along and above the highway for just under 2 mi, drops to cross Highway 550, then crosses Mineral Creek with a fixed rope; the organiser calls Mineral Creek the biggest water crossing on the course. The leg then climbs Cataract Putnam Pass (mile 7.2, 12,600 ft), Porcupine Cataract Pass (8.6, 12,230 ft), Grant Swamp Pass (14.9, 12,920 ft) and Oscar's Pass (21.3, 13,140 ft), with aid at KT (11.5) and Chapman (18.1) before the descent into Telluride. By mile 27.8 the runner has crossed above 12,000 ft four times and above 13,000 ft once.
- **Leg 2 (27.8–43.9 mi, Telluride → Ouray).** From Telluride's paved streets the course climbs the Cornet Creek Trail through Liberty Bell Basin to Mendota Ridge, traverses a scree field and reaches Kroger's Canteen (32.7 mi, 13,098 ft); the description warns space is very limited. After the descent from Virginius Pass, mining roads through Governor Basin lead to Governor (36.3 mi), then Camp Bird Road descends into Ouray (43.9 mi, 7,678 ft), the low point of the course. In the 2026 clockwise description, pacers may start at Ouray.
- **Leg 3 (43.9–72.0 mi, Ouray → Sherman).** Leaving Ouray, the Ouray Bear Creek Trail enters a steep, narrow gorge; the organiser warns of rockfall, huge drops on the right and difficult stream crossings after heavy rain, and tells runners to lean left. Engineer (51.9 mi, 11,798 ft) is a pack-in station at treeline; the route climbs Oh! Point (53.3, 12,910 ft), drops to Grouse Gulch (58.2, 10,729 ft), crosses American Grouse Pass (60.7, 13,020 ft) into American Basin, and reaches the 14,048 ft summit of Handies Peak (63.5). The descent goes to Burrows (67.8, 10,588 ft), then along Cinnamon Pass Road to Sherman (72.0).
- **Leg 4 (72.0–101.8 mi, Sherman → Silverton).** From Sherman the route climbs the Cataract Gulch Trail past Cataract Pole Pass (77.2, 12,200 ft) to Pole Creek (81.5, 11,244 ft), over Maggie/Pole Pass (85.4, 12,530 ft) to Maggie Gulch (86.5, 11,838 ft). It crosses Buffalo Boy Ridge (88.0, 13,214 ft) and Green Mountain / Stony Pass Ridge (89.5, 12,980 ft), reaches Cunningham (92.5) and descends into Silverton. The organiser warns the top of Buffalo Boy Ridge varies year to year with snow and cornice conditions, and parts of this section have a faint or nonexistent trail.

## Surface and terrain

The official documents publish no percentage breakdown for road, dirt trail, scree or cross country, so this page invents none. What can be confirmed is the official surface description: a loop on 4WD mining roads, dirt trails and cross country, with the course "on trails as much as possible". Long road sections and maintained trails may be unmarked, while cross-country sections are marked more heavily; this page records only that marking density varies by section and draws no runnability ratio from it.

Altitude gives the course a second structure. The official range is about 7,700 ft to over 14,000 ft, with local timberline around 11,800 ft. Below timberline are aspen, pine and spruce forests; above it are alpine tundra and low vegetation interspersed with krummholz, the stunted spruce, fir and willow. The aid table's spot elevations run from 7,678 ft at Ouray to 13,098 ft at Kroger's, so most of the route sits at or above treeline; Handies Peak at 14,048 ft is the high point. The average elevation is over 11,000 ft and the terrain is exposed, a different climate from the sheltered valley where the start and some aid stations sit.

The organiser also gives a concrete experience figure: in a typical year, runners' feet are wet 90% or more of the run. That is a proportion of time, not a proportion of surface, and it cannot estimate how much road or scree the course holds. The terrain warnings are direct: knee-deep ice-cold stream crossings, snow fields slick at night and post-holing by day, trails with 300 ft or more of cliff on one side, mild rock climbing that requires hands, and exposure above treeline in extreme thunderstorms.

| Road-side stations | Governor, Burrows, Cunningham | Road access but simple setup; Burrows has no drop bags and no crew |

Several points need to be explicit:

- **Every station has at least an EMT, a first aid kit and radio communication**, the course page's own wording; medical decisions and the authority to pull a runner are also written into the rules.
- **Stretches between stations can be long.** The manual notes gaps of six hours or more between aid stations; pack-in stations have a limited range and cannot restock a full food plan.
- **Aid is limited to the station area.** The rules prohibit stashing supplies and allow aid only within 400 yards of a designated aid station; do not plan to meet a crew or pick up a hidden cache outside those limits.
- **Pack-in stations are not unmanned.** Supplies are limited, but volunteers and the medical baseline are still present; do not expect a full major-station menu.

## In-town movement and lodging

- **Start, check-in and mandatory briefing:** Silverton School Gym on 12th Street between Snowden and Reese.
- **Drop bags and Camping Hardrock:** Kendall Mountain Rec Center, 1 Kendall Pl on the east side of town across the 14th Street bridge; camping opens on 8 July.
- **Public showers:** Anesi Park on Blair Street between 12th and 13th; the lodging page says open 7a–8p, 5 USD for a five-minute token, cash only.

## Weather and environment

The 2026 edition ran 10–12 July, starting 06:00 on 10 July. To prepare for that window, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 10–12 July in each year from 2016 to 2025 at the Silverton grid point, 30 days in total:

| Metric | Grid statistics (2016–2025, 10–12 July) |
|---|---|
| Daily maximum | 18.3°C to 26.4°C, median 22.1°C |
| Daily minimum | 1.8°C to 11.4°C, median 6.3°C |
| Precipitation | 11 of 30 days ≥1 mm, 5 days ≥5 mm, largest daily total 14 mm |

The data basis matters. Requested coordinates were 37.811N / 107.664W; Open-Meteo returned the grid cell 37.7856N / 107.7108W at 2,838 m (about 9,311 ft), timezone America/Denver. These are **gridded historical statistics, not on-course measurements**, and they are not readings from a specific Silverton weather station. The grid cell sits at 2,838 m, below the course's average elevation of over 11,000 ft (about 3,353 m); its minimum should not decide high-altitude kit.

The organiser's seasonal description supports the same caution:

- **Monsoon daily cycle:** calm mornings, thunderheads building as the day warms, intense afternoon thunderstorms possible, abating evening and overnight; part of the 48-hour cut-off exists so a runner can wait out a storm in a valley and still finish.
- **Lightning:** the organiser reports direct contact with lightning in past years and several near misses. If hair stands on end or skin tingles, a strike is imminent; assume the lightning position and spread the group at least 50 ft apart.
- **Snow and scree:** snow fields are slick at night and post-hole by day above sharp scree; cornices may fall from above or collapse underfoot.
- **Altitude and temperature swing:** the start and some stations sit in 7,700–9,000 ft sheltered valleys, while most of the run is above 11,000 ft in exposed terrain; temperature and wind differ markedly and can drop suddenly.
- **Wet feet:** feet are wet 90% or more in a typical year; with a 06:00 start, anyone over 40 hours sees two sunsets.

## Training transfer

The training question splits in two: first build the base capacity for a high-altitude mountain ultra, then add specificity for the passes, technical terrain and night exposure of this course. The table turns this page's verifiable dimensions into training check items; weekly sessions follow the site's ultra trail plan and long-run fueling knowledge.

| Phase | Key question | Verifiable basis on this page |
|---|---|---|
| Altitude adaptation | Prior training or stays above 11,000 ft? Altitude sickness or edema risk assessed? | Average elevation >11,000 ft, thirteen trips above 12,000 ft, high 14,048 ft; the organiser notes altitude can cause sickness or edema in some runners |
| Ultra endurance capacity | Can the body hold 163.8 km with 33,441 ft each of climb and descent? | Official distance and total elevation change; average finish 39:52:11 |
| Passes and scree | Leg strength, balance and downhill control for long steep ascents/descents and scree? | Thirteen major 12,000–13,000 ft passes; the Kroger's scree and Bear Creek gorge |
| Technical terrain | Experience with snow, water crossings, hands-on climbing and cliff-side trails? | Manual warnings: knee-deep ice water, post-holing snow, 300 ft+ cliff on one side, mild climbing |
| Night and navigation | Night navigation, snow judgment and unmarked sections handled independently? | Long road/trail sections may be unmarked; race framed as wilderness navigation; 40+ hours across two nights |
