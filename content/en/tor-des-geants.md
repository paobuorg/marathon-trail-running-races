## What this course is

Tor des Géants 330 (TOR330 – Tor des Géants®) is organised by VDA Trailers and starts and finishes in Courmayeur, Valle d'Aosta, Italy, at 1,224 m. It is not an out-and-back: it is an alpine loop beneath the highest summits of the Alps, crossing the territory of 34 municipalities on the official trails of the region. The route passes through Gran Paradiso National Park and Mont Avic Regional Park and follows the Alte Vie paths of Valle d'Aosta, repeatedly climbing above 3,000 m and dropping back to valley floors.

The race imposes no compulsory stages. The regulations describe a single stage, at free speed, within a limited time, under a semi-self-sufficiency regime. The organiser does not tell you where or how long to sleep; the winner is the runner who completes the course in the shortest time, making his or her own decisions about when and how long to stop for rest and refreshment.

On scale: the 2026 regulations cap the field at 1,100 bibs and set the entry fee at €990. Entry is open from age 20. ITRA rates the race at 6 ITRA points and 12 mountain points. There are six Life Bases (the regulations name Valgrisenche, Cogne, Donnas, Gressoney SJ, Valtournenche and Ollomont), and refreshment points roughly every 7–10 km.

One thing has to be understood before any other number: the official documents give at least three different lengths. The race page says 330 km and 24,000 m of climb. The regulations say approximately 350 km with around 25,000 m of climb, evaluated by ITRA. The official timetable totals 336.5 km with +26,182 m and −25,727 m. All three come from the organiser; none of the differences were created by this site. When you read any Tor des Géants material, first establish which of the three it is using.

The three sets serve different purposes and this page does not merge them:

- **The stat card at the top of this page uses 330 km and 24,000 m**, the pair used on the race page and in the name "TOR330".
- **The segment and aid tables use the official timetable** (336.5 km, +26,182 m / −25,727 m), because that document gives cumulative distance, cumulative climb, cumulative descent and cut-off times at every waypoint.
- **The regulations' "approx. 350 km / approx. 25,000 m"** is labelled an ITRA evaluation, not a timing measurement. It is about 13.5 km longer than the timetable but nearly 1,200 m lower in cumulative climb.

The timetable's highest point is Col Loson (km 90.6, 3,295 m) and its lowest is the Donnas Life Base (km 149.8, 323 m); start and finish are both 1,224 m. Total descent is 455 m less than total climb. The difference is kept as published in the official timetable and this page does not correct it.

Two caveats belong with this table:

- **The first Life Base boundary is stated explicitly in the official files.** The 2026 timetable labels the point at km 48.5 as `BaseVita-Valgrisenche` (1,693 m), and labels Cogne, Donnas, Gressoney, Valtournenche and Ollomont as the other five Base Vita points. The official GPX places the waypoint of the same name at 45.630N / 7.063E and 1,686 m, while the timetable gives 1,693 m at that point. Segment 1 therefore stops at km 48.5; Eaux Rousses at km 78.3 is not treated as Life Base 1.
- **The segment figures are subtractions**, not published per-segment values. The organiser publishes cumulative totals only, so the last digit of a derived figure can differ by a metre; the seven rows here sum exactly to the finish totals and no adjustment was made.

Further rules, all from the official regulations and timetable:

- **Food and drink are for on-site consumption only.** Regulation 20 states that official refreshment points supply drinks and food strictly for runners, to be consumed on site. Plastic cups are not supplied, so a cup or container is mandatory kit.
- **Refreshment points are checkpoints.** Regulation 20: every official refreshment point is also a checkpoint, and failure to register leads to disqualification. Runners must follow the marked route through each point even if they do not intend to stop.
- **Two hours maximum at a refreshment point.** Some refreshment points have short rest areas limited to two hours. If a runner cannot resume after two hours, volunteers contact the race organisers to assess whether the runner should be withdrawn. Rest at Life Bases follows the time barriers in regulation 21.
- **Only Life Bases offer sleep and showers.** Regulation 21: Life Bases provide a full hot meal, a hot shower and a longer rest within the time barriers; access is for runners only, with no assistants or coaches; every Life Base is a checkpoint and must be registered on entry and exit, with failure leading to disqualification.
- **Sleeping outside designated areas is banned.** Regulation 21 forbids sleeping in any structure, shelter or vehicle outside areas designated and controlled by the organisation. Regulation 17 adds that any personal assistance at the roadside, including sleep, food or other help, leads to disqualification.
- **TOR bags move only between Life Bases.** Regulation 22: the organisation transfers the personal TOR bag between Life Bases; runners must collect it in person on entering and hand it back in person on leaving; nothing may be attached to the outside; the organisation accepts no responsibility for loss or damage in transit.
- **"Wave 1 / wave 2" means the two starts.** Wave 1 leaves at 10:00 on 13 September, wave 2 at 12:00 the same day; the cut-offs differ by about two hours. D3 is 13 September and D9 is 19 September.

## Surface and terrain

The organiser publishes no surface percentages, and this page does not invent any. What is officially confirmed:

- The course follows the **official trails of Valle d'Aosta** (the Alte Vie), crossing 34 municipalities. It is not a temporary course laid out for the event.
- It passes through **Gran Paradiso National Park** and **Mont Avic Regional Park**.
- The official data confirms that the course includes **high-altitude sections above 3,000 m** (the timetable's highest point, Col Loson, is 3,295 m) and also passes through the Donnas valley at 323 m. The official weather bulletin highlights thunderstorm, rain, snow, wind, fog, cold and heat.
- The timetable's extremes: a high of 3,295 m at Col Loson and a low of 323 m at Donnas, an altitude span of 2,972 m. Seven waypoints sit above 2,800 m (Col Passo Alto 2,856 m, Col de la Crosatie 2,823 m, Col Fenêtre 2,844 m, Col Entrelor 3,004 m, Col Loson 3,295 m, Finestra Di Champorcher 2,827 m and Col Malatrà 2,921 m), with Col de Vessonaz at 2,788 m, Fenetre du Tsan at 2,739 m and Col de Champillon at 2,711 m close behind.

## Risks and escape points

This section only states what official rules, official route data and official transport notices support.

- **There is one prescribed way to withdraw: report at the nearest checkpoint.** Regulation 23 says a runner who wishes to stop must go to the nearest checkpoint and report the decision; the organisation then arranges transport back to the Life Base in Courmayeur.
- **Helicopters are not taxis.** Regulation 15: helicopter transfer is only for patients needing urgent medical attention and cannot be used by runners who withdraw.
- **Rescue points line the route.** Regulation 15: rescue points with radio or telephone contact to the organisers are set up along the course, with ambulances, security staff and doctors in the area; the full safety plan is approved and registered by the Valle d'Aosta Regional Prefecture.
- **Time barriers are hard escape lines.** Regulation 24: each Life Base has an entry and an exit barrier, and barriers at intermediate points operate as exit barriers; runners may not leave after the limit has expired. The organisation may change the route, rescue points, refreshment points and time barriers at any time without notice.
- **Weather can change the race.** Regulation 25: the start may be postponed in unfavourable conditions (severe low pressure with heavy rain or high-altitude snowfall, severe storm risk), and the race may be cancelled if conditions endanger runners, volunteers and staff. If the race is disrupted, runners reaching the finish within the barriers are still finishers, and the organisation may establish intermediate finish lines.
- **Personal assistance is tightly limited.** Regulation 17: any personal assistance along the route leads to disqualification; assistance from motorhomes, cars or other vehicles is prohibited; one assistant per runner is allowed, holding an organisation pass, and only in designated areas at refreshment points and Life Bases; assistants may not collect the TOR bag or give medical or paramedical help.
- **Pacing is banned.** Regulation 18: receiving pacing leads to disqualification. Pacing is defined as systematically running alongside a competitor, even for a short stretch. Cheering at aid stations is not pacing.
- **Communication and navigation.** Regulation 14: the mobile phone must stay on with the ringtone enabled so the Race Direction can reach the runner. The TOR330 mandatory list is a cup or container, a phone, an ID document, two survival blankets, food and water, and two working head torches with spare batteries; the handheld GPS navigator loaded with the course, route maps, altimeter and compass appear only in the TOR450 column.
- **A transport-side risk.** The Mont Blanc Tunnel operator states that no extended autumn closure is planned for 2026, but that from mid-September numerous midweek night-time closures (Monday to Thursday, 22:00–06:00, eight hours each, with two longer 11–12 hour closures) are scheduled; the final schedule is subject to prefecture approval. Anyone driving through the tunnel around race week must check the operator's notices.

## Getting there

Courmayeur sits in Italy's Valle d'Aosta and connects to Chamonix on the French side through the Mont Blanc Tunnel. The following comes from the official Courmayeur tourism page and the tunnel operator's own notice:

- **By air**: Turin Caselle airport is about 150 km from Courmayeur; Milan Malpensa 212 km; Milan Linate 235 km; Geneva 115 km. These are the four distances the official tourism page publishes; the page reviewed for this section does not give connecting timetables from each airport.
- **By train**: the railway line serving the locality is the Chivasso–Aosta line (the tourism page's own wording). The page stops at that line without describing the onward connection to the town, so this page does not invent one.
- **By bus**: Courmayeur has a public bus service within the resort with connections to and from Aosta, the Valle del Monte Bianco and major cities in northern Italy; the same service connects Courmayeur with Chamonix on the other side of Mont Blanc. The tourism page points to aosta.arriva.it.
- **By car**: from Italy, the main roads are the SS26 della Valle d'Aosta and the A5 Torino–Aosta–Courmayeur motorway; from France, Switzerland and northern Europe, the connection is the Mont Blanc Tunnel or the Gran San Bernardo Tunnel.
- **Parking in town**: the official tourism page lists Piazzale Monte Bianco P1 (maximum €15 per 24 hours), Viale Monte Bianco P2 (maximum €20 per 24 hours) and Tzan de Blà P3 (maximum €8 per 24 hours).
- **Race-week movements**: the official event schedule puts bib distribution, drop-bag deposit, the pasta party and the briefing at the Forum Sport Center in Courmayeur on 11–13 September; both starts are on Viale Monte Bianco; first arrivals and the finish are at Jardin de l'Ange. All pre-race administration is in the town centre.
- **Tunnel caveat**: see the previous section.

## Season and weather

The 2026 edition starts on 13 September in two waves and closes at 18:00 on 19 September. To prepare for the same window, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 13–19 September in each year from 2015 to 2025 at Courmayeur:

- Requested coordinates: 45.791N / 6.969E (Courmayeur town). The model grid point returned by Open-Meteo is **45.729N / 7.098E at 1,174 m grid elevation**, which does not coincide exactly with the request; that is normal for a gridded product and is recorded here as returned.
- Sample: 11 years × 7 days = **77 daily values**.
- Daily maximum temperature: 9.7°C to 28.6°C, median **19.6°C**.
- Daily minimum temperature: −1.7°C to 13.5°C, median **9.6°C**.
- Precipitation: 33 of 77 days recorded at least 1 mm, 14 recorded at least 5 mm, and the wettest single day reached 24.0 mm.

Those three lines are **modelled historical statistics for a valley grid point, not measurements on the course and not pass temperatures**.

The organiser runs its own weather service: the Valle d'Aosta regional Functional Centre publishes a dedicated TORX bulletin by 14:00 every day, sector by sector along the course, covering the next 36 hours in six-hour blocks, with wind-chill at the highest point whenever the temperature is below 0°C. Bulletins appear on the Telegram channel "Meteo VdA" and on the TORX Weather page at cf.regione.vda.it, issued sector by sector from the day before the start until the end of the race.

## Difficulty rating

The official documents publish no single "difficulty grade", and this page does not invent one. **This site's rating**: an extremely demanding long mountain ultra. The verifiable official figures:

- Distance 330–336.5 km depending on the official source; the ITRA evaluation in the regulations says approximately 350 km.
- Cumulative climb 24,000–26,182 m, cumulative descent 25,727 m.
- Highest point Col Loson at 3,295 m, lowest point Donnas at 323 m, an altitude span of 2,972 m.
- Official maximum time 150 hours, no compulsory stages, sleep managed by the runner.
- Semi-self-sufficiency, a mandatory kit list, and a longest gap between Life Bases of about 55.5 km.
- Official ITRA rating: 6 ITRA points and 12 mountain points.
