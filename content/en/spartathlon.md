This page covers the 2026 Spartathlon. Figures come from the official race list, the 2026 road-book PDF, the official route page, the official rules PDF, the official FAQ, the official GPX, the official results page, Open-Meteo's historical archive and two third-party transport guides. Verification date: 19 November 2026.

## 1. Three distances: 244.74, 245.3 and 244.045

The official Spartathlon material does not agree on the distance to the metre. The 2026 road book's final checkpoint is 244.74 km; the official route page ends its narrative at 245.3 km; the official GPX measures 244.045 km by haversine between consecutive points. The spread is under 1.3 km, but at 36-hour pace one kilometre is eight to nine minutes.

| Basis | Distance | What it is | Use here |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 2026 official road book | 244.74 km | Last row of the CP0–CP75 table | Card figure and checkpoint table |
| Official route page | 245.3 km | Rounded figure at the end of the route narrative | Section description only |
| Official GPX 2026 | 244.045 km | 4,830 track points, no elevation | Start/end and geometry check, not cut-offs |
| Third-party IUTA report | c. 246 km / +2,600 m | Post-race media approximation | Third-party context only; official road book gives no total gain |

The road book states a maximum elevation of 1,200 m but no total positive gain. The official GPX has no elevation field, so a total climb cannot be recomputed from it. IUTA's +2,600 m is useful training context, not an official figure. The only climb statements this page makes from official material are “maximum 1,200 m” and “about 960 m in 13 km on the Sangas section”, both from the official route page.

## 2. Six sections: from the Acropolis to Leonidas

The official route page divides the course into six sections; the road book gives the exact kilometres. Together they produce a more useful picture than “Athens to Sparta”.

1. **Corinth section (0 to about 81 km):** from the Odeon of Herodes Atticus below the Acropolis, via Apostolou Pavlou, Thiseo, Ermou and Iera Odos, through Daphni, Aspropyrgos and Elefsis, then Loutropyros, Megara, Kineta and Agioi Theodoroi to Isthmia and the Corinth Canal. The route page calls it gradual coastal climbing and descending, but the checkpoint table already gives 9:30 to reach Isthmia at km 79.8.
2. **Nemea section (about 81–124 km):** after the canal the course turns into the Peloponnese, through Examilia, Ancient Corinth and Assos, then up a narrow winding road to Nemea. The route page says 351 m of climbing in 24 km; the road book closes Ancient Nemea at km 122.7 at 23:00.
3. **Lyrkia section (about 124–148 km):** country roads with stones and potholes; many runners enter darkness here. Malandreni at km 140.2 is a dark-hours haven, then a stony descent and short climb to Lyrkia at km 147.8, closing at 03:10.
4. **Mountain and Nestani section (about 148–172 km):** 13 km and about 960 m of climbing from Lyrkia to the Sangas Pass, topping out around 1,100 m, then a zigzag descent via Kaperelli, Mountain Base at km 159.4 and Sangas village to Nestani at km 172. This is the most technical and most navigation-sensitive part of the race.
5. **Tegea section (about 172–195 km):** down to the Tripolis plain, through Zevgolatio to Tegea at km 193.9. The route page warns that Sparta is only 50 km away but this is often the hottest part of the second day.
6. **Sparta section (about 195–244.74 km):** a final climb from Tegea to about 975 m, through Kamari and Manthirea, then almost 28 km of descent into the Evrotas valley, past Voutiani and the Evrotas bridge, to the statue of King Leonidas in Sparta.

This is not a road marathon. The official route page names tarmac road, trail and mountain footpath; the mountain section has stones, potholes and zigzags. At night, headlamp and ankle protection matter more than flat-road pace.

## 4. The 36-hour arithmetic: the first 42 km are tighter than the finish

Divide the 07:00 start and each cut-off into “elapsed time / cumulative distance” and a counter-intuitive result appears: **the Megara cut-off at 42.2 km requires 8.9 km/h, while the finish only requires 6.8 km/h**. Spartathlon is not a gradual warm-up with a late chase; the first marathon has to be close to marathon race pace.

This is **elapsed time divided by cumulative distance**, not segment pace. It says the first marathon has to be faster than many people expect, while the last 50 km can be a walk near 8:45 /km. The 2026 winner, Tamás Bódis, ran 19:40:51, about 12.44 km/h or 4:49 /km; he finished more than 16 hours inside the limit.

The opening times matter too. Each checkpoint has an opening time; arriving too early means waiting outside, not banking time. The practical target is to reach each cut-off with 30–60 minutes of margin without arriving an hour before the station opens.

## 5. Crew: designated checkpoints only, car registered

Spartathlon's crew rules are stricter than many European ultras. The official FAQ says runners may receive help from an officially registered support team **only at specially designated checkpoints**; the support car must be registered in Athens; bicycles and motorcycles are forbidden. The 2022 rules add that runners may not enter a support vehicle to receive help, support cars may not stop between checkpoints, and they may not follow, lead, parallel or hover right behind a runner.

| Item | Official rule | Practical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Support point | Designated checkpoints only | No roadside hand-ups anywhere |
| Support car | Must be registered in Athens | Unregistered cars should not enter the course |
| Two-wheelers | Bicycles and motorcycles forbidden | No bike pacing or mobile bottle hand-ups |
| Runner in car | Forbidden to enter a support vehicle for help | The car stays in the designated area |
| Withdrawal | Hand in bib/chip, sign declaration | Official transport to the Sparta hotel |
| Aid | Every c. 3.5 km | Water, Coke, juice, fruit, bread, biscuits, chocolate, nuts, pasteli, etc. |

The FAQ also says a runner who drops out or is eliminated must sign a declaration and hand in the race number, and Spartathlon personnel are responsible for onward transport to the runner's hotel in Sparta. That is an official commitment, but it is not “a car is always immediately available”: in the mountain section, transport depends on the nearest road and official dispatch.

## 6. Weather: 26–28 °C by day, cooler valleys at night

Using Open-Meteo's ERA5 archive for 26–27 September in each year from 2015 to 2025 gives 22 daily records for Athens and 22 for Sparta:

| Place | Daily max range / median | Daily min range / median | ≥1 mm | ≥5 mm |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Athens | 19.7–30.0 °C / 25.3 °C | 13.9–20.5 °C / 17.6 °C | 6 / 22 | 2 / 22 |
| Sparta | 19.6–32.3 °C / 26.6 °C | 11.6–18.6 °C / 15.9 °C | 4 / 22 | 2 / 22 |

These are city stations. The Sangas Pass at about 1,100 m will be cooler at night, and valley moisture plus wind after sweating can push the felt temperature below 10 °C around CP47. The 2026 official reports mention rain, so “late September in Greece is always hot” is not a safe assumption. Prepare sun protection and electrolytes for the day, and a wind shell, long sleeves and dry spare layers for the night.

## 7. Travel and lodging: Athens airport to the Acropolis, Sparta back to Athens

The start is at the Odeon of Herodes Atticus below the Acropolis, near the Acropolis/Syntagma metro area. The common airport transfer is Metro Line 3: a third-party airport guide gives 40–45 minutes to Syntagma, €9 one way, about every 36 minutes; bus X95 takes about 60–70 minutes for €5.50. The race publishes no dedicated Spartathlon shuttle, so staying in Plaka/Acropolis and walking to the start is the low-risk plan.

The finish is in Sparta. There is no official post-race transfer back to Athens; the normal option is the KTEL Laconia coach to Athens Kifissos, which a third-party route summary puts at about 3 hours 30 minutes, roughly every four hours. Staying in Sparta on race night and travelling the next day is more robust than trying to catch a flight from the finish.

Entry facts: the official FAQ says a maximum of 400 runners, a €450 entry fee and no prize money; applications run from 25 January to 25 February, with payment due by 10 June. The 2026 official list records 403 registered, 323 started and 279 finished, an 86.4% finish rate among starters. The 403 is the official list's actual number, not an error in this page.

## 8. Difficulty and risk: a 244 km road ultra with a mountain in it

The difficulty breaks into five parts:

1. **The first 42 km are fast:** Megara's 11:45 cut-off requires 8.88 km/h. Many runners spend too much of the race here.
2. **The Sangas mountain at night:** 13 km and about 960 m of climbing, a zigzag descent and a stony surface. Headlamp, spare batteries and grippy shoes are the real priorities.
3. **Heat on the second day:** after Tegea the course is open plain; the official route page warns that Sparta is only 50 km away but this is often the hottest part of the second day.
4. **76 checkpoints:** one every 3.5 km sounds dense, but each has opening and closing times. Frequent stops eat time; practise fast in-and-out.
5. **Crew complexity:** registered car, designated checkpoints, no bikes. Runners without crew need to plan water and food earlier.

Withdrawal: under the 2022 rules and the FAQ, a runner who drops out hands in bib/chip and signs a declaration; the organisation arranges transport to the Sparta hotel. In the mountain section, do not leave the marked route to find your own way down; go to the nearest CP and speak to the station chief. Medical decisions belong to the official medical team; if intravenous treatment is required, the rules require withdrawal.
