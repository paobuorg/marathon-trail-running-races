## 60-second verdict

- **What it is**: The Comrades Marathon is a road ultra-marathon in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, organised by the Comrades Marathon Association (CMA) between Pietermaritzburg (PMB) and Durban. Founded by Vic Clapham in 1921, it carries the slogans The Greatest Story Ever Run and The Ultimate Human Race. The official Entry Terms state that the race is not affiliated to Athletics South Africa or KwaZulu-Natal Athletics and is not represented as a World Athletics sanctioned event.
- **Most recent edition**: Sunday 14 June 2026 was the 99th edition and the 50th Up Run, starting outside Durban City Hall and finishing at the Scottsville Racecourse in Pietermaritzburg over an official 85.777 km; 21,633 runners started and 18,523 finished. The next edition is **Sunday 13 June 2027**, the 100th edition and the 50th Down Run, starting in front of the Pietermaritzburg City Hall and finishing at the Hollywoodbets Kings Park precinct in Durban.
- **Entry threshold**: A qualifying result must be secured before entry. The qualification window runs 15 June 2026 to 3 May 2027; the qualifying event must be at least 42.2 km; the runner must be at least 20 years old on qualification day; only officially published results count, and Garmin, Strava or other personal-device records are not accepted. The 42.2 km standard is 4:59:59, with slower ceilings for longer qualifying distances.
- **The biggest misread**: Up Run and Down Run alternate each year and are two races with opposite start and finish, different distance and different climb structure. 2026 was the Up Run (Durban to PMB, about 1,800–1,900 m ascent); 2027 is the Down Run (PMB to Durban, about 1,200 m ascent and 1,800–1,900 m descent). Records are kept separately by direction; this page does not present a combined Comrades course record.
- **Cut-off and medals**: The overall limit is 12 hours. The 2026 edition used six intermediate cut-off points, all timed from the Group 3 start at 05:30. Medals and badges follow a ladder of Gold, Wally Hayward / Isavel-Roche Kelly, Silver, Bill Rowan, Robert Mtshali, Bronze and Vic Clapham; only official finishers inside 12 hours receive a medal and badge.
- **Three native judgments**: The real difficulty is the combination of an ~89 km road distance, repeated climbs and descents, 12 hours of exposure and late-winter South African conditions — not an ordinary marathon stretched longer. Runners with only a few full marathons, insufficient long-distance tolerance, no 60 km-plus long run in training, or an expectation of a following crew car or pacer should not treat this as their first ultra. The most easily misread numbers are the 18,523 finishers (not the starter count), the 85.777 km figure (belonging only to the 2026 Up Run), and the 12-hour overall limit (which does not mean a steady walk to the finish, because intermediate points also close).

## Entry: qualification first, then the ballot

The Comrades differs from an ordinary road marathon in that a runner must hold a qualifying long-distance result before entering.

### Qualification window and age

Entry Terms 2.1 requires entry information to be submitted by **16:30 on 3 May 2027**; a late submission forfeits the entry. Entry Terms 2.2 requires the runner to be at least **20 years old** on the day of qualification.

The qualification window runs **15 June 2026 to 3 May 2027**; the qualifying event must be at least **42.2 km**. Only officially published results are accepted; Garmin, Strava or other personal-device records are not. After entering, every athlete must complete the mandatory medical questionnaire in the online entry profile. Athletes who do not meet the qualifying criteria are rejected, removed from the entry database, and receive no race T-shirt or goodie bag.

## Entry fees, places and charity ballot

Fees fall into three bands: South African citizens **R1,200**, entrants from the rest of Africa **R2,000**, international entrants **R4,500**. Runners who have completed 25 or more Comrades Marathons are exempt from the entry fee. An entry is confirmed only once payment succeeds by card or online instant EFT.

Entries are **non-transferable, non-refundable and may not be deferred** to a future year, and may not be sold or transferred; the sole exception is the official substitution process, whose period will be announced at the start of 2027.

## Course geometry: Down Run and Up Run written separately

The Comrades alternates direction each year between PMB and Durban. 2026 was the Up Run: from Durban City Hall north to PMB, finishing at Scottsville Racecourse. 2027 is the Down Run: from PMB City Hall south to Durban, finishing at the Kings Park precinct.

### Finish venue changes

Two changes are recorded in `courseChanges`. First, the 2027 finish moves to the Hollywoodbets Kings Park precinct in Durban; the entrant-statistics blog calls it a new finish venue, providing extra capacity for the expanded field, spectators and centenary activities; the 2026 Up Run finished at Scottsville Racecourse in PMB. ### Seconding, pacing and physical assistance

The rules on crew and seconding need to be stated clearly, so this road ultra is not mistaken for a race with a following crew car:

- Supporters may provide drinks or supplies to non-elite runners from a **stationary position** beside the route; this is the permitted stationary "stand and hand" assistance, within the scope stated in the final Race Rules.
- **Mobile seconding and pacing are prohibited**, as is parking on freeways, restricted roads or unsafe areas to contact runners.
- No supporter or second may drive, cycle or run alongside a participant on the route or within **100 metres** of a refreshment station.
- Runners must move forward under their own power; anyone carried or physically supported on the route or across the finish line is stopped, referred for medical attention and subjected to disciplinary procedures.
- Official rescue buses carry only runners who drop out, are cut off or cannot continue; they are not transport to the start, and "taking the rescue bus to the start" is not a viable plan.

For family support planning see [crew and pacer planning](/en/knowledge/crew-and-pacer-planning/) and the [crew/pacer plan tool](/en/tools/crew-pacer-plan/). ## Gear and rules

The clauses in the official regulations and Entry Terms that bear directly on gear and conduct are:

- **Own power**: runners must move under their own power; carrying or physical support is prohibited; violators are stopped, referred to medical care and enter disciplinary procedures.
- **Vehicles and pacing**: unauthorised vehicles, mobile seconding, pacing, carrying and physical support are prohibited; stationary roadside handover is allowed only within the scope of the final Race Rules.
- **Closed course**: non-accredited vehicles, bicycles and other transport may not enter the route; selected access roads close as emergency escape routes.
- **Elite aid**: elite stations are for seeded athletes only; interfering with or taking elite supplies may lead to sanctions and future exclusion.
- **Retirement rule**: missing a cut-off requires immediate retirement; boarding a rescue bus records a DNF with no re-entry.
- **Medical questionnaire**: all entrants must complete the medical questionnaire in their entry profile.
- **Entry discipline**: non-qualifying entries are rejected and removed, with no T-shirt or goodie bag; places are non-transferable, non-refundable and non-deferrable.

There is no trail-style mandatory gear list, but an ~89 km road ultra practically calls for broken-in race shoes, fuel and electrolytes rehearsed on long runs, warm layers for the cool PMB morning in late June, a rain backup, and a bib and timing setup. For shoe wear-in, long-distance tolerance and gear checklist see [running shoes](/en/knowledge/running-shoes/), the [race-day checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/) and [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/).

## Training transfer

The Comrades is not a race where a full marathon simply gets a few extra kilometres: an ~89 km distance, a 12-hour limit, repeated Big Five hills and cross-city road exposure make independent demands on long-distance tolerance, fuel management, quadriceps eccentric load and pace patience. For planning, the in-site [marathon training plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/) gives a generic periodisation framework; [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) handles first- to second-half allocation; [uphill and downhill running](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/) covers hill posture and eccentric load; the [taper planner](/en/tools/taper-planner/) and [race predictor](/en/tools/race-predict/) check status in the final fortnight.

The training transfer for this course:

1. **Long distance first**: over several months, run multiple 30 km-plus long runs progressing toward 50–60 km, validating legs, fuel and aid rhythm rather than piling on marathon-pace work.
2. **Hill tolerance**: include repeated gentle climbs and up-and-down long runs; the Down Run's downhill eccentric load concentrates on quads and IT band, while the Up Run's climb loads calves and cardio, and the two directions should be assessed separately.
3. **Fuel in advance**: rehearse gels, drinks, solids and electrolytes on long runs; do not try anything new on race day.
4. **Conservative pace**: hold back in the first half along the corridor; with a 12-hour limit, the later hills and fatigue accumulation are the main elimination points.
5. **Check the qualifier**: compare your ultra result against the qualifying table; runners who have only finished marathons with no 50 km-plus result should build long-distance experience before entering.

### History and cancellations

The race was first run in 1921; apart from five missed years during the Second World War (1941–1945) and two COVID years (2020 and 2021), it has run annually in alternating directions. The 2025 Down Run measured 89.98 km; the longest historical route was the 92 km course of 1971. The 2027 race is the 100th edition and the 50th Down Run, described by the CMA as the centenary edition.

## Medical and long-duration exposure

Entry Terms 9.1 defines the race as an **extreme-endurance event**, with a risk list including dehydration, heat or cold illness, cardiac events, collapse, musculoskeletal injury, traffic or crowd incidents, and death.

The pre-race medical blog advises: keep replacing fluids and electrolytes throughout the full distance; eat lightly and regularly; **do not take painkillers or anti-inflammatories during the race**. The listed medical red flags are dizziness, confusion, nausea, muscle cramps, disorientation, chest pain and sudden chills; the race doctor notes temperatures often drop steeply after 4 p.m. Medical stations line the route and a finish medical tent is staffed by Netcare 911 paramedics, nurses and doctors.

A 2026 Frontiers in Physiology review (synthesising 42 Comrades-focused publications through December 2025) records that exercise-associated hyponatremia was uncommon, below 2%, while hypernatremia was substantially more prevalent; dehydration was the most common diagnosis at 7.5%; exercise-associated muscle cramping at 3.2%; medical encounters occurred at up to 20 per 1,000 starters, with the fluid/electrolyte system most affected at 8.8%. These figures show fluid replacement is not "more is better": balance fluids and electrolytes. The official doctor's fluid-and-electrolyte advice sits alongside the review's EAH below 2% and more common hypernatremia; read together, they explain why drinking alone is not the message.
