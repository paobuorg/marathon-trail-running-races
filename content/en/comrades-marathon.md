## What this is: the 100th edition, a Down Run

Official Entry Terms 1.1 is precise: this is the 100th Comrades Marathon, scheduled for Sunday 13 June 2027 as a Down Run from Pietermaritzburg to Durban over an approximate distance of 89 km, subject to final measurement and the Final Race Instructions.

The same clause states that the race is organised by the Comrades Marathon Association (CMA).

The organiser also states that the race is not affiliated to Athletics South Africa or KwaZulu-Natal Athletics and is not represented as a World Athletics sanctioned event.

The 2027 start is in front of the Pietermaritzburg City Hall, and the finish is at the Hollywoodbets Kings Park precinct in Durban.

That is the official entrant-statistics wording, and it matches the direction in the Entry Terms.

By contrast, the 2026 edition was the 99th race and the 50th Up Run, starting outside the Durban City Hall and finishing at the Hollywoodbets Scottsville Racecourse in Pietermaritzburg over an official 85.777 km.

The 2027 race is the 100th edition, which the organiser calls the Centenary edition.

This page therefore says “100th edition” and does not describe it as “the edition after the 99th” by inference.

The direction alternates each year, which means the Up Run and the Down Run are two different races.

They share a rulebook and a pair of cities, but the start, finish, gradient structure and records differ.

This page therefore does not apply 2026 Up Run data to the 2027 Down Run.

## Direction and distance: 89 km is not 85.777 km

The official 2027 distance is “approximately 89 km”, the wording used in Entry Terms 1.1.

Both “approximately” and “subject to final measurement” matter: it is not a final surveyed figure.

The official 2026 Up Run distance was 85.777 km.

The official post-race report adds that the 2026 route was 133 metres shorter than in 2024 and the shortest Up Run distance in recorded history.

The two figures cannot replace each other: one belongs to the Up Run, the other to the Down Run.

A 2026 narrative review of Comrades also notes that historical route lengths range from 86 to 92 km depending on the finish venue.

That explains why “how long is Comrades?” has no single answer across directions.

The final 2027 measurement will only be fixed when the Final Race Instructions are published.

Until then, this page writes “approximately 89 km”, not 89.000 km, and does not average other years into it.

The 2027 finish also changes: the official entrant-statistics blog calls the Hollywoodbets Kings Park precinct a new finish venue and says it provides additional capacity for the expanded field, spectators and Centenary activities.

The 2026 finish was Scottsville Racecourse, according to the 2026 Final Race Instructions.

So 2027 is not only a reversal; the finish venue also changes.

The 2026 route was closed to all non-accredited vehicles, bicycles and other individual transport, and driving, cycling or parking on the route was prohibited.

On the “Big Five”, the official website does not have a single dedicated definition page.

Public race guides commonly name five hills: Cowies Hill, Field's Hill, Botha's Hill, Inchanga and Polly Shortts.

One such guide lists them in Up Run order as Cowies Hill, Field's Hill, Botha's Hill, Inchanga and Polly Shortts, and places Polly Shortts at about 80 km on the Up Run as the last climb of that direction.

Those five names come from a third-party guide, not an official page; this page lists them as public material and defers exact mileages and the 2027 order to the official route map.

Those place names are a third-party description, and this page does not expand them into an official section-by-section course description.

The official 2027 page does not publish cumulative elevation gain.

Those are review estimates, not official race measurements or this page's GPS results.

The entity therefore leaves `elevationGainMeters` empty, and the body labels the review figures as estimates.

It does not turn 1,200 m into an “official climb”, and it does not move the Up Run's 1,800 m onto the Down Run.

## Refreshment, personal seconding and physical assistance

The 2026 edition had 49 official refreshment stations.

All stations offered water sachets, Thirsti Sport energy drink and Coca-Cola.

Selected stations also offered potatoes, orange segments, bananas, 32Gi supplements and Orange Grove Amahewu.

Those are 2026 arrangements, not a 2027 promise.

The 2027 station count, locations and products will only be confirmed in the 2027 Final Race Instructions; they are unknown for now.

The 2026 race also had dedicated stations for elite seeded athletes to collect pre-mixed drinks and race nutrition.

The organiser states that unauthorised removal or interference with elite supplies can lead to sanctions and exclusion from future races.

On personal seconding, the organiser allows supporters to hand drinks or supplies to non-elite participants from a stationary position alongside the route.

The condition is that it is safe and does not obstruct runners, officials, medical staff or race operations.

Mobile seconding and pacing are prohibited.

Supporters are also prohibited from parking on freeways, restricted roads or unsafe areas to reach runners.

During the race the route is closed to non-accredited vehicles.

No supporter or second may drive, cycle or run alongside a participant on the route, or within 100 metres of a refreshment station.

So a “personal seconding table” at Comrades is not a table supplied by the organiser, and it is not a crew-vehicle system.

It is a supporter carrying supplies and handing them over from one fixed roadside point.

This page states that plainly so readers do not treat a road ultra as a race with a private follow car.

Physical assistance is separately prohibited: participants must move forward under their own power.

Anyone carried or physically supported on the route or across the finish line will be stopped, referred for medical attention, and the people involved may face disciplinary procedures.

The 2026 race started in three groups at 05:00, 05:15 and 05:30, with the wheelchair group at 04:45.

In 2026, late starters were accepted only until 05:45, 15 minutes after the Group 3 start.

The 2027 start groups will be announced at the start of 2027, so they are unknown for now.

## Intermediate cut-offs and the 12-hour limit

For 2027 the organiser currently publishes one overall limit: 12 hours.

The medal rule also says that badges and medals go only to official finishers who complete the full distance within the 12-hour cut-off.

The 2026 edition used a Traffic Light Cut-off System with six intermediate cut-off points plus the finish.

The 2026 finish cut-offs were set per group, each corresponding to 12 hours.

A runner who missed a cut-off in 2026 had to retire immediately and could not continue along the route.

Official rescue buses ran at designated cut-off points and along the route to take retiring or cut-off runners to the finish area.

Boarding a rescue bus recorded the race number and marked the runner as a DNF, with no re-entry.

The 2027 intermediate cut-off locations and times are not published; Entry Terms 12.8 says the Final Race Instructions will confirm them after the start format is approved.

This page therefore does not present the 2026 cut-off table as the 2027 rule.

The only 2027 certainty is the 12-hour overall limit; intermediate cut-offs are unknown until the organiser publishes them.

## Qualification: the section a road marathon does not have

This is the biggest difference from an ordinary road marathon: you need a qualifying result before you can enter.

The 2027 information and entry deadline is 16:30 on 3 May 2027.

Late submission forfeits the entry.

An athlete must be at least 20 years old on the day of qualification.

The qualifying window runs from 15 June 2026 to 3 May 2027.

The qualifying event must be at least 42.2 km.

Only officially published race results are accepted; Garmin, Strava or other personal-device records are not.

The official FAQ gives this qualification table:

| Distance | Maximum qualifying time |
| --- | --- |
| 42.2 km | 4:59:59 |
| 48 km | 5:59:59 |
| 50 km | 6:09:59 |
| 52–54 km | 6:29:59 |
| 56 km | 6:59:59 |
| 60 km | 7:39:59 |
| 64 km | 8:14:59 |
| 80 km | 10:34:59 |
| 90 km | 11:59:59 |
| 100 km | 13:34:59 |

The table means the 42.2 km barrier is 4:59:59, not simply “finish”.

The medical questionnaire is compulsory and must be completed by logging into the online entry profile.

If an athlete does not meet the criteria, the entry is rejected, removed from the entry database, and no race T-shirt or goodie bag is issued.

Entry fees have three tiers: R1,200 for South African citizens, R2,000 for the rest of Africa and R4,500 for international entrants.

Runners who have completed 25 or more Comrades Marathons have the entry fee waived.

Entries are non-transferable and non-refundable, cannot be deferred to a future year, and the only exception is the official substitution process.

The official substitution period will be announced at the start of 2027.

2027 race-pack collection runs on 10 June 10:00–19:00, 11 June 09:00–19:00 and 12 June 09:00–16:00.

Entrants choose either the Durban Exhibition Centre or Comrades House in Pietermaritzburg.

Those dates and venues are the official FAQ wording, not this page's calculation.

## Field size and the ballot

The official entrant-statistics blog says 2027 received 56,283 entry submissions and ballot applications.

That is the total number of submissions and ballot applications in the three-part process, not the final number of starters.

The same blog says 28,070 athletes successfully secured an entry.

The 2027 entry cap was raised to 28,000 athletes.

The official comparison is 22,000 entries in 2026 and 24,000 for the most recent Down Run in 2025.

The organiser calls 2027 the largest field in Comrades Marathon history.

The three numbers must be read separately: 56,283 applications and submissions, 28,070 athletes with an entry, and 28,000 as the cap.

The 2026 edition had 21,633 runners and 18,523 finishers.

Those are 2026 figures and cannot be used directly as 2027 starters or finishers.

The actual 2027 starters and finishers will only be known from official results after race day.

The ballot timetable was also published: general ballot applications closed on 22 September 2026 at 16:30.

The initial draw took place on 23 September 2026, with notifications by email, SMS and WhatsApp.

Automatic debit payment began on 25 September and concluded on 26 September.

A redraw, if required, took place on 28 September, with payment processing on 29–30 September.

Those dates describe a process already completed in 2026, not a new 2027 entry window.

## Records: Down Run and Up Run separately

Comrades records must be split by direction because the two courses have different start and finish points and different gradient structures.

The official Best Down Run time is 5:13:58 by Tete Dijana in 2023.

The official Best Down Run women's time is 5:44:54 by Gerda Steyn in 2023.

The Up Run men's record is 5:15:56 by George Kusche in 2026.

The Up Run women's record is 5:44:53 by Gerda Steyn in 2026.

The two women's records differ by one second: 5:44:54 on the Down Run and 5:44:53 on the Up Run.

Kusche broke the 5:24:49 set by Leonid Shvetsov in 2008.

Steyn broke her own 5:49:46 set in 2024.

The 2026 Up Run course was 85.777 km, so those Up Run records were set over that distance.

The 2027 Down Run is approximately 89 km, a different course, so Up Run times cannot be compared directly.

This page therefore does not create a combined “Comrades course record”.

Down Run and Up Run are listed side by side, each with its own year, name and direction.

A 2027 Down Run record can only be judged after the final measurement and the 2027 results.

## Medical: heat illness, hyponatremia and prolonged exposure

Official Entry Terms 9.1 defines the race as an extreme-endurance event.

The same clause lists dehydration, heat or cold illness, cardiac events, collapse, musculoskeletal injury, traffic or crowd incidents, and death.

The official pre-race medical advice is to keep replacing fluids and electrolytes throughout the full distance.

The same article advises eating lightly and regularly, and explicitly says not to take painkillers or anti-inflammatories during the race.

The official medical red flags are dizziness, confusion, nausea, muscle cramps, disorientation, chest pain and sudden chills.

The race doctor also notes that temperatures often drop steeply after 4pm.

The 2026 edition had about 1.8 km of elevation gain, the Up Run figure given in the medical article.

But the main medical frame for this race is not hypothermia; it is heat illness, hyponatremia and 12 hours of prolonged exposure.

The 2026 review reports exercise-associated hyponatremia (EAH) at Comrades below 2%, while hypernatremia is more prevalent.

The same review records dehydration as the most common diagnosis at 7.5%, and exercise-associated muscle cramping at 3.2%.

It also reports medical encounters at rates up to 20 per 1,000 starters, with fluid and electrolyte problems accounting for 8.8%.

Those figures mean hydration is not “the more the better”; it is a balance between fluid and electrolyte replacement.

The official doctor asks for fluid and electrolyte replacement, while the review records EAH below 2% and hypernatremia as more common; together they show why fluid intake cannot be reduced to “drink more”.

The official risk list also includes cold illness, but this page does not make hypothermia the main framework.

For most participants, the more relevant preparation is heat, humidity, prolonged exposure and fluid-electrolyte management.

The medical questionnaire is compulsory, medical stations operate along the route, and there is a finish medical tent.

## Arrival and weather

The 2027 start is in Pietermaritzburg and the finish is in Durban, so the two ends are in different cities.

The organiser has not published a single participant-transport page.

The 2026 Final Race Instructions only cover route closures, the ban on parking on freeways and restricted roads, and official rescue buses.

Race-day public transport schedules, shuttles, last buses and parking capacity are therefore unknown on this page.

For self-driving, the N3 corridor links Durban and Pietermaritzburg, according to a third-party race guide.

On race day the route is closed to non-accredited vehicles and some access roads are closed, but the organiser has not published a closure timetable.

Official rescue buses are for runners who retire, are cut off or cannot continue; they are not transport to the start.

So “take the rescue bus to the start” is not a viable plan.

Accommodation and return travel must be arranged independently because the start and finish are in different cities, a fact fixed by the official start and finish descriptions.

The weather section gives regional climate only, not a race-day forecast.

Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis for Pietermaritzburg (29.6168 S, 30.3928 E, 636 m) over 300 June days from 2016 to 2025 gives a mean daily maximum of 22.1 °C, a mean daily minimum of 7.7 °C, mean precipitation of 0.4 mm per day, and at least 1 mm on 8.3% of June days.

The same dataset for Durban (29.8579 S, 31.0292 E, 21 m) over 300 June days gives a mean daily maximum of 22.5 °C, a mean daily minimum of 13.9 °C, mean precipitation of 0.8 mm per day, and at least 1 mm on 15.7% of June days.

The start city can be colder in the early morning, while the finish city is warmer and more humid; these are climate statistics for two cities.

But the course descends from Pietermaritzburg to Durban through a mountain-to-coast transition, so city-centre figures cannot represent the whole route.

These are regional climate statistics, not organiser data, and not measurements or a forecast for 13 June 2027.

A race-day forecast for 2027 must wait for the organiser or a meteorological service to publish one close to the event.

## Difficulty and risk

Comrades is not hard because of one gradient; it is hard because of 89 km of continuous running, the Big Five's repeated climbs and descents, a 12-hour limit, and June heat followed by a sharp afternoon cool-down. The official 2026 medical note gives about 1,800 m of cumulative climb for the Up Run; the Down Run's net descent loads the quadriceps and IT band eccentrically. The organiser calls the race an extreme-endurance event and lists dehydration, heat/cold illness, cardiac events, collapse, musculoskeletal injury and death. There is no altitude and no technical trail; the difficulty is road pace plus long climbs plus heat plus 12 hours. This page does not reduce it to "any marathon finisher can do it".
