## What this race is (distance disambiguation first)

Val d'Aran by UTMB takes place in the Val d'Aran in Spain, and is the European Major of the UTMB World Series. The 2026 edition was the sixth, with 6,935 participants from 91 nationalities across eight formats. The event was first held in 2021: the CDH on 9 July 2021 was 104 km with 6,500 m of gain and 951 finishers, won in 13:03:25. By 2026 the CDH was 110 km with 6,400 m. Distances within a format change between years, so the 2021 CDH figures must not be carried over to 2027.

The VDA is the Tour of the Val d'Aran, starting and finishing in Vielha and looping around the valley. The official description names wild valleys, abandoned mines and cirques of lakes, Aranese villages, and a high point of 2,551 m. The official track's named points run in this order: Vielha → Tuc Montcorbison → Bassa d'Oles → Artiga de Lin → Uelhs deth Joeu → Tuc dera Escaleta → Poilaner → Còth de Baretja → Portilhon → Bossòst → Les → Canejan → Refugi Honeria → Mines de Liat → Còth de Varradòs → Pòrt d'Urets → Montgarri → Beret → Salardú → Banhs de Tredòs → Estanh Obago → Colomèrs → Mont-Romies → Arties → Stet Escunhau → Tuc de Meddia → Vielha.

The 2026 VDA marked the return of the original course: the official race report states that the original VDA route had been unable to take place over the previous three years because of weather, and that it returned in 2026, with a winning time of 21:32:05. The 2027 race card carries the same VDA headline as 2026: 163 km and 10,000 m of gain.

The card at the top of this page uses the track's 163.011 km and 10,053 m of gain; the text keeps the race card's 163 km and 10,000 m alongside it. The difference is 11 m of distance and 53 m of gain, which is two precisions of the same course, not two courses.

The high point needs three separate statements: the track's maximum elevation is 2,551 m; the highest named point is Pòrt d'Urets at 2,520 m; the highest timed checkpoint is Còth de Varradòs at 2,048 m. Tuc dera Escaleta at 2,454 m is also higher than every timed checkpoint, but it is not the highest point overall.

Loss appears only in the track: −10,049 m. The race card publishes no loss, and the official pages do not describe loss as "roughly equal to gain". This page does not make that conversion.

- **Segment 1 (0 → 10.5 km, Bassa d'Oles):** climbs Tuc Montcorbison (2,158 m) and descends to Bassa d'Oles (1,600 m). This is the first timed checkpoint; cut-off 19:15, slowest 18:55, earlier than the cut-off.

## Official supplies, bag drops and assistance

Supply levels come from the official track's `supplies` field: 11 points are marked food, three are marked hotFood (Bossòst, Salardú, Arties), three are marked drink (Poilaner, Mont-Romies, Stet Escunhau), and the rest are marked none. The official page does not list the specific food and drink at each point in text, so this page uses only those four levels and does not claim that a given point serves hot soup or water only.

Bag rules: each VDA runner receives three 30-litre bags, delivered to Bossòst, Salardú and Arties. The runner collects each bag on arrival, puts it back in the designated area before leaving, and the organisation returns the bags to Vielha. Bags are handed over at the finish on presentation of the race bib and must be collected within two hours of finishing; after that the runner must go in person to the organisation headquarters in Vielha. The organisation transports bags for runners whose withdrawal has been registered, but does not guarantee that every bag reaches Vielha before the race ends.

Assistance rules: assistance is allowed only at Bossòst, Salardú and Arties, by one person carrying a bag of at most 30 litres. Pacers are not allowed. Assistance from a professional team, doctor or paramedic is forbidden. No other checkpoint and no other point on the course allows outside assistance.

Mandatory equipment: the official page publishes only a downloadable PDF, and this page does not reproduce the list. Runners must check the official 2027 PDF item by item; another UTMB event's list is not a substitute.

Withdrawal: the official transport page says that withdrawing runners are taken to the finish in Vielha or to a point served by the UTMB-Bus or urban bus, free on presentation of the race bib and wristband. The official pages do not state evacuation capability point by point, so this page treats it as unknown.

## Risks and escape points

- **Altitude and weather:** the course runs from 609 m to 2,551 m. July can be hot in the valley and stormy or cold at altitude; this page has no summit measurements and does not treat the Vielha grid-point temperature as the feel at 2,500 m.
- **Night:** with a 16:00 start and a 48-hour limit, every runner spends at least one night on the course; headtorch and spare batteries follow the official PDF.
- **Long descents:** segments 4, 9, 11 and 15 each contain 600–1,300 m of continuous descent; descending ability matters more than climbing in the second half.
- **Assistance limits:** only Bossòst, Salardú and Arties allow support; no other point does, so "a friend waiting on the road" is not a plan.
- **Escape:** Artiga de Lin, Bossòst, Beret, Salardú, Banhs de Tredòs and Arties are marked with a bus stop in the track; the official pages do not state evacuation capability for the other points, so this page treats them as unknown.
- **Cut-offs:** use the official displayed text. From Honeria onward the slowest time is later than the cut-off, so following the slowest schedule does not guarantee a finish and the slowest column is not a cut-off.
- **Cancellation or rerouting:** the 2026 VDA returned to the original route after three years in which weather prevented it, so this course is not guaranteed to run on the same geometry every year. The 2027 page publishes no cancellation or rerouting terms, so this page treats them as unknown.

## Getting there

The destination is Vielha, the VDA start and finish. Val d'Aran has no airport and no direct railway.

Nearest airports: Lleida 145 km, Pau 157 km, Tarbes 161 km, Toulouse 166 km, Zaragoza 290 km and Barcelona 350 km. Every one of them reaches the valley by road only.

Nearest railway stations: Luchon 40 km, Montrejeau 60 km and Lleida 165 km. Each also requires a road connection into the valley.

There are four road entries: the 5,173 m Vielha tunnel from Alta Ribagorça; the Bonaigua pass at 2,072 m from Pallars; the French N-618/N-230 through Pont de Rei; and the Portilhon pass from Bagnères-de-Luchon to Bossòst.

Buses: ALSA runs a daily direct service from Barcelona to Vielha and regular daily services from Barcelona and Lleida, with the Vielha stop at Av. Alcalde Calbetó Barra.

Event transport: the official transport page is still the 2026 version. It says "Looking ahead to 2026", gives 1–5 July dates, and lists TDL on Wednesday 1 July, PDA on Thursday 2 July and SKY on Saturday 4 July — not the 2027 dates. What can be confirmed is narrower: the UTMB-Bus connects almost all villages from Les to Beret at 30–60 minute frequencies, up to every 15 minutes at peak; VDA and EXP runners ride free without reservation; CDH, TDL and PDA runners must reserve a stop and time before 14 June; SKY runners must buy a ticket. The 2027 timetable, stops and reservation deadline remain unknown, and the 2027 transport page must be checked before travel.

## July season and weather

Open-Meteo's historical archive was queried for the Vielha grid point (42.7768 N, 0.7988 E, 967 m) for 5–13 July in each year from 2015 to 2026, a total of 108 days.

- Daily maximum: 13.0–35.4 °C, median 24.3 °C.
- Daily minimum: 7.8–18.8 °C, median 14.5 °C.
- Precipitation: 53 of 108 days reached 1 mm, 19 reached 5 mm, and the maximum daily total was 25.1 mm.

These are modelled grid-point values, not course measurements. The grid point is at 967 m while the course reaches 2,551 m, so the 24 °C median maximum must not be treated as the feel at Pòrt d'Urets or Colomèrs. The actual weather for the 2027 race week (9–11 July) is unknown as of the verification date and should be rechecked in the forecast before the race.

What is certain: 163 km, 10,053 m of gain, 10,049 m of loss, 48 hours, 16 timed checkpoints and 14 intermediate cut-offs. This is a long mountain trail race, not a road ultra.

Within the same series, the VDA is the 100M equivalent, the CDH the 100K and the PDA the 50K; their distances and gains are not interchangeable. The VDA's 163 km should not be rounded to "160 km" or "165 km".

That file is downloaded under the name `VDA_2027_COLOMERS`, but the folder it sits in is named `GPX 2026 DEFINTIVOS EVENTO`, its `<metadata><name>` and `<trk><name>` both say `VDA 2026_COLOMERS`, its author is `gpx.studio`, its creation time is 2024-05-30, and it contains Strava route links. In other words, the filename says 2027 while the folder name and the file metadata say 2026 — the three disagree. Measured point to point, the file is 160.297 km in plan; the 2027 race page publishes 163 km and the track embedded in that page is labelled 163.011 km, a gap of about 2.7 km. The gap can be located in the distance convention: adding each segment's vertical change to the same file gives 163.446 km, only 0.435 km (0.27%) above the official 163.011 km, and the file's first and last points sit 0.4 m and 0.3 m from the embedded track's endpoints, which does not support a "missing section" explanation. The remaining 0.435 km cannot be attributed: the official algorithm and elevation smoothing are not published. The file carries no licence or copyright terms. This page therefore treats it as a file "named 2027, labelled 2026", uses it only to sanity-check elevation magnitude, and does not treat it as 2027 course geometry or host, draw or redistribute it.
