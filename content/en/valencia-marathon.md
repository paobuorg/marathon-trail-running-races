## 60-second verdict

- **Should you treat it as a PB course: conditionally yes.** The route sits close to sea level, the official map reports a maximum difference of only 16.2 m, early-December morning temperatures have mostly landed in the 7-14 °C band, the course runs along wide urban asphalt avenues, it is AIMS-certified with a World Athletics Platinum Label, and an official pace group works the start. Those are the hard reasons it appears on European fast-course lists. It is not a guaranteed-PB course: 36,000 runners create a crowded start, the historic-centre section has dense turns, the seafront and lake finish can carry crosswinds, the organiser publishes no cumulative climb figure, and no downloadable GPX track is offered (sources: route-valencia-2026-regulations, route-valencia-2026-course-pdf, route-valencia-openmeteo, route-valencia-2026-course-page).
- **One-line judgement**: if your recent 10K or half-marathon fitness already matches your target marathon pace and you can earn an early wave, Valencia is a credible PB candidate. If your base is thin and you land in a late wave, the early kilometres behind the pack and the old-town corners will erode the pace advantage you expected.

The 2027 date, edition number and course were not published on the official site as of that date; third-party calendar dates for 2027 are unverified and must not be treated as confirmed.

## Entry and ballot

### Process and draw

The 2026 entry cycle combines a loyalty period with an open public ballot (source: route-valencia-2026-registration):

1. **Loyalty period**: 2025 participants get priority entry on 11-15 December.
2. **Ballot registration**: open 16-26 December for the remaining places.
3. **Notarial draw**: held on 12 January in the presence of a notary.
4. **Results**: winners and waitlisted runners are emailed on 13 January.
5. **Payment**: selected runners must complete purchase between 13 and 26 January; an unpaid bib is cancelled and offered to the waiting list in order.
6. **Waiting list**: released bibs are offered from 26 January in registration order.

Ballot entry itself is free, but it requires an active bank card on which a €5 hold is placed for at most 15 days; the hold is released automatically whether or not the runner wins a place. Personal details entered in the ballot cannot be changed later; one entry per person; group entries are limited to three people with separate email addresses. Loyalty and ballot bibs allow a holder transfer from 1 February; a ballot bib freed by a holder change goes exclusively to a waiting-list runner.

### Standards and elite criteria

- **Age**: 18 or over on race day.
- **Proof of performance**: runners aiming for a finish under 3:32:00 must prove a previous best in a marathon, half marathon, 15K or 10K within the last three years (source: route-valencia-2026-regulations, Article 3).
- **Sub-elite standards** on 2024-2026 approved courses: men under 30:00 for 10K/10,000 m, under 1:06:00 half marathon, under 2:20:00 marathon; women under 35:30, under 1:17:30, under 2:45:00.

## Course and geometry

### Start, finish and route character

The race starts at Plaça de la Marató and finishes on the footbridge over the CACSA lake in front of the Hemisfèric (sources: route-valencia-2026-course-page, route-valencia-2026-pacing-pdf). Start and finish sit in the same waterfront district by the City of Arts and Sciences. The 42.195 km route is an urban loop out through El Cabanyal, along the seafront and northern avenues, into the historic centre, and back to the City of Arts. It is **not a cross-city point-to-point race**, so there is no long transfer after the finish; equally, it is not a single greenway loop, repeatedly moving between city streets, squares and avenue junctions.

The official course page describes the route as flat and built for speed, running from the El Cabanyal neighbourhood through historic Valencia. The regulations specify an urban race on asphalt (Article 2).

### Geometry availability

- **No downloadable track.** As of 2026-11-25 the official course page offers only a static course map JPG/PDF, a per-kilometre split-time PDF and a Strava club link; the page's link list contains no GPX, KML, KMZ or GeoJSON file (source: link audit of route-valencia-2026-course-page).

### Recomputable margin example (3:30 target)

Assume a 3:30 target, a PB in the 3:20:00-3:31:59 band, wave 5 starting 08:55.

- Target pace: 210 minutes ÷ 42.195 km = **4:58.6 per kilometre**.
- Time to km 25: 25 × 4:58.6 ≈ **2:04:25**, clock about **10:59:25**; margin to the 12:57 sweeper about **1:57:35**.
- On target, finish clock **12:25**; margin to the personal wave 5:30 close of 14:25 is **2:00**; margin to the 15:15 course close is **2:50**.

### Recomputable margin example (2:45 target)

Assume a 2:45 target, wave 1 starting 08:15.

- Target pace: 165 minutes ÷ 42.195 km = **3:54.6 per kilometre**.
- Time to km 25: 25 × 3:54.6 ≈ **1:37:46**, clock about **09:52:46**; margin to the sweeper about **3:04:14**.
- On target, finish clock **11:00**; margin to the 15:15 close is **4:15**.

Both examples do pace and clock arithmetic only; they do not predict actual performance. Waves are assigned from the PB declared at registration; an overstated result may be moved to a later wave. ## Aid, logistics and crew/pacer support

### Aid stations

Article 16 of the regulations lists liquid stations at approximately km 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 32, 35, 37.5 and 40, plus a final liquid and solid station at the finish. The official course page specifies **9 refreshment points** with Aquabona 33 cl bottled water and cup-dispensed Aquarius, and **3 Enervit red points** with energy gels, caffeinated and non-caffeinated (source: route-valencia-2026-course-page).

The course map legend marks aid locations at 5.5, 10, 14.5, 16.2 (gel), 18.7, 23, 25.8 (gel), 28.3, 33.1, 34.0 (gel), 37.1 and 40.4; these differ slightly from the regulations' round-kilometre list. Follow on-course signage on race day; this page does not merge the two kilometre sets into one table.

Three Farmacia Ribera rapid care points sit at **km 24.5, 29.5 and 34.2**, providing vaseline and analgesic spray, with a post-finish point as well (source: route-valencia-race-services).

### Bag drop, engraving and showers

- **Bag drop**: the service must be booked in advance; runners who booked it carry a coat-hanger icon on the bib, and it cannot be added later.
- **Medal engraving**: an optional paid service next to the bag-drop area on Avenida del Profesor López Piñero, recording name and official time on the medal.
- **Showers**: post-race 10:00-17:00 at the San Luis municipal swimming pool.

### Official pace group

The race runs an official pace group, **MSC Prácticos**: pacers hold a steady pace from start to finish for a target finishing time and carry a highly visible banner that can be identified in the start area. Note that the reviewed services page does not list the target finishing times covered by the group; the information link points to an Instagram post, which this page did not take as a source. Check the official services page before race week for the confirmed pace bands.

### Crew viewing and arrival logistics

Start and finish share the City of Arts area, so spectators do not need a cross-city transfer. The 2025 municipal notice (the 2026 traffic arrangement was not yet published on 2026-11-25) set three controlled pedestrian crossings: the Xàtiva metro underpass, Colón/Sorní and Colón/Porta de Mar, staffed by service personnel and Civil Protection volunteers (source: route-valencia-city-traffic-2025).

**Bib collection (Expo 42K)**: Feria Valencia, Av. de les Fires s/n, 46035 València; Thursday 17:00-21:00, Friday 09:00-21:00, Saturday 09:00-19:00; no race-day pickup. After 7 November a third party may be authorised via the Runner's Area to collect the bib and goodie bag (source: route-valencia-2026-regulations, Article 10). The regulations carry a typo labelling the Thursday "4rd"; against the 2026 calendar it corresponds to Thursday 3 December.

**Public transport and parking**: the 2025 notice opened Oceanogràfic and Aqua shopping-centre car parks for runners from 06:00; alternatives were the cemetery car park with EMT line 99, or the Natzaret car park with tram line 4. EMT rerouted 32 lines; lines 35, 99 and C3 serve the start and City of Arts. Tram lines 4, 6, 8 and 10 run partial morning suspensions. The organiser also partners with Renfe for a minimum 15% discount on AVE, Alvia, Euromed and Intercity services to Valencia between 1 and 13 December 2026; the authorisation code is issued in the Self-Management Area after registration verification (source: route-valencia-renfe-discount).

## Gear and rules

- **Age and qualification**: 18 or over on race day; sub-3:32:00 entries must submit a performance from the last three years, and false qualification material may be rejected or moved to a later wave.
- **Bib**: worn visibly on the front throughout; handed back at the sweeper point.
- **Course conduct**: stay on the marked course; no accompanying bicycle, skateboard or external assistance; the regulations prohibit off-course help and support outside the official aid structure, and violations may invalidate the result.
- **Anti-doping**: the race may conduct mandatory anti-doping tests; violations lead to suspension and publication under the rules.
- **Appeals**: objections to ranking or timing must be submitted in writing to the organisers within 48 hours of results publication.
- **Weather gear**: early-December mornings are cool (observed start temperatures 7-14 °C across three editions); a vest under a discardable long sleeve or arm sleeves is more flexible than a heavy layer, and plan for wind on the seafront and lake finish. Follow the forecast in the week before; this page recommends no specific brand or model.

## Why it is treated as a PB course

This section answers one question: **what makes it fast, which conditions are data-backed, and which are only promotional impression.**

- **Altitude and terrain**: the official profile shows 1.8-18 m altitude with a 16.2 m maximum difference and no sustained climb; the city sits on a Mediterranean coastal plain. That is the firmest terrain evidence for speed. Note that no cumulative climb is published; the third-party Strava segment's 76 m works out to about 1.8 m/km, the magnitude of a low-relief urban race, but it is not a certified climb figure.
- **Gradient**: the official file publishes no section-by-section gradient percentage. By street sequence, the northern avenues (Tarongers, Cataluña, Aragón, Blasco Ibáñez) and southwest avenues (Tres Cruces) are relatively straight and wide, while old-town streets (Blanquerías, Poeta Querol, Guillén de Castro) are narrower with more junctions and corners.
- **Turn count**: the organiser does not publish a total turn count. The split sequence repeatedly moves through squares, avenue junctions and narrow old-town streets; wide avenues suit steady pace, while the historic-centre section requires slowing for turns and the crowd.
- **Wind**: sections include the seafront and the lake finish; the historical sample's daily maximum wind median is 15.4 km/h with a 36.1 km/h upper bound. The organiser publishes no windbreak or sheltered-route data; plan for crosswinds on seafront sections, bridges and the lake finish.
- **Temperature window**: observed start temperatures across three editions were 7-14 °C and finishes 9-17 °C; model sample daily maxima median 16.5 °C and minima median 9.3 °C. The start band lands in a favourable cool range for marathons, though humidity and occasional hot years erode that advantage.
- **Pacing and organisation support**: the official MSC Prácticos pace group works the race; nine seeded waves reduce fast-slow mixing; AIMS certification means the distance is measured to standard, so a short-course PB dispute does not arise; the City of Arts finish area has mature viewing and aid organisation.
- **Slowing factors**: 36,000 runners mean late waves are held up for the first kilometres; the old town has dense turns; high aid-station density still means queuing at peak times; wind and humidity are outside the organiser's control.
- **Judgement**: the terrain and temperature window support listing it as a PB candidate. Whether a PB actually comes out depends on your training reserve, the wave you earn, the day's weather and your cornering rhythm — not on the slogan "Valencia is flat".

## Training transfer

1. **Work out target pace before matching a wave.** Read [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/), convert targets such as 3:30 and 2:45 into per-kilometre pace with the [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/), and lay out 5 km segments with the [split tool](/en/tools/splits/). Then match yourself against the wave bands and the sub-3:32:00 proof requirement.
2. **Estimate the finish from recent results, not feel.** Use the [race predictor](/en/tools/race-predict/) with a recent 10K or half-marathon result to bracket the marathon finish; if the prediction sits on the edge of a wave band, start conservatively rather than overstating a result to reach an earlier wave.
3. **Confirm the 42.195 km training base.** Read [marathon distance](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/) and check long-run accumulation, taper week and race-week rhythm; a fast city course does not justify skipping the aerobic base build.
4. **Prepare for a cool start and coastal wind.** The 7-14 °C start samples mean layered warm-up matters more than heavy clothing; old-town turns mean training should include corner control and weaving through a crowd, not only steady pace on an empty track.
5. **Align fuelling with the official stations.** Match the regulations' liquid stations at km 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 32, 35, 37.5 and 40 plus the three gel points; rehearse water and gel rhythm in long runs so aid-station stops do not break pace on race day.
