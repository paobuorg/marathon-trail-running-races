## 60-second verdict

- **What it is**: The TCS New York City Marathon is a World Athletics Elite Platinum Label urban road marathon organised by NYRR. It runs point-to-point from Fort Wadsworth on Staten Island through all five boroughs of New York City, finishing at 67th Street on West Drive in Central Park, Manhattan. The official course distance is 26.2 miles; 42,195 m is the standard marathon conversion, but NYRR does not publish a metric figure.
- **Most recent edition**: Held Sunday, 1 November 2026. The official post-race release records 61,529 starters and 61,146 finishers from 153 countries, including 15,745 international finishers. The next edition is **Sunday, 7 November 2027**, per the official NYRR project calendar.
- **Entry threshold**: Places split into guaranteed entry and non-guaranteed ballot. Guaranteed paths include charity partners, International Tour Operators, the 9+1 program, time qualifiers, Team for Kids, Team for Climate, 15-plus finishers, 5K/10K membership, 2025 cancellation retention and Race Free. The ballot runs three pools — NYC metro, U.S. national, international — plus a member-only second-chance draw. Time qualifiers are split by age and gender; the 2027 entry qualifying window runs 1 January to 31 December 2026, evaluated on net chip time.
- **Course difficulty**: Reputation aside, this is a big-city sightseeing race with real load: the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, the Pulaski Bridge, the Queensboro Bridge, then the Willis Avenue and Madison Avenue bridges for the Bronx out-and-back. The opening bridge climb is the highest point on course, with exposed wind on the decks. NYRR has not published cumulative elevation, and this page does not invent an elevation figure.
- **Cut-off and recognition**: Five mass waves start between 9:10 and 11:30. The street schedule is built around a 16-minute-per-mile pace from the final wave. Runners back at the finish by 22:00 ET receive a medal, an official result on results.nyrr.org, 9+1 credit toward 2027 and an Abbott World Marathon Majors star; anyone finishing after 22:00 gets a medal only, with no official result.
- **Three native judgments**: The real difficulty is the combination of three bridge rollers, wave congestion and urban pavement, not a flat tour. Runners without a completed marathon, those hovering near or beyond a 16-minute-per-mile pace, and anyone expecting a private crew car or an official pacer should not treat this as a first marathon. The most easily misread numbers are the 61,146 finishers (not the starter count), the 22:00 medal line (not the official-result line), and net versus gun time.

## History and the five-borough course

The five-borough course was first run on **24 October 1976**; the 2026 edition marks its 50th anniversary. The 2026 edition carries a second milestone: NYRR describes the Bronx adjustment as the first course change in 15 years. The revised routing is detailed under Course segments and the 2026 Bronx change.

The race is organised by NYRR under World Athletics rules, with the wheelchair division governed by World Para Athletics rules; the course is certified by USATF and/or World Athletics. These affiliations appear at the head of the NYRR Rules of Competition.

The 2026 scale is another checkable coordinate: the post-race release records 61,529 starters and 61,146 finishers, a third consecutive year setting the world record for the largest marathon, with 32,047 men's finishers, 28,942 women's finishers and 157 nonbinary finishers. The main page lists 59,226 finishers in 2025, an average finish of 4:32:25 across 130 countries, and about $700 million raised for charity since 2006. These annual figures are comparison only and are not merged into the 2026 results.

![2026 NYC Marathon: finishers by division](/media/routes/new-york/finishers-2026-en.svg)

As a World Marathon Majors city race, New York's urban scale shapes its operating model: the start is on an island and the finish on another, so transport, bag check and wave assignment are split into separate official pages. Runners cannot assume the crew cars, bag vehicles or pacers that come with a loop marathon.

## Entry: ballot, 9+1 and time qualifiers

The 2026 event page splits entry into guaranteed methods and the non-guaranteed ballot. One caveat first: the dates below belong to the **2026** entry cycle. The 2027 application window, draw date and fees follow the NYRR announcement in due course; this page does not project 2026 dates onto 2027.

### Guaranteed entry methods

The official event page lists the following guaranteed paths:

1. **Official charity partner**: raise funds for an official charity partner in exchange for a guaranteed place; 2026 charity spots were announced on 4 March.
2. **International Tour Operator (ITO)**: non-U.S. residents can book a guaranteed-entry package with hotel and/or flights through an official ITO.
3. **9+1 program**: complete nine qualifying races plus one volunteer shift in the prior calendar year and keep active membership to earn a non-complimentary guaranteed place for the next edition.
4. **2025 virtual race**: runners who registered for and completed the 2025 virtual TCS NYC Marathon (guaranteed-entry group) earned a 2026 non-complimentary guaranteed place.
5. **Team for Kids**: the charity fundraising channel for NYRR youth programs (full for 2026, waitlist open).
6. **Team for Climate**: the sustainability entry raising funds toward carbon offsets and insets (full for 2026).
7. **15-plus finishers**: runners completing their 15th New York City Marathon earn guaranteed entry in future years.
8. **NYRR time qualifiers**: meet the standard at an NYRR in-person marathon or half marathon within the qualifying window.
9. **Non-NYRR time qualifiers**: meet the standard at a certified full marathon; places are limited and meeting the standard does not guarantee entry.
10. **5K/10K membership**: members at the 5K and 10K level receive annual benefits including this race.
11. **2025 cancellation retention**: runners who cancelled a 2025 place may apply for a 2026 guaranteed entry, but must register and pay the 2026 fee in the claim window.
12. **NYRR Race Free**: fee assistance for runners who qualify financially; 2026 offered 1,000 spots and more than 3,000 complimentary entries, with the 2027 application expected to open in October 2026.

### Non-guaranteed ballot

Runners without a guaranteed place enter the non-guaranteed general ballot. For 2026 the timeline was: applications opened 4 February at 12:00 p.m. ET and closed 25 February at 11:59 p.m. ET; the non-NYRR time-qualifier application closed 11 February at 11:59 p.m. ET; the **draw was held 4 March**. The ballot runs three separate pools:

| Ballot pool | Applicant definition |
| --- | --- |
| NYC metro area | Residents in and within 60 miles of New York City |
| National | U.S. residents outside the metro area, including Puerto Rico and other U.S. territories |
| International | Non-U.S. residents |

Eligible NYRR members who apply to the general ballot are automatically entered into the **member-only second-chance drawing**. Selection is confirmed by credit-card charge, email and the NYRR account status; once accepted, fees are non-refundable and non-transferable.

### 9+1 program details

The **2026 9+1 program** for a 2027 place requires: register for and complete nine qualifying races in 2026, complete one +1 volunteer opportunity, and hold active NYRR membership as of **31 December 2026**. Qualifying races are flagged on the race calendar; 2026 listed more than 30 in-person races and three virtual races (Virtual Resolution Run 5K, Virtual NYRR Global Running Day 5K, Virtual NYRR Run for Thanks 5K), and virtual 9+1 credit requires the Premium tier at registration.

Three limits matter: the 2026 program place applies **only to the 2027 marathon** and does not roll forward to 2028 or another future year; runners already entered for 2027 through another method cannot use the 2026 program for 2027 or later eligibility; and a guaranteed entry not claimed in the claim window is forfeited.

From the **2027 9+1 Pass program** (for 2028 entry), access changes to a free-to-enter member-only drawing: active membership by 16 September 2026 and through drawing day 1 October 2026; enter the drawing 17–29 September 2026; the draw is held 1 October 2026; selected members are charged **$99.00** including the processing fee and become 9+1 Passholders. In calendar 2027 they must complete nine qualifying races, one volunteer shift and keep active membership as of 31 December 2027 to earn the 2028 non-complimentary guaranteed entry.

### Refunds, cancellation and transfer

Once a guaranteed entry is claimed, or a ballot applicant is accepted, all entry fees are **non-refundable, non-deferrable and non-transferable**. A 2026 entrant may cancel and earn a non-complimentary guaranteed place for 2027, but must claim that entry and pay the 2027 fee; a runner who deferred in 2025 cannot defer a second time for a 2027 place.

## Time-qualifier standards

The NYRR time-qualifier page sets standards for **2027** entry: the qualifying window is **1 January to 31 December 2026**, evaluated on net chip time, and age is counted as of **7 November 2027**. NYRR time qualifiers may use an NYRR in-person marathon or half marathon; non-NYRR time qualifiers accept certified full marathons only, and half-marathon times do not count.

Non-NYRR courses must be certified by USATF, AIMS, World Athletics or the national distance-running governing body of the host country. Indoor marathons, virtual marathons, triathlons, other distances, and personal Strava or GPS records are not accepted; the time must be verifiable on the race results page. Submitting a qualifying time does not guarantee entry; if applications exceed spots, entry goes to the fastest in each age and gender category, with the rest moved to the non-guaranteed general ballot.

## Course geometry: three bridges and five boroughs

The course page states plainly: 26.2 miles through all five boroughs of New York City. The start is Fort Wadsworth on Staten Island, leaving over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge; runners move north through Brooklyn along Fourth Avenue, cross the Pulaski Bridge at mile 13.1 into Queens, return to Manhattan over the Queensboro Bridge at miles 15–16, then head north to the Bronx via the Willis Avenue Bridge and back to Manhattan over the Madison Avenue Bridge, before finishing south through Fifth Avenue into Central Park.

Bridges and spectator access must be kept separate: runners cross every course bridge; spectators are **not allowed on any course bridge**, and there is no viewing at the start or on the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge.

The course page publishes no downloadable, redistributable GPX, KML or GeoJSON, and no trajectory licence statement appears; it offers a 2026 PDF map and an interactive map. The entity card therefore keeps `geometryAccess` as `unavailable`: this page does not draw a line, bundle a trajectory or hand-place waypoints, and the globe map carries only an editorial overview anchor, not a precision start or finish coordinate.

On elevation, NYRR has not published cumulative gain. An NYRR staff Q&A quotes a coach placing the highest point in the first two miles at about 260 ft above sea level, with the Verrazzano Bridge the steepest climb on course; this is a coach's Q&A figure, not an official survey, and this page does not convert it to ascent metres or draw an elevation profile. For how distance, pace and climb shape training, see the in-site [marathon distance explainer](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/) and [uphill and downhill running](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/).

### Cut-off and finish recognition

The street schedule is designed to allow runners maintaining a **16-minute-per-mile pace**, based on the Wave 5 start, to complete the course on the streets. Sweep buses follow at roughly a 16-minute-per-mile pace from the final wave start, carrying runners who drop out to the post-finish area. Runners on the sweep bus may not cross the finish line, receive no 9+1 credit and are not included in the results.

The generic NYRR time cut-off policy took effect in January 2024; its generic formula is 20 minutes per mile plus the 20 minutes the start line remains open. The New York City Marathon is an exempt race, so its specific cut-off follows the race page rather than the generic formula.

**The 22:00 ET medal line**: runners back at the finish by 22:00 receive a finisher medal, an official result on results.nyrr.org, 9+1 credit toward the 2027 race and an Abbott World Marathon Majors star; those finishing after 22:00 receive a medal but are not recorded as official finishers and get no official result. Runners who have not completed the course must not go through the finish line, or they are disqualified and barred from future TCS New York City Marathons.

Timing points sit at the start, every mile from mile 3, every 5K from 5K, the halfway mark at 13.1 miles, 25.2 miles and the finish. Runners with missing or irregular split times at official checkpoints are reviewed and may be disqualified; anyone leaving the course may only rejoin at the point where they exited. ### Start village and pre-race services

The start villages and corrals hold more than 1,600 toilets; amenities include Gatorade Endurance, Dunkin' coffee, tea, hot cocoa and bagels while supplies last. Official advice is to bring extra layers, with discarded clothing going into labelled donation bins. The start village has lactation spaces, and personal breast pumps are transported from start to finish lactation tents. ASL interpreters are present in the AWD tent and general start village from 5:00 a.m. to 11:30 a.m., and a dedicated sensory space offers a low-stimulation environment. The NYRR Pace Team reports a roughly 96% historical success rate, leading groups from 6:40 to 14:18 per mile, corresponding to 2:55 to 6:15 finishes.

### Crew, private aid and pacer rules

NYRR rules on course assistance are strict: **unofficial pacers and unregistered persons are not permitted on the course**, and receiving assistance from anyone other than official personnel may lead to disqualification. Fluid containers are limited to one litre or less, carried in a hydration belt, vest front pockets or by hand; backpacks, hydration bladders or containers on the back are not allowed.

Spectators watch only from open viewing points and **cannot enter the course bridges**; the official rules do not set up runner private aid tables or a following crew car. Runners planning family support should use the designated Manhattan viewing points on First Avenue, East Harlem and Fifth Avenue, rather than assuming a private aid station beside a bridge or water stop. Bag check is entered at Central Park West at 62nd Street; after 19:00 unclaimed bags move to the NYRR RUNCENTER, available Marathon Monday, 2 November, 09:00–20:00.

## Gear and rules

Competition runs under World Athletics rules, with the wheelchair division under World Para Athletics rules; the course is USATF and/or World Athletics certified. The clauses that bear directly on gear and conduct are:

- **Wave and corral**: move to a slower wave only, never a faster one; an earlier start may be DQ.
- **Physical assistance**: runners must move under their own power; the rules do not permit a person to push a wheelchair, stroller or other equipment; athletes with disabilities using a wheelchair or allowable equipment must push themselves through the course; official support runners may accompany but cannot push.
- **Fluid container**: one litre or less, in a belt, vest front pocket or hand; no backpack, back bladder or back container.
- **Course completion**: anyone who does not finish the full course is disqualified; leaving the course allows rejoin only at the exit point.
- **Timing integrity**: missing or irregular split times are reviewed and may be DQ.
- **Closed course**: on race day NYPD closes streets along the route; unsanctioned bicycles and unauthorised activity are not allowed on the closed course.
- **After cut-off**: fluid stations and amenities may be removed and the finish area broken down; runners may be directed to sidewalks or onto the sweep bus; medical support remains available after the cut-off.

There is no official mandatory gear list, unlike a trail race. Practical urban-marathon preparation includes broken-in race shoes, fuel and fluid rehearsed on long runs, warm layers for the cold morning and bridge decks, a rain backup, and a bib-carry setup. For shoe wear-in and fuel rehearsal, see [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/) and the [race-day checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/).

## Training transfer

New York is not a course where a flat-road PB translates directly: three bridges plus the Bronx out-and-back rollers pull the heart rate up on the opening bridge, and the later Queensboro, Fifth Avenue and park sections tax the quads and pace patience. For planning, the in-site [marathon training plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/) gives a generic 16–20 week periodisation; [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) handles first- to second-half allocation; [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/) moves the fuel strategy into long-run rehearsals; the [taper planner](/en/tools/taper-planner/) and [race predictor](/en/tools/race-predict/) check status in the final fortnight.

The training transfer for this course: include bridge or repeated gentle climbs in long runs, and practise not chasing pace on the opening bridge; run several 30 km-plus long runs at target wave pace to validate fuel and legs after miles 12–18; compare your half or full marathon time against the qualifier table to confirm the entry path; keep speed in the taper rather than piling mileage. Urban crowds compress passing room, so pulling over to drink without sudden stops is itself a race-day skill.

### Course-change log

The only change recorded in `courseChanges` is the 2026 Bronx adjustment: after the Willis Avenue Bridge the route uses East 141st Street to Third Avenue and East 138th Street onto the Madison Avenue Bridge; NYRR calls it the first change in 15 years. Earlier change history was not available in an official handbook, and this page does not claim there were no earlier changes.

## Risk and withdrawal points

The risk structure differs from a trail ultra: no altitude, no technical surface. The main risks are fatigue accumulation over an urban distance, lower-limb load on the bridge and park rollers, cold morning and wind, and the sudden stops and passes forced by wave congestion. Official medical stations sit roughly every mile from mile 3, with a finish medical tent and AEDs in start and course tents; medical support remains after cut-off.

Signals for active withdrawal include chest pain, confusion, persistent dizziness, cramps severe enough to affect stride, disorientation or sudden chills. Once on a sweep bus the runner is marked DNF and cannot rejoin. Runners finishing near a 16-minute-per-mile pace should map withdrawal points and bus routes in advance rather than pushing to be picked up before the line.
