## What this race is

The Seoul Marathon is a point-to-point road course through central Seoul. Per the 2026 official venue-layout notice, the full course starts at Gwanghwamun, the 10 km starts at Jamsil, and both the full and 10 km finishes are at Jamsil Sports Complex. The 2026 city press release describes the full route as Gwanghwamun Square → Sungnyemun Gate → Cheonggyecheon Stream → Dongdaemun Design Plaza → Seoul Children's Grand Park → Seoul Forest → Jamsil Sports Complex.

The race is organised by The Dong-A Ilbo. The 2026 closing remarks list supporters: Seoul Metropolitan Government, Seoul Metropolitan Sports Council, the Korea Association of Athletics Federations (KAAF), the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and the Korea Sports Promotion Foundation. The organisers describe the race as Korea's only World Athletics Platinum Label road race for seven consecutive years, and it holds a World Athletics Heritage Plaque. The 2026 race was the 96th edition with about 40,000 runners; 2027 is the 97th.

The race's distinctive profile is that of an early-spring East Asian city marathon: the start window is still cold, the morning has wind, and March is a high-risk window for yellow dust and fine particulate matter. Compared with the Tokyo Marathon held in the same period, the Seoul route runs past historical gates and residential districts east of the Han River; crowd density, road width and post-race support should be read from Seoul's own venue materials rather than copied from the Japanese race experience.

**Selection**: the official FAQ states places are first-come, first-served, assigned by payment order; **there is no ballot or lottery**. The Gold Package is sold out, no post-purchase upgrade is offered, and no second registration schedule will open.

## Timing and cut-off: the five-hour limit and pace margins

The 2027 official rules state the time limits as: **five hours for the full marathon; one hour 30 minutes for the 10 km**. The rules say enforcement is strict; after the limit, traffic control is lifted and the organiser no longer bears responsibility for runner safety. Timing uses a disposable chip attached to the back of the bib, with net time recorded.

The official rules **do not publish intermediate cut-off times per kilometre or per aid station**; the 2026 venue-layout and traffic notices give only the overall control window, no checkpoint cut-off table. This section therefore works from the five-hour overall limit with reproducible pace and margin calculations, and it does not dress planning paces up as official gates.

| Target finish | Average pace (min/km) | Half-marathon split | Margin to 5 h limit | Reproducible calculation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5:00:00 (limit edge) | 7:06.6 | 2:30:00 | 0 min | 300 min ÷ 42.195 km = 7.1099 min/km |
| 4:00:00 | 5:41.3 | 2:00:00 | 60 min | 240 min ÷ 42.195 km = 5.6879 min/km; half 21.0975 km × 240/42.195 = 120.0 min |
| 3:30:00 | 4:58.6 | 1:45:00 | 90 min | 210 min ÷ 42.195 km = 4.9769 min/km; half × 210/42.195 = 105.0 min |

How to read the margins: a four-hour runner holding no slower than 5:41/km stays 60 minutes inside the five-hour limit; a 3:30 runner at about 4:59/km has 90 minutes of margin. But a cold March morning and a dense start corral can pull the first 5 km too fast. Execute by the pacing method in [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/) rather than chasing the cut-off at top speed. The 10 km limit of 1 h 30 min averages 9:00/km.

## Transport, accommodation and pickup flow

**Race-week traffic control (2026 city position; 2027 times follow the pre-race notice)**: general sections' representative control window is 07:20–12:55; main sections are controlled sequentially from 05:00 to 14:30 and released flexibly once runners pass. The Gwanghwamun start and Jamsil finish are expected to be congested; both the city and organiser strongly recommend public transport. In 2026 the start was moved 30 minutes earlier than the previous year under Seoul City guidelines, and the control release was moved 30 minutes earlier with it. Enquiries: race secretariat 02-312-1998 and the Seoul 120 Dasan call centre.

**Accommodation area choice (editorial advice, not an official hotel guide)**: the course is point-to-point, so after the race you cannot return along the route to the start. If start convenience is the priority, stay in Jongno-gu, along Sejongno, near City Hall, Gwanghwamun or Gyeongbokgung metro stations, with a short walk or metro ride to the start. If post-race exit matters more, stay near Jamsil Sports Complex and Jamsil Station, where collecting bags, changing and leaving the finish is smoother. The two districts are directly connected by metro; check the route to DDP for expo pickup the day before. The 2027 official accommodation guide has not been published.

**Pickup (overseas)**: the rules require overseas runners to collect packs at the Seoul Running Expo at DDP (Dongdaemun Design Plaza) no later than the day before the race; race-day on-site pickup is restricted. The pack contains a short-sleeve T-shirt or singlet (one choice), a finisher's medal, bib number and timing chip; expo items are not restocked. The 2027 expo dates, opening hours and size chart are not yet listed on the English site; the FAQ points runners to expo signage and email notices.

**Language and on-site information**: the secretariat provides an English email, marathon@donga.com; on-site materials are Korean and English. The 2026 race paired the event with the inaugural Seoul Running Expo; the 2027 pickup flow is expected to follow the expo model, but details await the official update.

The reference suggests the assembly and early kilometres sit near freezing, climbing into single digits to around ten degrees by midday; wind amplifies on open riverside sections. Prepare for cold and wind, not a warm spring run.

**Dust and air quality**: Korean yellow dust originates mainly from the Gobi/Inner Mongolian plateau (about 50% arrives directly; routes via northeast China deserts and the Loess Plateau bring the combined Gobi/Inner Mongolia share to about 81%), and it is most frequent in spring. The Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) yellow dust alert threshold is PM10 hourly average ≥800 µg/m³ expected to persist for at least two hours. Yellow dust differs from fine particulate matter: the former is mostly 1–10 µm soil particles, the latter carbon or ion components; KMA handles dust while the Ministry of Environment handles fine dust forecasts. Check both the KMA dust outlook and the environment ministry fine-dust forecast in race week; on dust or high-pollution days, sensitive people should cut outdoor exposure, carry a mask and remove it only at the start. General judgment on air quality and running is in [air quality and running](/en/knowledge/air-quality-and-running/).

## Overseas runner practicalities: visa, entry and city flow

**Visa and entry**: the Korean MFA position is that entering foreigners hold either a K-ETA or a visa; the passport only needs to remain valid for the stay, with no minimum remainder-validity rule. Apply for K-ETA at www.k-eta.go.kr; those not eligible apply through the Korea Visa Application Centre (KVAC); some nationalities and age groups are exempt, and the K-ETA exemption for listed countries is extended until 31 December 2026. An e-Arrival card must be submitted within three days before arrival; no vaccination certificate and no COVID-19 test are currently required. The specific visa category and documents for Chinese nationals follow the consular district's embassy or visa centre; this page does not replace consular advice.

## Training conversion

The core trade-off for Seoul in March is a cold start, city roads, a point-to-point finish and possible air disturbance. The training plan should centre on marathon-specific endurance, cold adaptation and recovery rather than piling on distance blindly.

1. **Marathon distance and weekly mileage**: the entry standard is an official sub-4:59:59 full marathon; training first builds the 42.195 km endurance base, see [marathon distance and preparation limits](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/).
2. **Cold adaptation**: a near-0°C start means winter training should include cold long runs, practising layering, warm-up rhythm and post-race warmth, see [cold-weather running](/en/knowledge/cold-weather-running/).
3. **Air-quality plan**: during the dust/fine-dust season, adjust workout intensity and length by AQI, moving indoors on sensitive days, see [air quality and running](/en/knowledge/air-quality-and-running/).
4. **Pace and target time**: reverse-engineer split paces from a target finish, keep margin to the five-hour limit, estimate a finish range with the [race-predict tool](/en/tools/race-predict/), and execute with [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/).
5. **Travel recovery**: international flight jet lag, pre-race taper and warm-up order are covered in [race travel and jet lag](/en/knowledge/jet-lag-and-race-travel/).

## Race-day execution

Assembly and start: the 2027 rules page gives assembly at 06:30 (tentative), gathering at Gwanghwamun Square for the full course. In 2026 the start was moved from 08:00 to 07:30 for both full and 10 km; the 2027 start time is not yet listed on the English site, so follow the pre-race official notice and SMS. The order is elites, Hall of Fame S, then waves A–H; the 10 km starts separately at Jamsil.

Day before: finish expo pickup, check the bib and the chip on its back and apparel size; confirm the metro route and first train to Gwanghwamun. Bags cannot be shipped overseas, and overseas packs cannot be collected at the start on race day.

During the race: avoid being pulled too fast in the first 5 km crowd; sponges only start after 7.5 km; food supplies sit at 20 km and 30 km; the Children's Grand Park section around 28 km calls for pacing reserves; watch crosswind on open riverside stretches. Medical points are staffed every 2.5 km; approach the nearest marshal for dizziness, chest tightness or signs of frostbite.

After the race: collect medal and poncho in the Jamsil finish zone; medals are issued on site only. Change and collect bags, then leave by public transport. Recover according to the routine for a cold long race; clean face and airways indoors after dust or high pollution.
