## 60-Second Takeaway

- **What kind of race this is.** The Shanghai Marathon is one of China's largest and most visible city marathons. In 2026 it starts on Sunday 6 December from the Bund Bull (Jinniu Square) on Zhongshan East 1st Road and finishes at Xujiahui Sports Park (Shanghai Stadium) on Zhongshan South 2nd Road, over the standard 42.195 km. The course runs mainly along city roads and riverside promenades and is essentially flat with very little elevation change. The 2025 edition was a World Athletics Platinum Label road race and a candidate event for the Abbott World Marathon Majors.
- **Who it suits, and who it does not.** It suits runners who have already completed a road marathon, can hold a steady pace inside the six-hour medal window, and know their own fueling and pacing rhythm. It is not a sensible first marathon, a race for runners without a long-distance base, or an option for anyone with a fever or cold within two weeks of the start, cardiovascular or metabolic contraindications, or other conditions listed as unsuitable in the race rules. Mass places are allocated after pre-entry and a ballot; direct-entry places are awarded by specified half-marathon placings under the competition rules.
- **Three decisions to make first.** First, compare your most recent full or half-marathon result against the segment cut-offs on this page to work out which finish-time band you realistically fit. Second, prepare layered clothing for a cool December morning and the wind along the Bund and riverside sections; decide what to keep and what to discard only after warming up. Third, there is no public crew zone and no pacer team for mass runners: fuel comes only from the organiser stations, and bibs and timing chips may not be transferred.

## Eligibility and Entry

**Age and health thresholds.** Marathon entrants must be at least 20 years old on 6 December 2026 and in good health with a sustained long-running background. The entry notice lists conditions that make participation inadvisable: congenital and rheumatic heart disease, hypertension and cerebrovascular disease, myocarditis and other heart conditions, coronary disease or serious arrhythmia, excessive or insufficient blood-sugar in diabetes, pregnancy, fever or a cold within two weeks before race day, and other conditions unsuitable for exercise.

**Result threshold and direct entry.** Outside the direct-entry tier, mass entry has no hard prior-result threshold; eligibility rests on age, the health declaration and the ballot. Direct-entry runners qualify under the competition rules: the 2026 direct-entry routes include top-200 men and top-100 women from the 2026 Shanghai Women's Half Marathon half event, top-200 men and top-100 women from the 2026 Shanghai Half Marathon, and top-150 men and a stated number of women from the 2026 Shanghai Suzhou Creek Half Marathon; the complete placing bands follow the rules. Direct-entry runners register and pay in the same entry window through the Shanghai Marathon website or app and do not enter the mass ballot.

**Ballot timeline (2026).** Mass pre-entry runs from 15:00 on 29 April to 12:00 on 29 May and closes after that. The ballot result is published on the afternoon of 30 June; selected runners must pay by 12:00 on 20 July, or the place is forfeited and cannot be obtained through any other route. In 2025 there were 356,589 pre-entries and a 7.2% marathon selection rate; the 2026 rate was not verified on this page.

**Fees.** Marathon entry fees are CNY 200 for Chinese nationals (including Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan) and CNY 1,150 (USD 160) for foreign nationals. Once entry is confirmed and paid, the fee is non-refundable for whatever reason, and the place is personal and non-transferable.

**Identity verification and pack collection.** Race-day entry uses facial recognition: runners who enrolled and captured a face in the registration flow pass through face check; those who did not must carry the original ID document used at registration. Race packs are collected in person and cannot be delegated; the organiser does not offer mailing. Collection runs 3–5 December 2026, 09:30–19:30, at the Shanghai World Expo Exhibition and Convention Center, 850 Bocheng Road.

**Next edition.** As of 2026-11-25, the Shanghai Marathon website and event detail page only carry 2026 arrangements; the 2027 date and entry window are **not published**. Third-party date speculation is not treated as official information. Check announcements on shang-ma.com before entering. Contact channels: domestic phone 400-101-5879, email general@shmarathon.com; overseas email foreigner@shmarathon.com; service hours weekdays 09:00–17:00 excluding weekends and holidays.

## Segment Breakdown

- **First 5 km (Bund–Nanjing Road–toward Jing'an Temple):** Leaving the Bull immediately enters Nanjing East Road pedestrian street and a dense spectator crowd; the first two kilometres are easy to run too fast with the flow and the crowd. This section is mostly city landscape roads with a smooth surface.
- **5–20 km (Jing'an Temple–Huaihai Road–Xintiandi):** The route moves into the western city and commercial-street network, with more junctions and turnarounds. Drink stations begin at 5 km; banana energy supply appears from 12.5 km.
- **20–35 km (Huangpu riverside–Longteng Avenue):** This opens into the riverside public space: wide roads, sparser spectators, alternating long straights and turnaround sections. It is the main test zone for marathon endurance and fueling rhythm.
- **35–42.195 km (Longhua–Xujiahui Sports Park):** After the Longhua Temple and Longhua Martyrs' Cemetery direction the course converges toward the finish, entering the sports park and Shanghai Stadium area; the finish flow enters through Passage 4, receives the finisher tag, and exits through Passage 1.

These segments are grouped from the official course description and the road order in the 2025 police notice. Actual mileage, surface and turns follow the 2026 official map; this page does not reverse-engineer geometry from the diagrams.

## Gear and Rules

**Bib and chip.** All competitors must wear suitable running clothing and fix the large bib on the front; the organiser may cancel results for improper wear, a missing or covered bib, or folding/obscuring the bib. The timing chip is attached to the back of the bib; the chip and foam must not be folded, torn, damaged or removed, and clothing or waist packs must not cover the chip. You must pass every ground timing mat on the course; a missing timing point means no ranking.

**Suggested pack.** On race day check: bib (with chip), clothes bag, safety pins, small bib (inserted in the clothes bag), original ID document (required for runners not enrolled for face verification). Shoes and clothing should already be broken in; do not wear new shoes, new socks or untested clothing on race day. The race requires road-running-appropriate clothing and footwear and prohibits costumes inconsistent with the event; check-in referees may ask for on-site adjustment if attire does not meet the rules.

**Violations and penalties.** The organiser monitors the start, course and finish on video. Depending on severity, penalties can range from cancellation of results to a two-year or lifetime ban, with cases reported to the Chinese Athletics Association for further action. Listed violations include: false age or false identity at entry; carrying two or more chips; finishing by relay; starting outside the assigned section or out of order; a rule-breaking start; continuing to race after the cut-off despite advice, or rejoining the course after withdrawing; not following the prescribed route, cutting corners or using transport; repeating through the finish, or entering the finish privately without completing the course to claim finisher goods and medals; forging a bib, using another event's or another year's bib, or finishing without a bib; altering or covering a bib; accepting or transferring a bib and chip; disobeying staff, disturbing order, gathering to cause trouble, or fighting; and other rule breaches.

**Insurance and medical.** The organiser purchases accident insurance for runners during the race: accident death and disability cover of CNY 500,000 per person (including sudden-death cover on race day), accident medical cover CNY 50,000 per person with no deductible and 100% reimbursement, plus an acute-illness rider; the policy period is 05:00–17:00 on race day. Fixed medical points are placed at the start, finish and along the route, with ambulance cars following the run; authorised referees and staff may stop runners who are clearly no longer able to continue. Personal injury or property loss caused by the runner's own condition is borne by the runner.

**Rule applicability.** The race follows the World Athletics competition and technical rules, World Athletics Label Road Race rules, the Chinese Athletics Association marathon management measures and the competition rules; the wheelchair event follows World Para Athletics (WPA) rules. This page does not interpret official clauses for the organiser; disputes are decided under that year's rules and on-site refereeing.

## Weather and Environment

**Climate window.** The race starts on the first Sunday of December in early winter Shanghai. Open-Meteo reanalysis for 6 December across 2015–2025 gives daily maxima of 10.1–18.0°C, median 12.6°C, and daily minima of −1.3–7.2°C, median 4.6°C; two of the eleven race days recorded at least 1 mm of precipitation (6.1 mm in 2015 and 23.5 mm in 2018). These are grid-point historical reanalysis values, not instrument measurements on the course.

**Recent weather reference.** The 2025 race-day grid values were max 22.8°C, min 8.6°C, precipitation 0.0 mm; that year's guide forecast a clear day at 10–22°C with a noticeable temperature spread. The 2026 participant guide forecasts light rain at 10–15°C, subject to the latest pre-race forecast. Start-time temperatures may be in single digits, and wind on the Bund and riverside sections makes it feel colder; after sweating, long straights can chill you quickly, so keep a warm jacket or disposable rain layer for the start and decide to discard it once warmed up.

**Environmental risks.** The course is at low altitude with no trail terrain; the main environmental factors are city roads, junction crowds, riverside wind and possible light rain on the surface. A December morning brings a wide swing in humidity and sunlight; pay attention to pre-race warm-up and post-race warmth. Signals such as cardiac discomfort, persistent chest tightness, dizziness or unsteady gait should be assessed at the nearest medical point rather than pushed through. General judgments on air quality and running are covered in the site knowledge entry, and race-day air quality follows the official Shanghai forecast.

## History and Background

The Shanghai Marathon began around 1996 and is one of the older city marathons in mainland China. The verifiable edition chain this round: 2023 was the 28th edition and 2025 was the 29th. The 2023 field was 20,000 full-marathon runners and 18,000 fitness-run participants; the 2025 marathon field was 23,000, with the fitness run as a separate event unit.

**Label and candidate status.** The 2025 Shanghai Marathon was a World Athletics Platinum Label road race. In the Abbott World Marathon Majors sequence, Shanghai has been a candidate event since 2024: in November 2025 it passed the first-stage assessment for a second time, and must meet the standard for a second consecutive year in the 2026 edition to be in line for Major admission in 2027. As of this round, both the official and Major pages call it a candidate, not a Major race.

**Changes in positioning.** From 2026, the 5 km health run runs as an independent event unit on 5 December, separate from the marathon; the professional standard of the marathon and the participation width of the mass event are therefore split. The racing wheelchair discipline was added in 2024, with the route adjusted to avoid sharp curves and narrow roads and with accessible toilets provided for wheelchair passage.

## Training Transfer

1. **Prepare for a road marathon, not a trail race.** The course is city roads and riverside promenades with no technical sections and no climbing burden; training should focus on continuous road mileage, pace stability and holding form in the second half. The training meaning of the marathon distance is covered in [Marathon Distance](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/), and the system periodisation plan in [Marathon Training Plan](/en/knowledge/marathon-training-plan/).
2. **Hold pace in the first half; don't let the Bund crowd pull you along.** The start has dense spectators and the first kilometres are easy to rush; plan a negative split, conservative early and steadier later. Pacing principles are in [Race-Day Pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/); cut a target time into 5 km blocks with the [Splits tool](/en/tools/splits/), and convert pace with the [Pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/).
3. **Make fueling a long-run habit.** Drink stations only begin at 5 km and water/sponge stations at 7.5 km, so do not rely on course water in the first few kilometres. Energy gels, bananas and cakes should already be tolerated in training; avoid trying new foods on race day. Full fueling strategy is in [Race-Day Fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/) and [Long-Run Fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), and fluid rhythm in [Hydration](/en/knowledge/hydration/).
4. **Adapt to the cool and wind of a December morning.** Put long runs in cool morning or evening slots; practise layered clothing, start warmth and wind protection after sweating. Low-temperature clothing and post-race recovery are in [Cold-Weather Running](/en/knowledge/cold-weather-running/). The open riverside sections carry wind, so training should include long straights in similar wind.
5. **Back-calculate your target from the cut-off buffers.** Against the segment table in Timing and Cut-offs, check which buffer band your most recent marathon or long run falls into. If the buffer after 35 km is tight, endurance or fueling rhythm is the gap to fix first, not a faster race-day target. The load-and-recovery framework is in [Load and Overtraining](/en/knowledge/load-and-overtraining/); check items with the [Race Checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/).

**Fueling rhythm.** Take a drink station every 5 km and a water/sponge station every 7.5 km. Long-run runners plan gels around 25 km according to their own tolerance; do not depend on exclusive course foods. Slow down to take cups and squeeze the rim rather than standing for a long stop.

**Retreat points and signals.** If any timing point falls behind its gate, or you develop persistent vomiting you cannot eat through, unsteady gait, confused awareness, chest pain or marked palpitations, leave the course, take the support vehicle or public transport to the finish, and go to a medical point for assessment. Bag collection closes at 14:30; late collection follows the rules' delayed procedure.

**Gear switch.** Before the start wear a warm jacket or rain layer; store or discard it before bag drop. Carry no extra items during the race; waist packs must not cover the bib or chip. Put warmth back on promptly after finishing, then collect the bag and leave along the finish flow.

- **Adjust clothing and fueling for the weather.** If the forecast brings rain or cooler temperatures, keep fueling unchanged and layer clothing; if the day is noticeably warm, raise the frequency of water and sponge use. Heat-stress judgment is in [Heat and Humidity](/en/knowledge/heat-and-humidity/).
- **Drop the target if long runs fall short.** If your last 30 km-plus long run is clearly slower than target pace, or you walk-run after 30 km, prioritise finishing on race day rather than holding a strength target.

## Common Misreadings

- **"Shanghai is already a World Marathon Major."** No. It is an Abbott World Marathon Majors candidate event; it passed the first-stage assessment in 2025, and 2026 is the second consecutive-year assessment window. Only after passing may it become a Major in 2027. Both the Major site and official pages use the word candidate.
- **"The official map is a downloadable track."** No. The organiser publishes JPEG images; there is no GPX/KML/GeoJSON and no redistribution licence. This page links externally only: no tracing, no bundling, no elevation measured from the pictures.
- **"The 38,000 in 2024 were all full-marathon runners."** No. The 38,000 were full marathon plus mini marathon combined; the 2025 marathon field was 23,000 with the fitness run counted separately. The two edition figures cannot be mixed.
- **"The selection rate is entries divided by 30,000."** Not exactly. 2025 had 356,589 pre-entries and a 7.2% selection rate; 2026 entry and selection numbers were not published this round, and direct-entry places do not enter the mass ballot pool.
- **"The six-hour medal line and the 13:30 finish gate are the same thing."** The wordings differ: the rules award a medal for finishing within six hours; the finish gate time is 13:30, and segment gates run on the natural clock. In a six-wave start, later sections should read their available time against their own gun time.

## Transport, Expo and Accommodation

**Pack collection.** 3–5 December 2026, 09:30–19:30, Shanghai World Expo Exhibition and Convention Center, 850 Bocheng Road. Collect in person with the original registration ID and sign the participation commitment on site; mismatched identity is not issued a pack; mailing is not provided and there is no race-day collection. Expo-area metro and bus links are in Aid, Logistics, and Crew/Pacer Support.

**Accommodation suggestion.** Before the race, choose lodging around Nanjing East Road, People's Square or Jing'an Temple, close to start-direction metro Lines 2/10, so you can walk or take a short transfer to your section entrance in the morning. Do not book on straight-line distance alone; include road closures, metro first-train times and the 10–25 minute walk to check-in. The finish is at Xujiahui Sports Park, leaving to metro Lines 1/4 at Shanghai Stadium station.

**Race-day traffic control.** The 2025 control window ran from 23:00 on 29 November to 13:30 on 30 November; roads around the start (Nanjing East Road, Jiujiang Road, Zhongshan East 1st Road) closed in stages, with some same-route buses stopped, shortened or diverted. 2026 closures, times and bus changes follow that year's Shanghai police notice; closed roads must not be parked on, and vehicles and pedestrians at junctions follow police direction.

**Intercity and airports.** Metro, maglev and airport bus times from Pudong and Hongqiao are listed in the logistics chapter. Runners arriving the day before should reserve time for expo collection, walking the start approach and dinner.
