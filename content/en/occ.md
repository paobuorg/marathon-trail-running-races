## What this course is

OCC is the **50K final of the UTMB World Series**, in the official page's own words. It shares the finals week with UTMB (100M final) and CCC (100K final); TDS is a series stop and PTL is not counted in the series. The series boundary is set out on the [UTMB page](/en/routes/utmb/) and the [CCC page](/en/routes/ccc/); this page covers OCC only.

The course starts in Orsières in the Swiss canton of Valais, passes Champex-Lac and Trient, climbs Col de Balme (2,206 m), then reaches **Tête de Balme (2,321 m)**, descends on the Swiss side and re-enters France through Vallorcine, before crossing Col des Posettes and Le Tour and following the northern balcony of the Chamonix valley to the finish. The official page states that OCC **crosses the Franco-Swiss border twice**; Col de Balme and Tête de Balme are two separate places and must not be merged into one checkpoint. The official page calls OCC "THE LITTLE SWISS SISTER".

The official course description gives **Tête de Balme (2,321 m)** as the highest point and states that it is the new high point of the 2026 course. The highest timed checkpoint is Col de Balme at 2,206 m. Tête de Balme comes after the col and is not a timing point; the two are not the same place, and 2,321 m must not be written as a checkpoint altitude.

## Entry requirements and the 2026 lottery

OCC sits inside the 24–30 August 2026 HOKA UTMB Mont-Blanc week, but its start is in Switzerland and its transport is not the Chamonix start/finish pattern used by UTMB and CCC. The 2026 lottery accepted **any UTMB Index category** but required at least one Running Stone; elites could qualify directly through UTMB World Series races. Pre-registration ran from **8 to 19 January 2026** and the lottery was held on **22 January**. Bibs cannot be transferred, deferred or moved to another category.

## Official services legend

The official "Offered services" legend has nine items: hot meal, solid refreshments, liquid refreshments, Näak, medical post, shuttle bus, toilets, showers, rest area. Compared with UTMB, OCC's legend has no Assistance, which matches OCC's no-assistance rule, and no Drop bag or Power charging.

**A missing Drop bag in the legend does not mean OCC has no finish bag.** The 2026 practical page separately states that OCC runners can deposit **one drop bag**, collected only at the finish. The legend describes on-course services; the finish bag is separate logistics and cannot be erased by the legend.

Important: the per-checkpoint Services column is empty in the captured timing table. This page therefore lists the legend but **assigns no item to any checkpoint**, and does not say that a given stop has a hot meal or a drop bag. To know which stop offers what, read the official page icon by icon.

The table also gives no per-checkpoint medical list. A "Medical post" in the legend does not mean every checkpoint has one.

## Risks and escape points

- **Cut-off versus reference times**: at all five checkpoints with a cut-off, the official Slowest time is later than the cut-off. Slower runners cannot plan to Slowest; the cut-off column is the limit.
- **High point separated from timing points**: Tête de Balme at 2,321 m is the highest point but not a timing point; the timing table stops at Col de Balme 2,206 m. Weather and kit should be planned for 2,321 m, not only for the checkpoint altitude.
- **Two border crossings**: OCC starts in Switzerland, climbs Col de Balme and then Tête de Balme, descends on the Swiss side and re-enters France through Vallorcine; it is not a single border crossing into France. The official page gives no per-checkpoint escape arrangements; this page does not assume that every point has a shuttle or vehicle access.
- **High-altitude sections**: Col de Balme and Tête de Balme are both above 2,200 m, and late August can still bring cold, wind and rain. This page has no pass-level measurements and gives valley grid data only (next section).
- **Semi-autonomy**: OCC allows no personal assistance anywhere; the base mandatory kit and any activated additional items must be carried throughout, with additional items confirmed no later than 24 hours before the start. Details are in the next section.

## Mandatory kit, semi-autonomy and penalties

OCC is built on **semi-autonomy**: between aid stations, runners must carry enough food, water and safety equipment for the next leg. The organiser publishes a base mandatory list plus additional items that can be activated individually for the weather; activated items are confirmed no later than 24 hours before the start. All mandatory kit must be carried for the whole race and be available for checks; sealed bags that cannot be reopened are forbidden. If poles are used, they must be carried from the start to the finish and cannot go in a drop bag; no disposable cutlery, cups or bowls are provided at aid stations.

**Assistance and pacing**: OCC allows no personal assistance anywhere, and pacing or being accompanied outside marked spectator areas is prohibited; the bib must be worn on the chest or stomach and fully visible throughout. CCC allows one person with a bag of at most 30 litres at designated aid stations; OCC has no such window.

**Penalties**: missing a critical safety item (water reserve, hooded waterproof jacket, headlamp, survival blanket or phone) is a disqualification; other missing kit is 30 minutes; accepting assistance outside authorised zones is 30 minutes; pacing is 10 minutes per notification; starting before an assigned wave is 1 hour; deliberately littering is 30 minutes. Time penalties are served on the spot and the race clock keeps running. Rescue insurance covering France, Italy and Switzerland is mandatory.

## Check-in, drop bags and bib

- **Bib collection**: at the OCC start village or in Chamonix; the 2026 booking window ran from **1 July to 10 August**. Bring photo ID.
- **PPS health pathway**: mandatory for adults at €5; from 2026 no medical certificate is required, but the PPS must be completed before collecting the bib.
- **Insurance**: individual accident cover for search and evacuation in France, Italy and Switzerland is mandatory; helicopter evacuation is chargeable and the organiser chooses the means.
- **Drop bag**: OCC allows **one bag**, deposited at the start and collected **only at the finish**. Only the organiser's bag is carried; no poles and no valuables; collect within two hours of finishing.
- **Bib**: worn on the chest or stomach and fully visible throughout; no transfer, deferral or category change. A DNF bib is deactivated but remains the pass for official shuttles.

## Who it suits, and how to run it

OCC's entry standard is a UTMB Index category plus Running Stones, not a 50K finish certificate. The course is 61.4 km with +3,342 m, a 2,321 m high point and two border crossings; it suits runners who have already completed 30–50 km mountain races independently and can handle high-altitude weather and long descents. It is not a road 50 km with a mountain sticker.

Do not pace off the winner's 5:10:08. The first 7.3 km to Champex-Lac climb 654 m, then 11.7 km to Trient still include +805 / −841 m; the real gate is Trient to Col de Balme, 9.6 km with +1,080 m, followed by Tête de Balme at 2,321 m. Col de Balme closes at 17:45 and Argentière at 21:15, and the final 10.8 km still include +187 / −407 m. A 30–45 minute margin at each point is more useful than working back from total time.
