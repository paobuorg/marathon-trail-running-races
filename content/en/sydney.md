## What this course is

The Sydney Marathon is a point-to-point city road race: it starts on Miller Street in North Sydney, crosses the Sydney Harbour Bridge, passes through the city centre and Moore Park, loops Centennial Park, continues via Mrs Macquarie's Chair and the Royal Botanic Garden, and finishes at the Sydney Opera House Forecourt. The organiser describes the start as celebrating the heritage of the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games marathon, and lists the Harbour Bridge crossing, the Centennial Park loop, Mrs Macquarie's Chair and the Opera House finish as the key course highlights. The distance is 42.195 km and the 2026 edition is labelled a World Athletics Platinum Label Marathon.

This is not a straight line from the bridge to the Opera House. The official course map places the route across North Sydney, McMahons Point, Kirribilli, Pyrmont, Ultimo, Sydney, Darlinghurst, Woolloomooloo, Surry Hills, Paddington, Woollahra, Kensington, Kingsford and Royal Randwick. The transport authority's road-closure notice lists North Sydney, the CBD, Pyrmont, Darlinghurst, Moore Park and Kingsford as the main affected areas. Anyone reading the route should separate "start north of the bridge, finish at the Opera House" from "the course also loops south", otherwise 42.195 km looks like a short harbour crossing.

The organiser says recent course changes were designed for faster times and a better participant experience. The 2026 results support the "fast course" description: men's winner Addisu Gobena ran 2:04:42, taking 1 minute 24 seconds off Hailemaryam Kiros's 2025 race record of 2:06:06; women's winner Peres Jepchirchir ran 2:18:31, nine seconds outside Sifan Hassan's 2025 race record of 2:18:22. The wheelchair races also produced verifiable results: Daniel Romanchuk won in 1:26:50, lowering Marcel Hug's 2025 record of 1:27:15, and Manuela Schär won in 1:42:04, taking 2 minutes 48 seconds off Susannah Scaroni's 1:44:52. Irine Cheptai was second in the women's race in 2:22:11 and Shure Demise third in 2:22:33; Alphonce Simbu was fourth in the men's race in 2:04:57, and the organiser's race report says five men finished inside 2:05. The Australian champions were Andy Buchanan in 2:10:14 and Ellie Pashley in 2:28:28. Those are results published by the organiser and World Athletics, not this page's measurement of course difficulty.

Field size needs separate planning. The Transport for NSW event page says 40,000 participants will take part in the 2026 marathon (that page was offline when re-checked on 7 November 2026, so the figure is as recorded); the course page says the mass start is at 6:30am, with exact start-group timings published closer to race day. The start area is served by three railway stations, each described by the organiser as capable of moving 25,000 people per hour into the starting corrals, and runners are asked to arrive at least 60 minutes before their wave. Treating this as a quiet city race where you can leave home at the last minute underestimates the start-line pressure.

The card at the top of this page uses 313 m of gain because both the official elevation profile and the Course Information text on the course page list it. Treating 313 m as "climbing only, no descent" misreads the course, and treating −83 m as "total climb" is equally wrong. The organiser also warns that point-by-point GPX elevation is not accurate, so gain should follow the published 313/396 values rather than a self-parsed GPX total.

The organiser publishes no per-5 km gain or loss, and no high or low point elevation. The World Athletics results page lists places and finishing times only, with no elevation field. Start with the supplies. The course page lists eight drink stations at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35 and 40 km. All have water, and all eight plus the finish-line recovery area have Powerade Mountain Blast. GU Energy Gels are available only at the 20 km and 30 km stations; the organiser says they are limited and subject to availability and strongly recommends carrying your own gels if they are part of your plan. Portable toilets are located near each drink station, and a refreshment station sits on the Royal Botanic Garden walk-off after the finish. The organiser publishes no elevation or cumulative gain for any of these points, so those two columns say "not published".

The course page also lists the 2026 pacer target times: several slots from 2:30 to 7:00 plus a sweeper. Pacers run to chip time, aim to be within 30 seconds of even pace at halfway, and wear red TCS Sydney Marathon Pace Team singlets. That is useful for choosing a group, but it is not an official pace requirement; runners still follow their own plan. Cross-checking the pace list against the course map, the 5 km markers match the drink-station positions, and only 2.195 km remain from the 40 km station to the finish. The 2026 pacer list reaches 7:00, so runners near the cut-off also have an official group to follow.

Now the cut-offs. The 2026 start window is 130 minutes, from 6:30 to 8:40; nobody may cross the start line after 8:40. The course limit is seven hours at an average of 10 minutes per kilometre or better. Cut-off and redirection times are counted from 8:40, so the "11:37", "12:11" and similar times in the table are day-clock times assuming an on-time start, not times counted from the moment an individual crosses the line. The PDF also states that if the start window closes late for transport, safety or police reasons, all cut-off times are recalculated from the moment the sweep pacer actually crosses the line. Use the race-day instructions for your edition; do not carry this 2026 table into 2027 unchanged.

There are three ways a missed cut-off can be handled. A runner may retire, either into a SAG vehicle or by walking to the finish inside or outside the field of play, which produces a DSQ and a DNF. A runner may be redirected to skip part of the course and continue along the field of play to the finish, but the number is recorded and the runner is disqualified, not an official finisher. Or the runner may be picked up by the SAG vehicle that tails the event. A tail control pacer/sweep issues two warnings to runners behind the cut-off before any of those outcomes. In the table, 29.1, 33.4 and 35.1 km explicitly mention SAG pick-up; 33.4 km is the Centennial Park medical tent and 35.1 km is the Driver Ave hard cut-off. The PDF does not list medical cover at every other point, and this page does not assume it.

## Risks and escape points

- **The time risk is hard.** The start line closes at 8:40, the total limit is seven hours, and the required average is 10 minutes per kilometre or better. The 2026 finish cut-off is listed as 3:40pm. If the start is delayed, all mid-course cut-offs are recalculated. It is wrong to read "seven hours" as "seven hours from my own crossing": the official document counts from the start-line closure.
- **There are many control points, with soft and hard versions.** The PDF lists the start line plus ten mid-course cut-off or redirection points, from Dacey Ave at 17.8 km to Mrs Macquarie's Rd at 38.75 km. The 28.3 and 29.1 km points are soft-skip redirections, and 30.26 km is a major redirection point; the 33.4 km Centennial Park medical tent and 35.1 km Driver Ave are explicit SAG pick-ups; 35.1 km is also a hard cut-off. Copy this table into your race plan rather than memorising only the finish time.
- **Redirection is not continuation.** A runner who takes the skip option has the number recorded and is disqualified; they may still cross the finish line but are not an official finisher. Treating "redirected but still crossed the line" as "result retained" is wrong.
- **Medical cover and collection.** The PDF says a tail control pacer and a participant tail support vehicle control the cut-off, and that runners behind it are warned twice before being managed; there is a medical tent at 33.4 km in Centennial Park. Beyond that, the official document does not publish a point-by-point medical list, and this page does not assume a doctor at every drink station.
- **Roads must reopen on time.** The PDF requires the event to reopen state and local roads and the Sydney Harbour Bridge by the advertised time. That explains the dense cut-off points and means the race will not extend road closures indefinitely for individual runners.
- **Weather and exposure.** The course includes the Harbour Bridge deck, the Centennial Park loop and open sections around the Royal Botanic Garden; the official map and highlights confirm those landforms, but no bridge wind speed or park felt temperature is published. Late August in Sydney can still be a cold morning with a sunny midday, so prepare for the current forecast. The official pages captured publish no automatic weather-cancellation rule, and this page does not promise one on the organiser's behalf.
- **Crowding and the start.** There are 40,000 participants, 6:15am and 6:30am starts, and start-group timings still to be published. The three stations serving the start are described as moving 25,000 people per hour each, and runners are asked to arrive 60 minutes before their wave; arriving late runs straight into the 8:40 start-line closure.
- **Do not plan a car-based escape.** The organiser states that race-day roads are extensively closed and that there is zero parking at the start and finish, making public transport the only way to travel. If you are injured mid-course, the official mechanisms are walking to the nearest cut-off or pick-up point or waiting for the SAG vehicle, not asking a friend to drive onto the course.

## Getting there

**Airport.** Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport has train stations at the domestic and international terminals, but Transport for NSW states that an additional station access fee applies at the airport stations and is not covered by the marathon entry or the event travel entitlement. In practice, the bib covers trains, metro, buses, light rail and ferries, but not that airport surcharge. The reliable pattern is to reach Central or the city first, then change to T1/T9 for North Sydney or to the Metro for Victoria Cross/Crows Nest. Flight and baggage arrangements are outside the organiser's pages and are not expanded here.

**Rail.** The closest station to the start is North Sydney on the T1 North Shore Line and T9 Northern Line. The organiser splits the flow by start group: Purple and Orange use North Sydney station or Victoria Cross metro, Green uses Crows Nest metro. Before 6am, leave North Sydney station via Blue St, turn right, cross Pacific Hwy to Walker St and continue to the start area; after 6am, use the Greenwood Plaza Pacific Hwy pedestrian bridge to Denison St, then Berry St and Walker St. The event page lists additional trains from 4am to North Sydney, including Berowra at 2:59am, Penrith at 3:30am, Hornsby at 3:10am and 3:40am, Ashfield at 3:58am, Liverpool at 3:45am, Bankstown at 3:25am and 3:50am, Cronulla at 2:47am and 3:17am, and Revesby at 3:10am. Those are race-day extras, not the normal timetable.

**Metro.** The M1 line runs frequently between Tallawong, Chatswood and Sydenham. The nearest stations to the start are Victoria Cross and Crows Nest: Purple/Orange use Victoria Cross, exiting at Denison St, turning right onto Berry St and left onto Walker St; Green uses Crows Nest, exiting at Clarke St and continuing via Willoughby Rd, Burlington St, West St and Falcon St to North Sydney Oval and St Leonards Park. The event page says extra metro services start around 3:30am from Tallawong and 4am from Sydenham, with trains every four minutes until 8am and every five minutes until 3pm. At the finish, Circular Quay is the closest station, with Wynyard and Martin Place within short walking distance; Martin Place is also a metro station.

**Bus and light rail.** Buses are the most disrupted mode. Services that normally cross the Harbour Bridge terminate at Crows Nest, St Leonards or Macquarie Park; city-east buses start and end around Kings Cross or Woolloomooloo. Only limited buses stop near St Leonards Park on Falcon St. Light rail runs early services from 3:45am on all lines, including L1 Dulwich Hill, L2 Randwick and L3 Kingsford; however, L3 services from Kingsford stop after 5:47am, so later travellers must use L2 from Randwick.

**Driving and drop-off.** The official position is explicit: extensive race-day closures and zero parking at the start and finish make public transport the only way to travel. For local arrivals, there is one dedicated drop-off at ANZAC Public School on Anzac Ave off Ernest St; accessible drop-off is in the North Sydney Boys High School car park on Falcon St. Do not read "drop-off" as "parking": the car drops you and leaves, and there is no participant parking around the course. The critical course lock-down is 5:30–10:30am; the Harbour Bridge, Cahill Expressway and Western Distributor close from 3am to 11am, Macquarie St and Oxford St from 4am to 4pm/4.30pm, and Anzac Pde from 5am to 2pm. The Cross City Tunnel is toll-free from 5am to 4.30pm and is one of the few motor routes across the course.

**Ticketing and expo.** Participants and volunteers can travel on Sydney Trains, NSW TrainLink Intercity services, Sydney Metro, regular metropolitan and outer-metropolitan buses, light rail and ferries on Saturday 29 and Sunday 30 August by showing a race bib, finishers medal or volunteer T-shirt; the entitlement is valid until 4am the next day. The airport station access fee is not included. The Running Expo runs from Thursday 27 to Saturday 29 August at Sydney Olympic Park, and volunteers can also travel there for free under the same arrangement. Keep the bib in your carry-on, not checked luggage: on race day it is your ticket.

## Difficulty

The organiser publishes no single "difficulty grade", and this page does not invent an official one. On verifiable dimensions this is a low-to-moderate-climb road marathon with a tight cut-off:

- Distance 42.195 km; the only official distance figure.
- Gain +313 m, loss −396 m, net −83 m (official course-page basis); that is about 7.4 m of gain per kilometre and about 2 m of net descent per kilometre. Both conversions are this page's arithmetic on official totals, not new official figures.
- Total limit seven hours at 10 minutes per kilometre; the start line closes at 8:40 and the start window is 130 minutes.
- Eight drink stations at 5 km spacing; GU gels only at 20 km and 30 km, and limited.
- The start line plus ten mid-course cut-off or redirection points; 35.1 km is a hard cut-off and SAG point.
- The course includes the Harbour Bridge, the Centennial Park loop and the Royal Botanic Garden; the organiser says recent changes were designed for faster times, and the 2026 men's winner set a course record of 2:04:42.

From those conditions, the technical difficulty is lower than a trail race and the climbing load is far below a mountain route, so it suits performance chasing. But "city course" does not mean "easy": a seven-hour hard limit, a 10 min/km minimum average, dense redirection points, a 40,000-person start and a cold late-August morning are all real risks. That conclusion comes from official figures and rules, not personal experience, and it is not an official rating.
