## What this course is

The Sydney Marathon is a point-to-point city road race: it starts on Miller Street in North Sydney, crosses the Sydney Harbour Bridge, passes through the city centre and Moore Park, loops Centennial Park, continues via Mrs Macquarie's Chair and the Royal Botanic Garden, and finishes at the Sydney Opera House. The organiser describes the start as celebrating the heritage of the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games marathon, and lists the Harbour Bridge crossing, the Centennial Park loop, Mrs Macquarie's Chair and the Opera House finish as the key course highlights. The distance is 42.195 km and the 2026 edition is labelled a World Athletics Platinum Label Marathon.

This is not a straight line from the bridge to the Opera House. The official course map places the route across North Sydney, McMahons Point, Kirribilli, Pyrmont, Ultimo, Sydney, Darlinghurst, Woolloomooloo, Surry Hills, Paddington, Woollahra, Kensington, Kingsford and Royal Randwick. The organiser's three area closure pages list North Sydney, the CBD, Pyrmont, Darlinghurst, Moore Park and Kingsford among the main affected areas. Readers should separate "start north of the bridge, finish at the Opera House" from "the course also loops south"; otherwise 42.195 km looks like a short harbour crossing.

The 2026 results: men's winner Addisu Gobena ran 2:04:42, taking 1 minute 24 seconds off Hailemaryam Kiros's 2025 race record of 2:06:06; women's winner Peres Jepchirchir ran 2:18:31, nine seconds outside Sifan Hassan's 2025 race record of 2:18:22. The wheelchair races also produced verifiable results: Daniel Romanchuk won in 1:26:50, lowering Marcel Hug's 2025 record of 1:27:15, and Manuela Schär won in 1:42:04, taking 2 minutes 48 seconds off Susannah Scaroni's 1:44:52. Irine Cheptai was second in the women's race in 2:22:11 and Shure Demise third in 2:22:33; Alphonce Simbu was fourth in the men's race in 2:04:57, and the organiser's race report says five men finished inside 2:05. The Australian champions were Andy Buchanan in 2:10:14 and Ellie Pashley in 2:28:28. Those are results published by the organiser and World Athletics, not this page's measurement of course difficulty.

Field size needs separate planning. The Transport for NSW 2026 event page recorded 40,000 participants for the 2026 marathon; that page has now been re-verified through a tool fetch, but it remains a 2026 edition page, so the figure is kept as a 2026 record. The course page says the mass start is at 6:30am. The start area is served by three railway stations, each described by the organiser as capable of moving 25,000 people per hour into the starting corrals, and runners are asked to arrive at least 60 minutes before their wave.

There are three ways a missed cut-off can be handled. A runner may retire, either into a SAG vehicle or by walking to the finish inside or outside the field of play. In the table, 29.1, 33.4 and 35.1 km explicitly mention SAG pick-up; 33.4 km is the Centennial Park medical tent and 35.1 km is the Driver Ave hard cut-off.

## Risks and escape points

- **The time risk is hard.** The 2026 course page gives a 6:30–8:15 start window and a 3:15pm cut-off; the 2026 cut-off PDF gives 6:30–8:40 and 3:40pm. The total limit is seven hours at 10 minutes per kilometre or better, and if the start closes late, all mid-course cut-offs are recalculated. It is wrong to read "seven hours" as "seven hours from my own crossing": the PDF counts from the start-line closure. Use the race-day handbook for your edition.
- **There are many control points, with soft and hard versions.** The PDF lists the start line plus ten mid-course cut-off or redirection points, from Dacey Ave at 17.8 km to Mrs Macquarie's Rd at 38.75 km. The 28.3 and 29.1 km points are soft-skip redirections, and 30.26 km is a major redirection point; the 33.4 km Centennial Park medical tent and 35.1 km Driver Ave are explicit SAG pick-ups; 35.1 km is also a hard cut-off.
- **Redirection is not continuation.** A runner who takes the skip option has the number recorded and is disqualified; they may still cross the finish line but are not an official finisher.
- **Medical cover and collection.** The PDF says a tail control pacer and a participant tail support vehicle control the cut-off, and that runners behind it are warned twice before being managed; there is a medical tent at 33.4 km in Centennial Park. Beyond that, the official documents do not publish a point-by-point medical list, and this page does not assume a doctor at every drink station.
- **Roads must reopen on time.** The PDF requires the event to reopen state and local roads and the Sydney Harbour Bridge by the advertised time. This is all the verifiable official content on this point.
- **Weather and exposure.** The course includes the Harbour Bridge deck, the Centennial Park loop and open sections around the Royal Botanic Garden, and the official map and highlights confirm those landforms. The Open-Meteo grid-point record cited on this page for 23–31 August 2026 gives daily minima of 8.7–14.1 °C and a race-day maximum of 19.1 °C, so prepare for the current forecast.
- **Crowding and the start.** The 2026 event page recorded 40,000 participants and has been re-verified through the tool fetch this round, with 6:15am and 6:30am starts. The three stations serving the start are described as moving 25,000 people per hour each, and runners are asked to arrive 60 minutes before their wave; arriving late runs straight into the start-window closure.
- **Do not plan a car-based escape.** The organiser states that race-day roads are extensively closed and that there is zero parking at the start and finish, making public transport the only way to travel. If you are injured mid-course, the official mechanisms are walking to the nearest cut-off or pick-up point or waiting for the SAG vehicle; with zero parking and extensive closures, a friend cannot drive onto the course to collect you.

**Bus and light rail.** The organiser's transport page states that buses are heavily affected by the closures, that the nearest stop to the start is at Crows Nest, and that only limited buses stop near St Leonards Park on Falcon St. Transport for NSW's 2026 event page added that buses which usually cross the Sydney Harbour Bridge end their trips in Crows Nest, St Leonards or Macquarie Park (or in the city during the 3–11am bridge closure), and that city-east buses start and end around Kings Cross or Woolloomooloo until mid-afternoon because Park St is closed; those rerouting details have now been re-verified through the tool fetch. Light rail runs early services from 3:45am on all lines, including L1 Dulwich Hill, L2 Randwick and L3 Kingsford; L3 services from Kingsford stop after 5:47am.

**Driving and drop-off.** The official position is explicit: extensive race-day closures and zero parking at the start and finish make public transport the only way to travel. For local arrivals, there is one dedicated drop-off at ANZAC Public School on Anzac Ave off Ernest St; accessible drop-off is in the North Sydney Boys High School car park on Falcon St. Do not read "drop-off" as "parking": the car drops you and leaves, and there is no participant parking around the course. The critical course lock-down is 5:30–10:30am; the Harbour Bridge, Cahill Expressway and Western Distributor close from 3am to 11am, Macquarie St from 4am to 4.30pm, Oxford St from 4am to 4pm, and Anzac Pde from 5am to 2pm, with the Driver Ave area of Moore Park closed until 3.30pm. The Cross City Tunnel is toll-free from 5am to 4.30pm; managed exit from the CBD, The Rocks and Circular Quay runs until 5:30am, with no entry from 4am.

**Ticketing and expo.** The organiser's transport page states that participants can travel on Sydney Trains, Metro, buses and ferries on race day by showing an official bib. Transport for NSW's 2026 event page added that participants and volunteers can travel on Sydney Trains, NSW TrainLink Intercity services, Sydney Metro, regular buses, light rail and ferries on Saturday 29 and Sunday 30 August by showing a race bib, finishers medal or volunteer T-shirt, with the entitlement valid until 4am the next day, and that the Running Expo runs from Thursday 27 to Saturday 29 August at Sydney Olympic Park.
