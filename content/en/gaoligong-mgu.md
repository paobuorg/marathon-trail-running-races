## What this race is

The Gaoligong Ultra is held in Tengchong and runs into the southern Gaoligong mountains. The 2026 edition is title-sponsored by ANTA GUANJUN, has a total field of 3,800 and offers six races. MGU is the flagship: 168 km with 8,540 m of ascent and 8,550 m of descent.

## The course

The regulations give MGU as a sequence of place names: Yanhu Park (start) → Dongshang Caodian → Ailingzi → Tianjiashan → Longwen Bridge → Tangliba → Sandieshui → Dahaoping → Dasheyao → Xiaodifang Sanjiebei → Gangou forest station → Gongguan → Qiaojie village → Machang village → Sunao River reservoir → Shaba forest farm → Datie'ao reservoir → Feihu Park → Shuanghong Bridge, Heshun (finish).

Only some checkpoint numbers can be confirmed from the text: CP4 Longwen Bridge, CP7 Dahaoping, CP9 Sanjiebei, CP12 Qiaojie village and CP15 Shaba forest farm. A CP15 implies at least fifteen checkpoints on MGU, but the regulations do not publish the full numbering, the kilometres or what each point offers, so this page does not complete the list.

The character of the course is long, high in cumulative climb, and inside a protected area. ## Aid and cut-offs

- **What stations provide:** the regulations say stations provide water and food and that runners must be self-sufficient between them. There is no per-station food list or water capacity. Stations provide no disposable cups or utensils; runners must carry their own.
- **Per-checkpoint cut-offs:** not published; the regulations say they will appear in the pre-race handbook and on the official website. Runners must check in at each cut-off point and leave before it closes. A runner still there is withdrawn, must wait in the holding area for collection and transport, and may not re-enter the course.
- **Support:** MGU allows outside support only at CP4 Longwen Bridge, CP7 Dahaoping, CP9 Sanjiebei, CP12 Qiaojie village and CP15 Shaba forest farm. Supporters must submit ID, an authorisation letter and a commitment letter at packet collection to obtain a support credential and wristband. RCE support is limited to CP4, CP7 and CP11.
- **CP7 category switch:** at CP7 Dahaoping an MGU runner may assess their condition and switch to RCE. The switch quota is 20% of the MGU field. A switched runner is not ranked or awarded in either race but receives an RCE-distance result, RCE finisher items and the corresponding ITRA points; the fee is neither refunded nor adjusted.
- **Sweeping:** withdrawn or cut runners wait in a holding area for collection and transport. The regulations give no vehicle schedule or maximum waiting time.

## Check-in, packet pickup and transport

- **Race-day check-in:** MGU check-in opens at 18:30 on 20 March at Yanhu Park for the 20:00 start. Runners must bring the full race kit for inspection or they cannot collect race materials, and the pack must be the one used in the race.
- **Packet pickup:** the regulations give no pre-race pickup date, time or venue. This page does not invent one.
- **Transport:** the regulations publish no arrival, start-line shuttle or return timetable. The wristband is described as the credential for event services including shuttle buses, which shows a shuttle exists, but no schedule was obtained.
- **Air access (post-race update):** a 21 May 2026 Ruili government notice announces a custom coach from Mangshi Airport to Tengchong Tourist Bus Station: CNY 57 one way, about 1.5 hours on the expressway, fixed departures at 09:20, 10:20, 11:20 and 13:40, extra services as needed, tickets through the Dehong Transport Group's official WeChat account. The service opened after the 2026 race, so it is not presented as the 2026 race shuttle; anyone using it for a future edition should first confirm the race date and the last departure.
- **Accommodation:** no official hotels or rates are listed in the regulations.

## Weather and season

Open-Meteo's Tengchong record for 20–22 March, 2015–2025 (33 days): daily maximum 16.2–26.9 °C, median 21.6 °C; daily minimum 4.1–11.1 °C, median 7.9 °C; 10 of 33 days had at least 1 mm precipitation, 4 had at least 5 mm.

This is the Tengchong town grid, not the Gaoligong ridge or high checkpoints. Late March can be 27 °C by day and 4 °C at night in town, with colder, wetter and foggier mountains; the MGU 168 night and high sections need a warm layer, headlamp and waterproof shell. The organiser publishes no race-period mountain measurements, and this page does not treat town data as ridge data.

## Difficulty (editorial judgement)

This is not an official grading. It is this page's reading of the official distances, gains, limits and course description.

- **47 hours, two nights, 8,540 m of climb.** MGU is the only race here that spans two nights. The limit works out at about 16:51/km arithmetic, but with per-checkpoint cut-offs unpublished that number is not a pace plan; the binding constraints are unseen.
- **Altitude and weather together.** Tengchong town already sits close to 1,600 m and the course climbs into the southern Gaoligong. The organiser writes thunderstorms, thick fog, rain and snow into the runner commitment, which tells you weather is the main variable.
- **CP7 is a psychological gate.** The organiser puts a 120 km exit in the middle of a 168 km race and caps it at 20% of the field. That is a safety design, and it also means making a decision while fatigued.
- **Station spacing is unknown.** Runners must be self-sufficient between stations, but the organiser does not publish the gaps. This page does not guess how much water to carry; the hard minimums are 1 L of capacity and 800 kcal of emergency food.
- **A protected-area course.** The Gaoligongshan National Nature Reserve management bureau is one of the organisers, and the route enters the reserve. Running surface and access constraints differ from a city-edge race, and the regulations give no surface breakdown.

## Training conversion: 168 km cross-night mountain preparation

The challenge of MGU is 168 km, 8,540 m of climb, a 47-hour limit spanning two nights, inside the southern Gaoligong protected area; night, low temperature, strong sun, wind, thunderstorms, thick fog, rain and snow must be handled alone. Preparation centres on ultra-long mountain endurance, cross-night rhythm and altitude adaptation, not raw road mileage. General methods are in [ultra-trail training plan](/en/knowledge/trail-ultra-training-plan/), [long-run fueling](/en/knowledge/long-run-fueling/), [recovery day](/en/knowledge/recovery-day/) and [load and overtraining](/en/knowledge/load-and-overtraining/).

| Phase | Focus | Practical content | Race-day constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-20 to T-14 weeks | Mountain aerobic base | One mountain long run plus easy runs a week; build weekly gain toward 2,000 m | 8,540 m of repeated up-and-down needs a steady aerobic base |
| T-14 to T-8 weeks | Long run and gain accumulation | Long run to 80–100 km; practise ridge and stair sections | Protected-area terrain is rolling; technical sections need early adaptation |
| T-8 to T-3 weeks | Loaded self-sufficiency and cross-night | Test ≥1 L water capacity and ≥800 kcal emergency food; one cross-night session | 47 hours includes two nights; headlamp and warm layers must be tested |
| T-3 to race | Taper | Shorten distance and gain, keep short rhythm; no new shoes or kit | Recover fatigue before race day; see [taper and strength](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/) |

Boundary: this is a generic preparation framework, not medical advice. Runners with pre-existing cardiopulmonary conditions, recent injuries or a history of altitude intolerance should consult a doctor first. MGU entry itself requires 4 ITRA points or a 100 km-plus trail finish; keep qualification thresholds separate from preparation targets.
