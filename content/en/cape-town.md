## What this course is

The Cape Town Marathon is a 42.2 km road marathon that starts and finishes in the Green Point precinct around the DHL Stadium, with the finish specifically on Vlei Road. It is not a point-to-point course and it is not a trail race: the route moves through city streets, a highway off-ramp, the southern suburbs, Rondebosch Common, the city centre, the Company Gardens and the Sea Point promenade, all at or near sea level.

The 2026 edition used two start areas. The Stadium start is on Fritz Sonnenberg Road next to the DHL Stadium; the Beach Road start is on Beach Road in Mouille Point. All events finish on Vlei Road. The split is not decorative. The organiser explained in 2025 that two starts and five alternating waves were introduced to reduce congestion in the first 10 km and to relieve pressure on the finish line at peak times; Stanhope Road and the Company Gardens were named as pinch points. The 2026 race kept that structure and published the five wave times in its pre-race bulletin.

The scale of the event shapes the experience. The 2026 edition had 27,000 marathon runners, about 32% of them running the race for the first time. There were 8,500 international entrants from 145 countries, led by the United States (1,571), the United Kingdom (712) and Germany (358); among African countries the largest groups were Zimbabwe (263), Botswana (177) and Kenya (133). A further 17,500 people took part in the 10 km and 5 km Peace Runs and the 43 km, 22 km and 11 km trail races, for a weekend total of 44,500 athletes. Another 1,800 runners contested the AbbottWMM Marathon Tours & Travel Age Group World Championships. These are the organiser's pre-race figures, not a final finisher count.

Two facts capture the course's character: it is fast, but it is not flat. In 2026, Mohamed Esa won the men's race in 2:04:55, taking more than three minutes off the previous course record of 2:08:16; Dera Dida won the women's race in 2:23:18. The first ten men all finished inside the old course record, and the organiser described the result as the fastest marathon ever run on African soil. At the same time, the official elevation profile gives 215 m of total ascent and 209 m of total descent, with a high point of 41 m and a low point of 8 m. This is not a Berlin-style near-zero course: the Company Gardens section contains the longest and steepest climb of the day.

Wind is the other variable that is easy to miss. The organiser's post-race report quotes Dida saying that wind and the curves in the route kept her from attacking the 2:22:22 course record; wheelchair racer David Weir mentioned early-morning humidity that made his breathing difficult for the first couple of miles. Those are post-race comments from athletes, not an official race-day forecast. They matter because they identify conditions that a simple elevation profile cannot show.

The two-start structure also means that a single GPS track cannot represent every runner. The Stadium and Beach Road starts converge near the corner of Fritz Sonnenberg Road and Beach Road. The organiser times the waves so that the last runner of one wave is past the convergence point before the next wave is released. A runner must confirm their batch, wave and start area before reading the route; copying someone else's track or an old-year route can go wrong at the start line itself.

## Official figures

The event FAQ gives the marathon distance as 42.2 km and the cut-off as 6.5 hours, but publishes no cumulative ascent or descent. The ascent and descent come from the 2026 official route profile: it states Total Ascent 215 m and Total Descent 209 m, and marks the start at 11 m, the highest point at 41 m and the lowest point at 8 m. The same profile gives 21 km of uphill running, 20 km of downhill running and 1 km of flat running; those three add up to 42 km, a 0.2 km rounding difference from the public 42.2 km figure, and this page keeps the official labels rather than redistributing the gap. What the table does and does not settle:

- **42.2 km is the organiser's public figure.** The FAQ and the route profile both use 42.2, not a separately surveyed number. This page does not rewrite it into the 42.195 figure used by other athletics contexts, and does not manufacture a third number by splitting the difference.
- **215 m and 209 m come from the same official profile.** The 6 m difference between ascent and descent is consistent with a start and finish in the same Green Point precinct but not at the same point. The organiser does not explain where the 6 m sits, and this page does not invent an explanation.
- **The 41 m high point is at Rondebosch Common.** The profile labels Rondebosch Common at the top of the curve. That is an official label, not this page's terrain reconstruction.
- **The World Athletics page gives no gain or loss.** It confirms the date and results, but it is not a course-profile source. Race results cannot be used to infer elevation.
- The card at the top of this page uses **42.2 km and 215 m of ascent**, because 42.2 is the organiser's most public distance and 215 m is the only total-ascent figure the official profile provides. The tables and segment notes below use the same basis.

The purpose of the table is not to let a reader pick the "most accurate" number. It is to show which official document each number belongs to. Entry pages, route maps, profiles and results pages each answer a different part of the question; stitching them into one tidy number would hide the boundaries of the official record.

## Aid stations and cut-offs

The official FAQ states that the 42.2 km route has **15 hydration stations**, approximately every 3 km. **13 of them serve water, Coke, Coke No Sugar and Powerade**; the other **2 serve water only**. Water is served in cups, not plastic sachets. The organiser does not publish a per-station kilometre table or say which two stations are water-only, so this page does not force 15 stations into a precise kilometre grid.

The rest of the official aid and medical picture:

- The 2024 official final instructions place a Woolworths food station before 24 km, after Hydration Station 8 and before Station 9. It is solid food, not a water station.
- The medical partners are Mediclinic and ER24. The 2024 instructions list three medical and physio points, three first-aid stations and a fully staffed hospital at the finish. The 2026 pre-race bulletin adds that five emergency misting zones are set up if race-day temperatures are high.
- Toilets are at the start, at every hydration station and at the finish.
- Hydration packs are permitted; headphones of any description are not.
- The 2026 FAQ says the 42.2 km cut-off is 6.5 hours and directs runners to the route map for all cut-off times. The organiser's pre-race bulletin separately gives the final cut-off as 15:50. The two use different reference points: the 6.5-hour race clock starts when the final runner crosses the start mat, while 15:50 is the actual finish cut-off. Do not add 6.5 hours to your own wave time.

## Surface and terrain

The organiser publishes no redistributable surface breakdown, and this page invents no percentages. What can be confirmed is the official definition of the course and the official profile:

- It is a 42.2 km road marathon starting and finishing around the DHL Stadium in Green Point, passing through city streets, the N2 off-ramp, the southern suburbs, Rondebosch Common, the city centre, the Company Gardens, Long Street, the Sea Point promenade and Green Point.
- The official profile gives a start at 11 m, a high point of 41 m and a low point of 8 m, with 215 m total ascent and 209 m total descent.
- The profile divides the course into four terrain zones: gentle opening rollers at 3–10 km; a long steady middle at 13–30 km; the primary climb at 31–32 km; and a gradual 10 km descent to the finish.
- It also gives 21 km of uphill running, 20 km of downhill running and 1 km of flat running. "Uphill running" is not "21 km of climb"; it describes the mileage with an upward grade in the running direction. Do not read 21 km as cumulative ascent.

The terrain landmarks:

- **Rondebosch Common** is the official high point at 41 m. The official spectator guide places it at 20–22 km, and the profile labels it at the top of the curve.
- **The Company Gardens** contain the longest and steepest climb. The official spectator guide places it at 30.7–32.1 km; the profile marks the primary climb at 31–32 km. It is not a mountain, but its position after 30 km matters more than its 41 m high point.
- **The Sea Point promenade** is an out-and-back. The official spectator guide says runners go about 3 km along the coast before turning back, so 36.3–41.4 km is covered twice; the stretch is exposed to coastal wind and sea mist.
- **Long Street** turns downhill after the Gardens climb; the official spectator guide places it at 32.1–33.3 km, and the profile calls the last 10 km a gradual descent. It is one of the few places where the course lets runners use gravity.
- **The N2 off-ramp** at 6.5 km is a highway viewpoint, not a trail section. The organiser lists it as a spectator spot, not as a difficulty.

The elevation range is only 8–41 m, so altitude is not a factor. The real environmental variables are coastal wind, humidity and sun, not thin air. The organiser publishes no surface ratio and no per-segment gradient percentages; any "X% asphalt" claim without an official source is left out. Shoes and kit should follow road-marathon practice, not trail-race practice.

## Risks and escape points

- **Wind**: the Cape Town sea wind is the variable runners mention most often. The organiser's 2026 post-race report quotes women's winner Dida saying that wind and the curves in the route kept her from attacking the 2:22:22 course record. That is an athlete's post-race comment, not an official forecast. The Sea Point out-and-back and the Green Point stretch are the most exposed; pace plans should carry a headwind margin.
- **Humidity and heat**: the 2026 pre-race bulletin says five emergency misting zones are set up if race-day temperatures are high. In the post-race report, wheelchair racer Weir mentions early-morning humidity. The race starts in waves from 07:50 and closes at 15:50, so later runners meet the warmer part of the day. The organiser does not guarantee a cool race day.
- **The Company Gardens climb**: 30.7–32.1 km is the longest and steepest climb, and it arrives after 30 km. It is not an alpine pass, but for legs that have already run 30 km, its position is harder than the 41 m number suggests.
- **The Sea Point out-and-back**: 36.3–41.4 km goes out and back along the same stretch. That range is the official spectator guide's, not this page's exaggeration. The mental and wind-resistance cost of covering the same ground twice belongs in the race plan.
- **Cut-offs and sweeps**: the 2026 FAQ gives a 6.5-hour cut-off, the pre-race bulletin gives 15:50 as the final cut-off, and the route map prints 10 cut-off points. The 2024 instructions describe mini sweep buses and mega sweep buses at cut-off points. What happens after a sweep, and how a runner gets back to the finish, follows the current rules; do not assume every hydration station has a vehicle.
- **Medical**: the 2024 guide lists three medical and physio points, three first-aid stations and a hospital at the finish; the 2026 pages do not repeat the kilometre positions. The 2024 guide gives the medical emergency number as 021 023 2720 and the general emergency number as 021 300 1286. Those are 2024 figures; check the current guide before relying on them.
- **Traffic and closures**: the 2024 official final instructions state there is no parking at the race village or on the route, and illegally parked cars will be towed; the 2026 transport page lists Green Point's P9, P12 and P13 as unavailable on Sunday. Beach Road and Portswood Road close to crossing at 07:30 because the wheelchair race starts at 07:50. Runners staying in Green Point, the V&A Waterfront or Mouille Point should use the official pedestrian routes; marshals are in place from 05:00.
- **Rules**: hydration packs are allowed, headphones of any kind are not; bibs are non-transferable and bib-swapping leads to disqualification; the marathon age limit is 18. The English official pages are the governing text.
- **Escape points**: the official system is the 10 cut-off points plus the finish, supported by sweep buses. Medical points are for emergency care, but the organiser does not publish a routine "medical point to transport off course" arrangement. If you withdraw between cut-off points, go to the nearest hydration or medical point and let the marshals or medical team handle it under the current procedure; do not cross closed roads on your own.

## Getting there

The official transport and Park & Ride pages list many options, but they share one premise: there is little or no access to parking at the race village or near the start/finish, and driving into Green Point can easily leave you with nowhere to park and no time to reach the start. The organiser's own wording is that closures leave little or no access to parking near the start/finish, and that congestion in Green Point means a driver may miss the start.

- **Airport**: the official travel page confirms Cape Town International Airport as the primary airport serving Cape Town and links to the airport's official site. It gives no airport-to-city or airport-to-Green Point travel time, frequency or fixed transfer. For airport movements, use the airport's current information plus MyCiTi/PRASA; this page invents no duration.
- **Rail**: the official transport page suggests setting Cape Town Station as the destination in Google Maps and using train mode to find the nearest station and schedule. From Cape Town Station it is a 10-minute walk to Thibault MyCiTi Station, then a free shuttle to the Stadium. The 2026 spectator guide adds that Western Cape Train Service runs a special event train on 24 May, with return trips costing R20; spectators can use the Southern Line to reach points along the route, and runners can use the free shuttle between Thibault and Stadium Stop. Tickets can be bought at the Expo or at stations on the day. The organiser also links a PRASA timetable PDF.
- **Free shuttles**: the official transport page lists V&A Waterfront, CTICC and Civic Centre & Thibault as the main shuttle points. Sunday start service runs 05:30–06:45; return service runs 11:00–17:30. Note that there is no V&A Waterfront shuttle to the marathon start on Sunday morning.
- **Park & Ride (R50)**: the official Park & Ride page lists UCT, Theo Marais Park, CTICC, Civic Centre and Century City, each with secure parking and toilets. First buses leave at 04:00; last buses leave at 04:30 from UCT, Theo Marais and Century City, and at 05:00 from CTICC and Civic Centre. Return buses run 10:00–14:30.
- **Canal Walk and UCT paid shuttles (R10)**: Sunday 24 May only. First bus 05:30, last bus 06:30; return 11:00–17:30. Tickets must be shown before boarding.
- **V&A Waterfront parking**: more than 7,500 open-air and underground bays, with four shuttle stops: Waterfront, Aquarium, Marina and Silo. There is no Sunday-morning shuttle to the start, but the return shuttle runs 11:00–17:30.
- **CTICC and Civic Centre**: CTICC P1 is accessed from Buitengracht Street, Lower Long Street or FW de Klerk Boulevard; Civic Centre parking is free on Sunday, with entrances on Hertzog Boulevard and Civic Avenue. Both are on the official free-shuttle list.
- **Green Point parking**: P9 is available only on Thursday and Friday for the Expo; P12 and P13 are available Thursday to Saturday but not Sunday.
- **E-hailing**: the official travel page lists ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Bolt; Green Point's P12 and P13 are unavailable on Sunday, and race-day pick-up points should be confirmed in the current notices.
- **MyCiTi**: MyCiTi is Cape Town's bus rapid transit; a Myconnect card costs R40 at kiosks. Weekend closures affect MyCiTi services, and this page recommends using the free event shuttles listed above.
- **Bike and walking**: there is a free bicycle park at P4 near the Stadium start. Runners staying in Green Point, the V&A Waterfront or Mouille Point can use the official pedestrian routes; marshals are in place from 05:00.
- **Driving advice**: the organiser discourages driving into the race village. If a car is unavoidable, use Park & Ride or Park & Walk, and park at Battery Park in the V&A (free, first come, first served) or Silos (paid, sold at the Expo). The 2024 guide required all cars to be parked by 05:00; the 2026 page does not repeat that sentence, but the closure logic is the same. "The organiser did not repeat it" is not permission to park anywhere.

Airport-to-city travel times, train frequency and the order in which roads reopen are not fully published by the organiser. Those are "official not published; check the airport, PRASA, MyCiTi and race-day notices" items, and this page does not invent them.

## Season and weather

The calendar needs correcting first. The official 2024 page gives 19–20 October 2024. But the 2025 race could not go ahead, the 2026 race moved to 24 May, and the 2027 race is set for 23 May 2027. The current official window is late autumn/early winter in the southern hemisphere, not October. Preparing with October weather for a May race mismatches temperature and wind.

To prepare for the same window as the 2026 race, this page queried the Open-Meteo historical archive for 17–25 May 2026 (seven days before to one day after race day) at the Cape Town grid point. The API returned a grid point at 33.848858 S / 18.49315 E, elevation 12 m, timezone Africa/Johannesburg, with nine daily records:

- Daily maximum: 17.2 °C to 24.0 °C, median 20.6 °C.
- Daily minimum: 10.6 °C to 15.5 °C, median 12.7 °C.
- Two of the nine days had at least 1 mm of precipitation: 19 May with 1.9 mm and 22 May with 1.2 mm. No day reached 5 mm.
- Daily maximum wind: 8.5 to 20.9 km/h, median 12.8 km/h.
- Race day, 24 May: maximum 20.6 °C, minimum 14.1 °C, 0.0 mm precipitation, maximum wind 11.6 km/h.

These are **modelled values for a grid point, not measurements on the course**, and they are not a forecast for 23 May 2027. They describe the scale of the window, not the wind shift at Sea Point, the felt temperature on the promenade or the sun exposure in the Company Gardens. Cape Town's coastal wind can be markedly stronger than the grid-point daily maximum. The race starts in waves from 07:50 and closes at 15:50, so later runners meet the warmer part of the day. Use the official and meteorological short-range forecast before race day, and plan for wind, rain and sun.

The organiser's pre-race bulletin says five emergency misting zones are installed if race-day temperatures are high. That treats heat as an expected risk, not an anomaly. In the 2026 post-race report, athletes mentioned early humidity and wind rather than extreme heat; both can occur in May. "May is autumn" is not a guarantee of cool weather.

## Difficulty

The organiser publishes no single difficulty grade, and this page does not invent an official one. On verifiable dimensions, this is a **moderate, fast road marathon** whose difficulty comes from wind, humidity and the position of the late climb, not from altitude or technical terrain.

- 42.2 km, starting and finishing in Green Point, with an elevation range of 8–41 m; there is no altitude issue.
- 215 m total ascent and 209 m total descent; 21 km uphill running, 20 km downhill running and 1 km flat. It is not flat-flat, but it is not a hilly course.
- The longest and steepest climb is in the Company Gardens at 30.7–32.1 km, after about three-quarters of the race.
- 36.3–41.4 km is the Sea Point out-and-back, with the same stretch covered twice and exposed to coastal wind.
- The official cut-off is 6.5 hours, the final cut-off is 15:50, and there are 10 cut-off points from 7.0 km to 41.4 km.
- The 2026 men's winner ran 2:04:55 and the women's winner 2:23:18; the first ten men all beat the old 2:08:16 course record. That proves the course is fast, not that it is easy for every runner.

From those conditions: a sub-3-hour runner's main risks are going out too fast in the first 20 km, losing speed on the Company Gardens climb, and fighting the headwind on the Sea Point return. A 4–5-hour runner's main risks are late-morning heat, hydration, and the time margin between cut-off points. Neither goal requires trail kit, but both require road-marathon pacing.

The site's [pace calculator](/en/tools/pace/) converts a target time into average pace; the [split table](/en/tools/splits/) can divide 42.2 km into 5 km or 10 km segments. For the training meaning of the marathon distance, read [the marathon distance](/en/articles/marathon-distance/). These tools do arithmetic; they do not replace the official cut-off table. Any split plan must treat the route-map cut-offs as hard limits.
