## 60-second summary: what this conservancy trail race is

The Lewa Safari Marathon is a loop race held inside the Lewa Wildlife Conservancy in northern Kenya, organised by Tusk in partnership with the Lewa Wildlife Conservancy and co-headline sponsored by Safaricom and Huawei. The event centres on fundraising for wildlife conservation and community development: the full marathon is 42.2 km (26 miles) over two conservancy loops, the half marathon 21.1 km (13 miles) over one loop, with a 10 km Executive corporate race and a 5 km Junior run on the same venue. The course follows rough dirt roads through savannah plains, river banks and acacia woodland; it is undulating dirt-track racing, not an alpine mountain trail.

Preparation for this race focuses on mid-altitude effort, dirt-road undulation, heat, dust and long-travel fatigue rather than altitude adaptation. Entry runs through two channels: international entrants register via the `.com` portal with a GBP 150 entry fee and a GBP 1,750 minimum fundraising requirement per person; Kenyan and East African residents register via the `.co.ke` portal with no minimum fundraising. Medically, the event places first-aid posts and Red Cross personnel every 2.5 km, with an AMREF tent at the finish, helicopter standby and medical aircraft on call; but the bundled AMREF cover is air-ambulance evacuation only, not medical insurance, and runners must hold their own comprehensive medical insurance covering emergency evacuation.

- **Distance and direction.** Full 42.2 km two loops; half 21.1 km one loop; 10 km and 5 km are shorter categories on the same venue; start and finish are at the unified event camp in the conservancy.
- **Elevation.** About page: about 5,500 ft (about 1,676 m); race rules PDF: average altitude 6,000 ft (about 1,830 m). Both official figures are kept side by side.
- **Next edition.** The official homepage and Tusk event page both state 15 May 2027; the 2026 edition was 27 June. Exact start time on the official pages reviewed is before 7:00 AM, per the edition manual.

## History and fundraising origins

The race began in 2000 with 180 entries at its debut. The conservancy experience page describes growth to just over 1,200 participants yearly; the 2025 official post records about 1,400 participants that year. The Tusk event page gives the cumulative basis: about 1,400 runners each year, more than 25,000 participants in the event's history, and over USD 9 million raised across 25 years for wildlife conservation and community development in Kenya, including black rhino and Grevy's zebra protection, healthcare, water, infrastructure and education. Tusk also records six hospitals and clinics supported each year in and around the conservancy, and more than 10,000 schoolchildren benefiting through bursaries and school support.

Conservancy wildlife basis: the Big Five, more than 70 animal species and over 400 bird species; Lewa holds about 12% of Kenya's black and white rhinos and the world's largest Grevy's zebra population (Tusk phrases this as more than 10% of the world population). Keep the fundraising public-good identity separate from the racing identity: the fundraising obligation is an entry condition on the international channel, while course difficulty, cutoffs and safety rules are not relaxed for the conservation context.

Age: full and half marathon open to entrants aged 18 and over; junior categories cover ages 10–13 and 14–17.

Corporate teams run 4–9 teammates; corporate registration centres on the half marathon, with full marathon and spectator entries addable.

## Course direction, loops and geometry

**Route shape.** The course sits inside Lewa Conservancy: the half runs one loop and the full two loops of the same loop; the Executive 10 km and Junior 5 km are shorter categories on the same venue. The rules PDF describes the 21 km loop as rough dirt roads undulating across the conservancy; the About page describes rocky trails across savannah plains, river banks and acacia woodland. The full's second loop re-enters at the split point, which is also the cutoff checkpoint.

**Elevation and surface.** Two official elevation figures sit side by side: About page about 5,500 ft (about 1,676 m), rules PDF average altitude 6,000 ft (about 1,830 m). This band is below mountain-race altitude; the load is dirt-road undulation, heat and dust rather than acute altitude illness. Scree and rutty surfaces demand stable downhill and cornering technique. The official pages reviewed this pass did not publish a per-segment climb table, cumulative ascent/descent totals or a split elevation profile; the rules and event pages provide downloadable route map, site map and terrain profile PDFs, but no downloadable GPX/KML/GeoJSON track or track redistribution licence was located.

## Common misreadings

- **"This is an African mountain trail race."** The course is about 1,700–1,800 m on undulating conservancy dirt loops, not an alpine mountain course; training should not copy a high-altitude race plan.
- **"GBP 150 is the whole entry price."** On the international channel GBP 150 is the entry fee, with a GBP 1,750 minimum fundraising on top; the local channel charges Kenyan shillings with no fundraising requirement, and the two bases must not be mixed.
- **"The AMREF bundle is medical insurance."** AMREF Maisha Bronze covers only air-ambulance evacuation within Kenya (up to two times a year); it is not medical insurance, and runners still need their own comprehensive medical insurance covering emergency evacuation.
- **"Wildlife charges runners on the course."** Armed rangers patrol the course at all times, and a helicopter plus Supercub light aircraft monitor large species and guide them away; rules keep runners on the designated course and official toilets, but official material does not promise animals will never appear near the route.
- **"The full is simply two halves, so time doubles naturally."** The second loop is constrained by the 10:30 AM split cutoff; those who miss it are recorded as half finishers and cannot make up the second loop later.
- **"The official PDFs are downloadable GPX."** The route map, site map and terrain profile are PDFs; no track file was published this pass, and the PDF line must not be treated as certified geometry.

## Timing, cutoff and downgrade rules

**Cutoff and split point.** The terms ask full entrants to declare they can finish within six hours; if they have not crossed the half/full split point by 10:30 AM, they are not allowed to start the second loop and are recorded as half-marathon finishers. The rules PDF likewise states full runners must start the second loop by 10:30 AM, three hours after the start. 10:30 is the latest split-point passage, not an overall finish limit; the six-hour finish ability is a self-declaration at entry.

**Category switches and disqualification.** On race day, category switches are not permitted: half to full, 10 km to half, half to 10 km; attempting one leads to immediate disqualification. The only exception is a marshal-ordered downgrade for medical reasons, safety concerns or official timing cutoffs; results are recorded at the permitted distance and carry no prize eligibility. The rules PDF also lists disqualification for a wrong or missing race number, swapping numbers with another runner, or deviating from the route.

**Items not published.** The official pages reviewed this pass did not publish chip-timing method, gun-vs-chip time distinction, split timing points, short-category finish limits, pacer arrangements, or post-cutoff sweep arrangements. These follow the edition runner manual, timing table and on-the-day bulletins.

## Aid, medical and support arrangements

**Course aid.** Water stations are placed every 2.5 km, offering water, energy drink, oranges and bananas; food is provided at the finish; misting stations are set up on course. Each station carries a first-aid post and a Red Cross member. The local FAQ mentions bush toilets at the stations.

**Medical and evacuation.** Medical cover is layered: first aid every 2.5 km; helicopter on standby at the AMREF finish tent; equipped aircraft on standby for evacuation to Nairobi; a recovery tent at the finish staffed by doctors, medics and physios; one Red Cross ambulance at the finish plus three ambulances around the course. The terms state only temporary medical facilities are available via AMREF and local hospitals; if the organisers pay evacuation, medical, accommodation or other expenses on the participant's behalf, the participant reimburses them.

**Medical and rescue resources (official page basis).**

**AMREF bundle and insurance boundary.** The international registration page lists an included AMREF Maisha Bronze month subscription (26 June – 9 July), covering up to two air evacuations within Kenya annually; this is not medical insurance, only air-ambulance cover, and all runners must hold comprehensive medical insurance. The two local FAQ pages differ: the registration FAQ lists compulsory subsidised AMREF medical evacuation cover at KSh 850; the 2026 general FAQ lists KSh 1,500 for 14 days for those not already holding cover. Evacuation without cover can cost over USD 4,000, payable before treatment or flight. Exact scope, duration and fee follow that edition's AMREF terms.

**Packet collection and check-in.** 2026 basis: packet collection Friday 26 June 9:00–18:00; race-morning check-in 5:30–6:00 AM; start before 7:00 AM; the disclaimer must be signed. The terms permit search of participants, belongings, vehicles, passengers and baggage at the venue, designated parking and before boarding event transport.

## Gear and race rules

**Official rules points.** The rules PDF and terms state: no route deviation; race numbers must not be wrong, missing or swapped; no on-day category switch; walkers keep left, runners keep right; bicycles are prohibited on course; spectators are marshalled by volunteers. Prohibited items include legal/controlled substances, performance-enhancing substances or methods, weapons, smoke bombs, compressed gas containers, blades of any kind, glass bottles, and unlicensed communication equipment including radio transmitters; smoking is restricted to designated areas. No littering or disturbance of the natural environment at or around the venue.

**Suggested carried gear (based on dirt road, heat and dust, not an official mandatory list).** Good-quality well-fitting trail/dirt-road shoes (the terms explicitly require this), sun hat and sunscreen, sunglasses, hydration and portable supplies, race number and ID; spare clothing and cooling items for the heat. The official pages reviewed this pass did not publish a standalone mandatory gear list; any edition gear inspection follows the runner manual.

**Environmental and venue rules.** The course passes through conservancy and community surroundings; follow marked routes and official instructions; use official toilets and do not relieve yourself in the bush; do not disturb animals or vegetation. If animals appear near the route, follow ranger, marshal and air-control instructions; do not chase, feed or approach on your own.

## Travel and safety: wildlife, medical and vaccines

**Wildlife-area rules.** The course is inside the conservancy; large species are monitored by armed rangers on constant patrol and by helicopter and Supercub light aircraft in the air, which guide animals away from runners (Tusk records the helicopter guiding animals such as white rhino away). Runners must stay on the designated course and use official toilets; they must not leave the route to chase, feed or approach animals; when animals approach, follow ranger, marshal and air-control instructions. Spectators are marshalled into designated areas by volunteers.

**Medical posts and withdrawal path.** First-aid posts run every 2.5 km; the finish AMREF tent holds the standby helicopter, with medical aircraft available for evacuation to Nairobi; the finish recovery tent is staffed by doctors, medics and physios, with an ambulance at the finish and three around the course. If you are unfit to continue, report to the nearest post or medical staff and stop, withdraw or transfer per medical instructions; do not leave the course to hitch a ride forward to keep scoring.

**Vaccines and travel health (official/WHO general requirements only; no individual medical advice).** Kenya Ministry of Health: travellers arriving from yellow-fever-endemic countries should present a valid yellow fever certificate; exemptions include infants under 9 months (except high-risk epidemic settings), pregnant women (except high-risk outbreak situations), severe egg-protein allergy, severe immunodeficiency due to symptomatic HIV/AIDS or other causes, and thymus disorders. WHO DON361: Kenyan authorities require yellow fever vaccination for international travellers over one year entering Kenya; WHO recommends vaccination from age 9 months except for itineraries limited to listed low-risk areas including Nairobi and Mombasa; the International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis (ICVP) becomes valid 10 days after vaccination and remains lifelong; a single approved dose gives lifelong protection and no booster is required as an entry condition. WHO general precaution: avoid mosquito bites with repellent and insecticide-treated nets; transmission risk is highest during the day and early evening; seek medical advice promptly if symptoms suggestive of infection appear. Malaria and other travel-health risks and preventive medication should be assessed before travel with a clinic qualified in travel medicine; this page does not replace a doctor's assessment of individual health.

**Legal and liability boundary.** The participation terms are governed by Kenyan law; the organiser may refuse admission or remove a participant without compensation; the event may be cancelled, postponed, interrupted or stopped for government acts, adverse weather, civil disorder, terror, pandemic or other unforeseeable circumstances. Except for self-catering camps, accommodation is provided by independent contractors, and the organiser disclaims liability for their acts, omissions or services.

## Training transfer

1. **Train for the 1,700–2,000 m mid-altitude dirt-road undulation.** This elevation does not create mountain-adaptation demand, but the two-loop dirt undulation loads muscular endurance, joint stability and cornering technique. Training should add dirt-road and undulating long runs with controlled downhill and cornering stride; altitude and warm-up framework: [Altitude and running](/en/knowledge/altitude-and-running/); uphill/downhill technique: [Uphill and downhill running basics](/en/knowledge/uphill-and-downhill/).
2. **Treat heat, dryness and dust as race-specific conditions.** The May and June grid highs run about 21–22 °C median, with direct sun, road dust and an on-course surface slightly below the grid adding heat stress; dehydration and heat illness are the main risks. Build hydration rhythm and sweat-rate self-testing: [Hydration basics](/en/knowledge/hydration/) and [Sweat-rate tool](/en/tools/sweat-rate/); heat adaptation: [Running in heat and humidity](/en/knowledge/heat-and-humidity/).
3. **Allow for long travel and jet-lag buffer.** International runners connect through Nairobi to the conservancy; travel fatigue, diet change and dehydration directly affect race-day state. Reserve an arrival adaptation day; travel-fatigue handling: [Jet lag and race travel](/en/knowledge/jet-lag-and-race-travel/).
4. **Back-count two-loop pace from the split cutoff.** The second loop must be entered by 10:30 AM; do not run the first loop on feel alone. Segment pacing: [Race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/); carried gear trade-offs: [Trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/); walk through the [race checklist](/en/tools/race-checklist/) before departure.

## Race-day execution card

**The day before.** Confirm bib, ID and signed disclaimer; check next-morning check-in (5:30–6:00 AM) and meeting point; inspect shoes, sun protection, hydration and spare clothing; leave insurance and emergency contact details with the team/event contact; rest early with no extra intensity.

**First loop.** Start at a held effort, adapting to dirt and undulation; hydrate and eat at each 2.5 km station; watch weather, dust and condition; before the half/full split, assess whether you can complete the second loop within the limit, and report early if condition drops.

**Second loop (full).** Enter the second loop by 10:30 AM; hold the planned rhythm through the second lap, where dehydration and muscular fatigue amplify dirt-track mistakes; follow medical-team guidance in recovery and do not force a make-up lap.

**Stop and withdrawal rules.** Persistent vomiting without food intake, unsteady gait, altered awareness, chest pain or obvious palpitations, or heat-exhaustion signs (dizziness, stopped sweating, abnormal temperature): take the nearest post for medical assessment and stop or withdraw per instructions. Missing the split cutoff means being recorded as a half finisher under the rules; do not switch categories on your own.
