## Verification status

- **Date and scale:** the 2026 race was held on 20 September; the organiser says the event was founded in 1984, and the 2026 field was 16,384 runners from 64 countries. Edition numbers differ across sources, so this page does not use them in any calculation.
- **Official page status:** as checked on 20 November 2026, `maratondebuenosaires.com` still presents 20 September 2026 as the “Próxima edición” and 21 September 2025 as the “Última edición”. It publishes no 2027 date, entry page or results link. That is an unmaintained page, not 2027 information.
- **Course map:** the official page displays a 42K map whose file is still named `Recorrido-42K-2024.jpg`; there is no 2026-specific map, no GPX and no per-kilometre geometry. ## 2. The route: Palermo out, around the eastern city, back to Palermo

Combining the 2024 official map with InfoBaires' post-race description gives a city loop: **Palermo — Recoleta — city centre — Puerto Madero — La Boca — Palermo**.

1. **Palermo opening (about 0–13 km):** from Figueroa Alcorta y Dorrego, west around the River Plate and Golf area, then back along Figueroa Alcorta past the Planetario and Rosedal. The road is wide, but the field is dense; the first 5 km are easy to over-run.
2. **Recoleta and the centre (about 13–22 km):** along Libertador / Santa Fe into Recoleta, past the Recoleta cemetery and Teatro Colón, approaching the Obelisco and 9 de Julio. Repeated turns and junctions break the rhythm.
3. **Plaza de Mayo and Puerto Madero (about 22–30 km):** past Casa Rosada and Plaza de Mayo into Puerto Madero, along the waterfront near Puente de la Mujer and the edge of the Reserva Ecológica. Open, little shade; late-morning heat is felt directly.
4. **La Boca (about 30–35 km):** south to La Boca, past Boca Juniors and the Caminito area, then back north. Narrow blocks and turns make this the section most easily slowed by crowds and surface detail.
5. **Return to Palermo (about 35–42.195 km):** along Paseo Colón / de Mayo / 9 de Julio toward the western centre, then along Figueroa Alcorta to the Palermo finish. There are small undulations and turns near the end; it is not a long straight sprint.

This is not a flat riverfront line with no turns. Bridges, plazas, narrow streets and repeated direction changes are part of the race; treating the map's kilometre markers as “one long avenue” will misjudge the second half.

## 3. Ten corrals from 06:57 to 07:11, and a hard 13:00 close

The 2026 start was staged by declared time, not a single gun. The regulations are more precise than the home page:

| --- | ---: | --- |
| P | 06:57 | Athletes with disabilities |
| Elite | 07:00 | Elite |
| 1 | 07:02 | Sub-3:00 |
| 2 | 07:03 | 3:01–3:30 |
| 3 | 07:04 | 3:31–3:45 |
| 4 | 07:06 | 3:46–4:00 |

| 6 | 07:09 | 4:16–4:30 |
| 7 | 07:10 | 4:31–5:00 |
| 8 | 07:11 | Over 5:00 |

The official limit is **6:00:00**, with the finish closing at **13:00** and “under no circumstances” extending beyond it. A runner in the last corral at 07:11 therefore has only **5:49** until 13:00, or **8:16 /km**, not the 8:31 /km that six hours implies. The regulations do not say whether the cut-off is gun time or chip time; the conservative reading is gun time, and later corrals should not treat six hours as net time from their own crossing.

## 4. Sixteen aid stations, but fruit is not at every one

The regulations place aid every **2.5 km** from **km 5** to km 40, plus the finish at 42.195: 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, 15, 17.5, 20, 22.5, 25, 27.5, 30, 32.5, 35, 37.5, 40 and 42.195.

The regulations promise “hydration and food, including fruit, from km 5” but do not list products station by station. Infobae's 2026 preview is more specific: water and isotonic drink every 2.5 km, and **banana only at km 20 and km 30**. This page follows the more specific report:

| Kilometre | Water / isotonic | Banana |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 / 7.5 / 10 / 12.5 / 15 / 17.5 | Yes | Not listed |
| 20 | Yes | Yes |
| 22.5 / 25 / 27.5 | Yes | Not listed |
| 30 | Yes | Yes |
| 32.5 / 35 / 37.5 / 40 | Yes | Not listed |
| 42.195 | Water, finish food | — |

The regulations also say each runner should take only one bottle at the tables, and that ambulances, medical tents, doctors and first-aiders are deployed along the course. For a 16,000-runner city marathon, the bottleneck is not the total aid but the crowd in the first 5 km and the queues after km 30.

Two finish rules are easy to miss: runners must leave the finish chute promptly and may not wait inside it, and warming up in front of the start line can bring disqualification.

Awards are trophies only: top three in the conventional, wheelchair, B1 and B2/B3 categories, with at least five entrants in a category; the regulations publish no prize money. The 2026 elite depth therefore came from performance and records, not from an advertised purse.

## 6. Weather: September is not automatically cool

Using Open-Meteo's ERA5 archive for 20 September in each year from 2015 to 2025 gives 11 daily records:

| Metric | Range | Median |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily maximum | 14.4–25.6 °C | 18.8 °C |
| Daily minimum | 5.7–15.3 °C | 10.7 °C |
| Days ≥1 mm | 2 / 11 | — |
| Days ≥5 mm | 1 / 11 | — |

Two things are true at once: dawn can be 6–10 °C, so do not start in a singlet only; the maximum can reach 25–26 °C, so the second half still carries heat risk. A 07:00 start usually means 10–15 °C in the first half and near midday by the second, with noticeably more heat on the unshaded Puerto Madero and La Boca sections. The regulations publish no humidity data and this page does not guess; prepare for a cold dawn, a sunny late morning and possible wind.

## 7. Travel: AEP is in Palermo, EZE is south of the city

| Airport | Location | To the Palermo start | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aeroparque Jorge Newbery (AEP) | In the city, Palermo, on the river | About 2–4 km, 5–15 minutes by taxi | Domestic and regional flights, least travel time |
| Ezeiza (EZE) | About 32 km south-west of the centre | About 50 minutes by road in normal traffic | Long-haul international flights |

The city tourism page says EZE is about 32 km from the centre and about 50 minutes away; AEP is in Palermo and within 25 minutes of the main hotels; AEP mainly serves domestic and neighbouring-country flights, while most long-haul traffic uses EZE; Tienda León runs buses from EZE to the city. The start is at Figueroa Alcorta y Dorrego, so staying in Palermo is the robust choice: no cross-city journey on race morning and easier bag drop, warm-up and toilets.

The regulations state that the event has no parking and that parking on the race streets is prohibited. Do not drive to the start; book a car or use a taxi/ride-hail, and build in the road-closure time before 07:00.

## 8. Difficulty: flat is not easy

The difficulty is not vertical gain but four things:

1. **Scale:** 16,384 runners from 64 countries in 2026, including 10,615 Argentines, 5,769 foreigners and 5,246 women. The first 5 km and the aid stations after km 30 are crowded, and the stop-start rhythm breaks pacing.
2. **Turns and surfaces:** the city loop has repeated turns, bridges, plazas and narrow blocks; asphalt, stone, speed bumps and manhole covers vary. Wet weather makes the turns slippery.
3. **Heat and wind:** late morning can reach 25–26 °C; Puerto Madero and La Boca have little shade, and river wind can raise or lower the felt temperature.
4. **The hard six hours:** the finish closes at 13:00; there is no “a few minutes late still counts”, and later corrals have less than six hours.

The route passes the Caminito area at about km 30–35; the photograph only identifies the La Boca street fabric, not the specific 2026 course line, street width or race-day crowd.*

Medical and withdrawal: ambulances, medical tents, doctors and first-aiders are along the course; use a medical tent or aid-station staff rather than leaving the course for a lift. Dizziness, chills or confusion are stop signals, not something to push through.

## 9. Course records: 2:05:00 and 2:23:36

The 2026 men's winner was 3:28 outside the course record; the women's winner broke it.

| Item | Time | Athlete / year | Note |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Men's course record | 2:05:00 | Evans Chebet, 2019 | World Athletics 2019 top list and the event record table; the 2019 race report says 2:05:02, a 2-second difference |
| Women's course record | 2:23:36 | Rebecca Chesir, 2026 | Official 2026 result; media reports say 2:23:31, a 5-second difference |
| 2019 women's record | 2:25:46 | Rodah Tanui, 2019 | Took almost five minutes off the then record |

2019 was the performance breakthrough: Chebet 2:05:00 and Tanui 2:25:46 moved Buenos Aires to “the fastest course in Latin America”. The 2026 edition is a different scale: 16,384 runners. The two years together explain why the race works both as an elite flat course and as a mass city marathon.

## Training conversion: city marathon preparation

Buenos Aires is a 42.195 km city asphalt loop with many turns, junctions and narrow streets, and little shade late in the race. Preparation centres on marathon-pace endurance, long-run break-in and warm-weather tolerance, not weekly mileage alone. General methods are in [marathon distance](/en/knowledge/marathon-distance/), [ultra-trail training plan](/en/knowledge/trail-ultra-training-plan/), [recovery day](/en/knowledge/recovery-day/) and [load and overtraining](/en/knowledge/load-and-overtraining/).

| Phase | Focus | Practical content | Race-day constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-16 to T-10 weeks | Aerobic base | Easy runs plus one tempo run a week; gradually extend long runs | The city course needs a steady aerobic base |
| T-10 to T-6 weeks | Long run and pace | Long run to 30–36 km; run segments at target pace | Many turns break the pace at junctions |
| T-6 to T-2 weeks | Fueling and heat adaptation | Test race fueling and salt plan; one warm long run | Late race heats up by late morning with little shade |
| T-2 to race | Taper | Shorten distance, keep short rhythm; no new shoes or kit | Recover fatigue before race day; see [taper and strength](/en/knowledge/strength-and-taper/) |

Boundary: this is a generic preparation framework, not medical advice. Runners with pre-existing cardiopulmonary conditions or recent injuries should consult a doctor first. Whether a runner can finish a marathon also depends on previous longest distance and training recovery.

## Race-day execution card: five-segment rhythm and cut-off buffer

| Segment | Mile window | Rhythm and fueling | Check |
